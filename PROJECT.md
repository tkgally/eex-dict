# PROJECT.md — the charter of the TKG English Learner's Dictionary

*Owner-set. Sessions never edit this file. A Routine session may not reopen any decision here; it may queue a question for the owner in `wiki/open-questions.md` with a working assumption and proceed. Each decision also has a page under `wiki/decisions/` that sessions cite.*

The TKG English Learner's Dictionary is an original English-English learner's dictionary written by language models under the direction of Tom Gally, a lexicographer, and published as a static website from this repository. It is built by scheduled autonomous sessions. The founding session ran on 2026-09-16.

## Product

- **Name and home.** TKG English Learner's Dictionary; repository `tkgally/eex-dict`. A domain name comes later; the site uses relative links so it works at any base URL.
- **Reader.** Intermediate learners and above, roughly CEFR B1 to C2. Entries are complete enough for advanced learners; definitions are plain enough for B1.
- **Variety.** American English is primary for spelling, pronunciation, and examples. British spelling, pronunciation, and usage are recorded in structured variant fields, never mixed into prose. **Later phase:** beyond the basic vocabulary the dictionary actively seeks vocabulary and grammar from World Englishes (Indian, Singaporean, Nigerian, Philippine, Caribbean, and others) and from forms of English evolving online without strong ties to a region. The region label set and the headword queue can grow to hold them.
- **Size and pace.** Milestone 1 is a closed defining vocabulary of about 2,500 words, every one with a complete entry, so the dictionary is self-contained. Then expansion by frequency band toward roughly 10,000 headwords, then by internal closure and learner need. Cap: 20 new entries per run, each fully verified before it is published.
- **Headword selection.** No external frequency data and no external word list. The defining vocabulary, the headword queue, and the frequency bands come from the models' own judgment of how basic, common, and important a word is, gathered from several models and settled editorially. Selection is editorial and the site says so.
- **What counts as a headword.** Phrasal verbs get their own entries. Idioms and fixed phrases are addressable sub-entries under a keyword entry. Affixes and common abbreviations are included. Proper nouns only when lexicalized ("to google"). Derived words get their own entries when their meaning is not predictable; otherwise they appear in the base word's word-family box.
- **Entry unit.** One entry per headword plus part of speech. Same-spelling, same-part-of-speech homographs get separate entries only when unrelated in meaning and origin. A word-family box ties related entries together.
- **Definitions.** A traditional phrasal definition is the primary field. An optional plain-prose explanation field exists for words that need it. The two are separate fields so a later adaptation can choose either.
- **Defining vocabulary.** A soft rule: a definition may use a word outside the defining vocabulary only if that word's entry exists and is linked; CI flags violations. Examples and notes are looser. The first sense of basic words is not held to the strict list.
- **Senses.** Most-useful-first ordering. A written splitting principle so the model does not over-split. Sub-senses allowed. A short "core idea" line at the top of polysemous entries.
- **Examples.** Two to four per sense, written by the model, carrying typical collocations and grammar patterns, with no checkable real-world facts (dates, statistics, named events, science claims). Settings culturally neutral. Personal names allowed but short and used across cultures (Kim, Mari, Sam, Ana, Lee, Omar, Yuki, Ravi, Sara); no names with strong American or British associations; no brand names.
- **Grammar.** A closed code set for countability, transitivity, verb patterns, gradability, adjective position, and number restrictions. On the site both the spelled-out label and the code are visible, the code linking to a grammar key page. In entries every term is spelled out; no abbreviations ("someone", "something", "for example", never "sb", "sth", "e.g."). Inflections are generated programmatically from rules plus an exceptions table.
- **Usage labels.** An original label set for this dictionary: closed vocabularies for register, region, domain, currency, and attitude. Plain words, no abbreviations.
- **Taboo, vulgar, and offensive vocabulary.** Vulgar and sexual vocabulary is included with clear labels; slurs are excluded for now. If the Routine model declines to write an entry, it records the headword and the reason in the curator queue, marks the queue item `declined`, and moves on. A run never stalls on a refusal.
- **Extras.** A collocation box, a word-family box, synonym-discrimination notes, and common learner errors, on every entry where they apply. Etymology only when verified against two open sources and relevant to understanding the word as used today; otherwise omitted.
- **Level indicators.** An editorial frequency band per headword (1 to 5, estimated by several models and settled by the session, shown on the site in words such as "very common" with a note that bands are the dictionary's own estimates) and a defining-vocabulary flag. No CEFR levels.
- **Adaptation layer.** Every entry carries brief language-neutral notes of five kinds (semantic splits common across languages; grammar traps such as countability or argument structure; cultural or encyclopedic background; false-friend risks for internationalisms; pronunciation traps) and empty per-language slots for equivalents and notes. After milestone 1, Japanese is seeded on about 200 entries to validate the schema.
- **Pronunciation.** IPA in General American with stress and syllable breaks, and a British transcription, both at launch. An audio slot left empty; text-to-speech audio is on the roadmap. Source: model knowledge, tested in the founding session and thereafter checked by agreement between models. Open pronouncing resources (the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary for American, Wiktionary for British) may be consulted at run time to check a transcription, but nothing from them is stored in the repository; an entry records only that a check was made and what it found.
- **Licence and openness.** Data CC0 1.0, code MIT, and the dictionary is to be as open and unencumbered as possible. The repository contains only the project's own work: no data file under any other licence is vendored, copied, or derived from, however permissive that licence is. That excludes frequency lists, word lists, pronouncing dictionaries, WordNet, and Wiktionary as data. Such resources may be consulted at run time to check a fact, in a temporary location outside the repository, and the entry then records only the verdict. Copyrighted dictionaries are never consulted while drafting. A periodic originality check confirms that definitions, labels, and notes are not inadvertently reproducing another dictionary.

## Process

- **Drafting.** The Routine's model drafts entries in-session from its own knowledge and the style guide. Two non-Anthropic reviewers on OpenRouter check every field. Switching to flagship drafting via OpenRouter is a decision the owner makes after seeing the seed set; the drafter is a configuration setting.
- **External verification.** Pronunciation and inflections are checked by multi-model agreement and, where a run can reach one, by a run-time consultation of an open resource that leaves nothing in the repository. Frequency bands and regional labels are checked by multi-model agreement. Definitions, examples, grammar, labels, collocations, notes, and adaptation notes rely on multi-model review that reads every field, including notes.
- **Seed set.** The founding session wrote the style guide and the exemplar entries through the full pipeline, for the owner's review before the Routine starts.
- **Cadence.** One to six Routine runs per day; runs may overlap. The model and cadence may change later; nothing in the repository may depend on either.
- **Knowledge base.** The knowledge-base framework in `framework.md`, kept deliberately small, with hard word caps.
- **Reuse.** Proven mechanics from the owner's earlier dictionary project were ported; the schema, tools, and site were designed fresh.
- **Owner involvement.** A plain-English journal entry per session, also published on the site; a curator queue for decisions only the owner can make; an open-questions file with stated working defaults; an `inbox/` the owner can drop notes into.
- **URLs and IDs.** The slug is the ID (`wiki/conventions.md`). Site pages are per headword with per-entry anchors. Nothing is ever renumbered or deleted; renames go through a redirects file.
- **Site.** Static, mobile-first, no framework, built by CI into `docs/` and never committed; an AI-authorship disclosure on every page; search that understands inflected forms; browse, random, recent; hover or tap previews on every linked word; frequency bands displayed; adaptation fields behind a "translator's view" toggle.
