# Inflection exceptions table: the two-model check

*Founding session, 2026-09-16. `schema/inflection-exceptions.json` was written from the session's own knowledge (247 irregular verbs, 231 nouns, 81 adjectives, 82 adverbs, and the spelling lists for doubling, single-l, kept e, ie-to-y, -oes, -ves, -ck) and then read once by the two reviewer roles (`config/models.md`); raw replies in `experiments/inflection-exceptions-check-v1/`. Cost US$0.09.*

**Applied** (the session agreed): *dive* (the note wrongly called *dived* British; both are American, the past participle is *dived*); *get* (*got* as a past participle is also American in *have got*); *shrink* (*shrunken* as a past participle before a noun); *stride* (*strode* also as the past participle); *dry* (*dryest* removed from the note); *antenna*, *medium*, *pike*, *octopus* (notes were too narrow or presented a disputed form as standard); added *shave* (*shaven* before a noun), *thrive* (*throve*, *thriven* old-fashioned), *brother* (*brethren*), and the adverb *ill* (*worse*, *worst*).

**Rejected** (the session disagreed, reason logged here): *apt* (*apter*, *aptest* exist but learners are served by *more apt*); *dead* (kept not gradable: *deader* is informal wordplay, which the dictionary can note in the entry); *cruel* in the single-l list (the list also drives the British spelling note for *crueller*, so an adjective belongs there); the modals *can*, *may*, *shall*, *will* (modal verbs do not inflect in this table; *could*, *might*, *should*, *would* are headwords of their own).

**Not a table matter:** one reviewer asked whether *cost*, *spit*, and other common irregulars were covered; they are.

**Precision of the check:** reviewer A raised 9 problems and 8 gaps, of which 8 problems and 4 gaps were applied; reviewer B raised 3 problems and 5 gaps, of which 2 problems were applied. The table is checked again only when a run's inflection output is disputed by the panel.
