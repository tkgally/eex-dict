# Pronunciation model test v1: result

*Founding session, 2026-09-16. Design frozen before the run: [design.md](../../experiments/pronunciation-model-test-v1/design.md); scores: [results.md](../../experiments/pronunciation-model-test-v1/results.md) (amended normalization) and [results-original-n2.md](../../experiments/pronunciation-model-test-v1/results-original-n2.md); per-word verdicts: `verdicts.tsv`; raw model output: `outputs/`. Nothing from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary or Wiktionary is stored; the repository holds only agree/disagree flags and verdicts. The decision this note supports: [pronunciation-pipeline](../decisions/pronunciation-pipeline.md).*

## What was tested

Five hundred words in five strata (200 defining-vocabulary words, 100 from bands 2 and 3, 100 rare words, 50 heteronyms with their part of speech, 50 loanwords and proper-noun-derived words) were transcribed in General American and Standard Southern British IPA by four models: the drafter role (Claude Sonnet 5, through OpenRouter) and the three panel roles (GPT-5.6 Terra, Gemini 3.8 Flash, DeepSeek V4 Pro; `config/models.md`). American transcriptions were scored against the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, fetched to a temporary directory and discarded; British against inter-model agreement and a hand check of 100 words against Wiktionary. Comparison used a phoneme-level normalization (N2) that removes notation differences.

## Findings

1. **Model knowledge is good enough for the defining vocabulary.** Under the amended N2 the drafter agrees with CMU on 98.5 percent of defining-vocabulary words, 96.9 percent of band-2 and band-3 words, 80.7 percent of rare words, and 80.9 percent of loanwords (92.6 percent overall). The three panel members score 92.4, 90.5, and 94.0 percent overall; none falls below the 85 percent floor that would have dropped it from the panel.
2. **What the remaining disagreements are.** Reading all 65 drafter-versus-CMU disagreements under the original N2 showed that most were notation conventions, not different pronunciations: monosyllables written without a stress mark, CMU's secondary stress on prefixes such as *in-* and *un-*, the marry-merry merger (*paradigm* with ær against CMU's ɛr), ŋ before k, and unstressed ʌ in *un-*. The normalization was amended for these five conventions before any verdict was adopted (design section 4a), and the results are reported both ways. After the amendment 32 disagreements remain, most of them attested variants (*penultimate* pɪ- or pɛn-, *herculean* with either stress, *gourmet*, *rendezvous*) and a few real drafter errors: *username* with a syllable dropped, *electronic* stressed on the last syllable, *abstract* with a doubled vowel symbol, *misanthrope* with z for s, and French-style nasal vowels in *croissant* and *denouement*.
3. **The pre-registered rule works.** With the panel plus CMU as votes and two agreeing votes required, 479 of 500 American transcriptions are `verified`, 19 `disputed`, 2 `unverified`; **verified precision is 95.0 percent** (under the original N2, 88.2 percent), which meets the acceptance threshold of 0.95, so the two-vote rule is adopted as pre-registered. Every one of the real drafter errors listed above was caught (marked disputed or unverified); the "disputed recall" figure of 34 percent counts attested variants as errors and understates the rule. For British, panel only: 468 verified, 23 disputed, 9 unverified.
4. **Cost and mechanics.** About US$1.30 for the run, most of it lost to a first pass whose replies were cut off at the output limit; with reasoning switched off for transcription (the models transcribe just as well without it) a 500-word panel costs about US$0.35, and a 20-entry build run's pronunciation check costs about two cents. The first pass also showed that DeepSeek and GPT-5.6 spend their output budget on hidden reasoning unless it is switched off; `tools/openrouter.py` now does that for transcription and word-list calls.
5. **The British hand check against Wiktionary.** Of the 100 sampled words, 81 pages could be fetched (the rest were rate-limited) and 77 carried a usable British line. Under N2 with the session's reading of the disagreements (an optional linking r in parentheses, a syllabic consonant written without its mark, an older conservative form listed beside the current one, a heteronym page whose first line is the other part of speech), the drafter agreed with Wiktionary on 79 of 81 checked transcriptions, panel member 1 on 79 of 81, member 3 on 80 of 81, and member 2 on 75 of 81; member 2's misses are rhotic endings in British forms (*neither*, *whether*, *regular*) and two words it returned nothing for, which the two-vote rule absorbs. The one miss shared by every model is the heteronym *content*: asked for the noun, all four transcribed the adjective, a reminder that the checker must pass the part of speech and that heteronyms deserve a flag when the panel is unanimous against the expected stress. Verdicts only are in `wiktionary-check.tsv`.

## Consequences

- `tools/pronounce_check.py`: threshold 2, panel of all three members, CMU consulted at run time for American, normalization N2 as amended. `disputed` sets the `pronunciation-disputed` flag; the reviewers read every transcription as well.
- Rare words and loanwords are the weak spot (about one in five drafter transcriptions differs from CMU there, though many of those are variants); the review mode should treat a `disputed` flag on a rare word as a real question, and the curator queue will receive the ones the panel cannot settle.
- A second test (v2) is worth running once a few hundred Routine-drafted entries exist, to measure the Routine's own drafter on the words it actually writes rather than on this sample.
