# Inline marks: words named as words, quoted illustrations, and the headword in examples

*Ruled by the owner through `inbox/` on 2026-09-18 (feedback on the seed set, archived as `inbox/archive/20260918_feedback_on_seed_set.txt`): a word that stands for itself in a definition, explanation, or note, a phrase quoted to illustrate use, and the headword inside an example sentence must each be visibly marked, which means tagging them in the JSON. The owner left the form of the tagging and the display to the session. Implemented in `tools/eexlib.py`, `tools/link_words.py`, `tools/build_site.py`, and `tools/validate.py`; the writer's rule is [style guide](../style-guide.md) section 6.*

**The three cases.** In *All goes directly before a plural noun (all children) or before the*, the first *All* and *the* are words named as words; *all children* is language quoted as an illustration; and in the example sentence *All children need love*, *All* is the headword in use. A reader must be able to tell the three apart, and a print or screen dictionary does it typographically.

**Design.**

1. **Two marks in the prose fields, borrowed from markdown** because every drafting model already writes them without being told: `**word**` around a word or phrase named as a word (the site renders it `<b class="mention">`), `*phrase*` around language quoted as an illustration (`<i class="illus">`). Words inside either are linked like any other prose. The test for the first mark: *the word* can be put in front of it (*the word* all *goes before a plural noun*); for the second: it could be an example sentence or a piece of one.
2. **The headword in an example is not tagged by the drafter: the site marks it at build time** from the entry's own headword, its listed inflected forms, and its variant forms (`<b class="ex-hw">`), including a contracted form (*doesn't*, *can't*) and a possessive. The fact is already in the entry, the rule is mechanical, and it would otherwise cost a hand mark in every one of thousands of examples. Where the automatic rule cannot find the form, a form absent from the inflections table (*am*, *are*, *were*), a separated phrasal verb (*pick it up*), a form on the far side of a contraction (*I've*), the drafter marks it by hand with `**...**` and then marks every occurrence in that sentence, because a hand mark switches the automatic marking off for that sentence. `tools/validate.py` warns about an example that contains no form of the headword and no hand mark; for affixes, which live inside their words, it does not check.
3. **Where marks are not used:** collocation items, the text of a phrase, and the `incorrect` and `correct` forms of a learner error, which are quoted language as a whole; single-asterisk marks inside an example, which is itself the illustration. An asterisk never marks an ungrammatical form; only `learner_errors[].incorrect` holds one.
4. **Validation (errors, not warnings):** a mark is paired, has no space just inside it, stays on one line, does not nest, and does not sit inside a `[[slug|text]]` link override (the mark goes around the override: `**[[all-pron|all]]**`). Any other asterisk is malformed.
5. **Display:** bold for a mention, italic for an illustration, bold for the headword inside the italic example sentence, in three CSS classes (`mention`, `illus`, `ex-hw` in `tools/site/site.css`) so that the owner can change the look, a colour for instance, without touching the data. Search glosses, previews, the originality check, and the vocabulary lint read the prose with the marks stripped.
6. **The seed set.** Its 82 entries predate the rule. This session marked up 40 of them by hand (the model entries, the function words, the entries the feedback named, and the four drafts), one field at a time; the other 42 carry `markup-pending` in `provenance.flags` (a new value in `schema/vocabularies.json`) and are converted by the Routine's review runs while each entry is open for its second reading, the flag removed when done. No script rewrites a prose field, then or now.

**Rejected alternatives.** HTML-like tags (`<w>`, `<i>`): verbose, and an invitation to arbitrary HTML in the data. A third bracket syntax beside `[[slug|text]]`: unreadable and unlike anything a model writes unprompted. Tagging the headword in every example by hand: thousands of marks for a fact the entry already states, and the most error-prone of the three cases for a drafter.

**Reviewers.** The prompt in `tools/review_panel.py` tells both reviewers that the marks are house style; a plainly wrong mark is a minor `spelling-or-format` issue.
