# Part-of-speech discipline, consistency within an entry, and plain pronunciation notes

*From the owner's `inbox/` note of 2026-09-18, which forwarded a Japanese colleague's reading of the entries for *another* and *money* (archived as `inbox/archive/20260918_feedback_on_seed_set.txt`). The colleague asked for three things to go into the instructions: a stricter division of uses by part of speech, a check that an entry agrees with itself, and pronunciation notes a learner can follow. This page records the rules the session adopted and how they were applied; the writer's text is in the [style guide](../style-guide.md) (sections 4, 8, 11, 12, and 16), and the reviewers are told the same in `tools/review_panel.py`.*

**1. One part of speech per entry.** The charter fixes one entry per headword plus part of speech. A use of the word that belongs to another part of speech in the closed `pos` list is therefore not a sense, a subsense, an explanation, or an example of this entry: *another* standing alone (*I'll have another*) is a pronoun, so it belongs in `another-pron`, not in a remark inside `another-det`. The entry that would naturally mention the other use points to it in one clause of its usage note and in `see_also`, and the target is queued when it does not exist (`tools/queue.py add`). The reviewer verdict `headword_and_pos` now covers this. Applied on 2026-09-18 to `another-det` (pronoun use), `all-det` (pronoun use), `alone-adj` (adverb use), and `favorite-adj` (noun use); queue rows added for *another* pronoun and *alone* adverb (*all* pronoun and *favorite* noun were already queued).

**The open case.** The seed entries `be-v`, `have-v`, and `do-v` carry the auxiliary uses as senses, and `one-num` carries the pronoun uses, by a judgment call the drafters flagged in [seed-set-drafting](../notes/seed-set-drafting.md); the queue rows *be aux*, *have aux*, *do aux*, and *one pron* are marked duplicate on that basis. Under the rule those would be four more entries. The session did not restructure four reviewed entries on its own reading of a forwarded remark; the question is in [open-questions](../open-questions.md) with the working assumption that they stay as they are until the owner rules, and every new entry follows the rule.

**2. The entry agrees with itself.** *Another* said "a plus other" in one explanation and "a or an plus other" in the synonym-discrimination note. Two rules follow. Before an entry is submitted, it is read as a whole for contradictions between fields (style guide section 16), and the reviewer checklist gains a `consistency` verdict over the whole entry. And a claim about where a word comes from lives only in `etymology`, verified against two open sources (style guide section 12); every other field describes the present-day form (*another* is one word and already contains *an*) without a history. The wrong claim was an origin claim that had slipped into an explanation unverified.

**3. A grammar statement with a real exception** states the rule for ordinary use and names the exception as one, in the same field: *money* is uncountable in ordinary use; the plural *monies* belongs to legal and financial documents. Two flat statements side by side (*uncountable*; *the plural monies*) read as a contradiction (style guide section 8).

**4. Pronunciation notes in plain words.** *Another*'s note said "an unpronounced-r schwa". A B1 reader does not know *schwa*, *rhotic*, *voiced*, or *diphthong*, so `pronunciation.notes`, `pronunciation.variants[].note`, and every `adaptation.pronunciation` note now say the sound by comparison with a common word and give stress by a respelling in capitals (*uh-NUTH-er*); `tools/validate.py` rejects a fixed list of technical terms in those fields. Applied to `another-det`, `big-adj`, `each-other-pron`, `good-adj`, `house-n`, `please-interj`, and `thing-n`.

**5. Also from the colleague.** The length of the entries is about right, and a link from each entry to a Japanese-English dictionary would make the material much more useful to Japanese learners. That is what the `l1` layer of the [adaptation layer](adaptation-layer.md) is for; the Japanese seeding after milestone 1 is where it starts.
