# 2026-10-03 (19): nine closure words, ignore to practical

This was the third cycle of the run that started at 16:43 UTC. The first two cycles (a second reading of twelve verbs, and twelve new nouns from *failure* to *feather*) merged as pull requests #210 and #211. For this cycle the selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **closure**. A closure cycle writes entries for words that the dictionary already uses in its own definitions, examples, or notes but that have no entry yet, so that a reader can always look up a word they meet.

## Choosing the words

The claim tool gave ten queue rows. Closure rows are added by a script that guesses the part of speech, and `NEXT.md` warns that the guesses are often wrong. I checked each one:

- Four were not real headwords: *ignored* and *threads* are inflected forms, *furthest* is the superlative of *far*, and *document* had been tagged as an adjective. These were marked *duplicate* in the queue, with a note naming the real word.
- *Christian* stays on hold: its definition needs the name *Jesus Christ*, and the vocabulary check cannot yet accept proper names (open question 7).
- *cheat*, *fail*, and *hollow* had been queued as nouns, but the sentences that used them needed the verb or the adjective. The noun rows went back to the queue.

In their place I claimed the words that were really needed: **ignore**, **cheat**, and **fail** (verbs), **far** and **hollow** (adjectives), and **document** and **thread** (nouns). With **planet** and **practical**, which were right as queued, that made nine entries.

## What was written

Each entry was written by hand to the style guide. Some points of interest:

- **fail** has five senses, from not succeeding to letting someone down (*I feel I have failed my children*), and separates *fail a test* from *fail to do something*.
- **far** (adjective) covers *the far side*, *the far north*, the predicative use (*Is it far?*), and the political *far left* and *far right*. Its note explains *farther* and *further*. The adverb (*We didn't walk far*) is a separate entry, still to be written; it is now noted in `NEXT.md`.
- **ignore** warns translators that in some languages a look-alike word means *not know*.
- **document** warns that in some languages a similar word means an identity card.
- **thread** includes the online sense (*start a thread*) and the idioms *hang by a thread* and *pick up the threads*.

All 18 pronunciations were confirmed by the pronunciation panel (three outside models compared with each other and with the open CMU pronouncing dictionary).

## What the panel found and what I decided

The reviewer panel (two outside models that read every field) raised 48 issues, 35 of them blocking. I applied 34 and rejected 14, each logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Most of the real catches were claims I had stated too absolutely, or definitions that did not cover their own examples:

- **fail**: the "stop working" sense did not cover *the crops failed*; it now says *stop working or growing as it should*. My notes said that **fail** is never followed by an **-ing** form; that is only true of the sense *not do*, and the notes now say so. A learner error (*I failed in the exam*) was dropped, because that wording is normal in some varieties of English.
- **hollow**: *a hollow laugh* has no real feeling rather than no meaning; the definition now says *without real value, meaning, or feeling*.
- **ignore**: ignoring a person is not always done in anger. The learner error now uses a sentence that really is wrong (*I ignore how to get there*).
- **cheat** and **far**: two notes on American and British usage were softened to *usually* and *more common*.
- **planet** and **thread**: an example that did not show its phrase was replaced, and a learner error that was not really wrong was removed.

Rejected, with reasons: the claim that predicative *far* is normal in positive sentences (it is, with words like *too* or *from here*, but the note says *mainly* in questions and negatives, which is standard teaching); a request to mark *farther* by hand (the site marks listed forms itself); and the slug `let-down-phrv` (the house form for *let someone down*).

Four definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary (*sexual*, *extreme*, *curving*, *deliberately*) and were reworded.
