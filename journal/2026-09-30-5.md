Some highlights:

- *write* has seven senses. They go from making letters on paper, through writing a book and sending a letter, to writing a check, saving data on a computer, and a pen that *won't write*.
- Three entries have a synonym discrimination, a short note on how to choose between close words: *worry / anxious / concerned*, *amount / number / quantity*, and *anger / rage / fury*.
- The learner errors target common traps: *I am worry* (use *worried*), *a large amount of people* (use *number* with plural nouns), *in this afternoon* (no preposition before *this*), *I have wrote* (*written*), and confusing *angle* with *angel*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field of every entry. Between them they raised 49 issues. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 23 applied, 26 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

The most useful corrections:

- *animal* drew fourteen issues, most of them fair. My first sense said an animal is a creature "that can move and feel". That is not how people decide what counts as an animal, so the sense now names examples and contrasts animals with plants and people. My science sense said an animal is "any living thing that is not a plant". That is wrong, because mushrooms, for example, are neither. My third sense (the loose everyday use in *birds and animals*) had become a definition of *mammal*; it is now worded as the loose use it is. The usage note and the note for translators were softened to match.
- *acid* said an acid "can burn skin and damage metal". Weak acids, like the ones in lemons, do neither, so the definition now says acids taste sour when weak and burn when strong.
- *write*, in the sense of writing a check, said "fill in and sign". Writing a check does not always include signing it, so *sign* is gone.
- *worry*: my usage note said English uses *I'm worried* for a feeling now, which suggested *I'm worrying* is wrong. It is not; the note now allows both.
- *afternoon*: the note for translators gave six o'clock as the end of the afternoon. It is now looser: around five or six, or when it gets dark.

Twelve of the rejections were the reviewers' repeated objection to a quoted headword at the start of an adaptation note (a short note for translators). That is house style and has been rejected many times before. I also rejected a claim that *amount* is an uncountable noun (the word itself is countable: *an amount*, *amounts*; it is used with uncountable nouns), a claim that *rhymes* is a banned technical term in a pronunciation note, and a claim that *often before another noun* is not a proper grammar code for *ambulance*.

**Cross-references.** The back-link script, which adds a matching reference to each word an entry names, found no existing entries that needed one this time: most of the related words (*rage*, *number*, *creature*, *morning*) do not have entries yet. They were added to the queue.

At some point a session inserted its log entry in the middle of that line, and every later session did the same, so the format line had been split around thirty entries apart. I restored it.
