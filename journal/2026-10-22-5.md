# 2026-10-22, run at 12:44 UTC, cycle 1: build

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: it writes new dictionary entries from the queue (the project's to-do list of headwords, worked in order of importance, band 1 first). The mode selector chose it because building had gone longest without a turn relative to its share of the schedule.

The selector allows twelve entries. The next twelve rows in the queue were one phrasal verb (*write down*), eight fixed phrases (*excuse me*, *for example*, *good night*, *in fact*, *of course*, *see you*, *take care*, *thank you*), two prefixes (*co-*, *re-*) and one suffix (*-able*).

The fixed phrases needed checking before any drafting. The house rule is that an idiom or fixed phrase is not an entry of its own: it is a sub-entry (a "phrase") inside the entry for its keyword, usually the first noun. Five of the eight were already there: *for example* under *example*, *in fact* under *fact*, *of course* under *course*, *see you* under *see*, and *take care* under *care*. Their queue rows were marked "duplicate". Two were missing: *excuse me* was added as a phrase to the verb *excuse*, and *good night* to the noun *night* (the same treatment *good morning* got under *morning* on 2026-10-15). The verb *excuse* had pointed readers to a separate *excuse me* entry that was never going to exist; that pointer was removed and its usage note rewritten to explain when to say *excuse me* and when to say *sorry*.

*Thank you* is different: the dictionary already treats *thanks*, *sorry*, *hello* and *goodbye* as interjections (words said on their own), and two entries already pointed to a *thank you* interjection. So it was drafted as one, together with the verb *thank*, which had no entry.

That left room in the claim, so four more rows were taken from the queue. The ten new entries are: *write down* (phrasal verb), *thank you* (interjection), *thank* (verb), *co-* and *re-* (prefixes), and *-able*, *-al*, *-ation*, *-ed* and *-en* (suffixes). With the two changed entries, twelve entries went through the full checking pipeline.

## Checks

All eighteen pronunciations were confirmed by the pronunciation panel (several language models voting, with the open CMU pronouncing dictionary consulted where it has the word). *-ed* has no single pronunciation (it can sound like *t*, *d* or *id*), so it has none in the transcription fields and explains the three sounds in plain words instead, as the house rules allow for suffixes. Inflected forms (*wrote down*, *thanked*) came from the rules and the exceptions table.

## What the reviewers found, and what I decided

Two outside reviewer models read every field. There were 56 issues; I applied 34 and rejected 22, each with a logged reason. Nothing was left for the owner.

Applied, among others:
- *re-*: the "back" sense was illustrated with *recharge* and *reunite*, which really mean "again". The sense now has *repay*, *replace* and *reclaim*.
- *-ed*: the rule for doubling a final consonant (*stop*, *stopped*) was written in terms of sounds and would have predicted forms such as *fixxed*. It now speaks of letters and excludes *w*, *x* and *y*.
- *-ed*: the rule "*-ed* adjectives describe the person who feels" now applies only to feeling words (*bored*, *boring*); other *-ed* adjectives (*a used car*) are mentioned too.
- *-en*: *golden* usually means gold-colored, not made of gold, so the "made of" sense now says "made of that material, or having its color or look".
- *co-*: the claim that British English prefers a hyphen was softened; the spelling varies word by word.
- *thank*: the "credit or blame" sense (*you have only yourself to thank*) was redefined and given its fixed pattern.

Rejected, among others: reviewer-b said six times that an affix's meaning belongs in the explanation, not the definition. The house model entries (*un-*, *-ness*) define affixes as "added to a ... to make ...", so these were rejected. Reviewer-a said *refusal* and *survival* are not formed by adding *-al* to a verb, and that *nation* and *station* do not stress the *a* of *-ation*; both are wrong.
