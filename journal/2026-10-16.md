# 2026-10-16, cycle 1 of the 00:44 UTC run: originality check, one rewrite

## What this cycle did

This was an **originality** cycle: a spot check that the dictionary's wording is its own and not copied from a published dictionary. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) forces one every tenth run, and this was run 440. It is the thirty-seventh such check.

The tool `tools/originality_check.py` picked ten fields at random from the whole dictionary: six sense definitions and three usage notes (the short boxes that explain how a word is used), plus one more definition. For each one I ran an exact-phrase web search to see whether any published dictionary uses the same words. I also asked one of the two reviewer models (reviewer-a, one of the two outside models that check every entry), in a single question, whether each text reads as copied, and to quote the published wording if it said yes. As in earlier checks, the search decides the verdict; the reviewer's answer is only a hint.

The ten fields and the verdicts:

- *half* (determiner), usage note on singular and plural verbs after *half*: grammar guides state the same rule, in their own words. **Original.**
- *court* (noun) sense 1, *a place where legal cases are heard and decided by a judge*: no exact match; a learner's dictionary says *the place where legal trials take place*, a different plain wording of the only possible idea. **Generic overlap** (short and plain by necessity, not copying).
- *kill* (verb), usage note on *killed in* an accident and on *murder*: no match. **Original.**
- *run* (verb) sense 6, *flow somewhere in a stream, or make water flow from a tap*: no match. **Original.**
- *bottom* (noun) sense 2, *the ground under the water of a sea, a lake, or a river*: no exact match; the closest page words it differently. **Original.**
- *cell* (noun) sense 5, the box in a table: no match. **Original.**
- *danger* (noun), usage note on *in danger* and *in danger of*: no match. **Original.**
- *research* (noun) sense 1, *careful study of a subject to find out new facts or information about it*: a well-known learner's dictionary has *a careful study of a subject, especially in order to discover new facts or information about it*. Ours is that wording with two words changed. **Rewrite.**
- *long* (adjective) sense 4, *measuring a particular distance from one end to the other, or lasting a particular time*: no match. **Original.**
- *mail* (verb) sense 1: no match. **Original.**

## The rewrite

*research* sense 1 now reads *organized work in which someone studies a subject closely, by reading, testing, or asking questions, to learn more than is already known about it*. It covers all four examples (research into a disease, a company spending money on research, research on the history of a city).

A rewrite counts as a review, so the entry went through both reviewers again. Each raised one blocking issue, and I rejected both:

- reviewer-a said *before you buy or decide something*, in the subsense about finding information before a choice, is ungrammatical. It is not: *decide* takes an object (*decide something*).
- reviewer-b said the inflections field, which records no plural, contradicts the usage note, which says the plural *researches* is rare and formal. The inflections field is written by a script (`tools/inflect.py`) and records no plural for uncountable nouns by house rule; the note is where a rare form belongs. No conflict to fix.

## About the reviewer's answers

In earlier checks reviewer-a said *copied* far too often, usually quoting our own text back. This time it went the other way: it said *copied* to none of the ten, including the one real case (*research*), where it only said the wording "resembles standard definitions". So its silence proves nothing either. Search still decides. I added a line to the note that tracks this (`wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`).
