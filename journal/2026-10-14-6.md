## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that so far had passed through the reviewers only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose it because reviewing was furthest behind its share of the work. No entry was waiting as a `draft`, so the cycle took the oldest one-round entries by creation date, all written on 2026-10-05: the infinitive marker *to* (`to-inf`), the seven religion nouns written after your rulings that day (*Buddhism*, *Buddhist*, *Christian*, *Christianity*, *Islam*, *Judaism*, *Muslim*), and the nouns *machine*, *man*, *manager*, *manner*, *map*, *mark*, *market*, and *marriage*. Before sending them to the reviewers I read each one myself. Three things needed fixing:

- *Buddhist* listed *Buddhism* both in its word family and in its "see also" list; the duplicate was removed.
- The usage note for *manner* put *her manner* in italics as if it were a wrong form. The house rule is that wrong forms never go in italics in prose; the sentence was rewritten.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two reviewer models from other companies read every field of every entry. They raised 30 issues, 28 of them marked blocking (the entry cannot be published until each is settled). I accepted 20 and rejected 10. None needed the owner. Nearly all came from the first reviewer; the second raised one minor point, which I rejected.

Accepted, among others:

- *Buddhism*, *Islam*, and *Judaism* said **the** is used *only when a particular form of it is meant*. That is too narrow: *the Buddhism of Tibet* is normal. All three now say that **the** is used when a phrase after it limits the meaning.
- *Judaism* called *Jewish* an alternative noun for a follower. It is an adjective; the note now says a follower is a **Jew** and the adjective is **Jewish**.
- *machine* sense 3 (*a political machine*) was limited to politics; it now names a party or an army as examples. Sense 4 (*she's a machine*) no longer says *without showing feelings*; it is praise for someone who works hard *without seeming to get tired*.
- *man* sense 2 (*made by man*) now says it is *usually* used without **a** or **the**, since *the history of man* is possible. *Man to man* now means *as one person talking to another as an equal*.
- *manager* said the sports sense is used in American English *mainly in baseball*. I kept that but reworded it so it no longer suggests other sports never use it.
- *market* sense 3 (*the housing market*) is now *the trade in a particular kind of thing, and the people and companies that buy and sell it*, which fits its examples.
- *map* sense 2 got two more natural examples (a map of a computer network, a map of your ideas before writing), and *put something on the map* now covers things as well as places and people.
- *marriage* sense 2 had the example *Their marriage took place at the town hall*, while the entry itself says *wedding* is the usual word for the ceremony. The example now reads *She changed her name after her marriage*.
- *black*'s idiom *in black and white* meant *so that it cannot be changed*; it now means *so that it is clear and can be checked*.

Rejected, among others:

- The reviewer wanted *map* sense 2 (a map of how parts connect) removed as really meaning *diagram*. *A map of the network* and *a mind map* are ordinary English; the sense stays.
- *black*, *brown*, and *white* each point to the other two in their skin-color senses. The reviewer called *brown* unhelpful; the three are a deliberate set.
- *leave a mark on someone or something* in *mark* was called a non-standard form of *leave its mark on*. *Left a deep mark on* and *left a lasting mark on* are common; the phrase stays.
