# Journal, 2026-10-24 (seventh run of the day)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each run's kind of work) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. It gave this reason: build had waited longest among the kinds of work that were allowed. Closure (writing entries for words the dictionary already uses in its definitions) is blocked until milestone 1 is finished. Milestone 1 is the first target: an entry for every word on the defining vocabulary list, the roughly 2,400 plain words that definitions are written in. Before this cycle it had 258 rows left.

The queue handed out twelve number words, and all twelve were written and reviewed:

- **Five "teen" numbers:** *eleven*, *fourteen*, *fifteen*, *seventeen*, *nineteen*. Each has one sense, the same shape as the *eighteen* entry from the morning run. Four of them carry a pronunciation note on the stress trap that learners really meet: *fourteen* and *forty*, *seventeen* and *seventy*, and so on are easy to mix up in speech.
- **Five "tens" numbers:** *forty*, *fifty*, *seventy*, *eighty*, *ninety*. Besides the number itself, each has a second sense for the plural: *in her forties* (age), *in the high eighties* (temperature), *the seventies* (the years 70 to 79 of a century). *Fifty* also has an informal paper-money sense (*Can you change a fifty?*). The usage note says that 41 to 49 and so on are written with a hyphen (*forty-one*). *Forty* and *ninety* keep real, common misspellings as learner errors (*fourty*, *ninty*).
- **Two ordinals:** *fourth* and *fifth*. (An ordinal is the number that gives a place in a series: *fourth*, *fifth*.) Each has three senses: place in order (*the fourth floor*), place in a competition (*fourth place*), and dates (*the fifth of June*). They follow the pattern of *second* and *third*, which were reviewed earlier.

Every entry went through the full pipeline: the schema check, the inflection tool, the pronunciation check (all 24 transcriptions, American and British, were confirmed by the voting models), the two outside reviewers, my decisions on each problem they raised, and the final checks.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 41 issues; I applied 28 and rejected 13, and none needed the owner.

- The biggest finding was good: in *fourth*, examples such as *He finished fourth in the race* use the word as an adverb, which belongs in a separate entry under the house rule of one part of speech per entry. I replaced them with uses that belong here (*He took fourth place*), and I made the same change in *fifth*, where the reviewers had not caught it.
- Both reviewers objected to the wording "used for" in the plural sense of the tens numbers, which is padding the style guide bans. I rewrote the definition in all five entries ("the numbers from 40 to 49, especially as a person's age, a temperature, or the years of a hundred-year period") and made the explanation more accurate: the plural usually comes after *the*, and after *her* or *their* only for age.
- One reviewer said *fiveteen* and *fiveth* were made-up errors. They are regularized forms, which the style guide does not allow as learner errors, so I removed them.
- I rejected these objections: that the date sense (*on the fourth*) belongs in a noun entry (the earlier ordinals settled this as the house pattern); that *fifty-fifty* should be a phrase inside *fifty* (it is a headword of its own, now queued); that *the Fourth of July* is missing from *fourth* (it is a holiday name, not a sense of the number); and that *ninty* is an invented misspelling (it is a common real one).

New queue rows: *fourth* and *fifth* as nouns (the fractions, *a fifth of the class*), *fifty-fifty* (adjective), and the ordinals from *eleventh* to *ninetieth*. They wait unbanded until the next band vote, the step where the word-list panel gives each row a frequency band.

**Spend.** About US$0.70 for this cycle (the reviewers and the pronunciation check). The day's total is about US$10.68 of the US$15 daily cap.

**What did not work.** Nothing broke. One reviewer raised seventeen issues on *fourth* alone; about half of them repeated the date objection on each field, which is noise rather than a fault.

**What is next.** The remaining numbers (*twelve*, *thirteen*, *sixteen*, *twenty*, *thirty*, *sixty*) are next in the queue and should follow the same shapes. After them, milestone 1 continues in queue order.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new from this cycle. The open questions in `NEXT.md` still stand.

## Cycle 2: site

**What the cycle did.** The scheduler chose *site*, the check of the public website, because the site check had waited longest among the allowed kinds of work. The website is not stored in the repository: it is built from the entry files by a script (`tools/build_site.py`) each time the project's automatic checks run, and published at <https://tkgally.github.io/eex-dict/>. In this cycle I built the site locally and ran the site checker (`tools/site_check.py`). The checker opens the pages in a real browser, at a phone width (360 pixels) and a desktop width, and tests what a reader does.

The build made 2,430 headword pages from 2,816 entries. A headword page can hold more than one entry: *forty* the number and any other part of speech of the same word share a page. No entry was held back as an unfinished draft. There were no redirects, the stub pages kept for renamed entries.

**What the checks found.** All 79 checks passed. They cover:

- search, including an inflected form (*ran* finds *run*), a phrase, and a misspelling (*abiilty* offers *ability*);
- the hover preview on linked words;
- the translator's view toggle, and whether it is remembered after a reload;
- dark mode;
- the menu;
- the page for an address that does not exist, which runs a search for the name;
- the sense menu on long entries;
- the grey display of a cross-reference whose target entry does not exist yet;
- the absence of sideways scrolling at phone width, on every one of the 2,436 pages.

I also opened the new number pages from cycle 1 (*forty*, *fourth*). They show the new plural sense (*in her forties*) and the *fourth place* examples.

**What changed.** Nothing in the site tools or templates; nothing needed fixing. The only file this cycle changes, apart from the record-keeping, is the metrics history (`metrics/history.jsonl`), which now records that the site build was fine.

**Spend.** None. This kind of work makes no paid calls.

**What is next.** The next cycle goes back to the scheduler. Building milestone 1 is still the main line of work. The remaining numbers (*twelve*, *thirteen*, *sixteen*, *twenty*, *thirty*, *sixty*) are next in the queue. A lint pass is due in about two runs. (Lint is the periodic full check of the wiki and the entries for contradictions, broken links, and stale notes.)

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new.
