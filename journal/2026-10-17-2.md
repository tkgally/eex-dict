# 2026-10-17, cycle 2 of the 00:44 UTC run: twelve adverbs, *really* to *somewhere*

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: new entries drafted from the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written). The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose build because it was the kind of work most overdue. The queue is in band order (band 1 holds the most useful words), and band 1 has reached the adverbs, so this cycle took the next twelve: *really, recently, regularly, right, safely, seriously, similarly, simply, slowly, so, sometimes, somewhere*.

I drafted each entry from my own knowledge and the house style guide, never from a published dictionary. Then each went through the full pipeline: the format check, the inflection tool (which records that these adverbs have no *-er*/*-est* forms, or take *more* and *most*), the pronunciation check (a panel of models plus an open pronouncing dictionary), and the two outside reviewers.

Some of these are large function words. *So* has five senses (*so tired*; *so cold that...*; *not so hard as*; *I think so*; *so am I*) and five phrases (*and so on*, *or so*, *so far*, *so as to*, *so much for*). *Right* has five (*right here*, *right after lunch*, *right through*, *spell it right*, *turn right*) with the phrases *right away* and *right now*. *Really* has four, including the softening *not really*.

All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each word) were confirmed on the first try.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

reviewer-a raised 46 issues; reviewer-b raised 2. reviewer-b's first reply on *sometimes* broke off before any verdicts, so I ran reviewer-b again on that entry alone; its second reply was complete and found nothing. I applied 35 issues and rejected 13. Nothing needed the owner.

Applied, among others:

- ***regularly***: the definition and usage note said it means *at the same times*; it can just mean *often* (*I see her regularly*). Both now say *often, or at times that follow a fixed pattern*. A learner error that was in fact grammatical (*I regularly am late*) was replaced with a real one (*He visits us regular*).
- ***right***, the sense *correctly*: a note said **right** comes after the verb *and its object*, but the entry's own example *Nothing is going right* has no object. It now says *after its object if it has one*.
- ***sometimes***: the definition said *not often*, but *sometimes* can be fairly frequent; it is now *at some times, but not always*. The learner error *I go sometimes to the movies* is possible English, so it became *I eat sometimes meat*, where the word really does sit wrongly between a verb and its object.
- ***somewhere***: *somewhere about forty* is not usual American English; removed. Two notes that said *somewhere* never takes a preposition were limited to verbs such as *go* and *come* (one can say *from somewhere*).
- ***really***: notes claimed it is *more common in speech than very* and *more informal*; both now just say it is especially common in conversation.
- ***so*** and ***seriously***: their opening *core idea* sentences did not cover every sense; rewritten.
- ***recently***: the comparison with *lately* and one learner-error note were made more exact.
- ***similarly***: *similarly dressed* was filed as adverb plus verb; by house practice a participle phrase is a plain phrase (*be similarly dressed*).

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Short definitions made of two glosses with a semicolon (*very; to a great degree*): house practice for degree adverbs.
- *Rarely* stays as the opposite of *regularly* in the *often* sense.
- *Seriously,* at the start of a sentence keeps its *informal* label; it is a conversational use.
- *Some* and *time* stay in the word family of *sometimes*; the entries for those words already point here.
