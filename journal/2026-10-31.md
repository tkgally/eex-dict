# Run report, 2026-10-31

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the tool that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to write. It claimed twelve band-2 verbs (band 2 is the second most important frequency group) in queue order: *interpret*, *interrupt*, *investigate*, *joke*, *justify*, *knit*, *label*, *leap*, *line*, *link*, *load*, and *loan*. All twelve were drafted from scratch, checked, reviewed by both reviewers, corrected, and marked `reviewed`, which means they will appear on the public site. The weekly digest was also due, so the cycle wrote `journal/digest-2026-10-31.md` first: the week's numbers, ten entries for you to sample, and the questions that wait on you.

**Checks before review.** The inflection tool generated every verb form from its rules and the exceptions table (*knitted*, also *knit*; *leaped*, also *leapt*; American *labeled*, British *labelled*). The pronunciation check, a panel of three models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary consulted at run time, agreed with every transcription but one: the American form of *interrupt* was first written with a doubled r sound; it was corrected to ˌɪn.təˈrʌpt, which then had every vote and the CMU dictionary on its side.

**What the reviewers found.** The two reviewers (reviewer A, a Claude model; reviewer B, a Google model) raised 84 issues, 10 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry can be marked reviewed). Every one was decided by hand: 66 applied, 18 rejected, none escalated to you. The main changes:

- *leap*: the sense *my heart leaped* was defined as if a person felt happy; both reviewers pointed out that the heart is the subject. It is now defined as the heart seeming to jump because of a strong feeling, fear included, and lost a *literary* label it did not need. *Leap at the chance* (accept eagerly), flagged by both, is now its own subsense.
- *link*: the sense *link arms* now covers hands too, and the computer sense (*this page links to...*) moved ahead of it because learners meet it far more often.
- *load*: two examples in the sense *give someone a lot of something* did not fit it. One, *the sauce is loaded with salt*, is really the adjective *loaded*; the other, about bags in a car, belonged to the first sense. Both were replaced.
- *loan*: both reviewers said the museum use (lending a painting) is a special case of the main sense, not a sense of its own; it is now a subsense. A usage note explains that American English uses *loan* for lending almost anything, British English mainly for money.
- *joke*: the surprised reply *You're joking!* / *You must be joking* is now a subsense.
- *interpret*: the note contrasting it with *translate* appeared twice; it now lives in one place, the learner-error note.

**What was rejected, and why.** Three requests to add idioms were refused under the keyword rule (an idiom goes under its first noun, else its first verb): *knit your brows* belongs under *brow*, *line your pockets* under *pocket*, *look before you leap* under *look*. Reviewer B's requests to hand-mark forms like *joked* in examples were refused: the site marks listed forms itself. Its objection to the corrected American *interrupt* transcription was refused against a unanimous panel and the CMU dictionary.

**Cross-references.** The cross-reference tool adds back-links automatically when it can tell which sense of the other entry to attach them to. In five cases it could not, so they were placed by hand: *join*, *disturb*, *research*, and *examine* now point back to the new verbs, and *loan* now treats *borrow* as a word to compare rather than an opposite, matching how *lend* already does it. One new queue row was added, the phrasal verb *load up*, which a reviewer asked for.

**Spend.** US$0.38 of the day's US$15 budget (reviewer B and the pronunciation panel; reviewer A runs at no charge).

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. Three definitions first used words outside the defining vocabulary (*complaint*, *needles*, *gallery*); the final check caught them and they were reworded.

**What is next.** More band-2 verbs in queue order; the next whole-dictionary lint pass is due in one run.

**What needs you.** Nothing new from this cycle. The digest lists six standing items, among them the two open questions and the pronunciation disputes.
