## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 24 issues: 22 from reviewer-a and 2 from reviewer-b. I agreed with 17 and fixed them in my own words:

- *smoke*: I had called smoke a gas; it is a cloud of tiny pieces and gases, and the definition now says *cloud*. The proverb *no smoke without fire* now says that something bad people are saying is probably partly true, without the *if* clause reviewer-b objected to.
- *smoke*, "have a smoke": the word *cigarette* is outside the defining vocabulary (the 2,394 plain words definitions must use) and has no entry, so the definition names a pipe as its example and the explanation says the word usually means smoking a cigarette.
- *size*: I had listed *What is the size of your shoes?* as a learner error. It is grammatical, if less usual than *What size are your shoes?*, so I removed it.
- *situation*: the formal sense (where a town or building is) now has natural examples instead of a hotel with "a beautiful situation".
- *a slice of life* now means any short, true picture of ordinary life, not only a film or a story.
- *smell*: *take a smell* was dropped as unnatural, and *odor* is now "often" rather than "usually" unpleasant.
- *sleep*: a note that *have a sleep* is mainly British was removed; the reviewer showed it is also American, especially of children.

I rejected 7. Reviewer-a said *go to sleep* and *get to sleep* belong in the verb entry; by the house rule an idiom goes under its first noun, and *sleep* there is a noun. It said the same of *put an animal to sleep*; here its point exposed a real slip, since by that rule *animal* would be the keyword, so I renamed the phrase *put something to sleep*, with an explanation that it is used of animals. It said the sense "the fact that something is very large" (*because of its size*) is covered by the first sense of *size*; I kept it, because largeness itself is a different idea from a measurement. Two label complaints asked to drop *informal* from conversational idioms (*the sky's the limit*, *I could do it in my sleep*); I kept the label. Reviewer-b objected to "One part" in a pronunciation note, which is the house form for one-syllable words.

The tool that adds reverse cross-references added five back-links (*condition*, *fur*, *piece*, *part*, *meal*); I checked each, and all five are on the right sense.
