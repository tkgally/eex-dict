The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that have so far been through only one round of the review panel (the two outside reviewer models that check every field). There were no drafts waiting, so the cycle took the twelve oldest one-round entries, all created on 30 September 2026: the verbs *angle, attend, author, authorize, avoid, baby,* and *behave*, and the nouns *drawing, painting, ball, band,* and *basis*.

Before sending them to the panel I read each one for missing common senses, idioms, and learner errors. They were in good shape; two gaps were filled by hand:

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 20 issues, 13 of them blocking (must be settled before an entry counts as reviewed). I applied 7 and rejected 6.

Applied:

- *drawing* had been defined as a picture made "with a pencil or a pen", which leaves out charcoal, ink, and crayon; both its picture and art senses now say "a pencil, a pen, or something similar", and the matching translator notes on *drawing* and *painting* were brought into line.
- The ball of the hand is now "at the base of the thumb" and "soft and round".
- The marching-band subsense of *band* no longer says such bands play "only loud instruments"; it now mentions sports fields as well as streets.
- *band*'s usage note says the British plural verb (*the band are*) works for the music and the group senses, but only the music sense carried the grammar code; the group sense now has it too.

Rejected, each with a logged reason:

- reviewer-a said *angle* meaning "fish" should be a sense of this verb; it is a word of separate origin, kept as a separate homograph (it is on the list of homographs still to be drafted).
- reviewer-b said *usually passive* is not a verb pattern; it is a value in the project's closed list of patterns.
- reviewer-a wanted *attention* out of *attend*'s word family; it is derived from *attend* and worth showing to learners.
- reviewer-a called the definition of the vulgar body sense of *ball* inaccurate; it is accurate in plain words, and the technical word is outside the defining vocabulary.
- reviewer-a said *It takes balls* conflicts with the note that the courage sense takes a plural verb; in that sentence the verb agrees with *it*.

Of the seven minor issues, one was applied (an illustration in *drawing*'s learner error now marked as one) and six rejected; five were reviewer-b's standing objection to an opening quoted headword in translator notes, which the project allows.
