# Journal, 2026-11-06

## Cycle 1: build

**What this cycle was.** The routine's mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's job from the state of the dictionary) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still waiting for an entry. Its reason was that build had the most "scheduler debt", meaning it was the job most overdue relative to its share of the runs. It allowed up to twelve new entries and a paid-call ceiling of US$3. No pull requests from earlier runs were open, no entry reports from readers were waiting, and the inbox (where the owner leaves instructions) was empty.

**What was written.** Twelve nouns, all in frequency band 2 (the second most common of five bands): *diet*, *disagreement*, *disaster*, *discipline*, *dishwasher*, *dispute*, *distinction*, *district*, *division*, *documentary*, *donation*, and *doorbell*. Each has definitions in the dictionary's limited defining vocabulary, two to four examples per sense, collocations (words that typically go with it), grammar, and cross-references. Some points worth noting:

- *diet* separates the everyday sense (all the food a person usually eats) from the weight-loss sense. It also has a note for translators: in many languages the matching word means only a weight-loss plan. It records a real learner error (*I'm making a diet* for *I'm on a diet*).
- *disagreement* carries a short paragraph explaining how to choose between *disagreement*, *argument*, *quarrel*, and *dispute*.
- *dispute* has the phrases *beyond dispute* and *in dispute*. The second also covers groups *in dispute with* each other.
- *disaster* has the phrase *a disaster waiting to happen* and an informal subsense for a person who is bad at something (*I'm a disaster in the kitchen*).
- *discipline* ended with five senses: rules, self-control, a subject of study, a kind of training, and an event within a sport.

**The checks.** Every entry went through the full pipeline. The inflection tool generated the plurals from the spelling rules. The pronunciation check compares each American and British transcription with three other models and, for American, with the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. It confirmed 23 of 24 transcriptions. The exception was British *documentary*, which I had written with a weak vowel in the second syllable while the related entry *document* uses the vowel of *put*. I changed it to match *document*, and the re-check confirmed it. I also re-ran the full check once by mistake; it cost a few cents and changed nothing.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Two reviewers from different AI labs read every field of every entry. Together they raised 31 issues, 6 of them "blocking" (an entry cannot be marked reviewed until a blocking issue is decided). I accepted 29 and rejected 2, and each decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

- The blocking issues were mostly example sentences that stated checkable facts, which the style guide forbids: how living things grow (*division*), how many soldiers make an army division, a claim that swimming has more events than most sports. All were replaced with ordinary sentences. In *discipline*, one example was meant to show the "punishment" subsense but really showed the general sense; both reviewers caught it and I replaced it.
- Useful minor findings: in American sports, league divisions are usually set by region, not by level, so the *division* definition now says *by level or by area*. A *section* is smaller than a *division*, so it is no longer listed as a synonym. Reviewer A also noted that *division* can mean a gap between groups (*the division between rich and poor*), and the sense now covers it. A *dispute* does not have to last long, so the definition no longer says it does.
- Rejected: a request to add *a recipe for disaster* to *disaster*. By the house rule, an idiom is filed under its first noun, *recipe*, and that entry already has it. Also rejected: a claim that *distinction* is uncountable in *makes no distinction*. There it is a countable noun after *no*.

After the review, the vocabulary check found two words outside the defining vocabulary in definitions (*honor*, *league*), and I reworded both. The cross-reference tool adds a matching link in each entry that the new entries point to. Seven of those needed a sense picked by hand (for example, *donation* added as a synonym in the "present" sense of *gift*), and I placed all seven.

**Spend.** US$0.36 of the day's US$15 budget, almost all of it on reviewer B and the pronunciation panel. Reviewer A runs on the owner's subscription at no billed cost.

**What is next.** The next cycle continues as the selector decides. The queue now continues after *doorbell*. A lint (a maintenance pass over the whole dictionary) is due in six runs.

## Cycle 2: review

**What this cycle was.** The mode selector chose *review*: re-reading existing entries rather than writing new ones. There were no draft entries and no reader reports, so the cycle continued the coverage pass. This is a re-reading of the most important band-1 entries (the most common words) to check that every frequent present-day use and idiom is covered. The pass works through entries with the most senses first. The previous runs had finished the six-sense entries, so this cycle started on the 124 five-sense entries that have not been read since 2026-10-24, in alphabetical order: *act* (noun and verb), *animal*, *appear*, and *area*. Five rich entries are about as many as one cycle can read with care.

**What I added before the reviewers saw them.** Reading each entry against my own knowledge of current English, I found these gaps:
- *act* (noun): three idioms, *in the act of doing something*, *clean up your act*, and *get in on the act*.
- *act* (verb): two subsenses, *act on* advice or information and *act for* someone (officially, as a lawyer does), plus the phrase *act your age*.
- *animal*: the informal phrase *a different animal* (something very different).
- *appear*: a subsense for arriving, especially late (*Our guests finally appeared at nine*).
- *area*: nothing missing.

I also cleaned up a problem in *animal*. The same fact (in science, birds and insects are animals too) was stated in three places: an explanation, the usage note, and a translator's note. The house rule is one fact, one field.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 48 issues, 3 of them blocking. I accepted 46 and rejected 2; every decision is logged.

- Blocking: both reviewers saw that the "law" sense of *act* carried the code *often before another noun*, which is wrong (*act* is the main noun in *the act was passed*), so I removed it. Reviewer A also saw that *She is the opening act* uses *act* for a performer, not a performance. That is a real, common use that was missing, so *act* now has a subsense for a performer or group of performers.
- Coverage: reviewer A found that the verb *act* lacked its very common "pretend" use (*She acted surprised*), now a subsense. *Appear* lacked the "be found in a text or list" use (*His name does not appear on the list*), now covered.
- Order: *appear* now puts its "seem" sense second, since it is one of the word's most frequent uses. The "published" use became its own subsense.
- Weak cross-references were removed: *action* as a synonym of *act*, and *move* as a synonym of the verb *act*. The house rule says a removed cross-reference loses its mirror link too, so I removed those from *action* and *move* as well.
- Notes: in *appear*, a learner error about *nervously* was removed (that sentence is grammatical in another sense), and the usage note no longer repeats the grammar. The translator's note now names a specific trap: in *it appears that*, the word means *seem*, not *become visible*.
- Labels: *area* in the measuring sense (*the area of a room*) lost its mathematics label because it is everyday language. The idiom *a gray area* now gives the British spelling *grey area*.
- Rejected: reviewer A called *verb + adverb or preposition* an invalid pattern, but it is one value of the closed list. Reviewer B asked for a hand mark on *act his age*. The site marks the headword itself, and the style guide allows hand marks only in three named cases.

**Spend.** US$0.24 for this cycle, US$0.60 so far today.

**What is next.** The coverage pass continues with *art* and the remaining 118 five-sense entries.

**For the owner.** Nothing new this cycle.
