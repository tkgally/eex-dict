## Cross-references

When entry A lists entry B as a synonym, an opposite, or a word to compare, the project keeps the link in both directions, so B also lists A. The tool that adds the return link (`tools/crossref.py`) cannot tell which sense of B the link belongs to, so it puts it on the first sense. Earlier lint passes found that this is often wrong, so every new return link is checked by hand.

This pass added 17 return links, all to adjectives written earlier today. Eleven were in the right place. I fixed six:

- *simple*: the link from *busy* (meaning *too full of details or colors*) moved from *easy* to sense 2, *plain*.
- *single*: the link from *divorced* moved from *only one* to sense 3, *not married*.
- *special*: the link from *general* moved to sense 2, *for one purpose*.
- *strict*: the link from *loose* (*not exact*) moved to sense 3, *exact*.
- *square*: the link from *round* had gone to the face-shape sense; it now sits on sense 1, the shape.
- *neat* and *straight*: *neat* (of a drink, with nothing added) pointed to *straight*, but the *straight* entry has no drink sense, so the return link landed on *not bent*. I removed the link on both sides. A link removed from only one side comes back the next time the tool runs.

## Reviewer precision

Each lint pass measures how often the session accepts each reviewer's findings, by kind of finding (the "family", such as *definition-meaning* or *label*). A family where fewer than 30 percent of a reviewer's findings are accepted, over twenty or more decisions, is switched off for that reviewer, because it costs more to read than it gives.

Nothing new crossed the line this time. reviewer-a's findings are accepted 72 percent of the time overall, reviewer-b's 53 percent. The eight families switched off earlier stay off. reviewer-b's *spelling-or-format* findings are at 28 percent but stay on by the owner's ruling of 5 October. The nearest live family to the line is reviewer-b's *phrase* findings, at 32 percent. Since the last pass, reviewer-b had 16 of its 24 findings accepted, which is good; most of them were adverb uses that had slipped into adjective examples (*sit up straight*, *acting strange*). The figures are in `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md`.
