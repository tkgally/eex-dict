# 2026-09-26 (ninth run): ten more verbs, *like* to *manage*

## What this run did

This was the third cycle of this session and the third **build** run in a row. A build run writes new dictionary entries. The first two cycles added *invest* to *kiss* (pull request #76) and *know* to *light* (pull request #77). The scheduler (`next_mode.py`, the script that picks each run's kind of work) chose building again, because the queue of words still to write is long and building remains the most overdue kind of work.

This cycle drafted the next ten verbs from band 1, the most basic band of the word list: **like, limit, list, listen, live, lock, look, lose, love, manage**. These are everyday verbs, and several have well-known learner traps. The entries cover them in their learner-error boxes, which show a common wrong sentence beside the correct one, and in their notes:

- *listen to* versus *hear*, with a note on how to choose between them;
- *look*, *see*, and *watch*, also with a note on choosing between them;
- *lose* versus *miss* (*I lost the bus* → *I missed the bus*), and *lose* versus *loose*;
- *live* with the present perfect (*I live here since two years* → *I have lived here for two years*);
- *like* and *love* with *would* (*I would like that you come* → *I would like you to come*).

The adaptation notes (short notes for future translators) point out where other languages often divide things differently. In many languages the thing liked is the subject of the sentence (*it pleases me*), and many use a single verb for *lose* and *miss*.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline:

- **Format and inflections.** All ten passed the format check. The inflection tool generated every verb form, with *lose* → *lost* taken from the table of irregular forms.
- **Pronunciation.** All twenty American and British transcriptions were verified.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 50 issues, 28 of them blocking (they must be settled before publication). Each got a logged decision: 28 fixes applied and 22 rejected.

**What was fixed.** The biggest change was to *love*. The draft mixed two uses in one sense (a separate meaning): liking something very much (*I love chocolate*) and the polite *would love* (*I'd love to come*, *I'd love you to meet my parents*). Reviewer-a showed that these differ in meaning and grammar. *Would love* is now its own third sense, matching how *like* treats *would like*. Other fixes:

- *look*'s sense for buildings (*the hotel looks out over the sea*) now says a place can be seen from it, not just that it faces a direction.
- In *like*, the example *how do you like your coffee?* could be read as asking for an opinion, so it was replaced.
- In *list*, a confusing "or be on it" was removed from a definition.
- *listen*'s learner error about the news was wrong, because *listen to the news* is fine; it was replaced with a clearer one (*I listened a strange noise* → *I heard a strange noise*).
- *manage*'s note that it is "followed by *to*" now applies only to the sense *succeed in doing something*.

**What was rejected.** Reviewer-b argued in four separate issues that *Look, I'm sorry, but...* is an interjection and belongs in a different entry. It is the command form of the verb used to get attention. The entry for *listen* treats *Listen, I have an idea* the same way, so the flags were rejected, and the rule was added to the list of settled questions in `NEXT.md`. Reviewer-a said *there's no time to lose* has no object, but *time* is the object. The reviewers' usual objection to the quoted headword at the start of adaptation notes was rejected again under the house rule. Reviewer-b was right on only about one issue in six this cycle, which is noisier than usual.
