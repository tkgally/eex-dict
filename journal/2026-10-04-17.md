# 2026-10-04 (17): twelve new nouns, *humor* to *industry*

*First cycle of the run that started at 16:44 UTC on 4 October 2026.*

## What this cycle did

The scheduler (the script `tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each cycle's job by weighing how long each kind of work has waited) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue of words still to be written. It allowed twelve. The next twelve core nouns in queue order were claimed and written: *humor, husband, ice, idea, illness, image, impact, importance, improvement, income, increase*, and *industry*. All twelve went through the full pipeline (format check, word forms, pronunciation check, a review by two outside language models, my reading of every objection, and a final check of links) and are now marked `reviewed`, which means they will appear on the public site at its next build.

Some of the entries are large. *Idea* has four senses (a plan, a picture in the mind, a belief, a purpose) and four fixed phrases such as *you have no idea* and *that's the idea*. *Image* has four senses, from a picture on a screen to the picture people have of a company, plus *the spitting image of someone*. *Ice* holds three idioms: *break the ice*, *be on thin ice*, and *put something on ice*. *Break the ice* used to sit under the verb *break*; the house rule is that an idiom lives under its first noun, so it was moved to *ice* in this cycle, as an earlier note had asked. Most entries also carry a short box comparing near-synonyms that learners mix up (*illness, disease, sickness*; *income, salary, wage*; *impact, effect, influence*; *increase, rise, growth*), a usage note, and one or two typical learner mistakes, such as *an increase of prices* for *an increase in prices*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers raised 34 objections, 20 of them marked blocking. I accepted 24 and rejected 10; each decision is logged with a reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

The accepted ones were mostly real improvements. One reviewer pointed out that the entry for *illness* defined it as "a particular disease" while the comparison box said illness and disease are different; the definition now says "a particular condition that makes someone sick". The *ice* entry had called the British use of *an ice* (one serving of ice cream) "older", and listed *Let's buy an ice* as a learner error; the reviewer noted that British speakers still say it, so the explanation now calls it "becoming old-fashioned" and the learner error was dropped. *Put something on ice* no longer promises that the plan will be taken up again. The claim that a salary is "usually paid every month" was removed, since many salaries are paid weekly or every two weeks.

The rejected ones were mostly wrong on the facts or against house style. One reviewer said British English spells the adjective *humourous*; it does not (it is *humorous* in both countries, which is exactly the point the usage note makes). Another objected that *idea* is uncountable in *no idea*; it is a countable noun used in the singular there, which is why *I don't have idea* is a learner error. Two objections asked for a capital letter where a note begins with the bold headword, which the house style allows.

## Links between entries

A tool (`tools/crossref.py`) adds a "back-link" to an existing entry whenever a new entry points to it, so that both sides agree. It added six. Five were right. One, which listed *idea* as a synonym of *aim*, was too loose; I removed both sides of it. The tool also noticed that *disease* listed *illness* as a plain synonym while *illness* listed *disease* under "compare" (words learners confuse); I made both say "compare".
