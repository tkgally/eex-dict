## What this cycle did

This was a **lint** cycle: a scheduled health check of the whole dictionary and its knowledge base (the `wiki/` folder of rules, decisions, and notes that sessions read). The mode selector forces one after five cycles without a lint. It makes no paid calls and writes no new entries.

The mechanical checks all passed:
- **Caps**: every file that has a word limit is within it.
- **Links**: every relative link in the knowledge base points to a file that exists.
- **Back-links**: the cross-reference tool added six reverse links. When entry A names entry B as a synonym or a word to compare, B should name A too; the tool adds the missing half. It has a known habit of attaching the link to the first sense of B even when another sense is the right one, so each was checked by hand. All six were on the right sense: *race* and *run* now appear as synonyms on the "move quickly" sense of *hurry*; *disable* on *turn off*; *put on* on *turn on*; *put down* (in its "write" sense) on *write down*; and *please* on the "accepting or refusing" sense of *thank you*.
- **Defining vocabulary**: no definition anywhere uses a word that is outside the defining list and has no entry.
- **Claims**: three old claim files (records of which run is drafting which word), all for words now finished, were deleted.

## Reviewer precision

Each lint measures how often each outside reviewer's complaints, by kind, turn out to be right. A kind of complaint that is right less than 30 percent of the time over twenty or more decisions gets switched off for that reviewer. Nothing new fell under the line. The eight pairs already switched off stay off. Reviewer-a is right about 71 percent of the time overall, reviewer-b about 51 percent. Reviewer-b's "definition style" complaints dropped to 46 percent after the previous cycle, where I rejected its six calls asking for affix meanings to be moved out of the definition field. The figures are in `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md`.

## The judgmental part

One real finding followed from the previous cycle. The queue holds rows for fixed phrases with the part of speech "phrase", but the house rule puts phrases inside their keyword entry instead of giving them entries of their own. I checked every such row. Of fifteen, twelve are now marked duplicate: nine were already covered by a keyword entry, and three (*by the way*, *my pleasure*, *go to bed*) were marked in this cycle. Three are still pending: *you are welcome*, *never mind*, and *bless you*. Each belongs under an entry that does not exist yet (*welcome* as an adjective, *mind* as a verb, *bless* as a verb). *Bless* was not in the queue, so I added it. A new note, `wiki/notes/queue-phrase-rows.md`, tells later build cycles to add these phrases to their keyword entry instead of drafting phrase entries. No tool change is proposed.
