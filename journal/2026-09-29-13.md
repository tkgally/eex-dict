Before the panel saw them, I added by hand what was missing:

- **New senses.** *carry* gained a sense for adding numbers (*carry the one*) and one for being pregnant (*She was carrying twins*). *to* gained the toast use (*Here's to your new job!*). *with* gained the "despite" use (*With all his faults, he is still a good friend*). *on* now covers musical instruments as well as machines (*played a song on the piano*).

- **Usage notes**: *carry* against *bring* and *take*; *neither ... nor* against everyday *not ... or*.
- **Learner errors** (mistakes learners of many languages make): *the man on the picture* (should be *in*), *Fill the glass of water* (should be *with*), and *As I was tired, so I went to bed* (the *so* is not needed).

The arithmetic sense of *carry* settles an old to-do item: *borrow*'s subtraction sense pointed to *carry*, which had no matching sense.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 46 issues, 34 of them blocking. (A blocking issue must be settled before an entry counts as reviewed.) I applied 27 and rejected 19; none needed the owner.

What I accepted:

- ***on*, sense 4.** It said *in* is used for a small boat. People say both *on a boat* and *in a boat*, so the note now says both are possible, with *in* more usual for a small open boat.
- ***on*, "save on".** *save on electricity* means using less, not spending; the example was replaced.
- ***to*.** Only some American speakers use *of* and *till* for the time (*a quarter of eight*); the note now says so. The short spoken form (*tuh*) is not limited to before consonant sounds.
- ***with*.** *Meet with* for a planned meeting is used in British English too, not only American. The collocations for the "because of a feeling" sense are now complete phrases (*shake with fear*).
- ***change*.** Three notes said a plural noun is used whenever one thing replaces another (*change trains*); people also say *change a tire*, so they now say "some nouns".
- ***charge*.** The core idea (the one-sentence summary at the top) said every sense means "put something onto"; it now lists what the senses do.
- **Other fixes.** *carry* now covers a gene as well as a virus. An *as* learner error about *the same ... as* concerned the preposition and was replaced.
- **My own duplicates.** Entry-level adaptation notes (warnings for translators) I had added repeated notes already on the senses; removed.

What I rejected:

- **The "formal" labels on *care for*** (*Would you care for some tea?*): formal-polite in tone.
- **Three objections to *chew* sense 2** ("bite something again and again without swallowing it"), said to leave out chewing gum, which is exactly that.
- **A "formal" label for causal *as*.** It sounds formal only in American English; the explanation now says so.
- **The claim that *the same phone as you do* is not the conjunction.** *as you do* is a clause, so it is the conjunction.
- **Calling *The light is on* an adverb rather than an adjective.** The house treats it as an adjective, as it does *Are you up?*

*check* and *examine* listed each other as both synonym and compare; the compare link went on both sides. *cause* keeps *result* as a synonym, noted as *result in*. The tool added one back-link, *despite* to *with*.
