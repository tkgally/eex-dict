# 2026-09-26 (thirty-first run): eight verbs, *rule* to *send*

## What this run did

This was the twelfth cycle of this session, and a **build** run. A build run drafts new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. The lint pass before it merged as pull request #99. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each run's kind of work) then chose build. The run continued with band 1, the most basic words, after *rub*.

The eight verbs were:

- **rule**: governing a country, a judge or a court deciding something officially, and fears or money controlling someone's life.
- **sail**: traveling by boat, sailing a boat as a sport, and a ball sailing over a fence.
- **satisfy**: pleasing someone, being enough for a need or a condition, and the formal "make someone sure".
- **save**: six senses. They are saving a life, saving money, saving time, keeping something for later (*save me a seat*), saving a file, and a goalkeeper's save.
- **score**: scoring in a game, scoring in a test, giving points, and the informal *score some cheap tickets*.
- **seem**: the linking verb (*You seem tired*, *She seems like a nice person*), and the softening use (*I can't seem to open this jar*). Learner errors cover *is seeming* and *It seems me that*.
- **sell**: selling something, something selling well, and selling an idea to someone. A learner error covers *sold to me her bike*.
- **send**: sending a letter or a message, sending someone somewhere, and *sent the papers flying*. A learner error covers *sent to her a letter*.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline, the fixed sequence of checks each entry must pass:

- **Format and inflections.** All eight passed. *Sell* and *send* took their irregular forms (*sold*, *sent*) from the table of irregular forms.
- **Pronunciation.** All sixteen transcriptions were confirmed by the panel of pronunciation models. The American ones also agree with the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 22 issues. Each has a logged decision: 17 applied and 5 rejected.

The main fixes:

- *Sell* sense 2 (*Her paintings sell for thousands*) is the use with no object. But one of its examples, *The book sold a million copies*, had an object. That example was replaced with *Tickets for the show are selling fast*, and the grammar now shows the sense as having no object. *Sell out* was removed from the collocations because it is a phrasal verb with its own entry. *Sell well* was taken out of sense 1, where it did not belong. The informal label on "sell an idea" was dropped, because the use is ordinary in business English.
- *Send* listed *send for a doctor* under "make someone go somewhere". That phrase means asking someone to come, so both reviewers flagged it and it was removed.
- *Sail* sense 2 was limited to "controlling" a sailing boat. People who *go sailing* are often passengers, so the sense now reads "control or travel in a boat that the wind moves, especially as a sport or a hobby". The core idea, the one-sentence summary at the top of the entry, now leads with travel by boat or ship.
- *Satisfy* sense 1 had been marked as never used in the continuous form, which is not true. *Satisfy the conditions* is now defined as doing what a rule asks for, not "being enough" for it.
- *Save*: the sports sense is now "stop the other team from scoring a goal by stopping the ball".
- *Seem* sense 2 was a "used to..." definition. The style guide keeps those for function words, so it was rewritten as a verb phrase.

Rejected: requests to drop explanations that state who a sense is used about (the style guide puts that in the explanation), a claim that *go sailing* uses a noun (*sailing* is the verb's *-ing* form), and the usual objection to the one-sentence core idea.

The cross-reference tool reported a mismatch: *look* lists *seem* as a synonym, but *seem* listed *look* only as a word to compare. *Seem* now lists *look* as a synonym, with a note that *look* is for what you can see.
