## What this cycle did

A **build cycle** drafts new entries from the queue of words still to be written, taking the most important words (band 1) first. Before starting, the pre-flight found no stranded pull requests; the three leftover `claude/*` branches on GitHub are fully merged into `main` and were already listed in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` for deletion, so nothing new was needed. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose a build because it had the largest backlog among the modes allowed today. The claim tool handed out the next twelve band-1 adjectives in queue order: *future*, *general*, *generous*, *gentle*, *gold*, *grateful*, *great*, *green*, *guilty*, *handsome*, *hard*, and *healthy*. All twelve were drafted, checked, reviewed, and are now `reviewed` (published on the next site build).

Some highlights. *Hard* has eight senses (firm, difficult, needing effort, full of problems, strict, provable facts, water, and drugs, with a subsense for strong drinks) and two phrases, *hard and fast* and *hard up*; its usage note separates the adverb *hard* (*work hard*) from *hardly* (*almost not*). *Great* has four senses plus an informal subsense for *a great big dog*, and it carries the *big* / *large* / *great* choice. *Green* has six senses, from the color to *environment*, *not ripe*, *no experience*, and *looking ill*. *Grateful* explains *grateful to* a person and *grateful for* a thing, and the polite *I would be grateful if you could...*. *Guilty* separates the feeling from the court's decision and contrasts *guilty* with *ashamed*. *General* carries the phrase *in general* and the common learner error *in generally*.

## Pronunciation and word forms

The pronunciation panel (three outside models, plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary for American English, consulted at run time and not stored) verified all 24 transcriptions on the first pass. The word-form tool made the comparative forms from the grammar codes: *gentler*, *greater*, *greener*, *guiltier*, *harder*, *healthier*; *more generous*, *more grateful*; none for *future* and *gold*, which are not compared. *General* first came back undecided because its *not detailed* sense had no gradability code; I added *comparative with more* (*more general*), and it then verified. *Handsome* is the one word-form decision of the cycle: I first coded it *comparative with more* while a usage note said *handsomer* is also used, and both reviewers spotted the contradiction. I added a line to the project's own table of irregular forms (`schema/inflection-exceptions.json`): *handsomer*, *handsomest*, with the note that *more handsome* is also common, the same treatment the table already gives *common*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models (reviewer-a and reviewer-b) read every field. reviewer-b's first reply on *grateful* came back without its list of verdicts; one re-run fixed it. In all, the reviewers raised 65 issues, 51 of them marked blocking. I applied 52 and rejected 13, each logged with a reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`; none needed the owner.

Applied, among others:

- *great*: sense 3 mixed *important* with *skilled and famous*; it now reads *important and admired, especially because of very high skill*, with *great thinkers* replacing *a great day*. Sense 4 (*a great fan*, *a great reader*) had *great friends* in it, which means close friends; that moved to sense 1. The *big* / *large* / *great* note wrongly said *great* avoids countable nouns (*a great distance* proves otherwise); it now says *great* goes with nouns of amount, distance, or degree.
- *hard*: sense 8 joined *hard drugs* and *hard liquor* under *harmful to the body*. It is now split: the drug sense, and subsense 8a *containing a lot of alcohol*.
- *healthy*: senses 3 and 4 were defined too narrowly for their own examples (*a healthy relationship*, *a healthy attitude*); they now read *in good condition and working well* and *reasonable and good for you, especially in the way you think or feel*.
- *guilty*: the legal sense now covers being judged guilty by a court, not only having done the act.
- *gold*: the note comparing *gold* and *golden* said *gold* before a noun means *made of gold*, while the entry's own sense 2 shows *gold paint*; rewritten.
- Several synonyms and antonyms that did not fit were removed (*later*, *yellow*, *famous*, *naive*, *assistant*).

Rejected: reviewer-b called *comparative with more* an invalid grammar code three times (it is in the closed list); reviewer-a objected to explanations such as *Used of fruit*, which the style guide requires for subject restrictions; reviewer-a said *soft* is no opposite of *hard drugs* (*soft drugs* and *soft drinks* are the real opposites).

The back-link tool added mirror cross-references to six older entries. Two landed on the wrong sense and were moved by hand: *handsome* on `fine-adj` went from *acceptable* to *good quality*, and *hard* on `gentle-adj` from *not strong* to *kind and calm*.
