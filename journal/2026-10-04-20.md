## What this cycle did

The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **build** again: writing new entries from the queue of words still to be written. The next twelve core nouns were *infection, influence, injury, insect, inside, instrument, intelligence, intention, interest, internet, iron*, and *island*. All twelve went through the full pipeline (format check, word forms, pronunciation check, review by two outside language models, my reading of every objection, a check of the words used in definitions, and a check of links) and are now marked `reviewed`. The dictionary now has 1,300 entries.

The largest is *interest*, with six senses (wanting to know, being interesting, a hobby, the money paid for borrowing, what is good for someone, a share in a business) and two phrases (*in the interests of safety*, *pay someone back with interest*). *Instrument* has four senses, from music to measuring devices; *island* four, including the raised area in a road and the counter in the middle of a kitchen. *Iron* holds two idioms, *have too many irons in the fire* and *strike while the iron is hot*.

Two definitions had to change before review because they used words outside the dictionary's defining vocabulary (the list of about 2,400 plain words that definitions must use, unless the word has its own entry). The golf sense of *iron* needed the word *golf*, which has no entry yet; rather than define it badly, I moved it into a one-line usage note (*a five iron*). *Interest* in the money sense had used *extra*; it now reads *money that you pay for borrowing money, or that a bank pays you for keeping your money*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 33 points, 26 marked blocking. I accepted 31 and rejected 2; the reasons are logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. This batch drew unusually sound objections, and some of them corrected real mistakes of mine:

- *Iron*: my note for translators said the **r** in *iron* is "written after the **o**". It is written before it (i-r-o-n); the point is that American speakers say it after the vowel. Fixed.
- *Injury*: I had added a formal sense, harm to feelings (*an injury to his pride*). The reviewer called it rare in current English, and I agree; the sense is gone. The comparison of *injury*, *wound*, and *damage* had said *damage* is "not for people"; but *brain damage* is ordinary medical English, so the note now says *damage* is used "mostly" for things.
- *Intelligence*: the secret-information sense also covered the people who collect it. The reviewer asked for two senses, and they are now separate (*receive intelligence about an attack*; *work for military intelligence*). The claim that the word has no plural was too strong (people speak of *multiple intelligences*), so the notes now say "uncountable in ordinary use".
- *Influence*: *under the influence* (drunk, in law) and *under the influence of someone* are now two phrases rather than one with a double explanation, and the usage note now lists *influence with* and *influence in* beside *influence over*.

The two rejections were a request to begin a note with a capital letter (house style allows the lowercase headword) and a request to restore the golf sense of *iron* as a full sense (it cannot be defined without *golf*).

## Pronunciation

The pronunciation panel (three outside models, plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary for American English) agreed with every transcription except the noun *inside*. I wrote it with the stress on the second part, as in the preposition; most of the panel preferred another form, probably stress on the first part, while the CMU dictionary agreed with mine. Following the rules, the entry keeps my form, carries a "pronunciation disputed" flag, and has a line in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` for the owner.
