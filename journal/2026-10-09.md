# Journal, 2026-10-09 (run of 00:43 UTC, cycle 1): twelve new nouns, *surface* to *tail*

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: the mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work from what is most overdue) chose to add new entries, because building had the largest backlog. The cycle took the next twelve nouns in the queue of words still to write, all from frequency band 1 (the most common words): *surface, surgery, surprise, survival, suspicion, sweat, switch, symbol, symptom, system, table,* and *tail*.

Each entry was drafted by hand from the style guide, with every field filled: senses with plain definitions, two to four example sentences each, collocations (the words a learner most often meets beside the headword), cross-references, learner errors, usage notes where they help, and adaptation notes (short warnings for future translators). Together the twelve entries carry 25 idioms and fixed phrases, among them *scratch the surface*, *take someone by surprise*, *surprise, surprise*, *no sweat*, *in a cold sweat*, *get something out of your system*, *all systems go*, *turn the tables*, *under the table*, *bring something to the table*, *turn tail*, and *with your tail between your legs*.

Some points of judgment:

- *surgery* keeps the British sense *a doctor's place of work*, labelled British, and a separate American sense for one operation (*three surgeries*).
- *sweat* has four senses, including the American informal plural *sweats* (soft clothes for exercise or relaxing).
- *table* holds the times table as a sub-sense of the facts-and-numbers sense.
- *tail* holds the coin side (*heads or tails*) and the formal coat (*tails*) as plural-only senses.

## The checks

- **Pronunciation.** All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, three outside models voting, and by the open CMU pronouncing dictionary.
- **Plurals.** These come from the rules script; *survival* has none.
- **Defining vocabulary.** The words used in definitions must come from the dictionary's basic list or have their own entry. Ten words broke this rule, among them *dentist*, *rows*, *columns*, *sweatpants*, and *embarrassed*. All ten were reworded. A table is now *facts or numbers arranged in lines that go across and down a page or screen*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside reviewer models read every field. Between them they raised 47 issues: 43 from reviewer-a and 4 from reviewer-b. I applied 38, rejected 9, and escalated none.

Main changes applied:

- *surgery*: I dropped the meaning *the hours when a doctor sees patients*, which is dated and was mixed into the place sense. The usage note now says that British English *more often* says *an operation*, instead of claiming it never counts *surgery*. The learner error *have a surgery* was removed, because that is standard American English.
- *tail*: the coin-side definition no longer says the other side shows a person's head, which is not true of every coin. The phrase *on someone's tail* now covers following someone to catch or watch them.
- *the survival of the fittest*: now means those *best able to deal with their conditions*, not simply the strongest.
- *sweat*: the hard-work sense lost its informal label, and the clothes sense was widened.
- *switch*: an unnatural example (*let's do a switch*) was replaced. The learner-error note no longer claims that *open a switch* is never said; engineers do say it.
- *symptom*: the usage note on symptoms versus signs was softened to *often*.

Main rejections:

- *in surgery* (*my father is still in surgery*) is the treatment sense and a common phrase.
- *asleep at the switch* keeps its American label. In this dictionary a region label means *mainly used there*.
- Reviewer-b objected twice that two phrase definitions were not base-form verb phrases, but they already were.

**Cross-references.** The cross-reference script reported five type mismatches: pairs where one entry called the other a synonym and the other called it a word to compare. I settled them so that both sides agree. For example, *desk* now lists *table* as a word to compare, not a synonym. The script also adds back-links (the mirror of a cross-reference) and placed seven. Two were on the wrong sense and I moved them by hand: *suspicion* now sits under the *belief* sense of `feeling-n`, and *tail* under the *forward position* sense of `front-n`.
