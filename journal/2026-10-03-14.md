## What was reviewed

The block was the twelve oldest entries with only one panel round. All were written on 2026-09-29: *committee* (noun), *discolor*, *foresee*, *foretell*, *hand* (verb), *head* (verb), *house* (verb), *identify*, *mail* (verb), *monetize*, *smell* (verb), and *snow* (verb).

`NEXT.md` warns that entries from the later cycles of long runs came out thin. I read every entry in full against the style guide before the panel saw them. Most were in good shape. These are the gaps I filled by hand:

- **smell** (verb) had five senses, but four of them had no collocations (the common word partners a learner should know). Each now has them: *smell smoke*, *smell gas*, *can smell something*, *smell the flowers*, *really smell*, *smell danger*, *smell trouble*. One example was also tagged with the verb pattern it shows (*Dinner smells wonderful*: verb + adjective).
- **mail** (verb) had no collocations for its email sense. It now has *mail someone a file* and *mail something to someone*.
- **head** (verb): the soccer sense listed fewer patterns than its own examples use, and one example carried a passive tag its sense did not list. Both are fixed. Links now point to the phrasal verbs *head off*, *head out*, and *head up*.
- **hand** (verb) now links to *hand down*, **smell** to *smell out*, and **discolor** to *fade* and *stain*.

## What the panel found and what I decided

The panel raised 14 issues, 6 of them blocking. I applied 9 and rejected 5, each with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

- Both reviewers said the British pronunciation of **discolor** put the syllable break in the wrong place. It now breaks after the prefix (*dis-*), like the American transcription. The pronunciation check confirmed it, with all three panel votes agreeing.
- One reviewer showed that the learner error I had just added to **discolor** was not a real error: *the fabric discolored yellow* is possible. I removed it. The other reviewer pointed out that **discolor** is mostly used in the active, so the "usually passive" pattern came off as well.
- In **smell** and **snow**, four notes said a rule held always or only. The reviewer was right that these were too absolute. Seeing something by smell is *not normally* put in a continuous form (*I can smell gas*), but the entry no longer says it never is. The subject of the weather verb *snow* is now *normally* the empty **it**. The grammar code that marks the verb as not used in continuous tenses stays, as the house rule in `NEXT.md` asks.
- Rejected: an objection that **committee**'s grammar code (singular or plural verb) contradicts its notes. The code says both are possible, and the notes add which is usual where. Also rejected: a claim that *be snowed under* is an idiom rather than the phrasal verb *snow under*. And two flags on **identify** that its pronunciation advice appears twice. The note for translators may repeat the note for readers.

The one new back-link check found nothing to add. Six target words named in these entries, such as *head-out-phrv* and *stain-v*, were added to the queue.
