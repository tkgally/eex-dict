## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Reviewer-a (the OpenAI model) raised 29 issues and reviewer-b (the Google model) 3. I agreed with 27 and changed the entries, and disagreed with 5.

The most useful catch, made by both reviewers: I had put *first-rate* under the noun *rate* as a phrase, but it is an adjective, and the house rule is one part of speech per entry. It was removed and queued as its own entry. *At this rate* was also listed twice (as a phrase and as a collocation of the speed sense) and is now only the collocation. Other fixes: the British *reader* (a university rank) is now defined more carefully; the definition of recovery from drink or drugs no longer sounds as if it were only "stopping"; *in receipt of* was removed, since it is used mostly about payments; a weak learner error on *range* (*a big range*, which is not really wrong) was replaced with a real one (*out of the range* for *out of range*).

What I rejected: reviewer-a said the two meanings of *race* are one word with one history; they are not, so the separate headword stays, though I took the history claim out of the usage note, since origin claims belong only in the etymology field. It wanted *right as rain* labeled British; it is used in American English too. It objected that the plurals table for *rain* says "no plural" while the entry has *the rains*; that table is written by a script that cannot yet record a plural-only use (a known gap), so I marked *rains* by hand in the examples instead.

Ten definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary (the 2,394 plain words that definitions are meant to use), among them *tropical*, *invitation*, *professor*, and *romantic*; all were rewritten in plain words. One pair of cross-references disagreed in type (*cause* listed *reason* as a word to compare, while I had listed *cause* as a synonym of *reason*); I followed the older entry.
