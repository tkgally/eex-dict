## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 24 issues. I applied 17 and rejected 7; none needed to go to you. One reply from the second reviewer ("reviewer-b"), on *passion*, was cut off; a second call gave a complete reply.

Applied, among others:

- *partner* in business said a partner owns the business and shares its money. In law and accounting firms, *partner* is also a senior rank, so the definition now covers "an important member of a firm, such as a law firm".
- *party* in its formal sense was limited to legal agreements and arguments; it now covers any agreement, legal case, or argument.
- *passion* as an interest was defined as "a very strong interest in something, or the activity that you love doing". The second half was wrong: the passion is the interest, not the activity.
- *past* (a person's life) said "especially things they want to keep secret". A past need not be secret; that part is gone.
- Two learner errors were weak and are removed: *the passengers of the car* is grammatical, and *put your password here* is natural.
- *patience*: the card game sense now names the American word *solitaire*, and a usage note that only repeated the sense was removed.
- The *pay* guide said a salary is paid every month and wages are for physical work; both were too strong and are now softened.

Rejected, among others:

- An objection that *off the beaten path* is not mainly American. A region label in this dictionary means *mainly* used there, and British English mainly says *off the beaten track*.
- An objection to *Let's make a pause* as a learner error: native speakers hardly say it, and learners often do.
- Objections that collocations such as *a pay raise* should drop the article; the house style keeps it.

The cross-reference tool also found that *break* names *pause* as a synonym while *pause* named *break* only as a word to compare; I made them synonyms on both sides.
