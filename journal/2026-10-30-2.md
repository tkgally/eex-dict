# Run report, 2026-10-30 (second run of the day, started 06:44 UTC)

## Cycle 1: build

**What this cycle did.** The selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work from the project's state) chose **build**: write new entries from the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written). It allowed up to 12 entries and US$3 of paid model calls. I wrote twelve common ("band 2") verbs, the next ones in the queue order: **drain**, **edit**, **embarrass**, **emerge**, **emphasize**, **enable**, **ensure**, **equal**, **erase**, **establish**, **estimate**, and **evaluate**. Every one went through the full pipeline: the schema check, generated verb forms, the pronunciation panel (three models plus an open pronouncing dictionary, voting on my transcriptions), both reviewers on every field, my reading of every issue, and the cross-reference check. The dictionary now has 3,341 entries, all reviewed.

Some choices in these entries:
- **drain** has five senses (empty something of liquid, liquid flowing away, a container or food becoming dry, using up someone's energy or money, color or feeling leaving someone), with *drain a glass* as a subsense of the first.
- **edit** warns translators that in several languages a look-alike word means *publish*.
- **embarrass** warns that in some languages a look-alike word means *pregnant*, and its learner error is *I was so embarrassing* for *I was so embarrassed*.
- **emphasize** has the real learner error *emphasize on*.
- **ensure** has a short comparison of **ensure**, **insure** and **assure**.
- **estimate** has a pronunciation note: the verb ends like *mate*, the noun does not.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 63 issues, 3 of them blocking (they must be settled before an entry is published). I applied 52 and rejected 11.

- **The three blocking issues**, all applied:
  - **drain**: a translator's note said only that the energy sense needs its own word, which names no real trap. I removed it.
  - **ensure**: *help ensure* was listed as "adverb + verb", but **help** is a verb. It is now listed as a fixed phrase.
  - **equal**: sense 3 joined two meanings with "or" (*result in something, or mean the same as something*). It is now one meaning, "be the same as something in its effect or meaning" (*More cars equal more traffic*). The informal label on that sense came off too, because both reviewers said this use is ordinary English.
- **Minor fixes worth knowing about.**
  - The first sense of **drain** now covers both the liquid and the container as the object.
  - *bath* became the American *bathtub*.
  - A battery example was added to **drain** (*Video calls drain the battery very quickly*).
  - Weak synonyms were removed: **correct** for **edit**, **let** for **enable**. **Calculate** and **humiliate** became "compare" words with a short note, because they are not true synonyms.
- **Rejected, with reasons logged:**
  - merging the computer sense of **edit** into the writing sense (it has its own objects and often its own translation);
  - adding *edit out* (phrasal verbs are separate entries);
  - merging the two senses of **ensure** (the two-object pattern, *ensured them a place*, is a grammar split);
  - marking up the verb-forms note on **equal** (that note is written by a script, not by the drafter).

**Pronunciation.** The panel disputed my British transcription of **ensure**, `ɪnˈʃɔː`: only 1 of 3 votes agreed, and the others gave `ɪnˈʃʊə`. Both are real British pronunciations. I kept `ɪnˈʃɔː` to match the existing entry **sure**, added `ɪnˈʃʊə` as a British variant, flagged the entry, and wrote a line for you in the curator file. This follows how **sure** (adverb) was handled on 2026-10-17.

**What did not work.** A word that one entry names as a synonym should list the first entry back, at the matching sense; these mirror links are called back-links. The cross-reference tool did not write seven of them because it could not tell which sense to attach them to, so I placed them by hand. Two of them (on **tire** and **appear**) I found only by checking myself, because the tool's report was cut short in my view. I also dropped **change** as a synonym of the computer sense of **edit**, since it was too loose to mirror.
