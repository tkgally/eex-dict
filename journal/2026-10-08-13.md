## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 56 issues: 50 from reviewer-a, 6 from reviewer-b. 43 were blocking. I applied 47, rejected 9, and escalated none.

Main changes:

- *contract*: the illness sense said "a serious disease". Reviewer-a was right that people also contract minor infections, so the sense is now "get a disease or an infection". The usage note and learner-error note (*caught a cold*, not *contracted*) were changed to match. American English often stresses the agreement sense on the first syllable (*KON-trakt*); this is now a pronunciation variant, and the adaptation note says so too. The mention of *contract out* moved to "see also", which queued the phrasal verb.
- *crack*: the claim that something that cracks "does not come apart" was too strong. The usage note now only says a cracked thing usually stays in one piece, and the learner error built on that claim was removed. The "lose control" sense no longer says "tell a secret". The *crack a code* sense lost its informal label.
- *crowd*: senses 2 to 4 were redefined (no "small space" or "pushing"; "too close" without the pressure clause; "mind or thoughts"). An unnatural example about a driver was replaced.
- *corner*: the vehicle and market senses were widened. Reviewer-b objected to an adjective use (*a cornered animal*), so the example was replaced with a passive one.
- *underneath* (preposition): the comparison of *under*, *underneath*, *below*, and *beneath* no longer says *under* fits every situation, and the learner-error note now gives only *under* for ages.
- *famous*: the adaptation note now says *famous* "almost always" means known by many people, since older English also has *a famous victory*.

Rejected: reviewer-a's objection to *look underneath* with no object (this is natural English); its call to drop the informal label on *crack a joke*; its view that *lend me 50 dollars until Friday* is unnatural; and a consistency flag that misread *the wood underneath*. Reviewer-b's objection to `core_idea` being a sentence is against house style. Its point that *crowded with* belongs to the adjective was also rejected, because it is the passive of the verb's first sense.

Back-links: `crossref.py` added mirror references to eleven existing entries. I checked each one. It put *contract* on the "take hold" sense of *catch*, so I moved it by hand to the illness sense. The rest were on the right sense.
