# 2026-09-29 (12) — Lint: the twenty-third housekeeping pass

## What this run did

This was the fourth and last cycle of the scheduled run that began at 12:44 UTC. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request; a run has at most four.) The first three cycles merged as pull requests #134 (a review of fourteen function words), #135 (the originality check), and #136 (twelve new verbs, *surprise* to *think*).

The mode selector forced a **lint** pass, because five runs had passed since the last one. A lint pass is housekeeping over the whole dictionary and the project's knowledge base. It uses no paid calls.

- **Word caps and links.** Every file is within its word limit, and every link between pages works.
- **Defining vocabulary.** Definitions must be written with a fixed list of about 2,400 basic words, or with words that have entries of their own. The scan of all 729 entries found no breaks of this rule.
- **Old claims.** Three claim files from earlier runs, which record which words a run is working on, were cleared. No word was left marked as claimed.
- **Cross-references.** When entry A lists entry B as a synonym, antonym, or word to compare, B should list A too. A tool adds the missing half, called a back-link, but it has to guess which sense of B to put it on, and it often guesses wrong. This time it added 21 back-links. I checked each one: 15 were right and 6 were on the wrong sense, and I moved those six by hand:
  - *break* (in *break a habit*) had gone under *stop* "pause for a short time"; it now sits under "no longer do something".
  - *exist* (stay alive on very little) had gone under *survive* "continue to exist"; it is now on the "stay alive" sense.
  - *imagine* and *say* were swapped on *suppose*. "Think something is probably true" and "imagine something is true as an example" each now carry the right one.
  - *reflect* (think carefully) moved from *think* "have an opinion" to *think* "use your mind".
  - *run* (a road runs from one place to another) moved from *stretch* "become longer" to *stretch* "cover a large area".

  A second run of the tool wrote nothing new. The wrong-guess rate this time was higher than usual (six of 21, against about one in five before), because the new verbs have many close senses.
- **Reviewer precision.** For each reviewer model and each kind of issue, I measured how often its objections were accepted. A kind of issue accepted less than 30 percent of the time, over twenty or more decisions, is switched off for that reviewer. Nothing new crossed the line. The closest is reviewer-a's objections to pronunciation: 32 percent, over 130 decisions. Nearly all its rejected objections are about a spelling convention for American English that the project has already settled.
- **The knowledge base.** The wiki index lists every page, and every listed page exists. The log has an entry for every cycle. The file of questions for the curator has no duplicates, and the open questions are unchanged.
