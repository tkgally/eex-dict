## The entries

- *view* has four senses (opinion, what you see, being seen, a way of understanding), the phrases *in view of something*, *with a view to doing something*, and *on view*, and a short guide to choosing between *view* and *opinion*.
- *voice* has five senses, from the sound of speaking to the grammar term (*the passive voice*), and the phrases *raise your voice* and *make your voice heard*.
- *walk* has five senses, including the distance (*a ten-minute walk*) and the way someone walks, and the phrases *a walk in the park* and *walk of life*.
- *wall* has the phrases *hit a wall*, *drive someone up the wall*, and *off the wall*.
- *wage* has a guide to *wage*, *salary*, *pay*, and *income*.
- Learner errors include *many violences* (it is uncountable), *a view to the river* (say *of*), *a ten-minutes walk*, *make the volume small* (say *turn it down*), and *a photo in the wall* (say *on*).

Before review, I removed two phrases I had drafted, because the house rule files an idiom under its first noun: *at the top of your voice* belongs under *top*, and *the writing is on the wall* under *writing*. Both are noted in `NEXT.md` for later.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 56 issues, 47 of them marked blocking. I agreed with 52 and fixed the entries myself, and rejected 4 with a logged reason. None needed the owner. Reviewer-a raised almost all of them; reviewer-b raised two.

The most useful corrections:

- *virus*: I had called a virus "a very small living thing". Whether a virus is alive is disputed, so the definition now says it can grow only inside living cells. The computer sense no longer says a virus is always hidden, and the usage note no longer says a viral illness is always mild.
- *voice*: I had told learners that *speak with a low voice* is wrong. It is grammatical; *in a low voice* is just more usual. The learner error was removed and the usage note softened.
- *vote*: *win the vote* means winning a vote, not gaining the right to vote, so it left the "right to vote" sense. *The popular vote* (all the votes of the public) is now explained correctly.
- *wall*: sense 3 now covers a mass of people or things (*a wall of people*), which one example already showed. Outdoor walls are no longer limited to stone or brick.
- *visit*: the informal American sense is now "time spent talking with someone in a friendly way, often in their home".
- *wallet*: the usage note no longer ties *purse* to women; it states the British and American meanings of *purse* plainly.

What I rejected: reviewer-a said *volume* cannot mean a book in general, but *the library has thousands of volumes* is ordinary formal English, so the sense stays and its explanation says it is formal. It also said the "singular only" grammar of *a ten-minute walk* conflicts with the article *a*; "singular only" means there is no plural, and *a* is allowed.

After the review, the cross-reference tool added seven back-links to existing entries. One was on the wrong sense (*bug*, "insect", instead of *bug*, "an illness"), and I moved it. *View* now lists *scene* as a synonym, to match *scene*.
