## What this run did

This was the thirteenth and last cycle of this session, and a **build** run, which writes new dictionary entries. When it merged, though, the run clock (the script that decides whether a new cycle may start) still allowed one more. The rules say to start a cycle whenever the clock allows, so this one ran. It took six words instead of ten so that it would finish well inside the time limit.

The six verbs, all from band 1, the most basic band of the word list, were **pronounce, protect, prove, provide, pull, punish**.

- *Pronounce* covers saying a sound and the formal "say officially" sense (*I now pronounce you husband and wife*). It warns that the noun is spelled *pronunciation*, not *pronounciation*.
- *Prove* covers proving something true, turning out to be something (*the job proved harder than we expected*), and *prove yourself*.
- *Provide* explains the pattern *provide someone with something*, which learners often get wrong as *provide us books*.
- *Pull* covers pulling toward you, pulling something out, vehicles pulling into a place, and pulling a muscle.

## Checks and reviews

All six entries went through the full pipeline:

- **Format and inflections.** All six passed the format check. The inflection tool took *proved* from the table of irregular forms, with its note "also proven".
- **Pronunciation.** All twelve American and British transcriptions were verified.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised only 8 issues. Each got a logged decision: 6 fixes applied and 2 rejected.

**What was fixed.** *Pull* got the most attention:

- Its sense for vehicles had been defined as "move in a particular direction", which is too vague. It now reads "move in a particular direction as you arrive at or leave a place", with the common combinations *pull into*, *pull out of*, and *pull up*.
- Its sense for taking something out had been defined using the word *pulling* itself. It now says "using force".

Two smaller fixes:

- In *pronounce*, an example that mentioned a bare letter (*the k in knife*) could not be marked properly under the house style, so it was replaced.
- In *protect*, *well protected* was recast as *be well protected*, following the house rule for participle phrases.

**What was rejected.** Reviewer-a wanted *proven* put in *prove*'s past-participle slot alongside *proved*. Each slot holds one form, and the alternative is already recorded in the inflection note, which the site shows. Reviewer-b's usual objection to the quoted headword in adaptation notes (short notes for future translators) was rejected again.
