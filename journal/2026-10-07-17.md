## What this cycle did

The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **closure**: drafting entries for words the dictionary already uses somewhere, in a definition or in a word-family box, but has no entry for yet. Closure keeps the dictionary self-contained, so that a reader who meets a word in a definition can look it up.

The first source, words used in definitions, offered a single row: *prepare*, queued as a noun. The scanning tool that queues these rows guesses the part of speech when the word gives no clue, and here it guessed wrong: the definition in *scene* that uses the word uses the verb. I marked the noun row as a duplicate, queued *prepare* as a verb, and drafted it. Surprisingly, this common verb had no entry until now, because it was not on the defining-vocabulary list (the 2,394 plain words that definitions are meant to use) and so had never been queued as a core word.

The second source, words named in word-family boxes, gave verbs that pair with nouns already in the dictionary: *carpet*, *chance*, *characterize*, *clothe*, *club*, *coin*, *computerize*, *condition*, *confide*, and *conflict*. Earlier cycles had skipped *carpet* and *chance* as too thin; this time I drafted them, and both turned out to have three real senses each (*carpet* includes *a hillside carpeted with flowers*; *chance* includes *chance it* and *chance on a café*). Rather than release it a second time and have it come back every closure cycle, I marked it `deferred` (set aside to revisit later). Eleven entries in all, under the per-cycle limit of twelve.

*Prepare* is the fullest entry: four senses (get ready, make something ready, make food, make someone ready), a usage note on *get ready* versus *prepare*, and the learner error *prepare the exam* (which means write the test, as a teacher does) for *prepare for the exam*. *Confide* has three learner errors about where the person goes (*confided to me that*, never *confided me that*). *Clothe* warns that it is not the everyday verb for getting dressed.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Every entry is read by two outside reviewer models, field by field; I then judge each problem they raise. Reviewer-b (the Google model) raised 3 issues; one of its replies came back empty and was re-run, after which it was complete. Reviewer-a (the OpenAI model) raised 49. I agreed with 45 of the 52 and changed the entries; I disagreed with 7 and kept the text, with the reason logged.

The useful catches: my *conflict* entry had collocations such as *conflicting advice*, which belong to the adjective *conflicting*, not to the verb; both reviewers caught this, and those items now wait for the adjective's own entry. My *coin* entry defined the minting sense loosely and used an example about a country issuing currency, which is a different thing; both were replaced. *Condition* had a hair-care definition that said *after washing* and an example that used the verb without an object; both fixed. *Chance to meet someone* is formal rather than literary, and the label, usage note, and learner error now agree on that.

What I rejected: reviewer-a said *club together* (share the cost of something) is as American as British; American speakers usually say *chip in*, so the note stays. It said the British sense of *carpet* (to tell someone off) is not old-fashioned; it is now rare, so the label stays. It said *chance it* is not informal; it is conversational, so the label stays. And two of its flags on *condition* misnumbered the senses: it read sense 3 as the leather-and-fitness sense, which is sense 4.

All twenty-two pronunciations (American and British for each word) were verified by the checking panel. One definition word outside the defining vocabulary (*British*, in a core-idea sentence) was rewritten, and *luck*, which has no entry yet, was replaced with *by accident* in *chance*.

Back-links (the tool adds the reverse of each new cross-reference to the other entry) went to ten entries. One landed on the wrong sense: *chance* was added to *find* under "be somewhere"; I moved it to "by chance".
