## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models (reviewer-a and reviewer-b) read every field. *Innocent* came back clean. The others drew 43 issues, 37 marked blocking. I applied 32 and rejected 11, each logged with a reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`; none needed the owner.

Applied, among others:

- *kind*: a subsense for polite requests (*Would you be kind enough to...*) was really sense 1 in a fixed phrase; it is now a collocation of sense 1. Sense 2 (*kind to your skin*) is now *not causing harm or damage to something*. An adaptation note claiming the adjective and the noun *kind* (*type*) are unrelated was removed.
- *informal*: sense 2 (language) no longer says informal words never appear in serious writing; sense 3 (*an informal agreement*) is *done or agreed in a less official way, without a formal record*. A usage note that explained the dictionary's own *informal* label was removed: a usage note is about the word, not the dictionary.
- *large*: *at large* now names both its meanings (not yet caught; *society at large*, in general). Both reviewers caught *This shirt comes in small, medium, and large*, where *large* is a noun; it became *We'll share a large popcorn*.
- *last*: the note said *the last week* always means the seven days up to now; it can also mean the final week of a period. The learner error is now *I saw him in last week*.
- *late*: the learner error was about the adverb (*I arrived lately*); it is now an adjective error, *Sorry for my late*.
- *lazy*, *individual*, *industrial*, *international*, *interested*, *impossible*: a core idea and a grammar code on *lazy* corrected; several cross-references that did not fit removed (*group*, *unique*, *agricultural*, *local*, *relaxing*); *interested* sense 2 now covers a personal connection; one adaptation note on *impossible* narrowed.

Rejected: reviewer-a wanted *at last*, *last but not least*, and *every last* moved out of the adjective entry. The house rule keeps an idiom under its keyword whatever the part of speech. It also said *It is impossible that he saw us* is not normal English; it is. *National* and *domestic* stay as opposites of *international*.

The back-link tool added mirror cross-references to five older entries (*difficult*, *bored*, *dead*, *active*, *careless*); I checked each, and all landed on the right sense.
