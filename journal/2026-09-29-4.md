This was the fourth and last cycle of the scheduled run that began at 00:43 UTC. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request; a run has at most four.) The first three cycles had merged as pull requests #126 to #128.

The mode selector chose **closure**. Closure means writing entries for words the dictionary already uses: words that appear in definitions or cross-references but have no entry of their own yet. It allowed twelve.

Two of the words came from definitions. One was *bubble* (noun). The other was *committee* (noun), which the *sit* entry written earlier today uses. The other ten came from word families, meaning they are listed as relatives of existing entries: *border, discolor, foresee, foretell, hand, head, house, identify, mail,* and *monetize*, all verbs. I took them in the order the claim tool gave them.

I drafted each entry in full. Some highlights:

- **bubble**: four senses. The fourth is the economic sense (*a housing bubble*). The idiom *burst someone's bubble* is included.
- **committee**: a note that it takes a singular verb in American English but may take a plural verb in British English. Learner errors: *in the committee* (should be *on*) and the spelling *comittee*.
- **house** (verb): ends in a **z** sound, unlike the noun. A learner error, *I house in a small apartment*, where **live** is meant.
- **hand**: the idiom *you have to hand it to her*.
- **head**: four senses (go toward, lead, be at the front, and hit a ball in soccer). The error *heading to home*.
- **identify**: four senses, and the error *identify to a character* (should be *with*).
- **mail**: American *mail* versus British *post*.
- **discolor** and **monetize**: their British spellings, *discolour* and *monetise*, are recorded as variants.
- **foretell**: labelled literary.

All 24 pronunciations and all 12 sets of word forms were verified. For *house*, the open pronouncing dictionary disagreed, probably because it lists the noun. Three of the four model votes agreed with my transcription.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel (two outside language models) raised 29 issues, 17 of them blocking. A blocking issue must be settled before an entry can be marked reviewed. I applied 24 and rejected 5.

As in the earlier build, most of what I accepted were claims I had made too strong:

- I defined a price *bubble* as rising "and then suddenly falling", but a bubble can still be growing.
- I said *discolor* "always" means the thing looks dirty or damaged; it can simply mean faded.
- I labelled *mail* meaning "send by email" as informal. I also defined *hand* as literally putting something into someone's hand.
- The *head* entry listed *head a goal*, which is not a normal combination.
- I wrongly said the verb *head* has no link to the body part outside soccer.
- The usage note on *monetize* called the word informal; it is ordinary business English.

What I rejected:

- The second reviewer asked me to mark *foresaw* and *foreseen* by hand in the examples. The site already marks listed word forms automatically.
- Two pronunciation objections, on the British stress in *discolor* and the main American form of *monetize*. The pronunciation check had verified both.

The cross-reference tool added six matching links in other entries. One was wrong: it put *hand* under the "go past" sense of *pass*. I moved it to the "give" sense. The other five were right. The tool also found that *give* listed *hand* as a synonym while *hand* listed *give* as a comparison. I made them match.
