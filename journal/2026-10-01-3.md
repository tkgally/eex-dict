## What the run did

The selector, the script that picks each cycle's task, chose **build**. In a build cycle, new entries are written from the queue of words still to be done. This cycle took the next twelve band-1 nouns. Band 1 is the most basic of the dictionary's five frequency bands. The words were *boat, body, bomb, bone, book, boot, boss, bottle, bottom, bowl, box,* and *boy*. The dictionary now has 873 entries, all reviewed.

Several of these are large words:

- **Body** has seven senses: the physical form, the main part, a dead body, an official group (*a governing body*), the main part of a thing (*the body of a car*), an amount (*a body of water*), and fullness in hair or wine.
- **Book** has six senses and eight idioms, among them *by the book*, *read someone like a book*, and *cook the books*.
- **Bone** has eight idioms, among them *have a bone to pick with someone* and *make no bones about something*.
- **Bottom** has six senses, including the lowest position in a list and the far end of a garden (British).
- **Box** has six senses, from the container to the square you check on a form.

Usage notes explain the traps between British and American English: the *boot* of a car (American *trunk*), *check* or *tick* a box, and *a bomb* meaning a failure in American English but *go down a bomb* meaning a success in British English. Before any reviewer saw the entries, the vocabulary checker found seventeen definition words outside the defining vocabulary of about 2,400 basic words. All seventeen were reworded, for example *mystery*, *sincerely*, *hollow*, and *nuclear*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside reviewer models read every field. They raised 71 issues, 39 of them blocking. I applied 56 and rejected 15, logging each decision with its reason.

The reviewers caught real overstatements:

- *Boat* had said that calling a ship a *boat* is informal. It is standard for a ferry, so the label is gone.
- *Boat* had also said that the choice between *in* and *on* depends on the boat's size. The usage note now explains it by how you travel: *in* a small boat you sit in, *on* a boat you travel on.
- Four learner errors were not reliably wrong: *a water bottle*, *a soup bowl*, *my chief*, and *in the big boat*. All four were removed.
- The leader sense of *boss* is not informal. It is standard in news reports, so the label was removed.
- A few British terms appeared in examples with no regional label. *Witness box*, *tracksuit bottoms*, and *tick a box* were replaced with general ones.

I rejected these objections:

- That *bottom*, meaning the part of the body you sit on, is British only. It is common in American English too, especially with children.
- That the **b** is pronounced in *bomber* and *bombing*. It is silent in both.
- That the American transcription of *boot* is wrong. American transcriptions here leave out the length mark by settled rule (a "fence").
- That *hit the books* is not mainly American. A region label in this dictionary means *mainly used there*, which fits.

When the cross-reference tool linked the new entries back from existing ones, it made one such back-link: *organization* now points to *body*, under its "group" sense. That is correct.
