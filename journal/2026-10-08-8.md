## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 56 issues, 50 from reviewer-a and 6 from reviewer-b. Reviewer-b's first reply on *report* came back in a form the tool could not read, so I ran reviewer-b again on that entry alone. *Hand* and *head* are long entries (over thirty phrases each), and most of the issues were on *head*.

I agreed with 40 and fixed the entries in my own words, for example:

- *escape*: the sense *escape the heat, the crowds, the pressure* had been defined only as "stop thinking about something unpleasant"; it now also covers getting away from it for a time. The sense for a sound that escapes someone (*a sigh escaped her*) now has a "literary" label instead of a note saying "mostly in writing". A learner-error note that called *escape from injury* simply wrong now says the direct object is the usual pattern.
- *milk*: breast milk and plant milks were defined as *white*, which is not always true (coconut milk, for one); the colour was dropped from those senses. A learner error said *a milk* is always wrong; it is fine when ordering a drink, so the error is now *I drink a milk every morning*.
- *head*: *head teacher* and *headmaster* are now *mainly* British, with American schools *usually* having a principal. The notes on counting uses now separate the singular (*ten dollars a head*) from the plural that looks the same (*fifty head of cattle*), and no longer call *head* in *head coach* an adjective. *Head office* was listed under the "person in charge" sense, which it does not mean, and was removed. One example showed the hyphenated adjective *head-to-head*, which belongs to a separate entry; it was replaced, and `head-to-head-adj` was added to the queue (band 3).
- *hand*: the rule that English uses *your*, not *the*, with body parts now says *usually*, since *hit him on the hand* is normal. *On the left hand* still names a particular hand; only the meaning *on that side* is old-fashioned.
- *sign*: the sense about signing a player now says which way round it goes: a team **signs** a player; a player **signs with** or **for** a team.
- *smile*, *smoke*, *shake*, *report*, *cry*: smaller rewordings, such as a handshake being *a common* greeting rather than *the usual* one, and removing *always* from a note claiming English always *smokes* tobacco.

Several objected to things that are house style: a translator's note that opens with the headword in quotation marks, an explanation that names what the subject of a sense can be (*used of an engine*), and a core idea written as a sentence. Reviewer-a also wanted to remove "see also" links from *smoke* to *cigarette* and *tobacco* and from *laugh* to *face* (which holds *laugh in someone's face*); see-also links exist for exactly such related entries. Reviewer-b said *verb + adverb or preposition* is not a valid pattern; it is in the closed list.
