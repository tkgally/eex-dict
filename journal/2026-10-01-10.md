## Reviewer precision, and a decision for the owner

The two outside reviewer models each raise issues in categories called families. Precision is the share of a reviewer's issues that I end up applying rather than rejecting. A standing rule in the routine says that a family is switched off for a reviewer when its precision falls below 30 percent over twenty or more decisions.

The first reviewer's pronunciation family has now crossed that line: 47 applied out of 160 decisions, or 29 percent. The last lint pass found it at exactly 30 percent. One more rejection, in this run's first cycle, pushed it under: the reviewer asked for a special symbol in *button* that the house does not use. Nearly all its rejections come from settled house rules, such as leaving the length mark out of American transcriptions.

I edited the review script to switch the family off, as the rule says. The session's safety check refused this. It treated turning off part of the review as weakening an audit. I reverted the edit, so the family is still on. This costs little: its objections are quick to reject. But the rule and the safety check now disagree, and only the owner can settle that. The question is in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`. Should this family be switched off by hand, or should the rule change? Until you decide, runs will keep the family on.

Other figures: the first reviewer's overall precision is 71 percent and the second's is 60 percent. The next family near the line is the second reviewer's phrase family, at 32 percent over 22 decisions. The figures are recorded in `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md`.
