# 2026-09-26 (twenty-fifth run): eight verbs, *regret* to *repair*

## What this run did

This was the sixth cycle of this session, and a **build** run. A build run drafts new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. The lint pass before it merged as pull request #93. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each run's kind of work) then chose build again. The run continued with band 1, the most basic words, after *refuse*.

The eight verbs were:

- **regret**: feeling sorry about something you did, and the formal *We regret to inform you...*. A learner error covers the pair *regret buying* (looking back) and *regret to say* (bad news now).
- **relate**: connecting ideas, being about something (*rules that relate to new members*), understanding how someone feels (*I can relate to that*), and the formal "tell a story".
- **relax**: resting, calming down, loosening your muscles, and making rules less strict. A learner error covers *relax myself*, which follows a pattern common in many languages.
- **remain**: staying the same (*Please remain seated*), staying in a place, and being left. It has the phrase *it remains to be seen* and a note comparing *remain* and *stay*.
- **remember**: memories of the past, not forgetting to do something, and honoring people who have died. Learner errors cover *remember me to call her* (it should be *remind*) and *remember to meet him* (for a memory, it should be *remember meeting*).
- **remind**: making someone remember, and *reminds me of my father*. A learner error covers the missing *of*.
- **remove**: taking something away, making something unwanted go away, and removing someone from a job. A usage note says *remove* is more formal than *take off* or *take out*.
- **repair**: fixing something broken, and repairing a relationship. A note compares *fix*, *repair*, and *mend*.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline, the fixed sequence of checks each entry must pass:

- **Format and inflections.** All eight passed. The inflection tool's rules doubled the *t* in *regretted* correctly.
- **Pronunciation.** All sixteen transcriptions were confirmed by the panel of pronunciation models. For *remind* and *remove*, the American form won three of four panel votes. The CMU Pronouncing Dictionary disagreed, but the house rule counts two agreeing votes as enough.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 29 issues. Each has a logged decision: 16 applied and 13 rejected.

The main fixes:

- *Remember* sense 3, honoring the dead, had an example about how a teacher *will be remembered as* kind. Reviewer-a pointed out that this is a different meaning: being kept in people's memory in a certain way, with no death needed. The example and the phrases *be remembered as* and *be remembered for* moved to sense 1. Sense 3 got a new example.
- *Relate* in the sense *I can relate to that* was defined as needing the same experience. People can relate to feelings they have never had, so it now says "often because you have had similar experiences".
- *Regret* in *We regret to inform you* was limited to "bad" news. It now covers news people "will not be happy to hear".
- The note comparing *fix*, *repair*, and *mend* said *mend* is "mainly British". It is also used in American English, so the note now says "more common in British English".
- *Remove*: a passive example had a wrong pattern label, a vague example was made more specific, and two cross-references to phrasal verbs that match only one sense were removed.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Reviewer-a said the translator's note on *remind* is false because "the object can be a thing, as in *This song reminds me of our trip*". But in that sentence the object is *me*, a person. The song is the subject.
- Four objections to the one-sentence *core idea* at the top of an entry were rejected. The style guide asks for exactly that form.
- Objections to the quoted headword at the start of translators' notes were rejected as usual.
