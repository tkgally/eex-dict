## What this cycle did

A **build cycle** writes new entries from the work queue and takes each one through the full review pipeline. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose build again because it was furthest behind its usual share. The claim tool gave out the next twelve band-1 adjectives in queue order (band 1 is the most common words): *daily*, *dangerous*, *dark*, *dead*, *deaf*, *deep*, *delicious*, *different*, *difficult*, *digital*, *direct*, and *dirty*.

I wrote each from my own knowledge, following the house style guide. Some points of interest:

- *dead* has six senses: not alive, not working (*my phone is dead*), very quiet, no feeling (*my arm went dead*), no longer used, and complete (*dead silence*). It has the phrase *dead and buried* and a usage note on *dead*, *die*, and *death*. Its learner errors are *He was died* and *My grandfather is died*.
- *deep* has seven senses, from "far down" to a deep voice and a deep red. Its learner error is *three meters depth*, and its phrase is *deep down*.
- *different* has a usage note on *different from*, *than*, and *to*, and errors with *with* and *of*.
- *difficult* warns that the thing is difficult, not the person (*I find this difficult*, not *I am difficult to understand this*).
- *deaf* has a usage note on *deaf people*, *hard of hearing*, and *Deaf* with a capital letter, which many people who use a sign language use for their community.
- *dangerous* and *delicious* have short notes on choosing between near-synonyms (*dangerous*, *risky*, *unsafe*; *delicious*, *tasty*).

Three uses belong to adverb entries that do not exist yet: *dead tired*, *dig deep*, and *fly direct*. Each entry's usage note points to the adverb, and the three adverbs were added to the queue.

## What the checks and reviewers found

The pronunciation panel (three outside models, plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary for American English) did not agree with two transcriptions: the American *different* and the British *direct*. For each I made the listed alternative the main form and the old main form the alternative. An earlier build did the same for *complex*. Both then passed. The British *different* was then disputed in turn. Its main form is now the three-part *DIF-er-ent*, with the two-part *DIF-rent* as an alternative, and it passed.

The two reviewer models raised 46 issues, 34 of them blocking. I applied 35 and rejected 11. Nothing went to the owner. The main changes:

- *dark*: the subsense for people said only "having brown or black hair". One example (*Both of the children are dark*) could be read as being about race. The subsense now says "dark hair, and often dark eyes or skin", the example was replaced, and the translator's note now agrees with it.
- *dirty*: the "dishonest" sense now covers unfair methods (*a dirty fight*). The pollution sense now covers water and land as well as air. Two loose synonyms (*rude*, *dishonest*) were removed.
- *different*: *similar* was wrongly listed as its opposite, and *various* as a synonym. Both were removed.
- *digital*: one example used *digital* as a noun (*moving its services to digital*), so it was replaced.
- *delicious*: the sense "very pleasant" is now labeled literary, and an unsupported claim about *very delicious* was softened.

Rejected: one reviewer said *deep in the forest* uses the adjective, but learner dictionaries treat "far into" as the adverb. Another wanted *deep down* moved to an adverb entry, but house rules keep idioms under their keyword whatever its part of speech. One reviewer called *low in sound* circular, but *low* is not a form of *deep*. Three issues asked to remove the house gradability codes.

The cross-reference tool added one back-link: *alive* (sense 1) now lists *dead* as its opposite. That is right.
