# 2026-09-26 (twenty-ninth run): *bell*, *motorcycle*, and six related words

## What this run did

This was the tenth cycle of this session, and a **closure** run. A closure run writes entries for words the dictionary already uses: first words that appear inside its own definitions (source *closure*), then related forms of existing entries (source *family*), then words named in cross-references (source *crossref*). The build before it merged as pull request #97.

Only three rows under source *closure* were waiting:

- **bell** (noun), used in the definition of *ring*
- **motorcycle** (noun), used in the definition of *ride*
- *upward*, used in the definition of *rise*. It had been queued as a noun, but *upward* is an adverb or an adjective. The noun row was marked declined, the adverb was queued in its place, and one line went to the curator file (`reviews/needs_curator.txt`), as the rules require for any declined row.

The rest came from source *family*:

- **oneself** (pronoun): the formal reflexive used with *one* (*One should be honest with oneself*). The usage note says everyday English uses *yourself*.
- **achieve**, **aim**, **arrange**, **contact**, **enclose** (verbs). Each is the verb of a noun or an adjective the dictionary already has: *achievement* (queued), *aim*, *arrangement*, *contact*, *enclosed*.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline, the fixed sequence of checks each entry must pass:

- **Format.** One problem was fixed before review: the music sense of *arrange* had only one example, and every sense needs at least two.
- **Defining vocabulary.** Three definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary, the list of about 2,400 basic words that definitions are written in: *target*, *upside*, and *envelope*. All three were reworded.
- **Pronunciation.** All sixteen transcriptions were confirmed by the panel of pronunciation models.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 18 issues. Each has a logged decision: 14 applied and 4 rejected.

The main fixes:

- *Aim* in the pointing sense included "a ball". Reviewer-a pointed out that people do not normally *aim a ball*. That example went, and so did *take aim*, where *aim* is the noun (both reviewers).
- *Enclose* had the example *Enclosed is a list of our prices*. Here *enclosed* works like an adjective in an inverted sentence, so it does not show the verb. Both reviewers flagged it, and it was replaced with *She enclosed a photo with her letter*.
- *Oneself* for emphasis (*check the facts oneself*) now means "personally, without help from others", not "no one else".
- The translator's note on *achieve* had said English uses *manage to* for "succeed in doing". It now gives *succeed in* as well, which agrees with the entry's own learner-error note.
- *Bell* listed *ring a bell* as an ordinary collocation. That phrase is the idiom meaning "seem familiar", which the entry already defines as a phrase, so the collocation was replaced.

Rejected: an old spelling *inclose* for *enclose*, which learners do not need; the usual objections to the one-sentence core idea; and the quoted headword at the start of translators' notes.

The cross-reference tool added four back-links, links pointing back from an existing entry to a new one. One was on the wrong sense: *achieve* landed on *reach* in the sense "arrive at a place". It was moved to the sense "get to a level or result" (*reach an agreement*).
