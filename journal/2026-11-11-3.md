**What the entries contain.** A few points worth knowing:

- *login* has two senses: the process of getting into a system (*after three failed logins*), and the name and password you use. Its learner error is the very common *I can't login*: the verb is two words, *log in*.
- *loser* separates the plain competition sense from the insult (*a total loser*), which is labeled informal and disapproving. It also has a subsense for *a good loser* and *a sore loser*.
- *luggage* carries two real learner errors, *three luggages* and *a new luggage*. Both come from the noun being uncountable.
- *maintenance* has three senses: repair work (with a subsense for the staff who do it, *call maintenance*), keeping something at a level (formal), and the British sense of money paid after a divorce. A pronunciation-and-spelling note warns that it is spelled *-ten-*, not *-tain-* as in *maintain*, and that the stress moves to the first part.
- *making* holds *the makings of* (*the makings of a fine player*) and three idioms: *in the making*, *be the making of someone*, and *of your own making*.
- *male* (the noun) mirrors the existing *female* entry, with the same usage note: as a word for people it belongs to science, police reports, and forms, and in conversation it can sound cold.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Each entry goes to two reviewer models from different companies, which check every field and mark each problem as blocking (must be settled before publishing) or minor. They raised 37 issues, 7 of them blocking. I accepted 30 and rejected 7, and every decision is logged with a reason.

- Both reviewers rightly objected that *password* was listed as a synonym of *login*. It is only part of a login, so it is now a "compare" word with a short note.
- Both also saw that *right after login* uses the noun without *a* or *the*, while the grammar said it was countable only. That sense is now marked "countable or uncountable".
- They were also right on several smaller points. *Hand luggage* is a noun + noun pairing, not adjective + noun. *Trick* and *repair* are related words, not synonyms. *Under new management* belongs to the "people in charge" sense. An example about soup "working its magic" did not fit the definition. The definition of *make* used the word *made*, which is circular, so it now says *produced*.
- I rejected three blocking flags on *making*. They said the entry claims "no plural" while one sense is used only as *makings*. The reviewers are right that this looks inconsistent, but the inflections field is written by a script (`tools/inflect.py`), never by hand. The script cannot record a plural for a noun whose other sense is uncountable. This is a known gap, already recorded in `wiki/notes/inflect-uncountable-plural-gap.md` from *money*, *finance*, *energy*, and *rain*, and *making* is now added to that list. The examples mark **makings** by hand, as the house rules require.
- I also rejected a request to add a register label to *male* for people. The usage note already says where the word is used, and the existing *female* entry does the same.

**Other fixes.** Two definitions used words that have no entry yet (*philosophy* in *logic*, *brand* in *loyalty*), so I reworded both. The cross-reference tool (it adds the matching link in the other entry) could not decide which sense to link in four cases, so I placed those links by hand: in *password*, *model*, *man*, and *leadership*. *Logic* now points to *reason* as a "compare" word, matching what the *reason* entry already said.

## Cycle 2: lint

**What this cycle did.** After the first cycle merged, the selector chose *lint*. Lint is the periodic housekeeping pass, forced every ten runs (each cycle counts as a run), and ten had passed. It changes no definitions; it checks that the dictionary, the queue, and the wiki agree with one another and with the rules.

**The mechanical checks.**

- **Caps and links.** The word caps on the rule files and the wiki are all respected, and every relative link in the wiki resolves.
- **Cross-references.** The cross-reference tool keeps every link two-way. If entry A lists entry B as a synonym, B should list A. Run over the whole dictionary, it added three missing return links: *lawn* now lists *grass*, *lifetime* lists *time* in its word family, and *luggage* lists *bag*. In two cases it could not tell which sense of the target the link belonged to, so I placed them by hand: *location* (the "place" sense) now lists *situation* as a synonym, and *mall* (the shopping sense) lists *department store* as a compare word. Both came from entries written earlier today.
- **Claims.** Six old claim files, the records by which a run reserves words before writing them, belonged to runs that had already merged and were removed.
- **Band vote.** 38 queued words were still unbanded, meaning they had no frequency band yet. These are words a tool added to the queue because an entry mentions them. Three models voted a band for each, at a cost of under one cent. For two words the votes were more than one band apart, so I settled them myself, both at band 3. *Dead* as a noun (*the dead*, *in the dead of night*) is met in reading but is not a first-need word. *Only* as a conjunction (*I'd come, only I'm busy*) is mainly spoken and informal. All 38 rows are now ready to be built in their turn.

**The reports.** Three standing reports list things for later review runs to judge, not errors to fix now:

- 1,004 places where a cross-reference may name the wrong sense, up 7 from the last pass, mostly from new entries.
- 321 definitions that use their own headword. Most are harmless, such as *walk* (the noun) defined with the verb *walk*.
- 6 notes that promise an entry that does not exist yet. These are the same six as last time; all are unrelated homographs or phrasal verbs already noted in `NEXT.md`.

**Reviewer precision.** Precision is the share of a reviewer's flags that I accepted. Reviewer A, the Claude model, is at 0.85 and reviewer B, the Google model, at 0.82. The rule switches off any issue type a reviewer gets right less than 30 percent of the time, once it has twenty or more decisions. One type is near that line: reviewer B on inflections (word forms such as plurals), at 0.32 over 25 decisions. Its recent misses come from a gap in our own tool, not from the reviewer: the tool cannot record a plural like *makings* when another sense of the noun is uncountable. So no type was switched off, and fixing the tool would remove the cause. This is written up in `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md`.

**The judgment pass.** I read the wiki index against the pages: every page is listed. The open-questions page has nothing stale, and `reviews/needs_curator.txt` (the list of things waiting for you) has no duplicate lines. The log has no gaps.

**Spend.** Under one cent this cycle. The day's total is US$1.75 of the US$15 cap.

## Cycle 3: review

**What this cycle did.** The selector chose *review*: a second reading of published entries, to find what a learner would expect and cannot find. No draft entries were waiting and no reader reports were open. So the cycle continued the coverage pass, the owner's ruling B1 of 2026-10-24. That pass reads the most important band-1 words one by one, the ones with the most senses first (band 1 is the commonest group of words). The next four unread entries, all with five senses, were *program* (noun), *push* (verb), *quiet*, and *ready* (adjectives). Two earlier entries in the list, *practice* and *reading*, had already been read since 2026-10-24 and were skipped. The pass now stands at 291 entries.

**What I added before the reviewers saw the entries.**

- *program*: a subsense for the items performed at a concert, in order (*What's on the program tonight?*). The entry had a culture note saying that British English spells it *programme*. That only repeated the spelling variant already recorded in the entry, so I removed it (one fact, one field).
- *push*: a sense for making a level rise or fall (*bad weather pushed the price of fruit higher*). Also an informal sense for being nearly an age (*my dad is pushing seventy*).
- *quiet*: a sense for feelings that are strong but not shown openly (*quiet confidence*, *quiet determination*).
- *ready*: a subsense for needing something after effort (*I'm ready for a cold drink*). The rule "*ready for* before a noun, *ready to* before a verb" was stated three times, in the usage note, an adaptation note, and the learner error. It now lives only in the learner error.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewer models raised 57 issues, 10 of them blocking. I accepted 52 and rejected 5. The useful finds:

- *Program*: an example about a schedule "on the website" sat under the sense defined as a small printed booklet. Both reviewers blocked it, and it is replaced. My own new example said music "by three young writers"; the natural word is *composers*. A synonym, *scheme*, often suggests a dishonest plan in American English and was removed. A remark that Americans usually say *show* sat in the explanation field, which is meant for function words, so it moved to the usage note.
- *Push*: my new example *pushed many small shops close to closing* was unnatural and did not show a level changing; it is replaced. The "persuade" sense mixed two uses, pressing a person and campaigning for something. *Push for something* (*the workers are pushing for higher pay*) is now its own subsense, and *push someone into something* was added.
- *Quiet*: *small* is not a real synonym and was removed (from *small*'s entry too). *Have a quiet word* is mainly British, so that example was replaced. A pronunciation note comparing *quiet* and *quite* repeated what the pronunciation field already says, and it was removed.
- *Ready*: both reviewers pointed out that *ready* compares as *readier*, *readiest* (*readier to help*). The entry had wrongly said it compares with *more*. I removed that grammar code, and the inflection tool now produces *readier* and *readiest* by its ordinary rules. The "available" sense joined two ideas: things kept for use (*ready cash*) and things that come easily (*a ready smile*). These are now a sense and a subsense. The idiom *at the ready* and the phrase *ready, willing, and able* were added. The learner error is now the more typical *ready for going*.

What I rejected: *push your luck* is already in the entry for *luck*, under the house rule that an idiom lives under its first noun. *Make ready* is formal and rare today. I also rejected two objections to the new *pushing fifty* sense. One said the grammar label "not used in the passive" is not on the closed list, but it is. The other said the note "used only in the continuous form" belongs elsewhere, but the style guide puts that kind of restriction in the explanation field.

**What did not work.** While checking how the decision tool reports several issues on one field, I accidentally logged two real decisions with a placeholder note. Both decisions were correct, and I replaced the two notes with real reasons in the ledger before committing.

**What this cycle did.** The selector chose *build* again, allowing up to twelve new entries. The other six came from the queue in order: *camp* (noun and verb), *locate*, *sue*, *junior* (noun), and *lecturer*. All eight were written, checked, and published.

**What the entries contain.**

- *brand*: three senses. The product name (*which brand of coffee*), with a subsense for a company's or person's image (*a personal brand*). A kind or style of something (*his own brand of humor*). The mark burned into a farm animal's skin.
- *media*: the news sense, with *social media* as a subsense of its own. The materials artists use (*mixed media*). The objects that computer information is stored on.
- *camp*: the noun has places where people stay outdoors, with subsenses for refugee and prison camps and for army and training camps; the American summer camp for children; and a group with shared views (*split into two camps*). The verb has staying outdoors, and waiting outside a place to get something (*fans camped outside the stadium*).
- *locate*: finding something, and *be located* for where a place is. It carries a real learner error, *the hotel locates near the beach*.
- *junior*: the American third-year student; someone younger or lower at work (*ten years her junior*), which mirrors the existing *senior* entry; a young sports player; and the informal American *Junior* for a son.
- *lecturer*: its usage note explains that the word is a regular job title in British universities. In American English, *professor* is the usual word for a university teacher.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 28 issues, 5 of them blocking. I accepted 27 and rejected 1.

- The blocking issues were mostly about one mistake in *locate*. I had defined its second sense as *put or build something somewhere*, but all its examples meant *be located*, that is, *be in a place*. The sense is now *be in a particular place*, used in the passive. The active use (*locate the new office in the city center*) is a subsense.
- The other blocking issue was the missing *Junior*, which was added.
- *Media* was reshaped on the reviewers' advice: *social media* got its own subsense, and the art sense and the storage sense were separated.
- *Brand* gained the image subsense.
- A note now says *make* is a synonym of *brand* mainly for cars and machines.
- I rejected *camp it up*, meaning act in an exaggerated way. It belongs with a separate adjective, *camp*, not with the verb for staying in tents.

Three definitions had used words that have no entry yet (*tent*, *vacation*, *rank*), so I reworded them. The cross-reference tool could not choose a sense in two places, so I placed those links by hand: in *side* (the sense for groups against each other) and *find* (the sense for finding after looking).
