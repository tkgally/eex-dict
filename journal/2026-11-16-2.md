# Journal, 2026-11-16 (second run)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector, the tool that picks each cycle's kind of work from how long each kind has waited, chose a build cycle: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. It allowed up to twelve. The twelve claimed words were nouns from **regret** to **requirement**. Eleven were written: **regret**, **regulation**, **release**, **reminder**, **rental**, **replacement**, **reply**, **report card**, **reporter**, **reputation**, and **requirement**. The twelfth, **relations**, was not written: the existing entry for **relation** already has a sense for the plural use (*relations between countries*), so a separate entry would repeat it. Its queue row is now marked as a duplicate. One older entry, **relationship**, still lists **relations** as a synonym; that reference now points at a word that will never have an entry, and it should be moved to **relation** the next time **relationship** is reviewed.

Before drafting, the opening checks found nothing waiting: no open pull requests, no reader reports, nothing in the owner's inbox. One old branch with no unmerged work was already listed for pruning.

**Pronunciation.** Each entry gives an American and a British pronunciation, which a panel of three other models (and, for American, the open CMU pronouncing dictionary) checks by vote. Three of the first transcriptions were disputed. For **requirement**, the American form was rewritten to match the CMU dictionary (*rih-KWIRE-ment*, with the *r* closing the stressed syllable). For **regulation** and **reputation**, the British forms were changed to the more usual British vowel in the second syllable (the *u* of *put* rather than the weak *uh*). After that, all 22 transcriptions were verified.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Each entry is read by two reviewer models from different companies, field by field. They raised 45 issues, 9 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before the entry can be published). I applied 39 and rejected 6, each with a logged reason.

The main changes:

- **release**: one reviewer showed that the "feeling" sense was defined as a feeling while its examples (*running is a release for me*) show an activity that gets rid of stress. The definition now covers both. An example about pills releasing medicine did not fit the sense about letting gas or chemicals into the air, water, or ground, and was replaced. *Press release*, a countable written statement, became its own sense instead of a part of the uncountable "making public" sense. Heat is no longer called a substance.
- **regret**: the formal sense (*It is with regret that...*) is now uncountable, and the plural *send your regrets* is a separate part marked "plural only", after a reviewer pointed out that *a regret* is not possible in that sense. The first definition was widened to cover regret about things that simply happen.
- **rental**: the first definition now covers both sides of renting (the shop that rents out skis, not only the person who pays), and the "rented car or house" sense moved ahead of the "money paid" sense.
- Several collocations were moved to the right type (*safety regulations* and *entry requirements* are noun + noun, not adjective + noun), and examples that showed a fixed phrase instead of the sense they sat under were replaced.

The rejections: one reviewer wanted the *regulation shirts* use moved to an adjective entry; the project's settled rule is that a noun used before another noun stays in the noun entry, so I rejected that but removed the one example where *regulation* followed *be*, which really is adjective-like. I kept the "action in return" sense of **reply** (*her only reply was to walk out*), which a reviewer thought too thin. I rejected *make reply* as a reason to call **reply** uncountable (it is old-fashioned), and the claim that the "money paid" sense of **rental** is mainly British.

Four existing entries gained a cross-reference to a new one, placed by hand because the tool could not tell which sense matched: **law** (one rule) to **regulation**, **launch** to **release**, **relief** (good feeling) to **release**, and **condition** (rule) to **requirement**.

**Spend.** US$0.33 this cycle. The day's total is US$1.21 of the US$15 daily cap.

**What did not work.** My first pronunciations of three words disagreed with the checking panel; the votes and the open dictionary settled them. One definition used the padding *the act of*, which the style guide forbids; it was rewritten before review.

**What is next.** The queue continues after **requirement**. The next lint pass (the periodic tidy-up of the whole dictionary) is due after five more cycles.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new.
