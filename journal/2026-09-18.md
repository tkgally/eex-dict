## What the session did

Your note had two parts: your request that words used as words, quoted phrases, and the headword inside example sentences be visibly marked, which needs tags in the entry files; and four observations from a Japanese colleague on the entries for *another* and *money*. Both are now written rules, enforced wherever a program can enforce them.

**Marking words.** Two marks now go into the entry files' plain text: double asterisks around a word named as a word (`**all**` goes before a plural noun) and single asterisks around a phrase quoted to show use (`*all the students*`). The site shows the first in bold and the second in italics. The third case, the headword inside an example sentence, is not tagged by the writer: the site finds the headword and its inflected, variant, and contracted forms and sets them in bold when the pages are built. Where the automatic rule cannot find the form (*are* under *be*, a separated *pick it up*), the writer marks it by hand, and the validator, the program that checks every entry file, warns about any example without a form of its headword. The validator also rejects malformed marks, and the reviewers are told the marks are house style. The design is in `wiki/decisions/inline-markup.md`; the writer's rule is section 6 of the style guide.

**The colleague's points.** Pronunciation notes are now written in plain words for an intermediate learner (a sound compared with a familiar word, stress shown by a respelling such as *uh-NUTH-er*); the validator rejects terms such as *schwa*, and seven notes were rewritten. An entry must agree with itself, and a claim about a word's origin may appear only in the etymology field, verified against two open sources: the "*a* plus *other*" slip in *another* was an unverified origin claim in an explanation. A use that belongs to a different part of speech gets its own entry, never a sense or a remark inside this one: *another* standing alone (*I'll have another*) is a pronoun, so the determiner entry now points to a queued *another* (pronoun); likewise *all* (pronoun), *alone* (adverb), and *favorite* (noun). A grammar rule with a real exception names the exception as one: *money* is uncountable in ordinary use; *monies* belongs to legal documents. The colleague also found the entries about the right length and suggested a link to a Japanese-English dictionary; that is what the per-language slots in each entry are for. These rules are in `wiki/decisions/part-of-speech-and-consistency.md`, the style guide, and the reviewer prompt, which now also judges the whole entry's consistency.

**The entries.** No program may rewrite a prose field, so the marks were added by hand to 40 of the 82 entries: the three model entries, every function word, the four unfinished drafts, and the three entries your note named. The other 42 carry the flag `markup-pending`; the scheduled runs convert them in blocks of 20 during the second review reading every seed entry is due anyway.
