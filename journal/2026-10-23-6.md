# 2026-10-23, cycle 2 of the 12:43 UTC run: three prepositions and nine verbs

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: new entries taken from the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written, `headwords/queue.tsv`). The scheduler allowed twelve, and I wrote twelve, all in frequency band 2 (common words just below the core vocabulary):

- three prepositions: **regarding** (marked formal, with a note that **about** is the everyday word), **unlike** (*different from*, and *not typical of*, as in *it's unlike Sam to be late*), and **versus** (*against* in a game or a case, and *compared with* when choosing);
- nine verbs: **access**, **adapt**, **alter**, **analyze** (with the British spelling **analyse** as a variant), **appreciate**, **assemble**, **assess**, **associate**, and **bang**.

Each verb entry has its senses with signposts (short labels that help the reader pick a sense), grammar and verb patterns, two to four examples per sense, collocations (typical word combinations), its word family, and, where learners really go wrong, a learner-error note: *access to my email* used as a verb, *I would appreciate if* without **it**, *adapt with* for *adapt to*, *adapted a baby* for *adopted*, and *the analyze* for *the analysis*. **bang** includes its vulgar slang sense (*have sex*), labelled as such, because the style guide includes such senses for completeness.

## Checks

All entries passed the validator. Inflections came from the rules. Pronunciations go to a panel of three outside models plus, for American English, the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. Twenty-three of twenty-four transcriptions were confirmed at once. The British **associate** was disputed: I had written the middle as *shee*, and two panel members agreed on *see*, which is in fact the more usual British form. I changed it, the panel then confirmed it, and the pronunciation note now says both are heard.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models (reviewer-a and reviewer-b, from two different labs) raised 25 issues across the twelve entries. I applied 9 and rejected 16, each decision logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Applied:

- **adapt**, sense 3: I had limited it to turning a book into a film or a television program; it now covers any change of form, including the stage and radio, and the film-and-television label is gone.
- **associate**, sense 2 (*associate with someone*): the definition had built disapproval into the meaning; it is now *spend time with a person or a group*, and the explanation says it is often used when others disapprove.
- **versus**: the usage note now says that American writing usually puts a period after the short form **vs**; and *compare* moved from the "compare" box to "see also", because learners do not confuse the two words.
- **alter**: the usage note's contrast with **change** was softened from a rule to a tendency.
- **adapt**: one ambiguous collocation replaced; **assemble**: a wrong pattern label removed from one example.

Rejected, with reasons:

- Seven of reviewer-a's issues on **unlike** said that *it's unlike him* and *very unlike her* are adjective uses. A word followed by a noun phrase as its object stays a preposition in this dictionary; that is a standing rule (a "fence" in `NEXT.md`), so all seven were rejected.
- reviewer-b twice said a core idea (the one-sentence summary at the top of a long entry) should not be a sentence. House style says it is a sentence.
- Smaller points: that a team is not "people brought together" (it is); that the idiom *bang your head against a brick wall* should be under **bang** (by the keyword rule it lives under **head**, and **bang** points there); that *associate with* is not formal (a register label means *mainly*, and it is mainly formal).

The cross-reference tool adds a matching link in each related entry, but it always attaches it to that entry's first sense. Two were on the wrong sense and I moved them by hand: **adapt** now sits under *make different* in **change**, and **appreciate** under *how or why* in **understand**.
