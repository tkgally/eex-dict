# 2026-09-26 (twenty-eighth run): eight verbs, *result* to *rub*

## What this run did

This was the ninth cycle of this session, and a **build** run. A build run drafts new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. The review cycle before it merged as pull request #96. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each run's kind of work) then chose build. The run continued with band 1, the most basic words, after *rest*.

The eight verbs were:

- **result**: *result from* (the cause) and *result in* (the effect). A learner error and a translator's note warn that mixing up the two prepositions reverses the meaning.
- **retire**: stopping work, withdrawing from a race or a sport, and the formal "go to another room, or go to bed".
- **return**: coming back, giving back, happening again, going back to an activity, and doing the same back (*return a favor*, *return a call*). A learner error covers *return back*.
- **ride**: controlling a bike or a horse, and traveling in a vehicle (*ride the bus*). A learner error covers *ride my car*, where English says *drive*.
- **ring**: a phone or a bell making its sound, making a bell sound, and the British "phone someone". It has the phrase *ring true* and a learner error for *ringed*.
- **rise**: going up, increasing, the sun rising, standing up, and becoming important. A note separates *rise* from *raise*.
- **roll**: turning over and over, moving on wheels, and making a ball or a round shape.
- **rub**: pressing and moving, spreading a cream, and shoes that rub, with the phrase *rub it in*.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline, the fixed sequence of checks each entry must pass:

- **Format and inflections.** All eight passed. *Ride*, *ring*, and *rise* took their irregular forms (*rode/ridden*, *rang/rung*, *rose/risen*) from the table of irregular forms. The rules doubled the *b* in *rubbed*.
- **Pronunciation.** All sixteen transcriptions were confirmed by the panel of pronunciation models. The American *retire* is written with two syllables at the end, the house practice for *fire* and *tire*.
- **Definitions.** Six definitions first used words outside the defining vocabulary, the list of about 2,400 basic words that definitions are written in. Examples were *bell*, *tube*, *cream*, *luck*, and *sore*. All were reworded before review.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 21 issues. Each has a logged decision: 15 applied and 6 rejected.

The main fixes:

- *Ride* in the passenger sense was defined as traveling "without driving it". A bus driver also *rides* the bus, so that limit was removed. Reviewer-a also corrected the British comparison: British English says *go by bus* or *travel on the bus*, not *ride on the bus*.
- *Return* in the sense *return to work* was "start doing or talking about something again". That did not fit its own example, *He returned to his book after lunch*. It now includes reading and says "after a break". The phrase *return a call* had been listed twice, and it now appears only in the "do the same back" sense.
- *Ring*: making a bell sound now covers pulling a rope as well as pressing a button.
- *Rub* in the sense *my new shoes rub* was reworded. Its example no longer has an unclear *it* (was it the rope or the rock that broke?).

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Reviewer-a wanted *ring* in the sense "put a circle around" (*The police ringed the building*) treated as a sense of this entry. That *ring* has a different origin and a regular past tense, *ringed*. By the house rule on homographs (words spelled the same but unrelated), it is a separate entry. It is not in the queue yet. The queue has no way to record a second homograph, a known gap written up in `wiki/notes/homograph-queue-and-inflection.md`, so `NEXT.md` now lists it with *lie* and *pen*.
- The British label on *ring* in the sense "phone someone" stays. A region label in this dictionary means "mainly used there", not "only used there".

The cross-reference tool added two back-links, links pointing back from an existing entry to a new one. *Cause* now points to *result*, and the phone sense of *call* points to *ring*. Both are on the right sense.
