# 2026-10-12, run at 00:43 UTC, cycle 1: build, twelve adjectives *long* to *mental*

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: the scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose to write new entries, because building had the largest backlog of the eligible jobs. The day's budget had just reset at midnight UTC, so the whole US$15 was available.

I took the next twelve band-1 words from the queue (band 1 is the most common vocabulary), all adjectives: *long*, *loose*, *loud*, *low*, *loyal*, *magic*, *major*, *male*, *married*, *mean*, *medical*, *mental*. Each was written from scratch from the style guide, then put through the full pipeline: schema validation, the inflection script, the pronunciation panel, the two-reviewer panel, my adjudication of every issue, the vocabulary check, and the cross-reference check. All twelve are now `reviewed` and will appear on the site at its next build. The dictionary has 2,156 entries.

Some notes on the content:

- *long* has four senses: length, time, amount of text (*a long letter*), and measuring (*two meters long*, *How long is the movie?*), with a usage note on *a long time* versus adverb *long* in questions.
- *low* has seven senses, including a subsense for *low on milk* / *running low* and one for a quiet voice.
- *mean* covers the unkind sense, the not-generous sense (labelled British), the American informal "aggressive" sense, the informal praising sense (*she makes a mean chocolate cake*), and the mathematical average. It also holds the phrase *no mean feat*-type pattern.
- *mental* includes the informal British "crazy" sense with an explanation that some people find it unkind.

## Inflections and pronunciation

All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were verified on the first pass by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU pronouncing data. The inflection script first produced *loyaller*, *loyallest* for *loyal*, because I had not marked how it compares. I added the grammar code *comparative with more* to *loyal* and to one sense each of *magic*, *married*, and *mental*; the script then wrote no -er forms, which is correct.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers (models from two other labs) raised 42 issues, 34 of them marked blocking. I applied 30 and rejected 12; none needed escalating to the owner.

Applied, among others:
- *mean*: the "violent" sense was redefined as angry and likely to attack; the mathematical sense was reworded so it describes a value rather than defining the noun; *average* was removed as a synonym (an *average score* usually means ordinary); a sentence in a translator's note about the word's relation to the verb *mean* was removed, because origin claims belong only in an etymology field.
- *male*: the first definition now says what the sex is (it produces sperm) rather than only what it is not; the plant sense was corrected.
- *major*: both reviewers said it can come after a verb (*the damage was major*), so the *attributive only* code went and the usage note now says that use is less common.
- *medical*: my note said the word is only an adjective; both reviewers pointed out the noun *a medical* (a health check). Corrected.
- *low*: I removed a learner error claiming *cheap prices* is wrong; it is acceptable English.

Rejected, with reasons logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`:
- The British label on *mean* in the sense "not generous". The house rule is that a region label means *mainly used there*, which is true here.
- *Keep the TV low* as an adverb use: it is the object-plus-adjective pattern, like *keep it quiet*.
- *Tall* and *high* as opposites of *low*: *a low building* / *a tall building* and *low quality* / *high quality* are standard pairs.

After adjudication, the vocabulary checker found five definition words outside the defining vocabulary with no entry (*rank*, *pollen*, *aggressive*, *impressive*, *crazy*); I reworded those definitions in plain words.
