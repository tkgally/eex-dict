# Journal, 2026-11-12, run ruj1dp

## Cycle 1: review

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks the kind of work with the most "debt" since it was last done) chose a review. There were no unreviewed drafts and no open entry reports from readers, so the cycle continued the coverage pass: a second careful reading of the most important everyday words (frequency band 1, the commonest words), taken in order of how many senses they have, to add any common meaning or idiom that the first draft missed. Four entries were read this time: **right** (adverb), **roll** (verb), **root** (noun), and **rule** (noun). The pass now stands at 299 entries; the next unread ones are **sale** and **scene**.

Before the reviewers saw the entries, I added what was missing:

- **right** (adverb): *go right home*, *come right in* (a new subsense meaning "directly"); *if I remember right* under the "correctly" sense; and the American informal phrase *right off* ("at once").
- **roll** (verb) was the thinnest entry of the four and grew from five senses to ten: throwing dice, turning over in bed, rolling a sleeve, waves and fog rolling in, a ship rolling from side to side, thunder and drums, rolling your r's, and cameras rolling. It also gained the idioms *be rolling in it* (be very rich) and *rolled into one*, and a note that **roll** sounds the same as **role**.
- **root** (noun): a math sense (*the square root of 16*), the saying *money is the root of all evil*, and the British phrase *root and branch*.
- **rule** (noun): *ground rules*, *house rules*, *the rules of the road*, and the saying *rules are made to be broken*.

**What the reviewers found.** Each entry goes to two reviewer models from different companies, who check every field and return closed verdicts. They raised 57 issues, 4 of them "blocking" (must be settled before the entry can stand). I read every one and decided: 53 applied, 4 rejected, none escalated. The real catches:

- *I'll be right there* had been filed under "exactly in a place", but it means "immediately"; both reviewers caught it, and it moved.
- The **right** usage note said **rightly** does not mean "correctly". That is false: *as you rightly said*, *she rightly guessed*. The note was rewritten.
- *The band's music has its roots in old folk songs* sat under a sense defined as where a *person* comes from. It now has its own subsense for the beginnings of a custom, an idea, or a kind of music.
- The **roll** "cameras rolling" sense had pulled in *let's roll* ("let's go"), which does not fit. *Let's roll* is keyed on **let** under the house rule for idioms, so it is noted for the next review of **let** instead.
- A reviewer pointed out that time passing (*the years rolled by*) was missing; it was added.
- Smaller fixes: four signposts (the short labels that help a reader pick a sense) were over three words; a tooth's root is not "under the skin"; *ruler* inside the definition of **rule** was circular; a note on **rule** only pointed to another entry and was removed; a doubtful learner error on each of **right** and **rule** was removed as probably invented.

What I rejected: a request to drop **government** as a synonym for the "control of a country" sense of **rule** (the **government** entry has a matching sense, "the system of ruling"); a request to add *the exception that proves the rule* (its keyword is **exception**, so it goes there, and is noted); a claim that the adaptation note on **rule** was generic (it names one specific pair of senses); and a request to add *up* to *rolled his sleeves to the elbow*, which is natural as it stands.

**Checks.** All four entries pass the schema validator, the inflection and pronunciation checks (all verified), the defining-vocabulary lint, and the cross-reference check. The full gate over 4,216 entries, the size caps, the link check, and the unit tests all pass.

**What did not work.** Nothing broke. One note for later cycles: the coverage list I computed contains four phrasal verbs (*come in*, *get back*, *get on*, *put on*) that earlier cycles passed over; they are recorded in `NEXT.md` so a later cycle can decide whether the coverage pass should include them.

## Cycle 2: closure

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose closure: building entries for words the dictionary already uses or points to but does not yet define. The first source, `closure` (words used inside definitions), is empty. So the cycle took the next rows from the `family` source, which holds related forms that existing entries list in their word families. I built five new entries in queue order. That is fewer than the cap of twelve, so that each entry is complete and the run has room for later cycles.

- **mention** (noun): a short remark, with no detail. It has a subsense for public praise without a prize (*an honorable mention*), and a usage note that *make mention of something* is formal and takes no article.
- **messenger** (noun): two senses, the person whose job is carrying letters and packages (*a bike messenger*, *by messenger*) and someone who brings news. It also has the idiom *don't shoot the messenger*.
- **military** (noun): *the military*, meaning a country's armed forces as a group. It takes a singular or plural verb. It has a learner error, *my father is a military*, because in several languages the matching word can mean one soldier.
- **milkshake** (noun): a usage note says an American milkshake almost always contains ice cream, while in Britain the word can also mean flavored cold milk.
- **millionaire** (noun): a pronunciation note gives the stress on the last part (*mil-yuh-NAIR*).

All five transcriptions and all inflections were verified on the first pass. (Transcriptions are checked by a panel of models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary; inflections are generated by rule.)

**What the reviewers found.** There were 11 issues, 3 of them blocking, and all 11 were applied. The three blocking issues all concerned a third sense of **messenger** that I had drafted: a phone app for sending messages. Reviewer A pointed out that native speakers say *a messaging app* or use a brand name, and that *a messenger service* is a courier company, not an app. I agreed and removed the sense; it was led by brand names more than by ordinary English. The other fixes:

- **milkshake** is now countable only. Its definition says ice cream is "usual", not required, so it agrees with the usage note.
- **millionaire** is now defined by the threshold itself: a person who owns at least a million dollars or pounds. I first wrote *property*, which the defining-vocabulary check refused because **property** has no entry yet, so I reworded it.
- A needless explanation came out of **military**.

Before the panel I also took out two cross-references of my own. **billionaire** is not a synonym of **millionaire**. **army** is only part of *the military*, so it moved from synonyms to "see also".

**Checks.** The gate over all 4,221 entries, the link check, the unit tests, and the vocabulary and cross-reference gates pass. The cross-reference tool added a mirror link from **billionaire** back to **millionaire**.

**Spend.** US$0.12 for this cycle. The day stands at about US$1.01 of the US$15 cap.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. The vocabulary lint had added a note to the queue row for *property* while **millionaire** still used the word. I removed that note by hand once the definition changed, so the row does not point to a use that no longer exists.

**What is next.** More `family` rows, then `crossref` rows. Band 3 and above stay paused until band 2 is finished, under the owner's ruling A3.
