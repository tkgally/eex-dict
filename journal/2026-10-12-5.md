This was a **build** cycle: the scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose to write new entries. No pull requests or branches were left over from earlier runs, and the inbox was empty.

I took the next twelve band-1 words from the queue (band 1 is the most common vocabulary), all adjectives: *messy*, *military*, *modern*, *monthly*, *musical*, *naked*, *narrow*, *national*, *natural*, *near*, *nearby*, *neat*. Some notes on the content:

- *narrow* carries a learner error and a translator's note for a common trap: in some languages one word means both *narrow* and *small* for a room.
- *naked* has a box separating *naked*, *nude*, and *bare*, and a pronunciation note that it has two syllables.
- *modern* has a box separating *modern*, *new*, and *recent*, and a translator's note on look-alike words that also mean *fashionable*.

## Inflections and pronunciation

The inflection script gave *messier*, *narrower*, *neater*, and (from the exceptions table) *nearer*, *nearest*; the other words compare with *more* or not at all, which I set with grammar codes. The table's adjective row for *near* carried a note, "next in the meaning of the one coming after", that made no sense on the site; I removed it, so the row now lists only the two forms.

Twenty-two of the twenty-four transcriptions were verified at once. *nearby* was disputed: I had stressed the first part, but all three panel models and the open CMU data put the stress on *by*. I changed it, and the note now says the stress often moves forward before a noun (*a NEAR-by town*); it then verified. The British form of *military* stayed disputed: the three panel models gave three different forms (*-tri*, *-t(e)ri*, *-teri*). I kept the three-syllable *ˈmɪl.ɪ.tri*, added the `pronunciation-disputed` flag, and wrote one line to the curator queue; my working assumption is that both forms are standard British.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers (models from two other labs) raised 39 issues, 30 of them marked blocking. I applied 31 and rejected 8; none needed escalating.

Applied, among others:
- *monthly*: sense 2 was defined as "for one month", but its examples (*monthly rent*, *monthly income*) mean "each month"; it is now *paid, earned, or counted for each month*. One reviewer showed *The meetings are monthly* is fine, so sense 1 lost its *before a noun only* code.
- *near*: my notes said plain *near* is not used before a noun, which contradicts *the near future* and *a near miss* in the entry itself. The box and the two learner-error notes now limit that rule to places close by.
- *narrow*: sense 3 now covers *a narrow escape* (only just avoided), not only a narrow win.
- *naked*: sense 3 is now *not hidden, and so plain for everyone to see* (*naked ambition*); sense 2 lost its *before a noun only* code because *the trees stood naked* is in its own examples.
- *neat*: I dropped an unsupported claim that *neat* is more common than *tidy* in American English.

Rejected, among others: three flags that called the house pattern "that use belongs to the noun entry" meta-text; a claim that *completely naked* makes *naked* gradable (it takes intensifiers but no *-er* forms); the region label on *neat* meaning "great" (by the house rule, a region label means *mainly used there*).

Twelve cross-reference mirrors that the cross-reference script added to older entries were checked by hand; five sat on the wrong sense and were moved (`false-adj`, `clever-adj`, `cool-adj`, `annual-adj`, `difficult-adj`).
