The mode selector chose **review** again: a second reading by the reviewer panel (two outside language models that check every field of an entry) of entries read only once. The next oldest were six verbs written on 2026-09-22: *breed, bring, brush, build, burn,* and *bury*. Like the first cycle's verbs, they were thin for such common words, so I added by hand, before the panel:

- **Missing senses.** These are uses a learner meets often:
  - *breed* for how a person is raised (*born and bred*)
  - *bring* someone to a place (*what brings you here?*), and *bring* in law (*bring charges*, *bring a case*)
  - *brush*: removing something (*brush the crumbs off*) now has its own sense, split from cleaning your teeth and hair, and there is a cooking sense (*brush the bread with milk*)
  - *build* something into something else (*a timer built into the door*)
  - *burn* for hot pain (*my eyes were burning from the smoke*), with *burn calories* as a subsense of using fuel
  - *bury* for hiding something in the ground (*the dog buried its bone*), *bury your face in the pillow*, and *bury yourself in your work*
- **A new phrase**: *bring yourself to do something* (*I couldn't bring myself to tell her*).
- **Learner errors.** These are common mistakes:
  - *I bought my laptop to class* (**brought** is the past of **bring**; **bought** is the past of **buy**)
  - *She brang some cake*
  - *I wash my teeth* (in English you **brush** them)
  - *My grandfather builded this house*
  - *They have breeded sheep*
- **Other fixes.**
  - A note on the past form **brought**, whose **gh** is silent.
  - A usage note on **burned** and **burnt**: both are used in British English, and **burnt** is the usual adjective everywhere (*burnt toast*).
  - A plainer pronunciation note for *bury*: "said the same as *berry*".
  - Notes that say how *bring* differs from *get*, *carry*, and *deliver*.
  - Four collocation boxes labelled "adverb + verb" held phrases like *burn slowly*. They are now labelled "verb + adverb".
