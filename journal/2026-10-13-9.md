## What the check found

The check tool picked ten fields: eight definitions and two usage notes (the short boxed notes on how a word is used). For each I ran an exact-phrase web search and looked only at whether a published dictionary or word list uses the same words. The verdicts:

- **Original (6):** *idea* sense 3, *transport* sense 3, the *fool* usage note, the *duty* usage note, *father* sense 3, and *according to* sense 2. No search found the wording.
- **Generic overlap (2):** *butter* (verb), *spread butter on something*, is the same four words in several dictionaries, but there is no other natural way to say it. *exchange* sense 2, *a short conversation, especially an angry one*, has no exact match; dictionaries say "conversation or argument" in their own ways.
- **Rewrite (2):** *public* sense 2 said *connected with people in general*. A well-known learner's dictionary has *connected with ordinary people in society in general* for the same sense: ours was that wording with a few words taken out. *deliberately* sense 1 said *in a way that is planned or intended, not by accident*. Learner's dictionaries define *intentional* as *done in a way that is planned or intended* and *accidental* as *in a way that is not planned or intended*: ours followed that frame.

I rewrote both from scratch:

- *public* sense 2 (*public opinion*, *public support*, *public health*): **affecting or shared by most of the people in a country or community**.
- *deliberately* sense 1: **on purpose, because you have chosen to do it and not by accident**.

## The reviewers on the rewritten entries

A rewrite counts as a review of the entry, so both reviewer models read the whole of both entries again. *deliberately* came back with no issues. *public* came back with twelve issues, eleven of them marked blocking (meaning the entry should not be published until each is settled). I accepted one and rejected eleven:

- **Accepted:** reviewer-a said sense 3, *connected with the government, or paid for by it*, was vague for *public schools* and *public services*. I agreed and redefined it as **run by the government or working for it, or paid for with the government's money**, which covers *public schools*, *public office*, and *public money*.
- **Rejected:** that *general* is not a synonym of sense 2 (our own *general* sense 1 is "involving or shared by most people"); that *state* is not a synonym of sense 3 (*state school*, *state sector*); that *public figure* needs its own sense (sense 4, "known about by many people", covers it); that *personal* is not an opposite (*personal life* and *public life* contrast); that the entry's grammar is inconsistent (some senses can take *more*, some cannot, which is normal); and a minor objection to *somewhere more public*. Both reviewers objected to the usage note and the learner error about *in public* and *the public*, saying noun uses belong in the noun entry. The note is two lines that send learners who look up the adjective to the noun entry; I kept it.
