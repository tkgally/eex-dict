# 2026-09-26 (eighth run): ten more verbs, *know* to *light*

## What this run did

This was the second cycle of this session, and again a **build** run, which writes new dictionary entries. The scheduler (`next_mode.py`, the script that picks each run's kind of work) chose building again. The first cycle's work, ten verbs from *invest* to *kiss*, had already been merged as pull request #76 (`journal/2026-09-26-7.md`).

This cycle drafted the next ten verbs in the queue, all from band 1, the most basic band of the word list: **know, land, lay, lead, learn, leave, let, lie, lift, light**. Seven of them are irregular (*knew/known*, *laid*, *led*, *left*, *let*, *lay/lain*, *lit*). Their forms came from the project's table of irregular forms, and all were correct. The largest entry is *leave*, with nine senses (separate meanings) and two phrases (*leave someone alone*, *leave it at that*). The *lie* written here means *be in a flat position*. *Lie* meaning *say something untrue* is an unrelated word with a different past form (*lied*), so it gets a separate entry later.

Several entries deal with the traps learners know best:

- *lay* and *lie* are cross-referenced, with a usage note and learner errors on each side (*I laid down* → *I lay down*).
- *learn* has a note on choosing between *learn*, *study*, and *teach*, plus the common error *learn me English*.
- *let* has a note on *let*, *allow*, and *permit*, including why *let* takes no **to** and has no passive.
- *know* covers *Nice to know you* → *Nice to meet you*.

## Checks and reviews

All ten entries went through the full pipeline:

- **Format and inflections.** Every entry passed the format check, and every inflected form was generated.
- **Pronunciation.** All twenty American and British transcriptions were verified, and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary agreed with every American one.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 45 issues, 21 of them blocking (they must be settled before publication). Each got a logged decision: 30 fixes applied and 15 rejected.

**Accepted.** Both reviewers pointed out that *lie* in *the village lies in a valley* is not formal English, so that label was removed. The advice that *forget my keys at home* is wrong was too strong. The entry now says *leave* is the usual verb when you name the place, and keeps the example pair as learner advice. *Learn* in the sense *learn from experience* no longer says the experience must be bad. *Lead* in *this path leads to the beach* no longer says the path "goes" anywhere; it now reads "go toward a place, or give a way into it". Some collocations were really phrasal verbs (*lay down*, *lift off*) and were removed. Past-participle phrases like *brightly lit* were rewritten in the house form (*be brightly lit*).

**Rejected.** Reviewer-a again asked for a length mark in the American transcription of *lead*; the house rule leaves it out, and the CMU dictionary agrees. It also doubted the note that many languages use one verb for knowing facts and another for knowing people. That split is well attested, and the note says "many", not "most", so the note stayed. Reviewer-b's usual objection to the quoted headword in adaptation notes was rejected under the standing house rule.

## Cross-references

When a new entry names an existing one as a synonym or a word to compare, a tool adds the matching link back. It added four links; two were on the wrong sense and were moved by hand. *Leave* now sits on *forget*'s "not remember to bring something" sense, and *lay* sits on *lie*'s "be flat" sense. Missing target words were added to the queue of words to write (13 new rows).

## A problem found

The queue of words to write lists each word by spelling and part of speech only. It has no way to hold a second, unrelated word with the same spelling. So *lie* meaning *say something untrue* could not be added to the queue. Also, the tool that writes verb forms looks words up by spelling alone, so it would give that second *lie* the forms *lay* and *lain* instead of *lied*. Following the project's rule, the run did not change the tools on the spot. The problem is recorded in a note (`wiki/notes/homograph-queue-and-inflection.md`) with a proposed fix for a later run, and `NEXT.md` lists the two affected words.
