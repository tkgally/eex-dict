# 2026-10-12, run at 04:43 UTC, cycle 4: build, eleven adjectives *open* to *patient*

## What this cycle did

This was the fourth and last cycle of the run (a run stops after four cycles, because entries written late in long runs used to come out thinner). The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose **build** again.

The queue gave me twelve band-1 adjectives (band 1 is the most common vocabulary). One of them, *other* as an adjective, should not be an entry: every use of *other* before a noun is already covered by the determiner entry `other-det`, and the house rule gives each use one part of speech. I marked that queue row as a duplicate and released it. The other eleven were written from scratch and put through the full pipeline: *open*, *opposite*, *orange*, *outdoor*, *paid*, *painful*, *pale*, *part-time*, *particular*, *past*, and *patient*. All are now `reviewed`; the dictionary has 2,191 entries.

Some notes on the content:

- *open* has seven senses (not closed, for business, not enclosed, for anyone, honest, not decided, willing to listen), the phrase *out in the open*, and two learner errors, among them *The shop is opened on Sundays*.
- *painful* carries a learner error and a translator's note on a common trap: a person who feels pain is *in pain*; the knee or the news is *painful*.
- *past* has four senses (before now, the recent period in *the past few days*, over, and grammar) and a learner error for the common spelling *in the passed few weeks*.
- *particular* has three senses and the phrase *in particular*.

## Inflections and pronunciation

Only *pale* has *-er* forms (*paler*, *palest*); the others compare with *more* or not at all, set by grammar codes. Twenty of the twenty-two transcriptions were verified at once. *part-time* was disputed: the three panel models gave different stress patterns, and their answers changed when I asked again. I chose the stress used before a noun (*a PART-time job*), added the flags `pronunciation-disputed` and `pronunciation-unverified`, and wrote one line to the curator queue.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers (models from two other labs) raised 29 issues, 22 of them blocking. I applied 22 and rejected 7; none needed escalating.

Applied, among others:
- *open*: three examples did not fit their senses (*an open, friendly face*, *keep your options open*, *keep an open mind*); each was replaced. The notes now say *opened* is used *mainly* for the action, since *an opened package* also exists.
- *opposite*, *paid*, *part-time*: reviewer-b pointed out that each learner error was really about another part of speech (the preposition *opposite of me*, the verb *I'm payed*, the adverb *work part-time*). Each now shows an adjective mistake: *opposite with mine*, *a well-payed job*, *a part time job* without the hyphen.
- *past*: the "recent" sense is now *during the period of time that ends now*, and two half-phrases in its box were completed.
- *orange*: my translator's note said its ending sounds like *change*; it does not, so it now says *hinge*.

Rejected: three flags claiming *comparative with more* is not a grammar code (it is in the closed list); a flag against the subject note on *pale* light (the style guide puts such restrictions in the explanation); a claim that *long past* is not a normal collocation; two formatting flags on words that are named as words and correctly take bold.

## A queue problem

The queue lost the hyphen in *part-time* again (a known problem, `wiki/notes/queue-hyphen-loss.md`): an old wrong row *part time* was marked done instead of the right one, a new wrong adverb row was added, and the frequency band written into the entry came from the wrong row (3 instead of 1). I fixed the rows by hand and added a line to the note; the band is written by a script, so it stays wrong until the tool is fixed, which shows as one validator warning.
