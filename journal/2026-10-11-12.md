## What the reviewers found and what I decided

They raised 26 issues; *bed*, *benefit*, and *deliberately* came back with none. I logged 26 decisions in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`: 17 applied and 9 rejected, none sent to the owner.

Applied:

- *cheat* (noun) had a subsense for a person who is unfaithful to a partner. The reviewer was right that this is not a separate meaning of the noun: someone who cheats on a partner is simply a dishonest person, and the main sense already covers that. The subsense was removed. Its learner error (*Don't make cheat in the exam*) was about the verb, so it was replaced by a noun error: *He is cheat* should be *He is a cheat*.
- *beach* (verb): sense 1 said "bring a boat out of the water", but beaching a boat is simply moving it onto the shore, so it now says that. Sense 2 was marked as both transitive and intransitive, but every example has an object or is passive, so it is now marked transitive.
- *bench* (verb): the weight-lifting sense described lifting one weight once; it now describes the exercise.
- *battle* and *beautify*: their usage notes said the words belong mainly to news or official writing. Both are also ordinary in speech (*battle the traffic*, *beautify a garden*), and the notes now say so. *beautify* no longer claims that the word sounds humorous about a person.
- Small fixes: *fridge*'s note on the British *fridge-freezer* now says what American English calls the same machine; *befriend* and *belt* have better collocations; a duplicate "see also" link on *battle* was removed.

Rejected:

- The American label on *bench* (keep a player out of a game) stays: the project's rule is that a region label means *mainly* used there.
- *beach*, sense 2: the reviewer wanted it widened to people and boats that end up on the shore. Those uses are figurative or already covered by sense 1; the sense describes stranded sea animals, and *whales beached themselves* is the ordinary wording in the news.
- Two "see also" links the reviewer thought unhelpful (*war* from *battle*, *shore* from *beach*) are kept, because both are related entries worth reading.
- *friend*: the reviewer asked for an attitude label on *friends in high places* and a "proverb" label on *a friend in need is a friend indeed*. The first is neutral in most uses, and the project's closed list of labels has no "proverb" value.
