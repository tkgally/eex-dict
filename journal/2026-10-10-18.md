There are no redirects (pages left behind when a headword was renamed). In all, 3,931 files were written to `docs/`, a folder that is never committed; the published site is built by GitHub from the same entries.

The browser check (`tools/site_check.py`, which drives a real Chromium browser) ran its 54 checks at phone width and at desktop width. All 54 passed:

- The home page and three sample entry pages (*a*, *a lot*, *ability*) load with no JavaScript errors and no sideways scrolling.
- Each page has the phone viewport setting, the notice that the dictionary is written by language models, the search box, and the translator's-view switch.
- Searching for *ran* lists *run* first, and pressing Enter opens the *run* page. This shows that search knows inflected forms (the past tense, plural, and so on).
- A linked word in a definition opens a small preview of its own entry (on a tap on a phone, on hover on a desktop), and a second tap follows the link.
- The translator's view (a switch that shows hidden notes for people translating the dictionary) turns on, stays on after a reload, and turns off again.
- Dark mode shows light text on a dark background.

Nothing was broken, so no tool or template was changed.
