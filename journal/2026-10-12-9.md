# 2026-10-12, run at 08:44 UTC, cycle 1: the sixty-seventh lint pass

## What this cycle did

This cycle was a **lint pass**: a scheduled check of the dictionary and its knowledge base for problems that a single entry's review cannot see. The scheduler forces one after five runs without one, and the previous run (04:43 UTC) was four cycles of building and reviewing, so this one was due. A lint pass costs nothing, because it makes no calls to paid models.

The pass has a mechanical half (scripts that check every file) and a judgmental half (reading pages and entries against each other). Nothing serious turned up. Fifteen entries changed, all in their cross-references; two curator lines were moved to the right place.

## The mechanical checks

- **Caps** (`tools/check_caps.py`): every size limit on the rule files, the style guide, and the wiki is respected.
- **Links** (`tools/check_links.py`): all relative links in the 513 knowledge-base pages resolve.
- **Back-links** (`tools/crossref.py --all --apply`): when entry A names entry B as a synonym, an opposite, or a word to compare, B should name A too. The script adds the missing mirror, but it has to guess which of B's senses it belongs on, so every one is checked by hand. This time it added fourteen, all on adjectives built in the last run (*modern*, *nearby*, *nervous*, *new*, *nice*, *normal*, *odd*, *official*, *online*, *outdoor*, *pale*, *part-time*, *particular*, *past*). Twelve were on the right sense: for example *fresh* went on the sense of `new-adj` meaning *different from the one you had before* (*a fresh glass*, *a new job*), and *green* and *gray* went on the face sense of `pale-adj`.
  One pair was wrong. `official-adj` received *formal* as a synonym and *informal* as an opposite on sense 1, *connected with the work of a government*. The senses of `formal-adj` and `informal-adj` that name *official* mean *done or agreed following the proper rules* (*a formal complaint*, *an informal agreement*). That matches `official-adj` sense 2, *said, done, or agreed by people in authority* (*the official results*). Both were moved there by hand.
- **Type mismatch**: the script also reports pairs where the two entries disagree on the kind of link. A new one: `international-adj` named *national* as its opposite, while `national-adj` named *international* only as a word to compare. `national-adj` sense 1 means *connected with a whole country, not just one part of it*; its natural contrast is *local*, not *international*, so the two are related but not true opposites. Both sides now say "compare". The older mismatch between *alone* and *lonely* was settled earlier and stays.
- **Vocabulary** (`tools/lint_vocab.py --all --queue`): no new words were queued for entries.
- **Claims**: three old claim files (records of which words a cycle took from the queue) were pruned.
