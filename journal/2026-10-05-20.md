What the entries contain:

- *Moment* has three senses (a short time, a point in time, a special time) and six idioms: *at the moment*, *for the moment*, *the moment (that)* meaning *as soon as*, *in the moment*, *have its moments*, and *the moment of truth*.
- *Mood* has four senses: a feeling; a bad feeling (*She's in a mood*); the feeling of a group or a place (*set the mood*); and the grammar term. It also has *be in the mood for something*.
- *Mouth* covers the mouth in the face, the opening of a cave or a bottle, and the mouth of a river. Its idioms include *keep your mouth shut*, *have a big mouth*, and *make your mouth water*.
- *Moon* covers our moon (with a subsense for *a full moon*, *a new moon*), the moons of other planets, and three idioms: *once in a blue moon*, *over the moon* (British), and *promise the moon*.
- *Mistake* separates a wrong action or decision from a mistake in a piece of work, and explains *mistake* / *error* / *fault*.
- *Morning*, *month*, and *mother* carry the idioms and fixed phrases learners meet first: *good morning*, *morning, noon, and night*, *mother tongue*, *the mother of all traffic jams*.

Learner errors cover the traps learners really fall into: *do a mistake* for *make a mistake*, *by mistake* (not *for mistake*), *in this moment* for *at the moment*, *a three months course*, *in this morning*, *in Monday morning*, *not in mood*, *see moon* without *the*, *two mouses*, *in the mountain* for *in the mountains*, and *my mother's tongue*. Usage notes explain the prepositions with *month* and *morning*, *in a mood* on its own, the capital in *the Moon*, *mom* and *mum*, and the plural of *mouse* for the computer object.

**Keyword rule.** An idiom lives under its first noun. *Promise the moon* was in the verb entry *promise*, waiting for *moon* to exist. It has now moved to `moon-n`, and `promise-v` points there in its "see also" list. The other idioms already in the dictionary that contain these words have an earlier noun, so they stay where they are. Examples are *in the heat of the moment*, *straight from the horse's mouth*, and *flavor of the month*.

## Checks and reviewers

Two reviewer models from other companies read every field. Reviewer B failed or gave only part of its answer on three entries. One re-run fixed *mouth*. *Moon* needed a third call, because a review record with no verdicts at all cannot pass the gate. *Mother* stayed partial.

There were 26 decisions: 18 applied and 8 rejected. The main changes:

- *next month*, *this month*, *this morning*, and *tomorrow morning* had been listed as "adjective + noun" collocations. Both reviewers pointed out that *next*, *this*, and *tomorrow* are not adjectives there, so they are now listed as phrases.
- The moons of other planets are no longer defined as "round", since many are not.
- The "mistake in work" sense now covers any error in a piece of work, not only a wrong word, number, or answer.
- *Mood*: one definition now has parallel wording, and the collocation *the mood in somewhere* became *the mood in the room*.
- *Keep your mouth shut* is now defined with a verb ("say nothing"), and the note on *a big mouth* was made clear.
- *At the moment* lost a remark that it is mainly British; it is common everywhere.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- making *mother tongue* a separate headword (the house rule keeps fixed phrases under their key noun);
- dropping the British label on *over the moon* (a region label means *mainly used there*);
- removing the countable subsense *a full moon* (it is a real grammar difference);
- objecting that the moon does not really shine (this is plain wording for learners).
