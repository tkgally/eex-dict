## Back-links: the main work, only half done

Cross-references in this dictionary are meant to be two-way: if *ceiling* says "compare *roof*", then *roof* should say "compare *ceiling*". A script (`tools/crossref.py`) adds the missing return links, but it must guess which sense of the target to attach them to, and it guesses wrong often enough that every lint checks each one by hand.

This pass the script added nine return links to six entries, all words drafted in this morning's two build cycles (*report, respect, result, rhythm, road, roof*). Six are in the right place: *account* beside the news sense of *report*, *product* and *fruit* beside the matching senses of *result*, *time* (the beat of music) beside *rhythm*, and *highway*, *lane*, *path*, and *way* beside the road itself.

Three are on the wrong sense:

- *record* (information written down to be kept) sits on the news sense of *report*; it belongs on sense 2, the document written after a careful study.
- *way*, in its meaning "one side of a matter" (*in some ways*), sits on the feeling sense of *respect*; it belongs on sense 4, "a particular part or detail" (*in some respects*).
- *ceiling* sits on the "top of the inside of your mouth" sense of *roof*; it belongs on sense 1, the top of a building.

I tried to move these three with a short one-off script, as earlier lint passes have done, and the session's automatic permission check refused it. The project's rules also say that no script may write the meaning fields of an entry, and cross-references are one of those fields. So I did not try another way. The three links stay where the script put them for now. They are harmless to readers (each still points to a real, related word), and they are listed at the top of item 5 in `NEXT.md` and in the back-link note (`wiki/notes/crossref-first-sense-backlinks.md`), so the next review or lint run can move them by hand.
