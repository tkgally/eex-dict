## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models review every field of every entry. They raised 42 issues: 40 from reviewer-a and 2 from reviewer-b. I applied 33 and rejected 9; none needed the owner.

Applied, among others:

- The baseball sense of *strike* said only "a good throw that the player does not hit". Reviewer-a pointed out that a strike can also be a good throw the player lets go past. The definition now covers both.
- *Hold a strike* is not a typical collocation; it became *organize a strike*, in the collocations and in the learner-error note.
- The word-stress sense of *stress* said the stressed part sounds "louder or longer". Stress is better described as the part that stands out, and the definition now says that. The collocations now give *primary stress* and *secondary stress*.
- *Avenue* in the street–road–avenue discrimination is no longer described as always wide.
- The *strength* pronunciation note and adaptation note disagreed about the light *k* sound many speakers put before the **th**; they now say the same thing.
- *Stroke* in *a stroke of luck* now has an explanation naming its two fixed phrases.

Rejected:

- Reviewer-a said that British English uses *on*, not *in*, with a named street. *She lives in Elm Street* is ordinary British usage. The entry now says British English "often" uses *in*.
- Reviewer-b flagged **St.** in the *street* usage note as a banned abbreviation. The note talks about the abbreviation as a word; it does not use it.
- Reviewer-a said *so much stuff to do* is a verb use. It is the noun.
- Two minor flags called *and so on* in a definition "padding". The house style allows it.
