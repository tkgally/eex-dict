# Journal, 2026-10-09 (run of 00:43 UTC, cycle 2): twelve new nouns, *talent* to *technology*

## What this cycle did

This was a second **build** cycle. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each cycle's kind of work from what is most overdue) chose building again. The cycle took the next twelve band 1 nouns (band 1 means the most common words) from the queue of words still to write: *talent, talk, tap, task, taste, tax, taxi, tea, teacher, team, tear,* and *technology*.

Each entry was drafted by hand from the style guide, with every field filled. Together the twelve carry 21 idioms and fixed phrases, among them *small talk*, *all talk*, *the talk of the town*, *take someone to task*, *be up to the task*, *in bad taste*, *to taste*, *a taste of your own medicine*, *an acquired taste*, *burst into tears*, *bored to tears*, and *team player*. Two entries also have a synonym discrimination (a short paragraph that tells a learner how to choose between near-synonyms): *talent, skill, ability* and *task, job, duty*.

## Two choices about words with the same spelling

Two of the claimed words are really two unrelated words each, so I had to choose which one to write.

- **tap.** The queue row did not say which word was meant. I wrote `tap-n` as the device that lets out water or beer, which also covers a phone tap. A quick, light hit (*a tap on the shoulder*) is an unrelated word. It will be `tap-n-2` (the second entry with the same spelling and part of speech), and the usage note points to it.
- **tear.** The row was linked from *cry*, so `tear-n` is the drop from the eye, which rhymes with *here*. The hole made by tearing, which rhymes with *hair*, will be `tear-n-2`. It will also give the verb `tear-v` its missing noun.

Both are now on the list of homographs to queue. The queue file has no column for homograph numbers yet; that tooling fix is still due.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Reviewer-a raised 50 issues. Reviewer-b returned complete verdicts with no issues on all twelve entries, which is the second cycle in a row with almost nothing from it. I applied 43 and rejected 7; nothing was escalated.

The most useful corrections:

- *tap*: the water-device sense is now labelled British, because American English usually says *faucet*. The learner error *Close the tap* was dropped, since that is normal British English.
- *tea*: the drink is now *usually* hot, not always hot, because iced tea is common.
- *Teacher* used as a form of address: the usage note, learner error, and adaptation note now say *many English-speaking countries*, not all of them.
- *team*: the entry now says that British English also uses *on a team*, not only *in a team*.
- *taste*: a learner error was removed, because *this soup has a taste of chicken* is grammatical. The core idea was rewritten.
- *technology*: sense 2 no longer claims that all technology comes from science.

Rejections:

- *talent show* and *talent scout* belong to the sense meaning talented people.
- `tap-n`'s numbering is consistent with a planned second entry.
- *bored to tears* and *end in tears* keep their informal labels.
- Articles inside collocation items (*a technology company*) are house practice.

**Cross-references.** The cross-reference script found two type mismatches: one entry called the other a synonym while the other called it a word to compare. *duty* and *flavor* were settled on our side, so both sides now agree. I also added by hand the mirror of an existing link: `duty-n` lists *tax* as a synonym, so `tax-n` now lists *duty*. Six back-links were added, and all six were on the right sense.
