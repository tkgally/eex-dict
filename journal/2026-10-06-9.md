## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 34 issues, 31 of them marked blocking. I applied 13 and rejected 21; none needed to go to you. Every decision is logged with a reason in the decision record.

Applied, among others:

- *building*: the activity sense now reads "the work or business of making houses, offices, roads, bridges, and other structures". People do speak of *road building*.
- *bridge*: the dental sense said the false teeth are held by real teeth on each side; that is usual but not always so, and it now says "usually". The new song sense was reworded more carefully.
- *under your breath* said "so that other people cannot hear"; it now says they do not hear, or can only just hear.
- *brother*: the sense for men who feel close through a shared fight or belief lost its *literary* label; it is ordinary in speeches and in unions (*brothers and sisters*).
- *bug*: the usage note claimed British speakers use *bug* "more informally"; that was too strong, and the note now says only that *insect* is the usual word in science and careful writing.
- *butter*: a note for translators said the plural *butters* is used for kinds of butter, while the forms table says there is no plural. I removed the plural claim so the entry agrees with itself; *peanut butter* is now "usually", not "always", preceded by the name of the nut.
- *brush*: the sense "a light touch" got its typical combination (*the brush of something against something*), and the sense for low bushes got an example of the cut branches its definition already names (*a pile of brush*).

Rejected, among others:

- Several objections to region labels (*brother* as a friendly address, *brush* for low bushes): a region label in this dictionary means *mainly* used there, which is a settled house rule.
- Objections to short explanations such as "Usually **the burn**". The style guide puts a restriction to one form in the explanation field.
- The claim that *rain buckets* and *cry buckets* are not established idioms. Both are common informal expressions and are labeled informal.
- Reviewer-b asked again for *to* at the start of verb definitions; the house style leaves it out.
- One reviewer said the *bug* explanation ("**the** plus an activity plus **bug**") had the word order backwards; it does not.

The first reviewer ("reviewer-a") raised 32 of the 34 issues; the second ("reviewer-b") raised two, both rejected (one of them concluded, in its own text, that the definition was fine). Reviewer-b's low issue count since the fix of 2026-10-05 continues; the next lint pass is due to measure it.
