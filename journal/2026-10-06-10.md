## What this cycle did

Every tenth run the scheduler forces an **originality check**. Its purpose is to catch any definition or note that, despite the rule against consulting published dictionaries while drafting, ended up with the same distinctive wording as one. The check takes ten random prose fields from the dictionary, searches the web for each one as an exact phrase, and also asks one of the outside reviewer models whether the text reads as copied. A field that matches a published dictionary's distinctive wording is rewritten; a short, plain definition that any dictionary would phrase the same way ("generic overlap") is left alone.

This time the sample held eight definitions, one phrase definition, and one usage note:

- *joint*, the meat sense;
- *pick up*, collecting someone in a car;
- *beach* (verb), bringing a boat onto a beach;
- *park*, the public area with grass and trees;
- *run out of gas* (under *gas*), losing the energy to continue;
- *enclose*, putting something in the same envelope as a letter;
- *up* (preposition), along the length of a street;
- *examine*, looking at something carefully;
- *solve*, dealing with a problem so that it no longer exists;
- the usage note of *bad* on *worse* and *worst*.

## What the searches found

Seven came back **original**: the exact-phrase searches found only forums, guides, council pages, and blogs, never our wording in a dictionary.

Two are **generic overlaps**: *enclose* and *examine*. Learner's pages use the same plain frame (*in the same envelope as the letter*; *carefully in order to learn more about it*), but not our wording, and there is hardly another plain way to say these things.

One needed a **rewrite**: *joint* in the British sense of a piece of meat. Our definition read "a large piece of meat for cooking, usually with a bone in it", and a search for the distinctive part, *usually with a bone in it*, brought up a well-known learner's dictionary page for *joint* among the first results. I did not open the page; the match in the search was enough reason to change the wording. It now reads "a big piece of meat, often still on the bone, that is cooked whole in an oven and then cut into slices", which also says more about what a joint is used for.

Because a rewrite counts as a review of that entry, *joint* went through the panel again. One reviewer raised one issue: the idiom *out of joint* was defined as "pushed out of its normal position", which is too general. It now reads "of a bone, pushed out of its normal position where it meets another bone". I applied this.

## The reviewer

I asked the first reviewer model ("reviewer-a") the standard question, with its hidden reasoning switched off. It said "copied" for nine of the ten and named the same learner's dictionary each time. But each "quotation" it gave was simply our own wording, with *to* or *somebody* added, or else a different wording from ours; search confirmed only *joint*. This is the same pattern seen in several earlier checks: the reviewer's "copied" calls cannot be trusted on their own, and the verdicts rest on the searches. I added a line to the project's note on reviewer noise.
