# 2026-09-26 (eighteenth run): the fifteenth lint pass, closing a twelve-cycle session

## What this run did

This was the twelfth and last cycle of this session, and a **lint** run. A lint run is a housekeeping pass over the whole dictionary and the project's notes. It makes no paid calls, and the scheduler forces one every five runs. After this cycle merges, the run clock (the script that decides whether a new cycle may start) no longer allows one, so the session ends here.

## What the checks found

**Mechanical checks.** All length limits were respected and every internal link resolved. No definition used a word outside the defining vocabulary (the limited word list definitions are written in). Three claim files from finished cycles were pruned; claim files record which words a run has reserved.

**Cross-reference back-links.** When entry A names entry B as a synonym, an opposite, or a word to compare, the tool `crossref.py` adds the matching link back from B to A, guessing which of B's senses (separate meanings) it belongs on. This pass added 22 links, mostly to the eighteen verbs built since the last lint. Four were on the wrong sense and were moved by hand:

- *go*'s "pass, as time does" and *run*'s "move your hand lightly over something" were both on *pass*'s "go past" sense. They now sit on "of time" and "go in a direction".
- *run*'s "flow" now sits on *pour*'s "flow" sense, not "make a liquid flow out of a container".
- *until* now sits on *by*'s "not later than" sense, where the two words make a useful pair. *By tomorrow* sets a deadline; *until tomorrow* means something continues up to then.

**A mistake of mine, corrected.** Two of those links were ones this session's second review cycle had removed from one side of a pair: *until* from *by*, and *out* from *into*. The other entries still named them, so this pass put them back. In that cycle's report (`journal/2026-09-26-16.md`) I wrote that the tool had confirmed no such leftover links existed. That was wrong: the tool's dry run, which changes nothing, does not print the links it would add, so the check proved nothing. Both pairs are now settled on their merits. *Until*/*by* stays, on the right sense. *Out* stays as an opposite of *into* beside *out of*, since both are correct. The limitation of the dry run is now recorded in the back-link note and in `NEXT.md`, so later runs do not repeat my mistake.

**A long-standing mismatch settled.** For several passes the tool had warned that *you* named *one* under one link type and *one* named *you* under another. The cause was an old synonym link left in *one* next to a compare link with a note. The stale link was removed. The two words (*you* and *one* for people in general) are now a compare pair on both sides.

**Reviewer precision.** Precision here is the share of a reviewer's flags that the run agreed with and acted on. The rule switches a reviewer off for a kind of issue when its precision falls under 30 percent over twenty or more decisions. No kind of issue crossed that line. Reviewer-a's pronunciation flags are at 33 percent over 105 decisions. Its only pronunciation flags today asked for an American length mark that the house rule deliberately leaves out, so two more such flags would switch that family off. Reviewer-b's formatting flags fell to 56 percent, mostly because of its repeated objection to the quoted headword in adaptation notes (short notes for future translators). The figures are in `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md`.

In all, 17 entries were touched.
