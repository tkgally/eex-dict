## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work by weighing how long each kind has waited) chose a *build*: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. The cap for a build cycle is twelve entries, and twelve were written, all now published as `reviewed`:

- **monument** (noun): a statue or building put up to remember someone; a protected old building or place; and something that shows a quality very clearly, good or bad (*a monument to bad planning*).
- **mortgage** (noun): the bank agreement and the money borrowed, with a pronunciation note that the **t** is silent (*MOR-gij*).
- **mosque** (noun): one sense, set beside **church** and **temple**.
- **motive** (noun): a reason that makes someone act, often a hidden one, with a short note on how it differs from **reason**.
- **mug** (noun): the large cup (with a smaller sense for the amount it holds, *a mug of tea*), an informal word for a face, and a British informal word for a person who is easy to cheat; the British idiom *a mug's game*.
- **napkin**, **nappy** (the British word for a diaper), **necklace** (with a note that the end sounds like *luhs*, not like the word *lace*).
- **nationality** (noun): belonging to a country, and a group of people with a shared history; a note that the stress moves to the third part (*nash-uh-NAL-uh-tee*).
- **navy** (noun): the armed forces at sea, and the dark blue color.
- **necessity** (noun): something needed, and the fact of being needed (*out of necessity*); the proverb *necessity is the mother of invention*.
- **neighborhood** (noun): an area of a town, with a smaller sense for the area near you (*Is there a bank in the neighborhood?*); the people who live there; the phrase *in the neighborhood of*, meaning "about" for an amount.

Every entry went through the full pipeline: the schema check, the inflection rules (all plurals regular), the pronunciation panel (three outside models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary; all 24 transcriptions agreed), and the two independent reviewers.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 28 issues in all; 2 were *blocking* (they must be settled before publication), and both concerned the same thing: an example for the "face" sense of **mug** (*The police put the thief's mug on the evening news*) that really described a police photo rather than a face. I agreed and replaced it with *Wipe that silly grin off your mug!*

Of the 26 minor issues, I accepted 25. The most useful were: the "lasting sign" sense of **monument** had been written as if it were always praise, but *a monument to stupidity* is just as common, so the definition now says "good or bad" and has a critical example; **nationality** had been defined as a "legal right", which does not fit *What nationality are you?*, so it is now "the fact of belonging to a particular country, by birth or by law"; *apply for nationality* and *have nationality* without a modifier are not natural English, so they were replaced; *close a necklace* became *fasten a necklace*; and three short explanation paragraphs on ordinary nouns, which only repeated what the grammar codes and word combinations already showed, were removed. I rejected one: a reviewer wanted the "face" sense of **mug** labelled British and old-fashioned, but it is used in American English too (*mug shot*, *ugly mug*). Every decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Separately, the vocabulary checker found three words in definitions that are outside the dictionary's defining vocabulary and have no entry yet (*statue*, *beads*, *English*); I reworded those three definitions. Three back-links that the cross-reference tool could not place on its own were placed by hand: **church** (the building sense), **reason** (the "why" sense) and **need** (the "being necessary" sense) now point back to **mosque**, **motive** and **necessity**.

## Cycle 2: review

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose a *review*: a second, careful reading of entries that were already published, looking for missing meanings, examples that do not fit, and notes that repeat each other. Under the owner's rulings of 2026-10-24 this is the *coverage pass*, which works through the 500 most important everyday words with the most senses first. This cycle took the next four: **share** (verb), **shower** (noun), **sick** (adjective) and **sign** (verb).

Before sending them to the reviewers I added what was missing:

- **share**: a new sub-sense for taking part of something like blame or credit (*We all share the blame*, *share in the credit*), the saying *share and share alike*, and online uses (*share a post*, *share a link*).
- **shower**: a sub-sense for many things received at once (*a shower of praise*), *a meteor shower*, *the showers* in a sports building, and *a bridal shower*. An example about throwing rice outside a church was replaced, since it assumes one culture's wedding customs.
- **sick**: a sub-sense for being so worried or upset that you almost feel ill (*sick with worry*, *It makes me sick to see food wasted*), plus *out sick* and *off sick*.
- **sign**: *sign a law* and *sign a book*.

Three notes that said the same thing as another field were removed (the dictionary's rule is "one fact, one field").

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 65 issues, 8 of them blocking. The blocking ones were all real and all fixed:

- Two definitions used the headword to define itself: **shower** as "washing under a shower" and **sign** as "communicate in sign language". Both now say what happens without the word.
- **funny** was listed as a synonym of **sick** meaning ill; it only works in *feel funny*, so it was removed.
- The British use *be sick* meaning "vomit" was sitting under the sense "feeling that food is going to come up", with an example that did not fit. It now has its own sub-sense, labelled British.
- *The officer signed for us to stop* is not natural in current American English; it was replaced.

Of the minor issues I applied most. One worth mentioning: the idiom *sign your own death warrant* had been filed under **sign**, but the house rule files an idiom under its first noun, so it moved to **death**. Because that changed **death**, I read **death** in full and sent it through the reviewers as well. That turned up a real gap: the literal *to death* (*starve to death*, *freeze to death*) was missing, and is now there. A usage note, an adaptation note and two learner errors that looked invented were removed.

I rejected 14 issues, each with a logged reason. For example, both reviewers said *humorous* is not a register label, but it is one in the project's closed list. *worried sick* was rejected for **sick** because the house rule files it under **worried**, where it already appears. *survival* stays as an opposite of **death** (as in *chances of survival*).

**What did not work.** Nothing failed.

**What is next.** The coverage pass now stands at 311 entries. Next come *single* (adjective) and *sister*, then *slow* and *smell*.

## Cycle 3: build

All eight are now published as `reviewed`:

- **noodle**: the food (*rice noodles*, *noodle soup*), and an old-fashioned, humorous American word for the head (*Use your noodle!*).
- **notebook**: the paper book and the small computer.
- **notice**: attention (*take notice of*), a sign in a public place, advance warning (*until further notice*, *at a moment's notice*), with a sub-sense for leaving a job or a home (*hand in your notice*), and a review of a play or book (*good notices*). All 16 transcriptions were agreed by the pronunciation panel. The plurals all come from the regular rules (**nonsense** has none).
