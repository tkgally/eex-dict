## What this cycle did

The queue gave *extra* (noun) and *flesh* (noun). Both come from the first definition of *fat*: *having a lot of extra flesh on the body*. The tool that finds these words has to guess their part of speech. Here *extra* is an adjective, not a noun. So I drafted the adjective entry, `extra-adj`, instead. The noun row stays in the queue for later, because the noun is a real word too (*an extra* in a film, *the extras* you pay more for on a car).

- **flesh** has three senses: the soft part of a body under the skin; the soft part of a fruit (*the flesh of a melon*); and, in literary use, the body as opposed to the mind (*the pleasures of the flesh*). It has two idioms, *in the flesh* (in person) and *make someone's flesh crawl*. A short note explains when to say *flesh* and when *meat*. The entry also points to *your own flesh and blood*, which is already in the entry for *blood*.
- **extra** has two senses: *more than is usual, needed, or expected* (*an extra chair*), and *costing more, not included in the price* (*breakfast is extra*). A note explains the difference between *extra*, *additional*, and *spare*. Another explains that *extra large* and *pay extra* use the adverb, which has its own queue row.

All four pronunciations (American and British) were confirmed on the first pass.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 7 issues, 5 of them blocking (must be settled before publication). I applied 4 and rejected 3; none went to the owner.

Applied:

- The translator's note on *flesh* said English uses the word for *the living body*. It is also used for dead bodies, and the comparison with *meat* already said so. The note now says *the soft part of a body, living or dead*.
- The usage note on *extra* said it comes after *a*, *an*, or a number. It also comes after *the* and *my* (*the extra chair*), and the note now says so.
- A learner error on *extra* now gives the correction *Breakfast is extra*, which uses the adjective this entry describes.

Rejected:

- reviewer-a called one usage-note sentence on *extra* ungrammatical. It has its main verb (*…is the adverb*); the reviewer misread it.
- reviewer-a wanted the "costing more" use of *extra* to change the grammar code of sense 1. That use is sense 2, which is coded for use after a verb.
- reviewer-a objected that the British form *make someone's flesh creep* sits in a phrase's explanation. Phrases have no field for regional forms, so the explanation is the right place.

The cross-reference script added *flesh* as a "compare" word on *meat* (the food sense) and on *skin* (the fruit sense). I checked both by hand, and both are on the right sense.
