## What this run did

This was the third cycle of the scheduled run that began at 00:43 UTC. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request; a run has at most four.) The first two cycles, pull requests #126 and #127, were reviews and had merged.

This time the mode selector chose **build**, which means writing new entries. It allowed twelve. I claimed the next twelve words in the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written). All twelve were common verbs from band 1, the most frequent words: *shave, shine, shock, shop, shout, show, shrink, shut, sing, sit, sleep,* and *slide*.

I drafted each entry in full from my own knowledge and the house style guide. Each entry has:

- **Senses in order of use.** *Show* has nine: let someone see, show how to do something, take someone somewhere, prove, let a feeling be seen, be easy to notice, give information on a map or clock, show a film, and informal *never showed*. *Sit* has six. *Sleep* includes the tent that *sleeps four*.
- **Grammar and collocations.** Each sense has its grammar and the word combinations learners need.
- **Learner errors.** These are the mistakes learners commonly make:
  - wrong past forms: *shrinked*, *sitted*, *sleeped*, *slided*, *has sang*
  - *I went to shopping*
  - *shouted to me* when an angry shout is meant
  - *Can you show me where is the station?*
  - *Please seat*
  - *I slept at midnight* (meaning "went to bed")
  - *I was shocking* (meaning "shocked")
- **Usage notes.** These cover *shone* versus *shined*, *shut* versus *close*, *shout at* versus *shout to*, and the forms *shrank* and *shrunk*.
- **Phrases.** Three idioms: *sit tight*, *sleep on it*, and *sleep tight*.

The pronunciation check verified all 24 transcriptions. The inflection tool verified all 12 sets of verb forms.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel (two outside language models that check every field) raised 53 issues, 42 of them blocking. A blocking issue must be settled before an entry can be marked reviewed. I applied 46 and rejected 7.

Most of the accepted issues were statements in my notes that went too far:

- I wrote that you *slide* "under control" but *slip* by accident. People slide on ice by accident too.
- I wrote that *teach* is "often a job".
- I wrote that *shouted me to come back* could be corrected only with *shouted at me*. *Shouted to me* also works.
- I had defined *sit* with "your back upright", but people sit in many positions.
- I had defined the "official group" sense of *sit* so that it mixed two uses: being on a committee, and a court meeting. The court use is now a subsense (a variation within a sense).
- *sleep* in its sexual sense is not "always" followed by *with* or *together*, as *sleep around* shows.
- A collocation, *shut someone out*, belonged to a different meaning.

The second reviewer caught a grammar pattern on *show us where to put our coats* that left out the object. The closed list of patterns has no exact value for that construction. I used the nearest one and recorded the gap in a new note, `wiki/notes/verb-pattern-gaps.md`, for a later decision.

What I rejected:

- The house rule for American transcriptions drops the length mark, so *sleep* is `slip`, which the reviewer objected to.
- The *finger*/*singer* note is correct: *finger* has a hard **g** after the **ng** sound, and *singer* does not.
- *go shopping* is the verb's commonest use, so it belongs in the verb entry.

The cross-reference tool added eight matching links in other entries (*cut, buy, explain, prove, decrease, increase, take, fall*). I checked each one. The link from *sit* (take an exam) landed on the "do an action" sense of *take*, which fits well enough. The tool also found a type mismatch: *call* listed *shout* as a synonym, while *shout* listed *call* as a comparison. I made them match.
