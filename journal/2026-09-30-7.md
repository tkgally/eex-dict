Some highlights:

- *area* has five senses, running from a part of a city to the size of a surface. It also has the phrases *a gray area* and *in the area of* ("about"), and a discrimination (a short note on how to choose between close words) for *area / region / district*; *answer* has one for *answer / reply / response*.
- The learner errors target common traps. One is *We rented a department* for *apartment*, and *apartment* also gets a false-friend note, since a similar word means a home in some languages. Others are *an answer of this question* (use *to*), *I did an argument* (you *have* one), *a date with the dentist* (an *appointment*), and *She has many arts in her house* (art is uncountable in this sense).

## Checks

Every plural comes from the project's rules. The pronunciation panel (several models voting, plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary for American English) confirmed 23 of the 24 transcriptions at once. The exception was the British *argument*, which I had written with a weak middle vowel. All three panel votes agreed on a different form, with the vowel of *put*, which matches how British reference works show it. I changed the transcription, and the panel then confirmed it.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field of every entry. Between them they raised 41 issues. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 26 applied, 15 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

The most useful corrections:

- *appointment*: my second sense mixed two things, choosing someone for a job and the job itself. They have different grammar, so they are now two senses. My notes had also said an appointment is only with a professional. That was too strong; the notes now say *mainly*, and that it sounds odd for a casual evening with friends.
- *arrest* said the police "take someone away". An arrest means the police officially hold someone, whether or not they take the person anywhere, so the definition now says that. Two definitions also changed "done a crime" to the standard "committed a crime".
- *arm*: the sea sense was worded as if the land stuck out from the sea. It now describes a narrow part of the sea that goes into the land.
- *artist*: the sense "someone who does something with great skill" had carried *con artist* (a person who tricks people). That does not fit the definition, so it was removed.
- *attack*, in the sense of illness, said the attack is always short. It now says "often short".
- *apartment*: my usage note said British English uses *apartment* mainly for holiday homes. It is more common than that in Britain, and the note now says so.

Rejected, with reasons logged: eight objections to the house style for adaptation notes and grammar codes, which are settled; a suggestion to use *history* in a definition (it is outside the defining vocabulary, the roughly 2,400 plain words definitions should use); and a claim that the plural *arts* conflicts with the uncountable senses of *art* (the plural belongs to the countable senses).
