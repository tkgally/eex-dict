## What this run did

This was the first cycle of the scheduled run that began at 18:43 UTC. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request; a run has at most four.) Nothing was stranded, and the inbox was empty.

The mode selector chose **review**: a second reading by the reviewer panel (two outside language models that check every field of an entry) of entries that had been read only once. The oldest such entries were six verbs written on 2026-09-22: *ask, boil, break, breathe, become,* and *begin*. Their entries were far too thin (*begin* had one sense), so I took six rather than ten.

Before the panel, I changed by hand:

- **Missing senses.** These are uses a learner meets often:
  - *ask* a price (*how much are they asking for the car?*), and *ask* meaning expect (*that's asking a lot*)
  - *break* for a pause (*let's break for lunch*), a habit, bad news (*break the news to someone*), and a voice (*her voice broke*); news breaking now has its own subsense
  - *breathe* of cloth, skin, and wine (*let the wine breathe*), and the literary use meaning say very quietly
  - *become* meaning look good on someone (*that color becomes you*), a formal use
  - *begin* at a place or time (*the path begins behind the school*), and *begin* for starting to speak
- **New phrases**: *if you ask me*, *don't ask me*, *for the asking*, *break free*, *breathe easy*, and *not begin to* (*I can't begin to tell you*).
- **Learner errors.** These are common mistakes:
  - *Can I ask to you a question?*
  - *She asked me where did I live*
  - *My computer is broke* (the adjective is **broken**)
  - *I became to love this city* (use **came to**)
  - *My son became ten* (use **turned**)
- **A wrong usage note.** The *ask* entry claimed that *ask him a favor* is wrong. It is standard English, so the note was rewritten.
- **House style.** Two restrictions that NEXT.md had listed as due were moved out of definitions and into the explanation field: "(of a liquid)" in *boil* and "of weather" in *break*. *become*'s bare synonyms (*go, run, come, fall*) gained notes, and *begin* a note on *begin*, *start*, and *commence*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

One reviewer's reply for *breathe* was malformed. I re-ran it.

In total the panel raised 27 issues. I applied 13 and rejected 14.

What I accepted:

- *ask* meaning expect did not cover *All I ask is that you tell me the truth*, so the definition now reads "want or expect". Two grammar tags were wrong.
- *breathe* of wine did not fit the definition "let air pass through", which now reads "let air pass through it or reach it". The note on *breath* and *breathe* spoke of a final "e sound" when it meant the letter e. *not breathe a word* used the "used to ..." wording, which house style keeps for function words.
- *break our trip at a small town* was unnatural, and *break the routine* did not belong in the "interrupt a journey" sense.
- The core-idea sentences of *boil* and *begin* were awkward (the core idea is the one-sentence summary at the top of an entry). The definition of *not begin to* missed the point of the phrase.

What I rejected:

- A claim that *ask someone to a party* is not English. It is ordinary, as in *he asked her to the dance*.
- A claim that *breathe* is transitive in *let your skin breathe*. The verb *let* takes the object, and *breathe* has none.
- Two label objections, and objections to core ideas written as sentences, which house style requires.
