# 2026-10-12, run at 16:43 UTC, cycle 2: build, twelve adjectives *related* to *scientific*

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: new entries written from the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written). The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose it after this run's first cycle, a review. I claimed the next twelve band-1 words (band 1 is the most common words, the ones learners meet first). All are adjectives: *related*, *religious*, *rich*, *right*, *rough*, *round*, *rude*, *sad*, *safe*, *same*, *satisfied*, and *scientific*.

Each entry was written from my own knowledge and the house style guide, then sent through the full pipeline: the format check, the script that writes the comparative forms (*richer*, *sadder*), the pronunciation check, and the two outside reviewer models. All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were confirmed on the first pass by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU pronouncing data. All twelve entries are now marked `reviewed`.

Some notes on what the entries contain. *right* has six senses, from *correct* to the informal British *a right mess*, with a learner error for *You have right*, a common mistake for learners whose languages say "have right". *same* explains that it takes **the** and **as** (*the same as yours*), with three idioms (*all the same*, *same here*, *one and the same*). Its use standing alone (*I'll have the same*) is a pronoun, so it is pointed to a separate entry, `same-pron`, now in the queue. *rough* covers six senses, including the informal British *I feel rough* (ill), and the idiom *rough and ready*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 43 issues, 36 of them "blocking" (an entry cannot be marked as reviewed until each one has a decision). I applied 24 and rejected 19. None needed to go to the owner. Every decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Applied (each fix written in my own words):

- *round*: the number sense had said a round number is "not an exact amount", but *ten* can be exact. It now says a round number is a whole number ending in 0 that is easy to use, often in place of a more exact one, and *exact* is no longer listed as its opposite. Two examples were replaced: *a round hundred dollars* sounded odd, and *about two thousand* needed *dollars*. In the curved-shape subsense, *round shoulders* became *round ends* (people usually say *rounded shoulders*).
- *sad*: the informal British sense (*That's so sad!* about someone's dull hobby) had been defined as "boring or not fashionable". It now says the word shows that someone has a dull life or strange interests, in a way others think is foolish. An example that could be read as ordinary sadness was replaced, and *cool* was removed as its opposite.
- *rich*: *heavy* is not a good synonym for rich food, and *light* is not its opposite; *plain* is now the opposite.
- *related*, *religious*: grammar codes that said a sense could be used only after a verb, or only before a noun, were wrong and were removed.
- *rough*: the sea sense no longer requires strong wind, and *a rough day* lost its "informal" label.
- *same*: the note on which words come before **same** now allows **my** or **their**, and the idiom *all the same* now has a plain phrase as its definition.
- *scientific*: an unnatural example about planning meals was replaced.

Rejected:

- reviewer-a said that **right** in *I got half the questions right* and *That's right* is an adverb, and that **safe** in *got home safe* is an adverb. In all three the word describes a noun (the questions, the thing said, the people), so they are adjectives.
- *rude jokes* stays labelled British. A region label in this dictionary means "mainly used there", not "used only there".
- *comparative with more* is a valid grammar code; reviewer-b thought it was not.
- Rich colors and voices are pleasant by definition, so the word *pleasant* stays in that sense.

Two definitions also had to change for the vocabulary check (which allows only words from the defining list, or words that have an entry). *right* sense 4 now says "the side of the body that has the hand most people write with", matching the noun *right*; it had used *east* and *north*. *rude* sense 2 now says "in a way that may shock some people" instead of *offensive*.

The tools added back-links (the mirror cross-references that keep links two-way) in nine entries. Three were attached to the wrong sense and I moved them by hand: *complete* (to its "total" sense, for *a right mess*), *deep* (to its color sense, for *rich*), and *hard* (to its "full of problems" sense, for *rough*).
