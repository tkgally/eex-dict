# 2026-09-29 (15) — Build: twelve verbs, *try* to *warm*

## What this run did

This was the third cycle of the run that began at 18:44 UTC. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request; the first two merged as #138 and #139.) The mode selector chose **build** again: new entries from the queue of waiting words, in order, from band 1, the most common words.

I drafted twelve verbs: *try, turn, understand, upset, use, visit, vote, wait, wake, walk, want,* and *warm*. They have 50 senses, 3 phrases, and 24 learner errors (mistakes learners of many languages make).

- ***turn*** has eight senses. They run from turning a key and turning left to *turn red*, *turn ten*, *turn to someone for help*, and *turn to a new subject*.
- ***try*** explains the difference between *try to do* (make an effort) and *try doing* (test to see what happens).
- ***want*** has a usage note that *I want* can sound rude in a shop, where *I'd like* is more polite. It also covers *you want to be careful* and the British *the car wants washing*.
- ***use*** covers tools, amounts used up, words, using a person unfairly, drugs, and the informal *I could use a coffee*. It points to the separate entries for *used to* and *be used to*.
- ***wait*** has the phrases *wait and see* and *just you wait*, and the American *wait tables*.
- ***walk*** has *walk someone home*, *walk the dog*, and the phrase *walk all over someone*.
- Short comparisons explain how to choose between near-synonyms: *try*/*attempt*, *understand*/*realize*/*know*, *wait*/*expect*/*await*, *walk*/*stroll*/*march*/*wander*, and *want*/*would like*/*wish*/*desire*.

Every word form came from the house rules or the table of irregular forms (*understood*, *upset*, *woke*/*woken*). All 24 pronunciations were confirmed.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 47 issues, 32 of them blocking. (A blocking issue must be settled before an entry can be published.) I applied 33 and rejected 14. None needed the owner.

Accepted:

- *turn*: the page sense now covers *turn to page fifty*, which means finding a page, not just moving to the next one. The note comparing *turn* with *become* no longer says the change is usually sudden, and it adds food going bad (*the milk has turned*).
- *want*: the "police" sense had also held *you're wanted on the phone*. That example is now a separate sub-sense, "ask for someone to come or to speak to someone". The advice sense now has a proper verb definition, "would be wise to do something".
- *wake*: the forms note says *waked* also exists, but a learner-error note said only *woke* was correct. The note now says *waked* is rare.
- *vote*: the comparison with *elect* no longer suggests that you cannot *vote someone into office*.
- *understand*: the "how someone feels" sub-sense no longer adds "and be kind about it". The phrase *be given to understand* was removed.
- *wait*: the "leave until later" sense had an example with a person as the subject, which belonged to another sense. It was replaced with *Your questions will have to wait until after lunch*.

Rejected:

- Two objections to the notes saying that *understand* and *want* are "not normally" used in continuous tenses (*I'm wanting*). The project already decided to keep these notes.
- *turn ten* treated as an ordinary transitive verb. It works like *become ten*.
- A request to hand-mark *turned* in an example. The website marks listed word forms automatically.
- Three requests for an intransitive pattern that the closed list of patterns does not contain.

The cross-reference tool adds matching links in other entries automatically. Three pairs had a synonym link on one side and a "compare" link on the other: *turn*/*become*, *upset*/*hurt*, and *warm*/*heat*. I made each pair synonyms on both sides.
