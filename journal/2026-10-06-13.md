## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 27 issues, 20 of them marked blocking. I applied 15 and rejected 12; none needed to go to you. Every decision is logged with a reason in the decision record (`reviews/decisions.jsonl`).

Applied, among others:

- *button*: the one-sentence summary at the top of the entry (the "core idea") left out the computer-screen sense and seemed to say clothing buttons are pushed with a finger; it now names both kinds plainly. The pronunciation note, which said many Americans "hardly say the t", now describes what they actually do: stop the air for a moment instead of a clear **t**.
- *cable*: the television sense said programs come "along wires"; modern cable often uses glass-fiber lines, so it now says "wires or similar lines".
- *capital*: the core idea called every sense "something that comes first or matters most", which does not fit money for a business; it now says "main or basic".
- *car*: the reviewer rightly said my new note was too narrow (British English also says *buffet car*), and that the train sense needed an American region label to match the note. Both done.
- *care*: the reviewer pointed out that in American English the full address form is *in care of*, not bare *care of*. The explanation now says the bare form is mainly British, and the examples show both. An example about driving "on snow" now says "in the snow".
- *calendar*: my new usage note was reworded slightly ("a small book in which you write down your appointments").

Rejected, among others:

- A request to label *on the button* (exactly on time) as British; it is common in American English too.
- The claim that **lowercase** is not an antonym (opposite) of the letter sense of **capital**. Capital and small letters are the ordinary opposed pair.
- The claim that `old-fashioned` was in the wrong label set. In this dictionary's closed list of labels it belongs to the set for how current a word is.
- An objection that an old-style *cable* (a telegram) was not necessarily short or sent far. Telegrams were paid for by the word and so kept short, and a *cable* was usually sent abroad.
- Three small points on *card* (that *put in your card* is not typical, that *in the cards* is not informal, and a rewording of the learner error).

The first reviewer ("reviewer-a") raised 25 of the 27 issues; the second ("reviewer-b") raised two, one applied (a collocation in the wrong group on *camera*) and one rejected (it concluded in its own text that the definition was fine). Six of the twelve entries drew no blocking issue at all.
