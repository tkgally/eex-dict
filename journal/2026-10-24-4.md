# 2026-10-24, run at 00:43 UTC, cycle 4: build

## What this run did

This cycle was a **build**: it wrote new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words the dictionary still needs. The queue is now at the band-2 verbs. Bands are the dictionary's own frequency levels, and band 2 is the second most common. This cycle took the next twelve, in queue order after *deal*:

- `debate-v`: discuss formally; think about a choice (*I'm debating whether to go*)
- `decline-v`: become less; say no politely
- `demand-v`: ask firmly; need (*the job demands patience*), with a warning for translators. Look-alike words in several languages just mean *ask*; English *demand* is much stronger.
- `demonstrate-v`: prove; show how; show a quality; protest in public
- `dip-v`: into liquid; go down; move down for a moment
- `dismiss-v`: refuse to consider; remove from a job; send away; end a court case
- `display-v`: put where people can see it; show a feeling; show on a screen
- `distinguish-v`: tell apart; make different; manage to see; *distinguish yourself*
- `download-v`: one sense. A note says the British verb is often stressed on the second part.
- `drag-v`: pull; make someone go; move on a screen; time passing slowly
- `drill-v`: make a hole; teach by repetition
- `dust-v`: clean; cover with powder. These two senses are nearly opposite, and a translator's note says so.

Each entry has its grammar patterns, examples, collocations (the words a word usually goes with), word family, learner errors where learners really make them, and notes for translators.

## Pronunciation and word forms

All 24 transcriptions were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, a group of models that vote on each transcription. For American English they were also checked against the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. The verb forms (*dipped*, *dragging*, *drilled*) came from the regular spelling rules and were confirmed.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

I sent the entries to the two reviewers in three groups of four, and every reply came back complete. *demonstrate* and *dismiss* drew no objections. On the other ten, the reviewers raised 49 issues. I accepted 34 and rejected 15, each with a logged reason.

Accepted, with my own wording:
- **dip**: the "go down" sense was limited to a level or amount, but two of its examples were about the sun setting and a road sloping down. The definition now covers position too. The core idea (the one-sentence summary at the top) no longer says that whatever dips always comes back up.
- **dust**: one example (*He dusts and cleans the house*) shared its object with *cleans*, so it was not really a use without an object. It is replaced. *Dust something off* was removed from the collocations, because it is a phrasal verb with its own planned entry. The "cooking" label was dropped from the powder sense, since snow or powder can dust things too.
- **drag**: on a screen, you drag by pressing the mouse button or your finger on the item, not by "holding the mouse". Reworded.
- **demand**: the usage note on *demand that he leave* wrongly offered the ordinary past form as a British alternative. It now gives only the British *should*.
- **drill**: the "make a hole" sense now covers drilling into the ground for oil or water. A new example shows *drill something into someone*. A translator note no longer makes a claim about where the two senses came from.
- **distinguish**, **download**, **display**: notes that said "always" or made claims that were too broad are softened. One learner error is rewritten so that the corrected sentence matches its note.

Rejected:
- That a core idea may not begin *To display is to...*. That is the house form.
- That a passive example cannot carry its active pattern label (*verb + object*). The house marks such examples "passive" in their note instead.
- That collocations may not show a *something* slot (*download something from the internet*). The style guide allows it.

The cross-reference tool added eleven matching links on other entries, such as *refuse*, *require*, and *show*. One landed on the wrong sense: *debate* was linked to *argue* "quarrel". I moved it by hand to *argue* "give reasons".
