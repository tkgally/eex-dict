## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The scheduler (the tool that picks each cycle's kind of work from what has been waiting longest) chose a *build*: writing new entries from the queue of words still to be written. It allowed up to twelve new entries and a paid-call budget of US$3. Before starting, the pre-flight checks found nothing stranded: no open pull requests, no leftover work branches, no entry reports from readers, and nothing in the owner's inbox.

Twelve nouns were taken from the queue in its set order, all in band 2 (the second most important of five frequency bands): *fiction*, *fighter*, *fighting*, *final*, *finding*, *fine* (the money you pay for breaking a rule), *fingernail*, *finish*, *fire engine*, *firefighter*, *fireplace*, and *firework*. All twelve were written in full, went through the whole checking pipeline, and are now published as `reviewed`. Highlights:

- *fighter* has four senses: someone who fights as a sport, someone in an armed group, a military plane, and the approving sense of someone who never gives up.
- *final* separates the competition sense from the exam sense, and the exam sense now shows the difference between varieties: in American English a *final* is the last exam in one class; in British English *finals* are the exams at the end of a degree, given as a sub-sense with a British label.
- *finish* covers the end of a race, the place someone gets in a race (*a second-place finish*), and the look of a surface (*a smooth finish*), with a sub-sense for the paint or oil that gives that look.
- *firework* has a sub-sense for the show itself (*watch the fireworks*) and an informal sense for angry arguments (*there were fireworks at the meeting*).

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Each entry was read by two reviewer models from different companies, field by field. Together they raised 27 issues, 2 of them *blocking* (an issue that must be settled before an entry can be published). Both blocking issues were right and were fixed: the definition of *fighting* used the word *fight*, which is circular, so it now says *attack each other*; and an example for *finding* stated a checkable claim about how much young people sleep, which the house rules forbid in examples, so it now describes a report about an old bridge. Of the 27 issues, 21 were applied and 6 rejected, each with a logged reason. The rejections: articles inside collocation lists (*a parking fine*) are house practice; a phrase named as a word takes the bold mark, which one reviewer misread as an error; and the idiom *a fight to the finish* belongs under *fight*, the first noun in it, under the house keyword rule. It is noted for *fight*'s next review. Useful minor fixes included moving *science fiction* and *street fighting* to the right collocation type, dropping the mainly Australian *grand final*, and widening the *firework* definition, since not all fireworks are small or fly up.

After the panel, the vocabulary check found two definitions that used words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words definitions are written in): *investigation* in *finding* and *polished* in *finish*. Both were reworded. Five *mirror links* (a cross-reference added on the other entry so the link goes both ways) could not be placed by the tool, because it could not tell which sense to attach them to. I placed them by hand, with short notes, on *argument*, *exam*, *result*, *nail*, and *end*.

**Pronunciation.** All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were checked by a panel of models and, for American, against an open pronouncing dictionary. Twenty-three were verified. The British form of *fire engine* was *disputed*: no panel vote agreed on the recorded check, though two of three agreed on a re-check a few minutes later. The two results differ only because the models vary. I kept the transcription, which matches the existing *fire* and *engine* entries, flagged the entry, and added a line to the curator list for a session with the owner.
