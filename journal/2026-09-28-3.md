# 2026-09-28 (3) — Review: fifteen verbs, *peel* to *protect*

## What this run did

This was the third cycle of the scheduled run that began at 00:44 UTC. A "cycle" is one unit of work, checked, recorded, and merged on its own. The first two cycles' pull requests (#110 and #111) merged before this one started. The mode selector (the script that decides what each cycle does) chose **review** for the third time: a second reading of entries that have passed the reviewer panel only once.

The block was fifteen verbs written late on 2026-09-26: *peel, perform, pick, place, plan, play, pour, practice, praise, pray, press, prevent, produce, pronounce,* and *protect*.

I read each one before sending it to the panel and added common uses that were missing:

- **pick**: a new sense, *eat only small amounts of food, slowly, because you are not hungry* (*She just picked at her dinner*).
- **place**: a new sense for finishing a race in a position (*She placed third in the race*).
- **play**: a new sense, *act as if you were something that you are not* (*The dog rolled over and played dead*; *Don't play innocent with me*), and the informal phrase *play it cool*.
- **pour**: a subsense for putting a lot of money or effort into something (*She poured all her energy into the business*).
- **press**: a subsense for flattening something or squeezing liquid out of it (*press flowers*, *press grapes*).

My first wording for the new *play* sense used *pretend* and *behave*. Neither is in the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words definitions are written in), and neither has an entry yet. The vocabulary check caught this, and I reworded the definition before the panel saw it.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 24 issues, 14 of them "blocking" (the entry cannot stay marked reviewed until a decision is logged). I decided all 24: 15 applied and 9 rejected. Each decision is in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. This panel was more useful than the last one: most of the first reviewer's blocking points were right.

Changes I accepted:

- **peel**: sense 2 joined two different uses in one definition: paint peeling by itself, and someone peeling a label off. They are now a sense and a subsense, each with the right grammar.
- **play**: three definitions were reworded. "act a character" became "take the part of a character". A definition with a semicolon in the middle was rewritten. *play it safe* began "not take risks", but a verb definition should start with a verb, so it is now "be careful and do what is least likely to go wrong".
- **practice**: the learner-error note said the verb is followed by the **-ing** form. That is true only when another verb follows; *practice the piano* takes a noun. The note now says so.
- **plan**: *aim* was listed as a synonym of the "think ahead" sense. Both reviewers said it belongs with "intend", and it has moved there.
- **pick**: the fruit-and-flowers sense now also covers vegetables and leaves. The note comparing *pick* with *choose* now calls *pick* "a little less formal", not "more informal".
- **prevent**: both reviewers questioned *guard* as a synonym. The *guard* entry has a sense meaning *guard against something*, so I kept the link and added a note saying that.
- **pour** and **praise**: a clearer subsense definition and a better example.

What I rejected, and why:

- The first reviewer wanted the British forms *practised* and *practising* in *practice*'s inflections. The forms follow the American headword; the British spelling *practise* is already recorded as a variant.
- It wanted a literary label on *pray* meaning *hope*. *I just pray that he's safe* is ordinary spoken English.
- The second reviewer said you shell an egg rather than peel it. *Peel an egg* and *peel shrimp* are everyday English.
- One-sentence summaries at the top of an entry (the core idea) take a sentence form by house style, and translators' notes open with a quoted headword by house convention; these objections were rejected again.
