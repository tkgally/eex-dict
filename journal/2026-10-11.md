The claim tool gave out the next twelve band-1 adjectives in queue order (band 1 means the most common words, those every learner meets first): *confident*, *confused*, *connected*, *conscious*, *convenient*, *cool*, *correct*, *creative*, *criminal*, *crowded*, *curious*, and *current*. I wrote each entry from my own knowledge, following the house style guide. Some points of interest:

- *cool* has six senses: a little cold (with a subsense for clothes that keep you from feeling hot), calm, unfriendly, the informal "good, fashionable" and "acceptable" senses, and colors. It also has a short note on choosing between *cool*, *cold*, and *chilly*, and the phrase *cool, calm, and collected*.
- *conscious* has four senses: awake, aware, done on purpose, and the *health-conscious* type. Its learner error is the common mix-up with the noun *conscience* (*a guilty conscience*).
- *convenient* warns that a time is convenient *for* a person, not that the person is convenient (*If it is convenient for you*, not *If you are convenient*).
- *current* has a note for translators: in several languages the word for "current" looks like English *actual*, which means "real".
- *confused* has a usage note on the *confused*/*confusing* pair, and *correct* has a note on choosing between *correct*, *right*, and *accurate*.

## What the checks and reviewers found

The inflection tool wrote the comparison forms. Only *cool* has its own (*cooler*, *coolest*); the others use *more* or are not compared. The pronunciation check (a panel of three outside models, plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary for American English) agreed with all 24 transcriptions the first time.

Two independent reviewer models from different labs then read every field. They raised 36 issues, 28 of them blocking (an issue that must be fixed or rejected with a reason before the entry can be published). I read each one, applied 26, and rejected 10. Nothing was escalated to the owner. The main changes:

- *creative*: the reviewer said that "using clever methods to get around rules" is not a general sense but is limited to *creative accounting* and *be creative with the truth*. I agreed and made it a subsense of the first sense, limited to those two uses.
- *crowded*: *a crowded market* (meaning many competing sellers) did not fit the definition "too many things in a small space or time", so I replaced it.
- *convenient*: three fields said a person can never be *convenient*. That is too strong (*a convenient scapegoat* is a person), so all three now limit the warning to times and plans.
- *conscious*: the learner error said *felt a guilty conscience*; people say *had a guilty conscience*.
- *current*: I removed a learner error, because *the actual situation* is also correct English in the sense "the real situation".

Rejections followed house rules. One reviewer twice objected to an explanation that states a subject limit (*Used of colors*), but the style guide puts subject limits exactly there. The other reviewer asked four times to remove the gradability codes (*comparative with more*), which the house uses to drive the inflection tool. That reviewer's precision this cycle (the share of its issues that I applied) was 0.29.

The cross-reference tool added five mirror links in existing entries. I checked each one by hand and moved three that it had attached to the wrong sense: *crowded* now sits under *busy* "place", *confident* under *certain* "sure", and *confused* under *clear* "easy to understand". *conscious* now lists *aware* as a word to compare, not a synonym, to match what *aware* already says.
