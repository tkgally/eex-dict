## What this cycle did

A **build cycle** writes new entries. The scheduler allows at most twelve per cycle and takes words from the queue (the list of words still to be written) in order, the most basic words (band 1) first. The queue has been working through basic nouns in alphabetical order, and this cycle drafted the next twelve: *ticket, tie, tip, tissue, title, today, tomorrow, toe, toilet, tone, tongue, tool*.

Each entry has its senses in order of usefulness, with examples, grammar, and collocations (words that typically go together), plus idioms, a usage note, learner errors, and translator notes where they help. Some points:

- *ticket* has six senses, from travel and event tickets to parking tickets, numbered tickets, *a ticket to success*, the American political sense (*on the same ticket*), and a request for computer help.
- *tie* covers the piece of clothing, an equal result, connections between people (*family ties*), fasteners (*a hair tie*), the British cup game, and the American railroad sense. The usage note explains American *tie* against British *draw* for an equal score.
- *tip* has the thin end, money for service, advice, secret information, and the British sense of a place for rubbish. These come from more than one origin, but published learner's dictionaries treat them as one word and the origins are not clear, so they share one entry. The idioms *the tip of the iceberg* and *on the tip of your tongue* go here, because *tip* is the first noun in each.
- *toilet* has a **synonym discrimination**, a short note on how to choose between *toilet*, *bathroom*, and *restroom* in American and British English.
- *today* and *tomorrow* are the noun uses (*Today is my birthday*, *tomorrow's meeting*, *the world of tomorrow*). The adverb uses (*I'm busy today*) have their own entries, still in the queue.
- Twenty-two idioms in all, such as *on your toes*, *step on someone's toes*, *like there's no tomorrow*, *hold your tongue*, *tongue in cheek*, and *the tools of the trade*.

Before the review, the vocabulary checker found about a dozen words in definitions that are outside the defining vocabulary (the list of about 2,400 words that definitions should use), such as *bond*, *concrete*, *extra*, and *publishes*. I reworded each definition.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

All 24 pronunciations were verified, and the inflections (plurals) were generated by the rules. The panel then raised 51 issues: 42 from reviewer-a and 9 from reviewer-b. I applied 41 and rejected 10. None needed the owner.

**Applied (the main ones):**

- Both reviewers said that *today* and *tomorrow* talked about the adverb use in their learner errors and translator notes (*I have a test in today*), while their usage notes say the adverb has its own entry. They were right. That material was removed, and `NEXT.md` now says it belongs in the adverb entries when they are written.
- *title*: the sense for words such as *Doctor* before a name was reworded, and an example about becoming queen (a rank, not a word before a name) was replaced. The legal-ownership sense was marked "countable or uncountable", because in American English *a title* is also the document that shows who owns a car.
- *tie*: an equal result can come from a vote, not only a game; ties between people need not be "strong"; and railroad ties are made of other materials as well as wood.
- *ticket*: *fine* moved from "synonym" to "compare", since a ticket is the notice and a fine is the money. The political sense lost a second meaning it had wrongly mixed in.
- *tissue*, *tip*, *toe*, *tomorrow*, *tone*, and *toilet* got smaller fixes: clearer idiom definitions, a countable example for body tissue, a British label on *go to the toilet*, and a softer statement about *restroom*.

**Rejected:**

- *pay a parking ticket* is natural American English.
- The American label on the political sense of *ticket* stays: a region label means "mainly used there".
- Reviewer-b asked for *down tools* to be defined as "to stop working". House style writes verb definitions without *to*.
- The other rejections are in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`, each with a reason. Examples: *a cup tie* is a knockout game, so the definition stands; and the formal label stays off the "language" sense of *tongue*, because the common phrase *mother tongue* is not formal.
