## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 51 issues: 48 from reviewer-a and 3 from reviewer-b. I agreed with 36 and fixed them in my own words. The most useful:

- *shore*: I had written that *shore* is used only for a sea or a lake, and made *the shore of the river* a learner error. Reviewer-a was right that a very wide river has a shore too. The definition now includes a very wide river, the notes say *bank* is *usually* the word for a river, and the learner error is gone.
- *shower*: the party sense (*a baby shower*) said the presents are for a woman. Such parties are now held for couples and parents too, so it now says *someone*.
- *sign*: the star-sign sense called a sign "a group of stars". A sign is a part of the year named after a group of stars; it now says so.
- *side*: the surface sense said *flat* surfaces, but a can or a ball has sides too.
- *sister*: the first sense now names *half-sister* for a sister who shares one parent; the British nurse sense is narrowed to an experienced nurse in charge of one area of a hospital.
- *look over your shoulder*: I had defined it as worrying in general; it means staying watchful because you fear someone may attack or catch you, and the example was changed to fit.
- *silence*: the first sense now allows *almost* no sound, and the usage note is limited to *Silence!* as an order.

I rejected 15. Reviewer-a wanted the American label taken off the road sense of *shoulder*; bare *shoulder* for the edge of a road is mainly American (British English says *hard shoulder* or *verge*), so the label stays. It said *big sister* does not mean an older sister; in ordinary use it does. It said *sister* in *a sister company* is an adjective; the house style treats it as a noun used before another noun. It said *programme* is not the British spelling; for broadcasts it is. Two of reviewer-b's three issues withdrew themselves within the same comment.

Four definition words were outside the defining vocabulary (the 2,394 plain words that definitions must use): *famous*, *collection*, *impress*, and *spiders*. I rewrote those definitions before the panel ran.

The cross-reference tool reported two pairs that named each other under different types: *bank* listed *shore* as a synonym while *shore* listed *bank* as a word to compare, and *program* and *show* the same way. I fixed both by hand: *shore* and *bank* are now "compare" on both sides, and *show* and *program* are synonyms on both sides.
