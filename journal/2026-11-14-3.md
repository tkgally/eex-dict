# Journal, 2026-11-14 (third report): scheduled run 8da3yb

## Cycle 1: lint

**What the cycle was.** A lint pass is the dictionary's periodic housekeeping: no new entries are written, and instead a set of checks runs over all 4,344 entries and over the project's own notes (the wiki). The selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) forced a lint because ten cycles had passed since the last one on 2026-11-13. This was the hundred-and-twelfth such pass.

**Before starting.** There were no unmerged pull requests left by an earlier run, no leftover work branches, nothing in `inbox/` (the owner's channel for instructions), and no open "Entry report" issues from readers.

**Cross-references.** Many entries point to each other: a sense of *penalty* lists *price* as a synonym, for example. The house rule is that such links work both ways, so the entry pointed to should point back. A script (`tools/crossref.py`) adds these return links where it can tell which sense they belong to. This time it added them in twelve entries, mostly word-family links for newly built nouns (*performer* now points back to *perform* and *performance*, *postcard* to *post*, *planning* to *plan*). In eleven other cases the script could not decide which sense of the target entry should carry the return link, so I placed each one by reading the definitions: *peak* (the "highest or most successful point" sense) now lists *height* as a synonym and *valley* as an opposite; *pile* lists *tower* (things stacked up) and *mountain* (a large amount) on the matching senses; *price* in the sense "something bad you must accept" lists *penalty*; *salt* and *spice* now point to *pepper* for comparison; *view*, *impression* and *insight* each point back to *perception* on the sense that matches; and the first sense of *tie* (fasten) lists *undo* as its opposite. These are links only; no definition or example was changed.

**Frequency bands.** Every word waiting to be written has a band from 1 (most important) to 5. Words added to the waiting list by tools arrive "unbanded" until three models vote on them. 47 such words were voted on this time, at a cost of under one US cent. None of the votes disagreed by more than one band, so the median band was applied directly and all 47 are now ordinary waiting rows.

**Reviewer precision.** Every entry is checked by two reviewer models, and each objection they raise is accepted or rejected by the drafting session. "Precision" here means the share of a reviewer's objections that were accepted. Reviewer A stands at 85 percent and reviewer B at 83 percent overall. The rule is that a kind of objection a reviewer gets right less than 30 percent of the time, over at least twenty decisions, is switched off for that reviewer. The three kinds below that line (reviewer A on inflections, reviewer B on phrases and on pronunciation) were already off; no new ones fell below it. Reviewer B's spelling-and-format objections stay on at 38 percent because the owner ruled they should.

**Reports for later review cycles.** Three reports list things for a person or a review cycle to judge, not errors to fix automatically. 1,023 pairs of entries where a cross-reference may sit on the wrong sense (this list grows as new entries arrive; review cycles work through it); 316 definitions that use their own headword (most are harmless, such as "take a dog for a walk" under *walk*); and the same six "promises" as last time, where a usage note says a related word has its own entry that does not exist yet (*storey*, the *corn* on a foot, *used to*, and others), all already known.

**The wiki.** Every page is listed in the index and every link in the index leads to a page; the run log has an entry for every cycle since the last lint; there are no open questions for the owner and no duplicate lines in the curator file (the list of matters left for a session with the owner). I found no contradictions between pages.

**Spend.** US$0.007 for the band vote. The day's total is US$2.12 of the US$15 daily cap.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. One small known friction recurred: the band-vote `settle` step needs to be told the name of the vote file (`--votes votes-unbanded.tsv`) when only unbanded words were voted that day. This is already recorded in the wiki and needs no change.
