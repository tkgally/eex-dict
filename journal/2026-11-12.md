**What the entries contain.**

- *means* has two senses. The first is a method (*a cheap means of transport*). The second is money, which is used only in the plural (*live within your means*). Its phrases are *by means of* (formal), *by all means* (polite permission), *by no means* (with *not by any means* shown too), *a means to an end*, and *a person of means* (formal). - *medal* has the phrase *deserve a medal* (informal). It also has a pronunciation note: in American English *medal* and *metal* often sound the same.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The reviewers raised 16 issues, 3 of them blocking. I applied 15 and rejected 1.

- Both reviewers were right that the idiom *deserve a medal* was defined with *used to say...*. House style keeps that formula for function words and interjections. It is now a verb phrase: *have done something very difficult or unpleasant, and so earn praise*.

- I rejected a claim that defining *membership* with *member* is circular. The circularity rule bans defining a word through a word that is itself defined through it. *Member* is not defined through *membership*.
