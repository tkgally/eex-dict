# Journal, 2026-10-09 (run of 00:43 UTC, cycle 4): twelve new nouns, *teenager* to *throat*

## What this cycle did

This was the fourth and last cycle of the run. Runs stop at four cycles, because entries written in later cycles of long runs came out thinner. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each cycle's kind of work from what is most overdue) chose **build**, so the cycle wrote the next twelve band 1 nouns from the queue. Band 1 means the most common words. The twelve were *teenager, telephone, television, temperature, term, test, text, theater, theory, thought, threat,* and *throat*.

Every field of each entry was filled by hand from the style guide. Together they carry 17 idioms and fixed phrases, among them:

- *in terms of something*, *on good terms*, *come to terms with something*, *in the long term*, *in no uncertain terms*
- *put something to the test*, *stand the test of time*
- *in theory*
- *on second thought*, *it's the thought that counts*
- *under threat*
- *at each other's throats*, *jump down someone's throat*

`theater-n` records the British spelling *theatre* as a variant. It has four senses: the building for plays, plays in general, the American movie building, and the British hospital room for operations.

## The checks

- **Pronunciation.** The pronunciation panel (three outside models) and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary confirmed all transcriptions but one. American *temperature*, written in three syllables (*TEM-pruh-cher*), got only one panel vote out of four. The CMU dictionary lists both the three- and four-syllable forms. I rewrote it in four syllables (*TEM-per-uh-cher*) and re-ran the check, which agreed four out of four. The pronunciation note says that many speakers use three.
- **Plurals.** All twelve came from the rules script.
- **Defining vocabulary.** Two definition words were outside the basic list: *figurative* and *mobile*. Both were reworded before the review.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Reviewer-a raised 36 issues. Reviewer-b returned complete verdicts with no issues on all twelve entries. I applied 30, rejected 6, and escalated none.

The pattern this cycle was learner errors that were not really errors. Five sentences I had offered as mistakes are in fact grammatical English:

- *The doctor measured my temperature*
- *I have a pain in my throat*
- *He took her by the neck*
- *My theory is the dog ate it*
- *I passed the test* (the original version, which I had already rewritten before the review)

I removed these or replaced them with real errors. For example, *My throat is sick* is now corrected to *I have a sore throat*. I should check more carefully that an *incorrect* sentence is wrong, not just less usual.

Other changes:

- `thought-n`: the kindness sense (*a kind thought*) is now *an idea or a small act that shows you care about someone*. Its old example was really about *thinking of* your safety, and it was replaced.
- `test-n`: *experiment* is no longer listed as a synonym.
- `theory-n`: no longer labelled as a science-only word. The British *theory test* was dropped.
- `term-n`: *win a term* and *a term paper* were removed.
- `threat-n`: no longer says that any danger is a threat.
- `television-n`: the learner-error note now allows *I saw it on TV*.

Rejected:

- The American labels on *movie theater* and *on second thought*. In this dictionary a region label means *mainly used there*, and British English says *cinema* and *on second thoughts*.
- *at each other's throats* is defined as *arguing or fighting angrily*, because the phrase follows *be*. It is not a verb phrase.
- *phone* stays a synonym of the telephone system (*by phone*, *on the phone*).

**Cross-references.** The cross-reference script adds back-links: when one entry links to another, it adds the matching link in the other direction. It placed seven this cycle, and two landed on the wrong sense. I moved them by hand: *term* now sits under the *rule* sense of `condition-n`, and *theory* under the *belief* sense of `idea-n`. `fever-n` already called *temperature* a synonym, so `temperature-n` now does the same.
