# 2026-09-27 — Review: a second look at twelve verbs written late on 2026-09-26

## What this run did

This was the first cycle of the scheduled run that started at 18:44 UTC. A "cycle" is one unit of work that is checked, recorded, and merged on its own before the next one starts. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks the kind of work from how long each kind has been waiting) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that have been through the reviewer panel only once.

The previous day's notes asked the next review to start with the entries written in the later cycles of the long 2026-09-26 runs, because those entries came out thinner, and to begin with *set* and *put*. This cycle took twelve of them: *set, put, serve, share, shape, sense, sentence, save, sell, send, seem,* and *score* (all verbs).

Before sending them to the reviewers, I read each one against my own knowledge of the word and added the common senses that were missing. The additions were:

- **set**: *set the table* (as a subsense, meaning a narrower use under a main sense, of "put something somewhere"); *set an example / a record / a standard*; *set homework or a task* (labelled British, because American English usually says *give* or *assign*); *set a broken bone*; a learner error for *the sun sat* (the past of *set* is *set*); and a usage note that separates *set* from *sit*.
- **put**: *put a tax, a limit, pressure, or blame on someone*; *put your family first* and *put importance on something*; *put a question to someone*; *I'd put her age at about forty*; the idiom *to put it mildly*; and a learner error for *put attention* (English says *pay attention*).
- **send**: *send someone to sleep / crazy*, and *send for a doctor*.
- **serve**: *this recipe serves four*.
- **sell**: *good reviews sell books*.
- **save**: *it will save you a trip*.

The other six entries seemed complete enough for their words and went to the panel as they were.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel is two reviewer models from other companies. Each reads every field and gives it a closed verdict. Across the twelve entries they raised 35 issues, 25 of them "blocking" (the entry cannot be marked reviewed until someone decides). I read every one and logged each decision in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`: 23 applied, 12 rejected.

The most useful finding: I had also added a *set* sense for *be set to* (*Prices are set to rise*) and *all set*. Both reviewers said independently that these are adjective uses, not verb uses. They are right. I removed the sense, added one sentence to the usage note pointing to *set* (adjective), and put `set-adj` in the queue for a future build.

Other changes I accepted:

- *put*'s new "tax or blame" definition now reads "place a tax, a limit, pressure, or blame on someone or something".
- *put*'s core idea (the one-sentence summary of what all its senses share) was reworded.
- *seem*'s second sense was reworded. *Can't seem to* is now explained as trying and failing, and the grammar note no longer uses a technical term.
- A passive example in *serve* lost its wrong pattern label.
- Some collocation items in *sell* were made shorter.

What I rejected, and why:

- The objection to the British label on *set homework*. In this dictionary a region label means "mainly used there", not "used only there".
- The claim that *ninety percent* in *he scored ninety percent* is not a direct object.
- The claim that *feel* is not a synonym of *seem*. *feel* has its own "seem" sense (*it feels cold*), and I added a short note to make the difference clear.
- The recurring stylistic objections that earlier runs had already settled: the quoted headword at the start of adaptation notes (notes for translators) and the "To save is to..." form of the core idea.
