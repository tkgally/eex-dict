## What this cycle did

A **build cycle** writes new dictionary entries from the queue (the list of words still to be written, ordered by how common they are). The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose build again because it was furthest behind its target share of cycles.

I drafted the next twelve very common nouns (band 1, the most common of five frequency bands) in queue order: *wind, window, wine, wing, winner, winter, wire, wisdom, wish, witness, woman,* and *wood*. Each went through the full checking pipeline and is now marked `reviewed`, so it can appear on the public site.

Some highlights:

- ***wind***. Three senses: moving air, breath (*knock the wind out of someone*), and gas in the stomach (marked British; *gas* is the American word). Four idioms: *get wind of something*, *a second wind*, *take the wind out of someone's sails*, *like the wind*. The notes warn that the noun rhymes with *pinned* and the verb (*wind a clock*) with *find*.
- ***window***. The opening in a wall, with the glass as a subsense (*break a window*); a store window (*window shopping*); a window on a computer screen; and a short time when something is possible (*a window of opportunity*).
- ***wing***. Six senses: of a bird, a plane, a building, a political party, a sports field, and *the wings* of a stage. Three idioms, including *take someone under your wing*.
- ***wood***. The material and a small forest (*a walk in the woods*). Its idioms include the American *knock on wood* and the British *touch wood*, each with its region label.
- ***woman***: notes on the plural *women* (*WIM-in*), and learner errors matching *man*.

## What the checks found

**Pronunciation and inflections.** All 24 transcriptions were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, and the American ones also by the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. All plurals were confirmed, *women* from the project's table of irregular forms.

**Vocabulary check.** The check found five words outside the defining vocabulary (the plain words that definitions are meant to use): *independent*, *luck* (three times), and *romantic*. I rewrote those definitions before the panel saw them.

**The two reviewers** raised 29 issues, 13 of them blocking. I accepted 18 and rejected 11.

*Accepted:*
- *wind* sense 2 had been defined narrowly as breath for running; it is now "breath, or the ability to breathe easily" during hard exercise. One unnatural example was replaced.
- *wine* had a subsense saying wine can be made "from other fruits or plants". That was too wide: *rice wine* is a fixed name, and plants in general are not used. The subsense now says "other fruits, or rice", and its example is *apple wine*. The note against *a cup of wine* is now softer, because wine is sometimes served in cups.
- *woman* listed *female* as a member of its word family. Both reviewers said *female* is a separate word, not a form of *woman*. It is now under "see also".
- Smaller fixes: the hyphenated adjectives *left-wing* and *right-wing* in *wing*; better examples in *winner* and *wire*; *a working woman* in place of *a business woman*; *hard wood* and *soft wood* dropped from the collocations (the usual forms are written as one word); and the *witness stand* and *witness box* note moved from an explanation into the usage note.

*Rejected:*
- Both reviewers wanted *wine* defined with the word *grape*. The house rule allows a word outside the defining vocabulary only when it has its own entry, and *grape* does not have one yet. The definition keeps "a small round fruit that grows in bunches". `NEXT.md` asks for *grape* to be written and the definition updated.
- Reviewer-a said the breath sense of *wind* is British. *Knock the wind out of someone* is everyday American English, so it has no label.
- Reviewer-a wanted *bear witness to something* moved to the verb *bear*. The house rule files an idiom under its first noun, which is *witness*.
- Four minor objections did not hold up.

**Back-links.** One back-link was added: *gas* sense 4 (gas in the stomach) now names *wind*. I checked it, and it is on the right sense.
