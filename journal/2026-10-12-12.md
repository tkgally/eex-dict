# 2026-10-12, run at 08:44 UTC, cycle 4: originality check, one rewrite

## What this cycle did

This cycle was an **originality check**: a test of whether any of the dictionary's definitions copy a published dictionary. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) forces one every tenth run, and this was run 400. It was the run's fourth and last cycle, because a run stops at four cycles.

The tool `tools/originality_check.py` picked ten fields at random from the published entries. Seven were sense definitions and three were definitions of idioms (fixed phrases kept under a keyword entry):

- `thumb-n`, the phrase *thumbs up*
- `aim-v` sense 3, *make or plan something so that it is suited to a particular group of people*
- `try-v` sense 1, *make an effort to do something*
- `lung-n` sense 1
- `negative-adj` sense 3 (*a negative answer*)
- `loss-n`, the phrase *your loss*
- `damage-v` sense 1
- `back-n` sense 4, the back of a chair
- `do-v` sense 5, *be enough or right for a purpose* (*That will do*)
- `tip-n`, the idiom *on the tip of your tongue*

For each I ran an exact-phrase web search, the whole definition in quotation marks, and looked only at whether a published dictionary showed the same wording. I copied nothing from any result.

## What the searches found

Six fields had no match: *thumbs up*, *negative*, *your loss*, *damage*, the back of a chair, and *on the tip of your tongue*. They are marked **original**.

Three came out as **generic overlaps**. That is the verdict for a short, plain definition that any dictionary would write in much the same way, which the style guide says is not copying:

- *try*, "make an effort to do something": this is a stock gloss on phrase-learning sites.
- *aim* sense 3: a learner's dictionary uses "suitable for a particular group of people" for the word *geared*. Only that ordinary phrase is shared; the rest of our wording is different.
- *do* sense 5: dictionaries define *sufficient* as "enough for a particular purpose". The words are plain, and there is little other way to say it.

One field needed a **rewrite**. `lung-n` sense 1 read "one of the two organs in your chest that fill with air when you breathe". A well-known learner's dictionary describes the lungs as the two organs inside your chest that fill with air when you breathe in. Ours was not word for word the same, but it followed that dictionary's frame closely. Earlier checks rewrote *egg*, *weekend*, and *confuse* on the same grounds. The definition now reads "one of a pair of soft body parts behind the ribs that you use to take in air". It uses only words from the defining vocabulary (the list of words definitions are written with). I sent the entry back to both reviewers, and neither found a problem.

## The reviewer's answers

The checklist also asks one reviewer model a fixed question: does each field read as copied? I asked reviewer-a, with its hidden reasoning turned off, as the notes recommend. It answered *copied* for all ten. It named the same learner's dictionary each time and "quoted" that dictionary's wording, which was simply our own text repeated back. This is the third check in a row with that pattern. By chance the dictionary it named is the one the *lung* search found, but its quotation of that dictionary was our sentence, not theirs. So its answers again carry no weight, and the searches decide. I added a paragraph to `wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`.
