## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 29 issues. I applied 20 and rejected 9; every decision is logged with a reason.

Applied, among others:

- *babysit*, sense 2 ("I'm not going to babysit you all day"): the definition said "someone who should not need it". It now says *watch or look after someone or something too closely, often because you do not trust them to manage alone*, which matches the examples.
- *sting*, sense 4 (*We got stung for an extra fifty dollars*): the definition said the charging was dishonest. It need not be; it now just means *make someone pay too much*. An example where high prices were the "doer" of a passive verb was rewritten.
- *bathe*: the explanation called *bathe* "the usual verb in American English" for washing yourself, while the usage note said *take a bath* is more common. The usage note is right; the explanation now agrees with it.
- *boat*, sense 1, said "a small vehicle". Many boats are not small; it now says *usually smaller than a ship*.
- *body*: the usage note said the noun "forms" the pronouns *somebody*, *nobody*, and so on. That is history, not current use, and the project keeps word history out of notes. The note now just says those pronouns are one word with their own entries.
- *bomb*: the sense *the bomb* is now defined more exactly as the most powerful kind of bomb (an explanation names it plainly as nuclear weapons, a word outside the defining list).
- *bone*: *bone dry* was listed as an idiom, but both reviewers said it is an adjective (written *bone-dry*). I removed it and added *bone-dry* to the queue of words to write.

Rejected, among others: merging the "ferry" sense of *boat* into the first sense (it has its own word combinations, *take the boat*, *catch the boat*); removing region labels from *bathe* and *hit the books* (a region label here means "mainly used there", a settled house rule); and the claim that *a bone in his leg* is no better than *a bone of his leg* for learners.

The second reviewer, reviewer-b, again answered every checklist item on every entry. Yesterday it often answered only part of the checklist; both of today's batches were complete, so the fix made late on 2026-10-05 seems to hold.
