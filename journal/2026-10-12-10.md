## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle. A review sends existing entries back to the two outside reviewer models (reviewer-a and reviewer-b) for a second reading, to catch what the first reading missed. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose it after this run's lint pass. No drafts were waiting, so I took the twelve oldest entries that had been through the panel only once. They are all nouns written on 4 October: *hill*, *hit*, *hobby*, *hole*, *holiday*, *home*, *honey*, *hope*, *horn*, *horse*, *hospital*, and *host*. About 910 entries still have had only one reading.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 43 issues, 31 of them marked blocking. I applied 18 and rejected 25; none needed escalating to the owner. All of them came from reviewer-a. reviewer-b answered every field and found nothing. *holiday* and *hospital* came back clean.

Applied:
- *hobby*: the learner-error box (a note on a mistake learners often make) called *My hobby is to read books* wrong. It is unusual but grammatical, so it was not a fair "incorrect" example. The box now shows a real error, *My hobby is read books* (the bare verb), corrected to *My hobby is reading books*, and its note says *to read* is possible but much less common.
- *hit*: the baseball use (*She got two hits in the game*) had "in some ball games" at the front of its definition. The house style puts that kind of restriction in the explanation, so the definition is now plain and the explanation names baseball and softball. The internet sense said a hit is "one visit to a website". Strictly, one visit can produce several hits, so it now says "one time that someone looks at a page on a website". The core idea (the one-sentence summary of what all the senses share) no longer calls a hit "a moment". *get a hit* was taken out of the collocations of the "blow" sense, where it does not belong.
- *home*: sense 2 (*two hundred new homes*, *home prices*) was defined as a house "thought of as a building that people buy and sell". That is too narrow, since homes are also rented. It now reads "thought of as a building rather than as the place where someone lives". A translator's note that described *go home* now only points to the adverb entry, which is where that use belongs.
- Smaller fixes: *in the hole* now means having lost money or owing money (not "owing more than you have"); the *host* core idea now includes organizations, which sense 3 already covers; the *horn* learner-error note is now in the active voice (*you sound, honk, or blow a horn*); a British-sounding example in *hope* (*They haven't got a hope*) became *They don't have a hope*; and *the hills* explanation now reads "means" instead of "is".

Rejected:
- Splitting the *horse* gymnastics sense into the vaulting horse and the pommel horse. The definition already covers both: people "jump over" one and "move on using their hands" on the other.
- Moving *hope against hope* out of the noun entry because its examples use the verb. Under the house keyword rule, an idiom goes under its first noun, and that is *hope*.
- Removing the American label from *blow your own horn*. British English says *blow your own trumpet*, and a region label means "mainly used there".
- Hyphenating *a hole in one*. The open spelling is standard for the noun.
- Several complaints that a short explanation such as "Used in golf." repeats the domain label. The label says only *sport*, so the explanation adds which sport.
