## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: new entries written from the queue of words the dictionary still needs. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose it because building was again furthest behind its share of the work. The queue continued the band 1 adverbs (band 1 is the most common words) after *certainly*, which cycle 1 of this run had reached. This cycle took the next twelve: *clear*, *clearly*, *closely*, *commonly*, *completely*, *correctly*, *daily*, *deeply*, *definitely*, *differently*, *directly*, and *down*.

Most of these are the *-ly* adverbs of adjectives the dictionary already has, so each one says in its usage note how it differs from the adjective or from a plain adverb of the same form. Some points worth knowing:

- *clear* (adverb) covers *stand clear of the doors*, the American informal *clear across the field*, and *five points clear* in sport, and points to *clearly* for *speak clearly*.
- *closely* and *close*: *come close*, but *watch closely* and *closely related*.
- *deeply* and *deep*: *dig deep*, but *deeply sorry* and *breathe deeply*.
- *directly* has five senses, including the British *I'll be with you directly* (soon).
- *down* has six senses, from *sit down* and *prices are down* to *write it down* and the payment sense (*nothing down*), and the idiom *down and out*.
- Two common spelling errors are recorded as learner errors: *definately* and *dayly*.

## Pronunciation and word forms

All 24 transcriptions were confirmed by the pronunciation check the first time. *differently* and *directly* also record their other common pronunciations (three syllables for *differently*; *dye-REKT-lee* for *directly*). Seven of the twelve form their comparative with *more*; the other five have none.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two reviewer models from other companies read every field of every entry. They raised 56 issues, 45 of them blocking (the entry cannot be published until each is settled). I accepted 36 and rejected 20. None needed the owner. The second reviewer's reply on *definitely* came back broken (it could not be read), so I ran that one review again; the second reply was complete and found nothing.

Accepted, among others:

- *deeply* was the most changed. Its third sense mixed *cut deeply into her hand*, *think deeply*, and *deeply rooted*. Now *deeply rooted* sits with *deeply sorry* in sense 1 (to a great degree), sense 3 is only physical (*far into something*), and a new sense 4 holds *think deeply*. The definition of sense 1 lost a semicolon list.
- *closely* told learners to use *close*, not *closely*, for being near, while its own sense 2 shows *walked closely behind her father*. The note now limits *close* to verbs like *come*, *stand*, and *sit*.
- *completely* had a learner error (*I completely don't understand*) that the reviewer showed can be natural with stress. I removed it; the usage note still explains *not completely* and *not at all*.
- *commonly* had been defined as *often*; it now means *by many people, in many places, or in many cases*, and *often* is no longer listed as a synonym.
- *down* sense 5 (*drove down to the coast*) said *toward the south*; the word *south* has no entry yet, so the dictionary's own check stopped it. It now says *lower on a map*. The American label on the payment sense was removed, since *money down* is used in Britain too.
- Smaller points: *daily* and *commonly* lost over-absolute rules about word order; *differently than* is now described as common in American English; *directly*'s *soon* sense lost an *old-fashioned* label.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- The first reviewer said five definitions in *directly* must be verb phrases. The style guide says an adverb is defined by an adverb phrase.
- The second reviewer said four times that *comparative with more* is not a valid grammar code. It is in the closed list of codes; this objection has been rejected before.
- The American label on *clear across the field* stays: British English usually says *right across*, and a region label means *mainly used there*.

The cross-reference tool added four word-family back-links (on *clear* adjective, *close* verb, *correct* verb, and *down* preposition), all correct.
