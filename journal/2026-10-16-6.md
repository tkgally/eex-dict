# 2026-10-16, cycle 2 of the 12:44 UTC run: the seventy-fifth lint pass

## What this cycle did

This was a **lint** cycle: a periodic health check of the whole dictionary and of the project's knowledge base (the wiki), rather than new or revised entries. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) forced it, because five cycles had passed since the last lint pass. A lint pass costs nothing: it makes no calls to the paid outside models.

It has two halves. The mechanical half runs the project's checking tools over everything. The judgmental half reads the wiki's catalog, log, and open questions to make sure they agree with each other and with the files.

## The mechanical checks

- **Size caps** (`tools/check_caps.py`): every capped file is within its limit.
- **Links** (`tools/check_links.py`): every link between the project's pages works.
- **Cross-references** (`tools/crossref.py --all --apply`): when entry A points to entry B as a related word, B should point back to A. The tool found one missing back-link and added it: *near* (the preposition) names *nearly* in its word-family box, so *nearly* now names *near* (the preposition) in return. That is right; the word-family box belongs to the whole entry, not to one sense, so there was no risk of the link landing on the wrong sense, which has happened with other kinds of back-link before. The tool also reported, as it has for several passes, that *lonely* calls *alone* a word to compare while *alone* calls *lonely* a synonym. That stays: the two entries are talking about different senses of *alone*, and both are correct.
- **Vocabulary** (`tools/lint_vocab.py --all --queue`): definitions are meant to use a fixed list of plain defining words, or words that have their own entry. The check queued no new words.
- **Claims** (`tools/claim.py --prune`): two old claim files (records that a cycle had reserved some headwords) from the morning's build cycles were removed, since those words are finished.

## Reviewer precision

The lint pass measures, for each reviewer and each kind of issue, how often its objections turned out to be right (the share of its issues that I or earlier sessions *applied* rather than *rejected*). A kind of issue that falls below 30 percent over at least twenty decisions is switched off for that reviewer, because it costs more reading than it saves. Nothing new crossed that line. reviewer-a is right about 72 percent of the time overall, reviewer-b about 53 percent. reviewer-b's spelling-and-format objections are below the line (28 percent) but stay switched on by the owner's ruling of 2026-10-05. Its objections about idioms and phrases, at 31 percent, are the closest to the line. In the five cycles since the last lint pass, 6 of reviewer-b's 16 objections were applied. The figures are in `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md`.
