# Run of 2026-11-21

## Cycle 1: lint

**What the cycle was.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose *lint*, because ten cycles had passed since the last one. A lint cycle builds no new entries. It tidies the dictionary as a whole: it checks word caps and links, completes cross-references, refreshes the queue of words still to write, and reads the wiki (the project's own rule book and notes) for contradictions. This was the hundred-and-seventeenth such pass. Before it, because a week had passed, the run wrote the weekly digest for you: `journal/digest-2026-11-21.md`.

**Housekeeping first.** No pull requests were left open by earlier runs. Three old working branches still exist on GitHub; all three are already fully merged into `main` and were already listed in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` (the list of things for you), since the tools available to a run cannot delete branches. No entry reports (problem reports filed from the site's "Report a problem" link) were open, and `inbox/` was empty.

**Cross-references.** When entry A names entry B as a synonym, an antonym, a word to compare, or a member of its word family, B should name A back. A script adds these mirror links when it can tell which sense of B to attach them to. This time it added them in five entries: *stripe* now lists *band* as a synonym, *subway* lists *way* in its word family, *suitcase* lists *bag* and *case*, *sunlight* lists *sun*, and *sweatshirt* lists *hoodie*. In four more pairs the script could not tell which sense was meant, so I placed them by hand after reading both entries: *battle* goes on the first sense of *struggle* (a long, hard effort), *instruction* on the first sense of *teaching* (the work of helping people learn), *reach* on the fifth sense of *stretch* (something hard to believe, as in *that's a bit of a stretch*), and *victim* as an antonym on the first sense of *survivor*. Nine entries changed in all; no definition, example, or other written content was touched.

**The queue.** The vocabulary check found no new words to queue. Seven claim files (records of which words a past cycle had reserved) were spent and removed. Twenty-seven new queue rows had no frequency band yet. Bands run from 1 (most frequent) to 5, and they come only from a vote of three outside models, so the vote was run, at a cost of half a US cent. Two words had votes more than one band apart, which a session must settle by judgment: *dress down* (votes 4, 4, 2) and the verb *effect* (votes 3, 4, 2, as in *effect change*). I put both in band 4, because both are uncommon compared with their related words. All 27 rows are now ready to build.

**Reports that a review cycle will use.** About 1,052 cross-reference pairs still need a person to check which sense they belong to; 317 definitions use their own headword, which is usually harmless (*water a plant*: *pour water on plants*); and the same six notes mention words the dictionary does not have yet. None of these numbers moved much from the last pass.

**The reviewers.** Each entry is checked by two reviewer models, and every issue they raise is decided as applied (they were right) or rejected (they were wrong). Over all decisions so far, reviewer A (a Claude model) has been right 85 percent of the time and reviewer B (a Google model) 84 percent. The rule is that any kind of issue where a reviewer is right less than 30 percent of the time, over at least twenty decisions, is switched off for that reviewer. No new kind fell under the line; the three already off stay off. Reviewer B's spelling-and-format flags (39 percent) stay on by your earlier ruling.

**The wiki.** Every page is listed in the index and every index line points to a real page; every cycle since the last lint has a log entry; no open question is stale; the curator list has no duplicates.

**Spend.** US$0.005 (the band vote). The day's cap is US$15.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed.
