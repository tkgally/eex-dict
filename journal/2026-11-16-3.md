# Journal, 2026-11-16 (third run)

## Cycle 1: originality

**What the cycle did.** The selector, the tool that picks each cycle's kind of work, chose an originality check. This was not a judgment call: the project's rules force one every twentieth run, and this was run 820. An originality check takes a random sample of ten definitions or notes already published in the dictionary and asks whether any of them reads as if it had been copied from a published dictionary. The dictionary is meant to be written from scratch, so a match would mean rewriting that field.

Before starting, the opening checks found nothing waiting: no open pull requests left by an earlier run, no reports from readers about particular entries (these arrive as GitHub issues titled "Entry report"), and nothing in the owner's inbox folder. One old working branch, `claude/zealous-mayer-8da3yb`, still exists on GitHub, but it has no unmerged work and was already listed in the curator file for deletion, which the automated runs cannot do themselves.

**The sample.** The ten sampled fields were:

- **sun** (noun), the sense *any star, especially one with planets moving around it*;
- **yourselves** (pronoun), the phrase *see for yourselves*: *directly, by your own experience, and not because other people tell you*;
- **pair** (noun), *two people who are together or who do something together*;
- **help** (interjection), *used to shout to people that you are in danger and need someone to come and save you*;
- **offer** (noun), *a price lower than usual, or something more that you get free when you buy something*;
- **some** (determiner), the ironic sense (*Some friend you are!*);
- **doubt** (noun), the phrase *in doubt*: *not certain, so that it may not happen or may not be true*;
- **bald** (adjective), *said or written plainly, without any extra detail or explanation*;
- **he** (pronoun), the usage note on the old generic *he* and the modern neutral *they*;
- **pull** (noun), *an act of drinking from a bottle or of taking in smoke from a cigarette or a pipe*.

**How each was checked.** For each field I ran a web search for the exact wording in quotation marks. What counts is only whether a published dictionary or word list shows the same words; nothing found is stored in the repository except the verdict. Separately, one of the two reviewer models (reviewer B, from a different company than the model writing the entries) was asked once, for all ten together, whether each reads as copied or original, and told it may answer "copied" only if it can quote the matching published wording.

**What was found.** None of the ten searches found the same wording in any dictionary. The nearest results were different wordings for neighbouring words (a published definition of *couple* near the **pair** sense, a glossary definition of *possibility* near *in doubt*, published definitions of *plain* and *plainly* near the **bald** sense) and, for the rest, encyclopedia or advice pages on the topic. Reviewer B called all ten original and named no source. All ten verdicts are therefore "original"; there were no "generic overlap" verdicts (short plain definitions any dictionary would word the same way) and no rewrites. This is a cleaner result than the previous check on 2026-11-14, which found two definitions (**sea** and **figure**) close enough to published wording that they were rewritten. The record is in `reviews/originality/2026-11-16.md`.

**Spend.** One paid call to reviewer B, about US$0.004. The day's total is about US$1.63 of the US$15 daily budget.

**What did not work.** Nothing went wrong in this cycle.

**What is next.** The run continues with further cycles while the run clock allows, each picked by the selector; the build queue, the coverage review of the most important entries, and the "closure" work (writing entries for words the dictionary already uses in its own definitions) all remain open as described in `NEXT.md`. The next routine maintenance pass (lint) is due in three cycles, and the next weekly digest on 2026-11-21.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new from this cycle. The standing items remain: the pronunciation disputes in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`, the ten-entry samples in the weekly digests, and the old branch that only a person can delete.

## Cycle 2: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose a build cycle: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. It allowed up to twelve, and all twelve claimed words were written, all nouns: **rescue**, **reservation**, **reserve**, **resident**, **restroom**, **résumé**, **revolution**, **reward**, **rival**, **romance**, **roommate**, and **row**. Each went through the full pipeline (schema check, generated plurals, a pronunciation vote, two reviewers, my decisions on what they raised, a vocabulary check, and cross-reference checks) and is now published as reviewed.

Some choices worth knowing about. **Résumé** is written with its accents as the headword, with *resume* and *resumé* listed as spellings; the entry warns that it is said *REZ-uh-may* and is a different word from the verb **resume**, and that in several languages a similar word means only "summary". **Row** covers only the line of things (seats, a line in a table, and the phrase *in a row*, "one after another"). The British word **row** meaning a noisy argument, which rhymes with *cow*, is a different word with its own history, so it needs a separate entry; one older entry, **argument**, already lists **row** as a synonym and should point to that second entry once it exists. This is noted in `NEXT.md`. **Romance** carries a warning for translators that in several languages a similar word means any novel.

**Pronunciation.** Each entry gives American and British forms, checked by a vote among other models and, for American English, the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. All 24 transcriptions were verified on the first pass.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewer models raised 35 issues, none of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before publishing). I applied 29 and rejected 6, each with a logged reason. The main changes:

- **reserve**: the military sense was defined as soldiers, but its examples used *the army reserve* as an organization. It is now defined as the force, and a confusing grammar note was removed. The opening summary of the entry's senses (the "core idea") now covers protected land too.
- **rescue**: a reviewer pointed out that *a rescue* is also a group or place that takes in animals and finds them homes (*she volunteers at a dog rescue*). That is now a sub-sense.
- **romance**: the "love story" sense can be uncountable when it names the kind of book (*she mostly reads romance*); the grammar and examples now show this.
- Several collocations were corrected or replaced, and the culture note on **reservation** was cut down so that it only says what a translator would get wrong.

The rejections: I kept a valid grammar code ("often before another noun") on **rescue**; I did not label the sports sense of **reserve** British, because *reserves* for the players on the bench is common in American sports reporting; I kept *pay a reward*; and I did not add *death row* to **row**, because the project's rule files an idiom under its first noun, here **death**.

Three definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary, the list of about 2,400 simple words that definitions must use: *native*, *Canada*, and *stadium*. They were rewritten (for example, the land sense of **reservation** is now "kept for one of the first peoples of North America"). Three older entries, **bathroom**, **toilet**, and **punishment**, received back-links to the new entries, placed by hand on the right sense.

**Spend.** About US$0.35 for the reviewers and pronunciation checks. The day's total is about US$1.98 of the US$15 daily budget.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. The first drafts had more noun-modifier collocations and a few loose ones (*look at a résumé*, *share a room with a roommate*) than they needed; the reviewers caught them.

**What is next.** Another cycle if the clock allows. The second **row** entry is queued for hand-claiming.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new from this cycle.
