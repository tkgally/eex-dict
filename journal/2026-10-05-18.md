## What this cycle did

The scheduler chose a build cycle. A build cycle writes new entries from the queue of words still to do, starting with band 1, the most common words. The cycle can write up to twelve entries.

The claim tool (which marks words as taken so that other runs leave them alone) picked the auxiliary verbs *do* and *have* first. The owner's rulings of 2026-10-05 say these should be split out of the verb entries one at a time, in review cycles, so I put them back in the queue as `NEXT.md` asks. Ten nouns were left:

*message, metal, method, middle, midnight, milk, mind, minister, minute, mirror*.

I stopped at ten rather than twelve because two of the words are large. *Mind* has four senses (thinking, attention, opinion or plan, a clever person) and fifteen idioms, among them *make up your mind*, *keep something in mind*, *slip your mind*, *speak your mind*, and *be of two minds* (British *in two minds*). *Minute* has four senses (sixty seconds, a short time, an exact moment, and *the minutes* of a meeting) and six idioms.

Each entry has its senses with examples and collocations (the word combinations learners need), idioms where the word has them, and learner errors where learners really make them. Some examples: *a five minutes walk* for *a five-minute walk*, *at the middle of the room* for *in the middle*, *I decided my mind* for *I made up my mind*, and *look at the mirror* when you mean *look in the mirror*. Usage notes cover *metal* before a noun versus *metallic*, the confusion over *midnight on Friday*, and the fact that the United States says *secretary* where many countries say *minister*. Synonym notes explain *method* / *way* / *technique*, *middle* / *center*, and *mind* / *brain*.

Every idiom went under its key word. None of the idioms already in the dictionary had to move: *at the back of your mind*, *a load off your mind*, and *frame of mind* all have an earlier noun, so they stay where they are.

## Checks and reviewers

All twenty transcriptions (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel and by the open CMU pronouncing data. All plurals came out right; *midnight* and *milk* have none.

Two reviewer models from other companies read every field of every entry. Reviewer B had trouble again: on six of the ten entries it returned only part of its answer, or none at all. I ran both reviewers again on those six, as the working note says to do. That fixed *midnight*, *milk*, and, on a third try, *middle*. *Message*, *minister*, and *minute* are still partial: reviewer B listed its problems but gave no verdict on the other fields. This has now happened in three cycles today, so the fix to the review tool is overdue. I added this to the note (`wiki/notes/reviewer-b-partial-verdicts.md`).

There were 20 decisions: 15 applied and 5 rejected. None needed the owner. The main ones:

- *Minute*: a reviewer pointed out that *the minute (that)* before a clause (*Call me the minute you get home*) works like a conjunction meaning *as soon as*, not like the noun sense "a particular point in time". I moved it into its own phrase entry inside *minute*, and the sense now has plain noun examples (*At that minute, the door opened*, *Come inside this minute!*).
- *Middle*: my note said *center* is "never used for time" and is the point "the same distance from every edge". Both were too absolute, so I softened them.
- *Mind*: *come to mind* now means "come into your thoughts", not "be the first thing you think of". The *of two minds* example now uses *about whether*.
- *Milk*: I made the main idea name human milk as well as animal milk. Phrases like *a glass of milk* moved to the phrase type of collocation.
- Rejected: an "informal" label on *burn the midnight oil* (it is common in ordinary writing), and the objection to *reflection* as a synonym of *a mirror of society* (*a reflection of society* means the same).

The vocabulary check found seven definition words outside the defining vocabulary (the plain words definitions are written in), such as *halfway*, *crazy*, *intelligent*, and *guitars*. I rewrote those definitions in plainer words.

The cross-reference tool added back-links on three other entries. One was on the wrong sense: *middle* went on the clothing sense of *waist*, and I moved it to the body sense. *Center* already listed *middle* as a synonym while *middle* listed *center* under "compare", so I made them synonyms on both sides.
