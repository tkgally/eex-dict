## What this cycle did

A **build cycle** writes new entries from the queue (the list of words still to be written, ordered by how common they are). The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose a build and allowed twelve. But only US$0.79 of the day's US$15 budget was left, and twelve new adjectives cost about US$0.90 to check. So I took eight and wrote each one in full: *legal*, *level*, *light*, *likely*, *little*, *living*, *local*, and *lonely*. All eight are among the most common words (band 1).

Some points about the entries:

- *light* has seven senses (not heavy, bright, pale in color, small in amount, food, not serious, easy work). It also has a short note on choosing between *light*, *pale*, and *bright* for colors.
- *likely* explains its two patterns (*it is likely that…* and *she is likely to…*). It has a note on *likely*, *probable*, and *possible*, since learners often write *she is probable to win*.
- *little* separates the adjective (*a little house*) from the determiner (*little time*), which already has its own entry. It also explains that *little* usually adds a feeling of liking or of being sorry for someone, where *small* does not.
- *living*, *lonely*, and *local* each warn about a common confusion: *alive* against *living*, *alone* against *lonely*, and *local* used as a noun for a room or a shop, as it is in some other languages.

**A fix to the inflection table.** Inflections (comparative and superlative forms) are written by a script from rules plus a table of exceptions (`schema/inflection-exceptions.json`). That table gave the adjective *little* the forms *less* and *least*. Those belong to *little* meaning *not much* (*less time*). Nobody says *a less house*. The line now gives *littler* and *littlest*, with a note that these are informal and that people usually say *smaller* and *smallest*.

All sixteen pronunciations were confirmed on the first pass.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewer models raised 39 issues, 35 of them blocking (must be settled before publication). *Lonely* came back clean. I applied 25 and rejected 14; none went to the owner.

Applied, among others:

- *legal*: my notes said that before a noun *legal* means *connected with the law*, not *allowed*. That is wrong for *legal drugs* or *legal parking*. The usage note and the translator's note now say that both meanings occur before a noun and that the noun shows which.
- *light*: the food sense now reads *not making you feel too full, or containing less fat, sugar, or alcohol than usual*. The color note no longer treats *bright* as the opposite of *light*.
- *little*: sense 2 now reads *young, especially still a child*. The note on comparison no longer suggests that *smaller* is a form of *little*.
- *living*: I had marked it *only before a noun*, but *is your grandfather still living?* is normal, so the code is gone and the notes say *usually*.
- *local*: a learner error claiming *local foods* is wrong was removed, since *foods* is fine for kinds of food.

Rejected:

- reviewer-b twice called *comparative with more* an invalid grammar code. It is in the dictionary's closed list.
- reviewer-a objected seven times to sense 3 of *little* (*short in time or distance*: *a little while*, *a little way*). These are ordinary English, so the sense stays.
- reviewer-b wanted *little by little* moved out of the adjective entry. The house rule keeps idioms under their keyword entry whatever their part of speech.

## Cross-references

When entry A lists B as a synonym or opposite, a script adds the return link on B. I checked all ten new return links by hand. One was on the wrong sense: *equal* got *level* on its *same rights* sense, so I moved it to *the same in amount or value*. The script also reported two pairs where one entry said "synonym" or "opposite" and the other said "compare". I fixed *light*/*bright* and *local*/*central* (*local government* against *central government* is now an opposite on both sides). A third, *alone*/*lonely*, is correct as it stands: *alone* has one sense that compares with *lonely* and another that means the same.
