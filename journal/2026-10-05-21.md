## What this cycle did

The scheduler chose **review**: a second reading of entries that the two outside reviewer models (the "panel") have seen only once. I did two things.

**1. The owner's ruling on helping verbs, last part.** On 2026-10-05 the owner ruled that *be*, *have*, and *do* used as helping verbs ("auxiliary verbs") should each get their own entry, one per review cycle. *Be* and *have* were done earlier today. This cycle did *do*, so the ruling is now fully carried out.

The new entry `do-aux` has five senses:

- questions and negatives in the simple present and past (*Do you know the way?*, *She doesn't like coffee*), with the rule that the main verb stays in its base form, and that questions whose subject is *who* or *what* do without it (*Who called?*);
- negative orders (*Don't touch that*, *Don't be silly*), the one place where *do* goes with *be*;
- emphasis (*I do like your haircut*, *Do come in*, *I did lock the door!*);
- standing in for a verb already said: short answers, question tags, *so do I*, *than I do*;
- the formal word order after *never*, *rarely*, *not only* at the start of a sentence (*Rarely do we see snow here*), labeled formal.

Its three learner errors are *I no like it*, *Does she likes coffee?*, and *Who did tell you?* Its usage note lists the forms (*do, does, did, don't, doesn't, didn't*) and says that *do* is not used with *be* (except in orders), with modal verbs such as *can*, or with helping *have*.

The main-verb entry `do-v` lost those three helping senses. Because it was being rewritten anyway, I also filled gaps a learner meets early: *What do you do?* (your job) and *doing a course* (study); everyday jobs (*do the dishes*, *do the shopping*, *do your hair*); having an effect (*A few days off will do you good*, *The storm did a lot of damage*); and informal speed or distance (*The car was doing ninety*). It gained a phrase, *you would do well to listen*, and two new learner errors: answering *What do you do?* with what you are doing now, and *I did a mistake* (it is *make a mistake*). Both entries point to each other.

I also checked the number entry *one* for any pronoun use left after `one-pron` was created; there is none.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

- In `do-v`, the old entry already had a sub-sense for household jobs, which my new "clean or arrange" sense duplicated. I merged them into one sense, *everyday jobs*, defined as a job that has to be done often, which now covers shopping and cooking too.
