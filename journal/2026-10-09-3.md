# Journal, 2026-10-09 (run of 00:43 UTC, cycle 3): a second reading of *definition* to *desk*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each cycle's kind of work from what is most overdue) chose review after two builds. A review cycle gives a second reading to entries that the two outside reviewer models have read only once. The oldest such entries go first, because the entries written late in the long runs of late September and early October came out thinner than the rest.

The twelve entries this time were all created on 2026-10-02 between 21:04 and 21:17 UTC: *definition, degree, delay, demand, democracy, department, depth, description, desert, design, desire,* and *desk*. About 760 entries are still at one round of review.

## What I added by hand before the panel

I read each entry whole, looking for missing senses, idioms, learner errors, and notes. The entries were in fair shape: every one had a core idea where needed (a one-sentence summary of what the senses share), full collocations, and at least one learner error. What was missing was mostly small:

- **Pronunciation notes** for five words that had none: *definition*, *degree*, *delay*, *demand*, and *department*. The note on *demand* points out that American English uses the *a* of *hand* and British English the *a* of *father*. The note on *definition* warns that the verb *define* is stressed on a different syllable.
- **A learner error** on `delay-n`. Many learners say *sorry for my delay* when they mean *sorry I'm late*.
- **A new idiom** on `desk-n`: *clear your desk*, meaning to take your things away because you are leaving your job.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 15 issues: 14 from reviewer-a and 1 from reviewer-b. I applied 11, rejected 4, and escalated none.

On `democracy-n`, reviewer-b's first reply was cut off and could not be read as data. I re-ran that reviewer alone on that entry, and it came back complete with no issues.

The main changes:

- **delay.** The entry's notes said that *delay* is used *mainly for trains, flights, plans, and replies*. Reviewer-a showed that this conflicts with the entry's own examples about roads and bad weather, and it was right. The usage note now covers traffic, deliveries, and other processes. The adaptation note (the short warning for future translators) now says *events, journeys, and processes*. The new learner error became a clearer case: *I'm sorry for my delay to the meeting* is corrected to *I'm sorry I'm late for the meeting*. Reviewer-b noted that the *late* point appeared three times, so it was taken out of the usage note.
- **demand.** The usage note said *demand* is uncountable but often takes *a*. It now gives the rule for ordinary use first and names the exception after it: *a* is used when an adjective describes the level of demand (*a growing demand for cars*). The style guide asks for exactly this pattern.
- **degree.** The note on Fahrenheit now says *the United States and a few other places*, not only the United States.
- **definition.** The collocation *clear definition* was replaced by *poor definition*, because *clear definition* reads too easily as the meaning of a word.
- **desert.** The phrase *a desert island* no longer says such islands are small and warm.

The rejections:

- Reviewer-a said *a desert island* is not a fixed phrase. It is one: the island is not a desert in the dry-land sense; *desert* here means that nobody lives there.
- Reviewer-a said `department-n` should not list `school-n` as a synonym. The link mirrors `school-n` sense 5 (*part of a university*). The two entries must agree, so it stays, now with a short note explaining it.
- The learner errors on *description* and *desire* were already worded as *usually*, so they stand.
