## What this run did

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's job from what is most overdue) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue of words the dictionary still needs. The queue was at the last of the high-frequency suffixes, the word endings that make new words, such as *-less* in *careless*. Twelve entries were written:

- **-less** (*homeless*, *careless*; and a small group made from verbs, *countless*, *tireless*)
- **-like** (*childlike*, *lifelike*), with a comparison to *-ish* (*childish* criticizes; *childlike* does not)
- **-ly** in three uses: adverbs (*quickly*), adjectives (*friendly*), and how often (*weekly*)
- **-ment** (*agreement*, *payment*)
- **-ous** (*dangerous*, *famous*), with its spellings *-ious* and *-eous*
- **-s** for plurals (*cats*) and for verbs after *he*, *she*, *it* (*she works*), with its three sounds explained in plain words
- **-ship** (*friendship*, *leadership*; and skill, *workmanship*)
- **-tion** (*description*, *reduction*), pointing to *-ation* and *-sion*
- **-ward** (*backward*, *upward*), with the British form *-wards*
- **-wise** (*clockwise*; and the informal *money-wise*)
- **-y** (*rainy*, *sleepy*; and the friendly form in *doggy*, *Mommy*)
- **a.m.** (*9 a.m.*), with notes on not using it with *o'clock* and on why *noon* is clearer than *12 a.m.*

Each entry went through the full checking pipeline: the format check, the inflection script (suffixes have no inflections), the pronunciation check, and the two outside reviewer models. All 22 pronunciations (American and British for every entry except *-s*, which has no single sound) were confirmed by the three-model pronunciation panel; *a.m.* also matched the open CMU pronouncing dictionary.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers raised 43 points, 21 of which I accepted and 22 rejected, each with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Accepted changes that mattered:

- **-less**: the reviewer was right that *endless* and *ageless* are made from nouns, not verbs, so they did not fit the second sense ("made from a verb: cannot be done, or never stops"). *Endless* moved to the first sense, and the second now lists only *countless*, *tireless*, *ceaseless*.
- **-s**: the usage note said "a plural never takes an apostrophe", which is false for plural possessives (*the students' books*). It now says a plural *on its own* never does.
- **-tion**: the spelling note mixed up *-sion* and *-ion*. It now says verbs in *-d* or *-de* often take *-sion* (*decide*, *decision*) and verbs in *-ss* add *-ion* (*discuss*, *discussion*).
- Several over-absolute statements were softened (*-ward* adjectives are "usually", not "always", *-ward*; *-y* doubles the final consonant only in short words).

Rejected points: reviewer-b's only blocking objection, repeated six times, was that a suffix definition should not begin "added to a noun to make…". That wording is the house pattern for every affix entry already published, so I rejected it each time. Reviewer-a made several claims that do not hold, such as that *penniless* keeps the *y* of *penny*, that *workmanship* comes from *work* (it comes from *workman*), and that *fatherlily* is an English adverb.
