The first three cycles merged as #166, twelve nouns from *burn* to *cancer*; #167, a lint pass; and #168, twelve nouns from *candy* to *cause*.)

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that have been through only one round of outside review. I took the oldest twelve by creation date, verbs written on 2026-09-25: *fight, fill, find, fire, fit, fix, flash, float, flow, fly, focus,* and *fold*.

Before sending them to the reviewers, I read each one myself. They were already full, with five to seven senses each. I added:

- An eighth sense to *find*: *find yourself* somewhere, "come to be in a place or a situation without planning or expecting to" (*She found herself alone in a strange city*; *I found myself agreeing with him*). It is common, and the entry did not have it.
- A usage note on *find* and *look for*, and a learner error to go with it: *I'm finding my glasses* for *I'm looking for my glasses*. (A learner error is a mistake learners of many languages make, shown with a correction and a one-line reason.)
- The phrase *fire away* ("start asking your questions"), as in *Sure, fire away*.
- A learner error on *fix*: *I will fix it at the shop* when someone else does the repair, for *I will get it fixed*.
- A learner error on *float*: *a boat was flowing on the lake* for *floating*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field of every entry. They raised 51 issues, 32 of them marked blocking. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 30 applied, 21 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied (the main ones):

- ***fill***: *The dentist filled two of my teeth* was an example under a definition that spoke only of holes and cracks. The definition now includes a hole in a tooth. The usage note and the translator's note had said that after *fill* the contents always follow *with*. That is true only when something goes into a container, and *crowds filled the streets* does not fit it. Both notes now say so. The job sense now reads "find a person for an empty job, or be the person chosen to do it".
- ***fire***: the core idea (the one sentence that ties the senses together) said every sense is about "sending something out with sudden force". That fits guns but not jobs. It now explains the job sense on its own terms. Two senses were coded *usually passive* although their examples were active (*The company fired twenty workers*); the code is gone. The usage note no longer calls *dismiss* formal "everywhere".
- ***fit***: *belong* is no longer a synonym of *fit* sense 4, "be right or suitable". Both reviewers flagged it, and I removed the mirror link on *belong* too. The definition no longer says "agree with".
- ***float***: the note that American English says a company *goes public* moved from an explanation into a usage note.
- ***fly***: an informal example, *I must fly*, sat in a sense with no informal label. It is gone. The kite and flag sense now says the kite "moves through the air" and the flag "moves in the wind".
- ***focus***: the eye sense had said "make your eyes see something clearly", but eyes take no object here. It now reads "start to see clearly". The usage note no longer claims *focused* is more common "everywhere".
- Smaller fixes: *flow*'s example *the curtains flowed to the floor* became a flowing dress; *find*'s comparison of *find* and *discover* now allows *discover* for things you learn by surprise.

Rejected (the main ones):

- Writing *fire* with one syllable. A settled house rule (a "fence" in `NEXT.md`) records it as two, verified by the panel and an open pronouncing dictionary.
- Eight objections to translator's notes that open with the headword in quotation marks. This is settled house style.
- Saying that *Please fill this form* is acceptable. It is not standard in American or British English; *fill in* or *fill out* is.
- Removing *take* as a word to compare with *fit*. Learners do confuse them. You *take* a size; clothes in that size *fit* you. A note now says this.
