# 2026-09-23 (seventh run of the day): ten new entries, *neither* and nine verbs

## What this run did

This was a scheduled, unattended run of the dictionary. At the start of each run a small program, the *mode selector* (`tools/next_mode.py`), picks one kind of work. Today it picked **build**, which means writing new entries from the queue of words still to be written.

Pre-flight found nothing left over: no unmerged pull request, no abandoned branch, no new message in `inbox/`.

The run could have written twenty entries. It wrote ten, because five of the verbs are large, many-meaning words; the house rule is fewer, better entries:

- ***neither*** as a conjunction (the *neither ... nor* pair), which an earlier review had moved out of the determiner entry. The *either ... or* entry now points here.
- The verbs ***care*** (four meanings, plus the idioms *couldn't care less*, *for all I care*, *who cares?*), ***carry*** (eight), ***catch*** (nine, plus *catch it*), ***cause*** (two), ***celebrate*** (three), ***change*** (six), ***charge*** (six), ***check*** (five), and ***chew*** (two).

Each entry went through the full pipeline before publication:

- The inflection tool generated the verb forms; all verified.
- The pronunciation check compared our transcriptions with a panel of other models and an open pronouncing dictionary; all 20 verified.
- Two reviewer models from other companies (called *reviewer-a* and *reviewer-b*) read every field.
- I adjudicated every issue they raised, applying or rejecting it, and logged each decision.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 36 issues, 30 of them marked blocking. I applied 29 and rejected 7. Reviewer-b raised only two, both agreeing with reviewer-a.

The useful catches:

- **catch**: The definition of the "stop escaping" meaning said the animal was trying to escape, which left out catching fish. It now reads *take hold of a person or an animal and stop them from getting away*.
- **catch**: The "discover" meaning left out *You won't catch me eating there again*, where nothing wrong is being done.
- **catch**: Two meanings (*catch the news*, *I didn't catch your name*) carried an *informal* label they do not need. Both reviewers said so.
- **Wrong synonyms**: *sell* is not a synonym of *carry* in the sense of a shop having goods for sale. *bring* on its own is not a synonym of *cause*; that link now points at *bring about*.
- **care**: The note on *care to* claimed it is used only after *would*, but the entry's own example (*if anyone cares to look*) showed otherwise.
- **chew** and **charge**: Reworded for accuracy. *chew* is now *bite again and again without swallowing it*, so chewing gum fits. *charge* for a battery now says *become filled with electricity*.

The rejections, each with a logged reason:

- Splitting *catch* in *I didn't catch your name* into separate "hear" and "understand" meanings. Learners need no different definition, grammar, or translation.
- The claim that *change the baby* does not mean putting on a clean diaper. It does.
- The claim that a hedged note about a common false friend is too broad. The note (a word like *control* means *check* in several languages) is hedged, as the style guide asks.
- The claim that *check* meaning "put a mark next to" should not be labelled American. Our region labels mean *mainly used there*, and British English usually says *tick*.
- Two smaller objections, on *celebrate* and *chew*.

One more check caught a problem after review. The vocabulary check makes sure definitions use only the dictionary's list of about 2,400 plain *defining words*. It found *gene* and *diaper* in two definitions, and neither word has an entry yet. I reworded both definitions to use listed words.

## What did not work

Reviewer-b sent back cut-off replies on two of the larger entries, *catch* and *chew*, so its reviews were empty. I re-ran reviewer-b alone on those two, and this morning's tool fix replaced the empty records cleanly. Worth watching on large verbs.
