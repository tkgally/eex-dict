I drafted twelve very common nouns (band 1, the most common of five frequency bands), in queue order: *war, warning, wash, washing machine, waste, watch, wave, weakness, wealth, weapon, weather,* and *web*. Each went through the full checking pipeline and is now marked `reviewed`, which means it can appear on the public site.

## The entries

- *war* has three senses (fighting between countries, a strong competition such as *a price war*, and a long effort against something bad such as *a war on crime*), the phrases *a war of words* and the British *be in the wars*, and a short guide to choosing between *war*, *battle*, *conflict*, and *fight*.
- *waste* has four senses, from *a waste of time* to the literary *frozen wastes*, and the phrases *go to waste*, *a waste of space*, and *waste not, want not*. Its usage note explains when English says *trash*, *garbage*, or *rubbish* instead.
- *watch* includes the American weather sense (*a storm watch*), with a note on how it differs from a *warning*.
- *wave* has five senses (water, the hand movement, a sudden increase such as *a wave of strikes*, energy such as *radio waves*, and hair) and the phrase *make waves*.
- *weather* has the phrases *under the weather*, *in all weathers*, and *make heavy weather of something*.
- Learner errors include *in war* (say *at war*), *a nice weather* and *What a terrible weather!* (it is uncountable), *My watch is late* (say *slow*), *wash machine*, *I found it in the web* (say *on*), and *a weakness to sweet things* (say *for*).

## Pronunciation

All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, three or four models voting, and, for American English, by the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, consulted at run time and not stored.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two reviewer models from other companies read every field. They raised 27 issues, 21 of them marked blocking. I agreed with 20 and fixed the entries myself, and rejected 7 with a logged reason. None needed the owner.

The most useful corrections:

- Six phrase definitions began with *to* (*to cause trouble...*). The house style defines verb phrases in the base form without *to*, so all six were changed.
- *weather*: I had given *the weather* (meaning the forecast on television) its own sense. The reviewer argued it is only a shortened form of *the weather forecast*. I agreed that it is not worth a separate sense, and moved it into the usage note.
- *weakness*: I had defined *a weakness for chocolate* as a liking for something "not good for you". That overstates it; it now means a liking you find hard to say no to.
- *weapon*: the note on *arms* now says it means weapons as a group, mainly soldiers' weapons, and is always plural.
- *watch*: a note for translators had said English uses *watch* only for one worn on the wrist, which forgot pocket watches. Fixed.

Rejected, with reasons: a claim that *old-fashioned* is in the wrong label group (it is in the right one by the project's own list); a claim that *do the wash* is not American (it is ordinary American speech); a request to drop the learner error *wash machine* (it is a common mistake).

One reviewer's first reply on *war* came back without its list of verdicts; I re-ran that one reviewer and the second reply was complete.
