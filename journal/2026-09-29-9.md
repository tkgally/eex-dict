## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 32 issues, 26 of them blocking (a blocking issue must be settled before an entry counts as reviewed). I applied 24 and rejected 8; none needed the owner.

What I accepted:

- Several definitions were too broad or too narrow. *toward* in its feelings sense read "in relation to someone or something" and now reads "directed at someone or something"; its fourth sense now reads "to help pay for or reach something". *under* in *under construction, under control, under pressure* said the force "has not yet ended", which does not fit *under control*; it now reads "being affected, controlled, or dealt with by an action, a process, or a force". *through* in *Monday through Friday* now names the two end points, and its subsense for *through security* and *through a red light* now covers both.
- *until* said a situation continues "and then no longer". That is usual but not always true; the definition, its explanation, and a note comparing it with **by** now say "usually".
- Absolute claims were softened: *that* is "kept more often" in formal writing, not always; **on** can "almost always" replace **upon**; *without no* is wrong "in standard English"; starting a sentence with *So* is fine, though very formal writing often uses *therefore*.
- A collocation I had just added, *just so*, has a different meaning and was removed. A compare link from *through* to *via* was on the wrong sense and moved.

What I rejected:

- The claim that *than* before a pronoun (*taller than me*) should not be kept apart from the conjunction. The house splits these by part of speech, and a separate *than* (preposition) entry is queued.
- The claim that *Are you up?* and *Time's up* are not adjective uses of **up**. Treating them as adjectives is ordinary practice.
- The claim that *such as* can take more than a noun, pronoun, or *-ing* form: the reviewer's example, *such as the following*, is a noun phrase.
- A request for a "spoken" label on the conversational use of *so*: no such label exists, and "informal" would be wrong.
