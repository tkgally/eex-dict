## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 62 points. I accepted 24 and rejected 38; every decision is logged with its reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Accepted changes:

- **square** and **staff**: the opening "core idea" sentence (which says what all the senses share) claimed too much. For *square* it said every sense has the square shape, which is not true of a dull person; for *staff* it said a military staff is only officers. Both are now accurate, and the military sense of *staff* now reads "a group of people, mostly officers, who help a military leader to plan and organize". The usage note now says British English *also* uses *stave* for the music sense, not that it uses only *stave*.
- **star**: the horoscope sense used *read your stars*, which is mainly British. The example and collocations now use *what the stars say* and *written in the stars*.
- **crack**: the joke sense mentioned *crack a smile*, which is a different meaning. By the house rule, an idiom goes under its first noun, so it belongs to *smile* (where it already is). It was removed from *crack* and a "see also" link to *smile* added.
- **statement**: a statement to the police can be spoken as well as written; the definition now says "often written and signed".
- **contest**, **credit**, **crowd**: definitions widened or made clearer (*contest* now includes competing for a position; *credit* now covers results that are not always good; *crowd* "come into or fill someone's mind in large numbers at the same time").
- **contract**: a transitive example was added (*Contract your stomach muscles…*), and a grammar pattern with no example was removed.
- **corner** ("make someone talk to you") lost its informal label; it is ordinary English.
- **famous**: *rich and famous* moved to the right collocation type, and the translator's note was made language-neutral.

Rejected, with reasons:

- Reviewer-a said the ceremony sense of **state** (*a state visit*, *lie in state*) is an adjective, not a noun. *Lie in state* and *in state* are noun uses, so the sense stays.
- Several objections ran against settled house rules: an explanation that names the kind of subject (*Used of a voice*) is where the style guide puts such restrictions; a region label means "mainly used there" (the British label on *credit* "believe", as in *I couldn't credit it*).
- Reviewer-b objected that **crowd** discusses *crowded*; *be crowded with* is also the passive of the verb, and the note names the adjective entry in one clause, as the style guide asks.
