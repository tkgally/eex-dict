## What this cycle did

A **review cycle** gives existing entries a second reading. Two outside reviewer models check every entry when it is first written, and a second round catches what the first missed. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose a review because, after two builds, it was furthest behind its usual share. No drafts were waiting, so the cycle took the twelve oldest entries that had been through the reviewer panel only once. All are nouns written on 4 October: *game*, *garage*, *garden*, *gas*, *gate*, *gear*, *gender*, *generation*, *glass*, *glasses*, *glove*, and *glue*.

First I read each entry myself, looking for missing senses, idioms, and learner errors. They were in good shape. I added one sense to *game*: the plural *games* for a large event with many sports competitions. The reviewers then showed that this use lives almost only in the names of particular events, and that the examples read oddly without a name, so I removed it again.

Then the two reviewer models read every field of all twelve entries again. On *garden*, reviewer-b's first reply came back with no verdicts, so I ran it again by itself; the second reply was complete and raised no issues.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

They raised 49 issues, 41 of them blocking. *Glasses* came back clean. I logged 49 decisions in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`: 34 applied and 15 rejected. None went to the owner.

Applied:

- *gender*: sense 1 defined gender as a group *such as man or woman*. It now reads *the fact of being a man, a woman, or something else, as part of who a person is and of how society treats them*, and the usage and adaptation notes say the same.
- *gear*: the definitions said gears control how *wheels* turn; they now say gears change how fast a machine moves and how much force it has. The toothed wheel (subsense 1a) can turn another part or be turned by it.
- *glass*: a drinking glass need not be made of glass (plastic ones are common); the definition and the adaptation note now say *usually*.
- *garden*: sense 3 called a public garden *a public park*; it is now *a large area of land with many flowers, plants, and trees, which is open to the public*, and *park* is no longer listed as its synonym.
- *glue*: sense 2 (*the glue that held our family together*) is now uncountable, matching its examples.
- *garage*: the repair sense lost its British label; American English uses it too.
- *gas*: sense 4 (gas in the stomach) is now *gas that forms in your stomach and can make you feel uncomfortable*, and the synonym *wind* is marked British.
- *generation*: the process sense (*the generation of electricity*) lost its *formal* label; the usage note now says a singular verb is usual and British English only sometimes uses a plural one.
- *game*: the note on *game* and *match* no longer says British English always says *match*; one learner error (*We made a game of cards*, which is grammatical with another meaning) was removed. One learner error on *glove* (*I bought a new glove*) was removed for the same reason.

Rejected:

- reviewer-a wanted *lead someone down the garden path* changed to *up the garden path*. *Down* is the usual American form; the entry already notes that British English says *up*.
- *gas* sense 4 keeps its American label. By the house rule, a region label means *mainly used there*; British English mostly says *wind*.
- *play* and *toy* stay as cross-references from *game*: many languages use one word for *game* and *play*, and some for *game* and *toy*.
- *out of gear*, the learner error *Close the garden door* (for a gate), and *male gender* (for *male*) stay; all are real.
