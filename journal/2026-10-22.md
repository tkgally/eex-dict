# 2026-10-22, cycle 1 of the 00:44 UTC run: *balance*, *excuse*, *hurry*, and nine more

## What this cycle did

This was a **closure** cycle. Closure means writing entries for words that the dictionary already uses in its own definitions but has no entry for yet, so that a reader who meets such a word can look it up. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose closure because it had the largest backlog among the work types, and allowed up to twelve new entries.

The closure list (words found in definitions) held only four rows, and all four were common words that should have had entries long ago:

- *balance* (noun), found in the definition of *fall over* (*lose your balance*)
- *excuse* (noun), found in *make up*
- *hurry* (noun), found in *come on*
- *misunderstanding*, found in *clear up*. The queue tool had guessed it was a verb; I marked that row as a duplicate and wrote the noun.

Because the verbs are just as common, I wrote them in the same cycle: *balance* (verb, which I added to the queue by hand), *excuse* (verb), *hurry* (verb), and *misunderstand*. That made eight. The remaining four came from the word-family list (words related to an existing entry), in queue order: *entertainer*, *entertaining*, *entertainment* (all from *entertain*), and *entry* (from *enter* and *entrance*).

Some points about the content:

- *balance* (noun) has five senses: not falling, the right amounts of different things (*work and family*), the money in a bank account, money still owed, and the weighing instrument; plus the phrases *on balance*, *in the balance*, and *throw someone off balance*.
- *excuse* is spelled the same as a noun and a verb but ends in an **s** sound for the noun and a **z** sound for the verb. Both entries say so in plain words, and the pronunciation checker confirmed both.
- *excuse me* is not in the *excuse* verb entry, because it is already queued as an entry of its own (a fixed phrase at the top frequency band). The verb entry points to it.
- *hurry* (noun) carries the common learner error *I'm in hurry* (the phrase needs **a**).

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewer models raised 37 issues across the twelve entries; three entries drew none. I made 37 decisions: 27 applied and 10 rejected. The main changes:

- *balance* (verb): I had mentioned *balance out* inside an explanation; reviewer-a pointed out that it is a phrasal verb and needs its own entry. It is now a cross-reference and queued. Reviewer-a also showed that my learner error (*balance between work and family*) is in fact used by native speakers, so I removed it.
- *excuse* (verb): my note comparing *excuse* and *forgive* said you cannot excuse a serious wrong, which contradicted my own sense 2 (*Nothing can excuse that kind of behavior*). The note now says that with serious wrongs *excuse* usually comes with a negative. A learner error built on *Excuse me for my mistake* was also removed, since the sentence is possible.
- *misunderstand*: both reviewers objected to a usage note about the adjective *misunderstood* in a verb entry, which breaks the one-part-of-speech rule. It was removed, and *misunderstood* (adjective) is now listed in the word family and queued.
- *entertainment*: my notes said it has no plural; reviewer-a pointed out the rare plural for separate shows. The notes now name it as a rare exception.
- *entry*: the summary sentence was rewritten to cover the book-and-list sense properly, and *refused entrance* was removed as a less usual equivalent of *refused entry*.

I rejected, among others: reviewer-b's view that phrase definitions must start with *to* (house style writes verb definitions without it); its objection to the way an adaptation note opens (a settled house practice); and reviewer-a's claim that *entry fee* belongs only to competitions (it is also usual for museums and parks).

## What did not work

The cross-reference tool adds a link back from each entry I point to. Two of mine went wrong: I had linked *balance* (money still owed) to *rest*, meaning *the rest of the money*, and the weighing sense to *scale*. But the existing *rest* entry is only the noun for relaxing, and *scale* is only size and levels; *rest* meaning the remainder and *scales* for weighing are separate words with the same spelling, and neither has an entry yet. I removed both links and recorded the two missing words in the note on same-spelling words. A third tool message showed that *entrance* lists *entry* as a synonym while I had listed *entrance* as a comparison; I moved it so both agree.
