# 2026-10-17, cycle 1 of the 00:44 UTC run: second review of *January* to *pan*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that had been through the two outside reviewers (the review panel, two language models from other companies) only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose review because it was the kind of work most overdue, and it picks the oldest such entries first. This block was twenty entries drafted on 2026-10-06: the month names *January* and *June*, the adjectives *rocky* and *shiny*, and sixteen nouns in alphabetical order (*official, oil, operation, opinion, opportunity, order, organ, outside, oven, owner, pack, page, pain, paint, pair, pan*).

Each entry went to both reviewers again; I read every issue and decided to change the entry (*applied*) or leave it (*rejected*, with a logged reason). I also read every entry myself.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

reviewer-a raised 51 issues, 34 of them marked blocking (meaning the entry should not stand without a decision). reviewer-b raised none at all, though it gave a verdict on every field of every entry, so its replies were complete; it was simply silent. Six entries drew no issues from either reviewer (*January, June, official, operation, owner, pain*). I applied 15 and rejected 36. Nothing needed the owner.

Applied:

- ***pan***: the entry had a third sense, *the amount that a pan holds* (*a pan of water*). The reviewer was right that this is just the pan with something in it, which any reader understands from the first sense; the style guide says not to split a sense for that. The sense is gone; its water example moved to sense 1 with a short note, and the *core idea* sentence (needed only for three senses or more) was removed.
- ***shiny***: a learner-error note said *shining has one n*, which is confusing, since the word has two. It now says the **n** before **-ing** is not doubled.
- ***opportunity***: a note said the word always needs *the* or *an*, but the entry's own example (*There wasn't much opportunity for rest*) has neither. The rule is now limited to one particular chance (*have the opportunity to*).
- ***oven***: the usage note now says British English also says *oven*; *cooker* is the British word for a stove with an oven.
- ***pair***: the note comparing *pair* and *couple* now gives *a couple of* as *two or a few*.
- ***organ***: *organism* was listed as a family word. It is not formed from *organ* in today's English, so it came off.
- ***order***: the usage note gave the start of a sentence as a reason to use *in order to*, which is no reason; it now says to use it for a very clear purpose, especially in writing.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- *Pack* meaning a bag on your back gets no region label; it is ordinary English everywhere (*a hiker's pack*).
- *At the outside* (at most) is used in American English too, so it is not labeled British.
- *Take a page from someone's book* keeps its *American* label: in this dictionary a region label means *mainly used there*, and British speakers usually say *take a leaf out of someone's book*. *Open at page 20* is common British classroom English, so the learner-error note stands.
- *In order to* and *in order that* stay under *order*: the house rule files an idiom under its first noun.
- Minor objections to see-also links and to the synonyms *attitude* and *mind* on *opinion* were rejected; *Out of order* (wrong sequence), *like watching paint dry*, and a few collocation types were also left as they were. One small collocation fix on *oil* was applied.

## My own additions

- The idiom *a tall order* (a very difficult task or request) added to *order*.
- *page*: the usage note said a page is one side of the paper and the whole piece is a *sheet*, but sense 1 says *page* can mean the whole sheet (*tear a page out*). The note now says so, and the two agree.

No new cross-links were created, and every definition word has an entry.
