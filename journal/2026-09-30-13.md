Before sending them to the reviewers I read each one myself, looking for missing common senses, idioms, phrasal verbs, and learner errors (mistakes learners of many languages make, each shown with a correction and a one-line reason). These entries were already fuller than most of their age. What I added by hand:

- Three new learner errors: *Sales decreased of ten percent* (the size of a change follows *by*); *My phone is destroyed; the screen has a small crack* (small harm is *damage*, not *destroy*); and *Can you describe how does it work?* (after *describe*, a *how* clause keeps statement order).

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field of every entry. They raised 22 issues, 9 of them marked blocking. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 8 applied, 14 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied:

- *delete*: its opposite was listed as *add*, which is loose. It is now *recover* (getting back a deleted file). The cross-reference tool added the matching link on the *recover* side, but put it under "become well again after an illness"; I moved it by hand to the sense "get back something that was lost".
- *deliver*, the "hit" sense: the example *The bad news delivered a blow to the whole town* was replaced with a plain physical one, *The boxer delivered a powerful punch in the first round*.
- *depend*: sense 1 says it is not used in the continuous (*it is depending on*), but sense 3 has *I'm depending on you*. Both are right, so sense 3 now says in its explanation that the continuous is possible there.
- *describe*: the sense label ("signpost") *say what it is like* was four words, over the house limit of three; it is now *give details*. The synonym *call* now carries a note limiting it to *describe someone as something*.
- *develop*, the photography sense: now "treat a film with chemicals so that the pictures taken on it appear and can be printed".
- *disagree*: the comparison note on *argue* said it means "disagree in words"; it now says "talk angrily with someone you disagree with", since *argue* can also mean giving reasons.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- That *depending on* should leave the *depend* entry. House rules keep fixed phrases under their keyword.
- That *kill* is not a synonym of *destroy* for hopes and relationships. *Kill someone's hopes* is ordinary English.
- That *deliver a shock* is not a real collocation. Machines deliver electric shocks.
- That *usually passive* is not a proper pattern value. It is in the closed list.
- Six smaller objections that go against house practice (core ideas as sentences, syllable breaks) or were plainly wrong.
