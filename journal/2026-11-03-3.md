# Run report, 2026-11-03 (third run of the day)

## Cycle 1: build

**What this cycle did.** The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose *build*: writing new dictionary entries from the queue of words still to be written. It allowed up to twelve. I took the next twelve band-2 nouns in queue order (band 2 is the second-most-common group of words in our editorial frequency scale): *atmosphere*, *attorney*, *August*, *avenue*, *award*, *awareness*, *backpack*, *backup*, *bacon*, *badge*, *baggage*, and *bakery*. All twelve are now published-ready (`reviewed`), bringing the dictionary to 3,623 entries.

**How they were checked.** Each entry went through the full pipeline. The validator (schema and house-style checks) passed. The inflection script generated the plurals (*bakeries*, *Augusts*) and marked *awareness*, *bacon*, and *baggage* as having no plural. The pronunciation panel (three outside models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, consulted at run time and not stored) agreed with every one of the 24 American and British transcriptions. Then the two reviewers, reviewer A (a Claude model) and reviewer B (a Google model), read every field.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** They raised 43 issues, 8 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry can be marked reviewed). I agreed with 35 and rejected 8.

- *atmosphere*: I had written a fourth sense, the air inside a room (*the atmosphere in here is very smoky*). Reviewer A objected on four counts that English speakers say *the air in here*, and that the remaining uses were really the mood sense. I agreed and removed the sense. The entry now has three senses: the mood of a place, the special quality that makes a place interesting (*the old town has lots of atmosphere*), and the gases around a planet.
- *awareness*: an example, *Good drivers keep an awareness of everything around them*, was unnatural; both reviewers also noticed that it used *an awareness* while the grammar said uncountable. I rewrote the example and removed the *a growing awareness* collocations so the entry agrees with itself.
- *bakery*: both reviewers said *baker* is a person, not a synonym of *bakery*. That is true in American English, but the existing *baker* entry has a "shop" sense (British *the baker's*) that points to *bakery*, and our cross-reference tool mirrors such links. So I kept the link and added a note saying it is British, in the form *the baker's*.
- Coverage: reviewer A pointed out two real gaps, which I filled. *Backup* now has an American sense for a line of traffic or a blocked drain (*a long backup on the bridge*), and the money sense of *award* now covers money given to pay for study, not only money a court orders.
- Smaller fixes: shorter signposts (the two-or-three-word labels that help a reader pick a sense), plainer wording for *bacon* (*salted or smoked meat*), a non-circular definition for the idiom *save someone's bacon*, the British *pay award* example replaced, and a political slogan on a *badge* replaced with a sewn team badge (Americans would say *button* for the slogan kind).
- Rejected: *power of attorney* is an idiom whose keyword is *power*, so it belongs in the *power* entry, not *attorney* (I added it to the to-do list in `NEXT.md`); idiom examples need no hand-marking because the site marks the headword itself; a British spelling *badge of honour* has no place to go, since phrases are written in American spelling; and a pronunciation objection on *bakery* that the panel and the CMU dictionary both contradict.

**Problems along the way.** Four words in my first definitions were outside the defining vocabulary (the 2,394-word list definitions should use): *certificate*, *straps*, *walkers*, *travelers*. I rewrote those definitions. A synonym link from *backup* to *support* had no matching sense in the *support* entry to receive the mirror link, so I removed it rather than force it.

**Spend.** US$0.35 for this cycle (pronunciation panel and reviewer B; reviewer A costs nothing in the ledger). The day stands at US$1.75 of the US$15 cap.

**What is next.** More band-2 nouns in queue order, starting after *bakery*. New words the drafts referred to (*luggage*, *rucksack*, *reward*, *ham*, *solicitor*, *atmospheric*, *backpacker*, and others) were added to the queue to wait for a band vote.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new from this cycle.

## Cycle 2: review

**What this cycle did.** The scheduler chose *review*: re-reading entries that are already published. With no drafts waiting and no open entry reports (the "Report a problem" issues readers can file), the work was the coverage pass the owner asked for on 2026-10-24: reading the most important band-1 entries (the most common words), most senses first, to check that every current meaning is there. I read four six-sense entries not read since that date: *reach* (verb), *queen* (noun), *place* (verb), and *piece* (noun). That makes 169 entries done in the pass; 41 six-sense entries remain, starting with *period*.

**What I added from my own reading.** *Reach* gained a subsense for getting through to a person (*nothing seemed to reach him*). *Queen* gained a subsense for beauty and prom queens. *Place* gained a seventh sense, finding a home or job for someone (*the agency placed her with a family*), and *place a bet* in its order sense. *Piece* gained the idiom *say your piece*. I also tried a subsense for *a five-piece band*; reviewer A pointed out that *five-piece* works like an adjective in front of a noun, not like the noun *piece*, and I agreed and withdrew it.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 69 issues, 7 blocking. I applied 56 and rejected 13.

- *queen*: the playing-card sense defined *queen* with the word *queen* (circular; now *a woman ruler*). An example said every group of bees has only one queen, a science claim that is not always true; replaced. The *drama queen* idiom sat awkwardly inside the "best in a field" sense; its keyword under our rule is *drama*, so it moves to the *drama* entry when that is written (noted in `NEXT.md`). Reviewer B noticed the American bed size (*a queen bed*) was missing; I added it.
- *reach*: two examples did not fit their senses (a ladder reaching a roof was under "stretch your arm"; news reaching us was under "arrive at a place"); both replaced. A grammar note in the translator notes repeated the learner-error box; removed.
- *place*: signposts shortened, *trust* added to the importance sense, *place a call* and *place pressure on* added as collocations, and an example about children placed in care moved to the new sense.
- *piece*: *block* and *section* were removed as synonyms (from both entries, since links are kept in pairs); a game-rule fact and an odd *gold piece* example replaced.
- Rejected: reviewer A wanted *make* and *touch* removed as synonyms of *reach*, but the linked senses of those entries mean exactly "arrive at a place" and "reach a level"; it wanted the chess piece defined with the word *chess*, which is outside the defining vocabulary and has no entry yet; and it asked twice for "not used in continuous tenses" to move from the patterns list to the codes list, but that value belongs to the closed list of verb patterns.

**What did not work.** One new definition used *mattress*, outside the defining vocabulary; I rewrote it. One decision command failed on a case mismatch in its quote and was re-run.

**Spend.** About US$0.20 for this cycle; the day stands at US$1.95 of US$15.

**What is next.** The coverage pass continues with *period*, *on*, *old*, *off*, and *note*.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new.
