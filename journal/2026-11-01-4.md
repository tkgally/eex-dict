# Run report, 2026-11-01 (fourth run of the day)

## Cycle 1: lint

**What this cycle was.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose a lint pass, because ten runs had passed since the last one. A lint pass is housekeeping: it builds no new entries and instead checks that the dictionary and its knowledge base (the `wiki/` folder of rules and notes) agree with themselves. This was the ninety-seventh such pass. Before starting, the run checked GitHub for work a previous run might have left half-finished: there were no open pull requests, no leftover branches besides `main`, and no open entry reports from readers. The `inbox/` folder, where the owner leaves instructions, was empty.

**Mechanical checks.** The word caps on the project's files, the internal links, and the entry validator all passed. The wiki is close to its 30,000-word cap (29,954 words after this pass), so one long note, the record of reviewer precision, was condensed again to make room.

**Cross-references.** Entries point to each other: a synonym in one entry should be mirrored by a link back from the other. The cross-reference tool wrote 7 such back-links on its own, where the right sense of the target word was obvious. It left 24 pairs for a person to place, because the target word has several senses and the tool cannot tell which one is meant. I placed 19 of them by hand, in 16 entries. Almost all were older entries pointing at verbs built earlier today: for example, *baby* (treat someone like a baby) now appears as a comparison under the sense of *spoil* about children, *cloud* (make a time less happy) as a synonym under the first sense of *spoil*, *pause* as a synonym of *suspend* and an opposite of *resume*, and *rise* as an opposite of *sink* in its sense "move slowly down". The remaining five pairs are known problems that the earlier references themselves get wrong (for example, *slightly* pointing to *bit* as a synonym, though *bit* is a noun); they wait for the cross-reference audit the owner ordered on 2026-10-24.

**Band vote.** Every word waiting in the build queue needs a frequency band from 1 (most common) to 5. Words added to the queue by tools arrive without one, marked `unbanded`. Three language models voted on the 133 such rows, at a cost of US$0.023, and the middle (lower median) vote became each row's band. Three words drew votes more than one band apart; I settled each at the middle vote: *resumption* 4, *scrape through* 4, *scrape together* 3. The record is in `experiments/band-vote-2026-11-01/`.

**Reviewer precision.** Each entry is checked by two reviewer models, and every issue they raise is either applied or rejected. Precision is the share applied. A kind of issue that falls below 30 percent over twenty or more decisions is switched off for that reviewer. No new kind fell below the line this time. Reviewer A, a Claude model, stands at 0.83 overall; reviewer B, a Google model, at 0.81. Reviewer B's pronunciation flags passed twenty decisions for the first time and are at 0.38, above the line. Its spelling-and-format flags (0.26) stay on because the owner ruled so.

**Reports for later review cycles.** The tools found 329 definitions that use their own headword (most harmless: *walk a dog* under *walk*), 904 pairs where a synonym or antonym points at an entry that may lack the matching sense (up from 836, because more entries exist), and 7 usage notes that promise an entry that does not exist yet (unchanged).

**Curator list.** Five lines in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` asked for old branches to be deleted; those branches are gone, so the lines were marked resolved.

**Spend.** US$0.023 this cycle; US$2.87 of the US$15 daily budget so far today.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. The wiki word cap remains tight; each lint pass now has to condense something before it can add anything.

**What is next.** The next lint is due in ten runs. The build queue continues with band-2 verbs and closure words (words the dictionary already uses in its definitions but does not yet define).

**For the owner.** Nothing new. The open questions in `NEXT.md` stand: names after titles (*Dr. Lee*) in definitions, the two British pronunciations of *ensure* and *insure*, and the charter line about reviewers.
