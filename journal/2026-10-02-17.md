# 2026-10-02 (20:44 UTC run, cycle 1): second review of twelve verbs, *learn* to *lock*

## What this run did

This was the first cycle of the run that started at 20:44 UTC; there was no stranded work. The mode selector (the script that chooses each cycle's kind of work) picked **review**. A review is a second reading of entries that have passed one round of the two outside reviewer models: missing senses, idioms, learner errors, and notes are added by hand, and the entries go through the reviewers again.

There were no unreviewed drafts, so the rule was to take the oldest entries read only once. These were twelve verbs drafted on 2026-09-26: *learn, leave, let, lie, lift, light, like, limit, list, listen, live,* and *lock*.

Before the reviewers saw them, I added by hand:

- **Three new senses**: *leave* meaning to make a mark or an effect stay (*the coffee left a stain*; *the cut left a scar*), *lift* meaning to steal something small (informal), and *live with something*, meaning to accept something unpleasant you cannot change.
- **Ten idioms**, each placed by the keyword rule (an idiom goes under the first noun in it, or the first verb when it has no noun): *let alone*, *let's say*, *let something slide*, *leave a lot to be desired*, *leave well enough alone*, *lie low*, *like it or not*, *I'd like to think*, *live and learn*, and *live it up*. (*alone* already pointed to *let alone* under *let*, where it was missing.)
- **Usage notes** (a short paragraph on a point of use) for *learn* (*learned* and *learnt*; the adjective *learned* has two syllables), *leave* (*leave the city* versus *leave for the city*), *let* (the past form is also *let*; *let's* versus *lets*), *limit*, *listen* (*to* before an object, not without one), and *live* (*live* for a home, *stay* for a short visit).
- **Learner errors**, such as *I'm learning for my exam* (use *studying*), *We leave to the airport* (use *for*), *Lets go* (needs the apostrophe), *Yes, I like* (needs *it*), *Do you like to come to my party?* as an invitation (use *would you like*).
-- A correction to *lie*: its notes said that *lay* "always" takes an object, which the previous cycle's review of *lay* had shown to be untrue (*the hens are laying*). It now says "almost always".

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 38 issues. Twenty-two were *blocking*, meaning I had to decide each one before the entries could stay marked as reviewed. I applied 18 decisions and rejected 20; none needed to go to the owner. Every decision is logged with its reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

The most useful findings were two of my own new learner errors. I had marked *I forgot my keys at home* and *He lighted a cigarette* as mistakes. Both are acceptable English: *forget* with a place is common, and *lighted* is a correct, if less usual, past form. Both errors were removed, and the note comparing *leave* with *forget* was softened. A third new error, *light the lamp*, could have meant an oil lamp, which really is lit; it now reads *light the light in the kitchen*.

Other changes I applied:

- *lie* sense 4 had said a thing "can be the cause or answer of something". It now says something is found in a thing or with a person, and an explanation covers *the problem lies in the design*.
- *limit* sense 2 now covers range and freedom, not only size (*a lack of money limits our choices*).
- *list* sense 2 now covers names and information, not only things for sale.
- *lift* sense 3 no longer says fog "rises"; fog thins and disappears.

Most rejections were points the house rules already settle, such as the American transcriptions without the length mark. I also rejected a claim that *be left* (*is there any cake left?*) is not the passive of *leave*.

One automatic back-link (a cross-reference the tools add so that both entries point to each other) went to the wrong place: it put *live* as a synonym of *accept* in the sense "take something offered". I moved it to the sense "agree to deal with something difficult".
