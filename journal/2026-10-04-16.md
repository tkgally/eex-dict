## What this cycle did

The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) forced a **lint** pass, because five cycles had passed since the last one. A lint pass is the regular health check of the whole dictionary and its working notes (the wiki). It costs nothing, because it makes no paid model calls.

The mechanical checks:

- **Caps and links.** The project limits how long some files may be (for example, `NEXT.md` may have at most 60 lines), and every link between pages must work. All limits were respected and every link resolved.
- **Back-links.** When entry A names entry B as a synonym, a "compare" word, or a member of its word family, the dictionary also puts a link back from B to A. The cross-reference tool (`tools/crossref.py`) adds these back-links, but it has to guess which sense of B should hold the link. This time it added eleven, all leading to the twenty-four nouns written earlier in this run. I checked each by hand. Nine were right and two were wrong:
  - *block* (a set of things dealt with as one unit) had been attached to the sense of *group* that means companies with one owner. It now sits under the first, general sense.
  - *cave* had been attached to the golf sense of *hole*. It now sits under the first sense, an empty space in the ground.
- One back-link puts *way* into the word family of *highway* again. The reviewers had removed it earlier today because *way* is only a part of the compound. *Way*, though, lists *highway* in its own family, and the house rule makes word-family links work both ways, so I kept it.
- **Vocabulary and claims.** The vocabulary check over every entry ran, and three old claim files were cleared. A claim file is the record of which words a cycle has reserved.

The judgment checks:

- Every page in the wiki is listed in its index, and every index line leads to a real page.
- Every cycle today has its entry in the log.
- The open questions for the owner are still current.
- The curator file (`reviews/needs_curator.txt`, the list of questions only the owner can settle) has no duplicate lines.
- Two figures in `NEXT.md` were out of date and were updated: the precision of the two held reviewer checks.

## Reviewer precision

Each lint pass measures how often each reviewer's flags turn out to be right, by kind of issue ("precision"). The house rule says that a kind of flag that is right less than 30 percent of the time, over twenty or more decisions, should be switched off for that reviewer.

No new kind of flag fell under the line. The two already under it are still held for the owner, as before:

- Reviewer-a's pronunciation flags are right 26 percent of the time (54 of 205). Most are the same rejected objection about the American length mark.
- Reviewer-b's spelling-and-format flags are right 28 percent of the time (145 of 524). They fell again this run, because the review cycle rejected nine flags against the opening headword of an adaptation note, all in one block of twelve entries.

In both cases a session tried to switch the check off on 2026-10-01, and the session's safety check refused the edit. The question waits for the owner (`NEXT.md`, "For the owner").
