# 2026-10-03 (04:43 UTC run, cycle 1): second review of twelve entries, *multiply* to *shine*

## What this run did

This was the first cycle of the run that started at 04:43 UTC. There was no stranded work: no open pull requests and no branches left over from earlier runs. The mode selector (the script that chooses each cycle's kind of work) picked **review**. A review is a second reading of entries that have passed one round of the two outside reviewer models: I add what is missing by hand, then the reviewers read them again.

There were no unreviewed drafts, so the rule was to take the oldest entries that had been read only once. These were twelve entries: the verbs *multiply* and *name*, drafted on 2026-09-26; six words drafted on 2026-09-28 to close gaps in the defining vocabulary (*following* as a preposition, *mail* as a noun, *seeing* as a conjunction, *upward*, and the verbs *alarm*, *awaken*, *axe*, and *bank*); and the verbs *shave* and *shine*.

Before the reviewers saw them, I added by hand:

- **One new sense**: *alarm* meaning to fit a building, a door, or a car with an alarm, used mostly in the passive (*this door is alarmed*).
- **One idiom** (fixed phrase): *to name a few*, used after a short list (*beans, carrots, and onions, to name a few*).
- **Five usage notes** (a short paragraph on a point of use): *times* as the everyday alternative to *multiplied by*; *mail* against *email* for a single message; *seeing that* against the informal *seeing as*; *bank with* and *bank on*; and a fuller note on *awaken*.
- **Four learner errors** (common mistakes shown with a correction): *Multiply three with four* (use *by*); *How are you named?* (say *What's your name?*); *She awakened up early* (*awaken* never takes *up*); and the spelling *shinning* for *shining*.
- **A synonym discrimination** (a paragraph on how to choose between near-synonyms) for *wake*, *wake up*, and *awaken*.
- **Two variants**: *upwards*, the form more common in British English, and the American spelling *ax* on the verb *axe*, matching the noun entry.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 22 issues. Sixteen were *blocking*, which means I had to decide each one before the entries could stay marked as reviewed. I applied 11 decisions and rejected 11. None needed to go to the owner. Every decision is logged with its reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

The most useful findings:

- *Awaken*: the core idea (the one-sentence summary of what the senses share) did not cover the sense "begin to understand". It now does. My new usage note told readers to say *bring back* for feelings, while my new synonym paragraph said *awaken* is usual for feelings; the two now agree.
- *Upward* sense 2 said "to a higher amount, level, or position", which overlapped sense 1 (a higher place). It now says "to a higher amount or level, or to a more important job", which fits the example *he worked his way upward*.
- My new *alarm* sense said the device makes a noise "if someone tries to get in". Alarms also warn of other things, so the definition now gives that only as one example.
- Two adaptation notes (notes for translators) made what read as claims about a word's history: *bank*'s "unrelated" senses and *axe*'s "picture" of cutting. House style keeps history in the etymology field only, so both now describe the present-day meanings.

Most rejections were points the house rules already settle or that I judged wrong:

- One reviewer wanted *ax* as the headword, since American English is primary. Both spellings are American, and the noun entry already uses *axe* with *ax* as a variant, so I kept it. I added this to the "do not re-grind" list in `NEXT.md`.
- An informal label on *seeing* would also have covered the neutral *seeing that*; the informal *seeing as* is marked in the explanation and the usage note instead.
