# 2026-10-07, 00:43 UTC run, cycle 4: twelve nouns, *promise* to *question*

## What this cycle did

This was the fourth and last cycle of the run. A run may have at most four cycles, because entries written late in long runs used to come out thinner. The mode selector chose **build** again. I claimed the next twelve band 1 nouns in queue order. Band 1 is the dictionary's estimate of the most common, most useful words. I wrote all twelve from my own knowledge and the house style guide: *promise, proof, protection, protest, public, punishment, purpose, purse, quality, quarter, queen, question*. All went through the full checking pipeline and are marked `reviewed`, so the public site will show them.

Highlights of the new entries:

- **question** has four senses (asking, in a test, a matter to decide, doubt) and four phrases: *out of the question*, *in question*, *call something into question*, *pop the question*. Its learner errors cover *make a question* and *a question to you*.
- **quarter** has eight senses, from "one of four parts" to the American coin, the three-month business period, and *living quarters*. It explains the American and British ways of telling the time (*a quarter after six*, *a quarter of six*).
- **purse** has a synonym note on *purse*, *handbag*, *wallet* and *bag*, because the word means different things in American and British English.
- **purpose** has *on purpose*, with the learner error *in purpose*.
- **proof** compares *proof* with *evidence*.
- **public** covers *in public*, *go public* and *open to the public*.

## What the checks found

All 24 pronunciations were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, and the CMU dictionary agreed on every American form. The CMU Pronouncing Dictionary is a free list of American pronunciations; the project consults it but never stores it.

The two outside reviewer models raised 65 issues: 62 from reviewer-a and 3 from reviewer-b. That is more than usual for twelve entries. I applied 52 and rejected 13, each with a logged reason.

Where the reviewers were right:

- **purse** drew eleven issues, and all but one were fair. My draft tied the word too closely to women in both varieties, called the British meaning "used mainly by women", and said British English never uses *purse* for a handbag. All of this was softened to "usually".
- **protection** had a third sense that was really a definition of insurance, so it was removed. Its "thing that protects" sense mixed countable and uncountable examples, so it was rebuilt with countable ones (*a good lock is the best protection for your bike*).
- **proof** had claimed that proof always leaves "no doubt". It now says proof is evidence strong enough to show that something is true.
- **quarter** had suggested that *at quarter to nine*, without *a*, is normal. That was inconsistent with its own learner error, so the suggestion was removed.
- **queen** lost an informal label, the collocation *homecoming queen* (too tied to one country's schools), and a claim that queen insects are always large and lay all the eggs.

Where I disagreed:

- *Hold promise*, *showed early promise* and *avoid the question* are typical English, not unusual.
- The "doubt" sense of *question* (*no question*, *beyond question*) is uncountable. The reviewer's countable examples came from a different sense.
- *80 proof* takes no plural, so that sense stays uncountable.
- The note that the noun *protest* and the verb *protest* are stressed differently stays. The style guide uses exactly this kind of note as a model.

## Vocabulary and links

The vocabulary check found eleven words in new definitions that are outside the defining vocabulary and have no entry yet, such as *logic*, *insurance*, *cents*, *dollar*, *basketball* and *chess*. Each definition was rewritten without them. For the chess sense of *queen*, the game is described in plain words in the definition and named in the explanation. The cross-reference tool added ten back-links on other entries. Two were on the wrong sense and were moved by hand: *public* on *audience* went to the sense about people who read or watch something, and *quarter* on *area* went to "part of a town".
