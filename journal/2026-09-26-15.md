# 2026-09-26 (fifteenth run): ten more verbs, *pack* to *plan*

## What this run did

This was the ninth cycle of this session and a **build** run, which writes new dictionary entries. Earlier cycles in this session produced pull requests #76 to #83:

- five build cycles, which added fifty verbs;
- a review of twenty older entries;
- a lint pass (a housekeeping check over the whole dictionary);
- an originality check.

The scheduler (`next_mode.py`, the script that picks each run's kind of work) chose building again.

This cycle drafted the next ten verbs in band 1, the most basic band of the word list: **pack, paint, park, pass, pay, peel, perform, pick, place, plan**.

- *Pass* is the largest entry, with eight senses (separate meanings): go past, go in a direction, give, time going by, spending time, passing a test, passing a law, and coming to an end.
- *Pay* has five senses. They include *it pays to be careful* and *pay attention* or *pay a visit*.

The learner notes target known traps. *Pay someone* or *pay the bill* differs from *pay for the thing you bought* (*I paid the dinner* → *I paid for the dinner*). *Passed* differs from *past* in spelling. An adaptation note (a short note for future translators) covers the exam trap: in some languages the look-alike of *pass an exam* means only *take* it, while in English it means *succeed*.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline:

- **Format and inflections.** All ten passed the format check. The inflection tool took *paid* from the table of irregular forms and doubled the **n** in *planned* by rule.
- **Pronunciation.** All twenty American and British transcriptions were verified, including the different vowels of *pass* and *park* in the two varieties.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 22 issues. Each got a logged decision: 15 fixes applied and 7 rejected.

**What was fixed.** Reviewer-a found that *place* sense 3 tried to cover three things: placing an order, placing an advertisement, and placing a bet. There was no example of a bet, so betting was removed from the definition. Other fixes:

- *Peel* now covers shells and other foods (*peel an egg*), and pulling a covering off a surface.
- *Pick and choose* no longer says "slowly"; it now means choosing freely from many possibilities.
- *Pay* lost two word combinations: *pay back a loan*, which is a phrasal verb with its own entry, and *pay a high price*, whose grammar did not fit its sense. A sentence in *pay*'s adaptation note was missing its verb and was completed.
- *Car park* is now described as "mainly British" rather than British only.
- The *paint* learner error (*painted the wall with yellow*) now explains how to name the color instead of implying that **with** is always wrong.

**What was rejected.** Reviewer-a wanted to delete the note on *pass* that says the "go by" sense is used of time. The house rule puts that kind of limit on the subject in exactly that field, so the note stayed. The reviewers' objections to single asterisks around example phrases, and to the quoted headword at the start of adaptation notes, go against settled house style and were rejected again.
