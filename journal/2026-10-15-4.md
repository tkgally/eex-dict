## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Reviewer-a raised 21 issues, 17 of them blocking; reviewer-b gave a verdict on every field of all twelve entries and raised none. I accepted 15 and rejected 6; none needed the owner.

Accepted:

- *extremely*: the usage note and a learner error said *extremely huge* and *extremely amazing* are wrong. They are only less usual than *absolutely huge*, so the note now says *absolutely* is "more usual", and the learner error was removed.
- *forever*: I had listed *since forever* as an error. It is established informal English, so that learner error was removed. The "all the time" sense (*She's forever losing her keys*) gained the pattern *be forever doing something*.
- *forward*: the "earlier date" sense was already labeled British, but the entry's opening summary and the translator's note spoke as if it were the general meaning. Both were corrected, and the clock-changing remark was taken out of that sense.
- *far*: the first definition now reads "to or at a long distance"; the explanation covers *How far is it?*.
- *finally*: the "formal" label on *finally decided* was removed; the use is neutral.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- *fast asleep*: the reviewer said it is an adjective phrase belonging under the adjective *fast*. In *fast asleep*, *fast* describes the adjective *asleep*, which is an adverb's job, so it stays here.
- The *fairly*/*quite* comparison: the reviewer said British and American *quite* cannot be described this way. The description (British *quite good* often means *fairly good*; American *quite good* is close to *very good*) is standard, so it stays.
- *forward* "to an earlier date": kept as the definition of a sense labeled British.

The cross-reference tool reported two pairs where one entry called a word a synonym and the other called it a word to compare. Following the project's practice, I kept the older entry's choice: *extremely* now lists *very* as a synonym, and *fast* now compares itself with *quickly*. The tool also added *far* (adverb) to the word family of *far* (adjective).
