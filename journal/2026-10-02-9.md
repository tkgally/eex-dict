# 2 October 2026, 12:43 UTC run, cycle 1: twelve new nouns, *cost* to *crack*

## What this run did

This was the first cycle of the run that started at 12:43 UTC. A "cycle" is one complete unit of work with its own pull request (a proposed change to the repository, checked automatically and then merged); a run may do up to four. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks the kind of work by how long each kind has waited) chose **build**: write new entries from the queue of words still to be written.

I wrote twelve nouns, the next twelve in the core list (band 1, the most useful words): *cost, cotton, cough, council, country, couple, courage, course, court, cousin, cow*, and *crack*. Each has every current sense with its own short label (a "signpost", such as *of a street* or *in law*), two to four example sentences per sense, typical word combinations, grammar, and, where they help, idioms, a note on common learner mistakes, a short paragraph that tells near-synonyms apart, and notes for translators.

Some highlights:

- *course* has seven senses (classes, part of a meal, direction, way of acting, development, sport, medical) and holds *of course*, with a usage note that *of course* as an answer to a question of fact can sound rude.
- *country* separates the nation, its people, the land outside towns (*live in the country*), a kind of land (*open country*), and the music, and tells *country*, *nation*, and *state* apart.
- *couple* covers *a couple of* (often "two or three") and the two-people sense, and tells *couple* from *pair*.
- *cow* includes the insult (labelled offensive, British and Australian) and the idioms *till the cows come home*, *a sacred cow*, and American *have a cow*.
- *crack* runs from a thin break to a sharp sound, a try (*have a crack at it*), an unkind joke, and the drug, with *at the crack of dawn* and *fall through the cracks*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Every entry went through the full pipeline: format check, inflections from rules, pronunciation checked by a panel of models and against an open pronouncing dictionary (all 24 transcriptions verified), then two independent reviewer models from different companies reading every field. They raised 47 issues, 27 of them marked blocking. I applied 32 and rejected 15, each with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`; none needed the owner.

Where the reviewers were right:

- *cotton* had a fourth sense saying Americans call cotton wool simply "cotton". One reviewer flagged it six ways; the usual American terms are *cotton balls* and *absorbent cotton*, so I removed the sense.
- Two learner-error notes were too absolute: *What is the cost of this shirt?* is a normal question, and a crack can be *on* a glass surface. Both were removed.
- Wording fixes: the royal *court* now names the people first and the place second; the "similar thing" sense of *cousin* now needs both likeness and relation; *bravery* is no longer said to be only physical; *coursework* and *racecourse* are one word; *cow elephant* replaces *a whale cow*; *go to the country* (British: call an election) no longer says the election must be early.

Where I rejected the reviewers: one reviewer again objected to adaptation notes that open with the headword in quotation marks, which house style allows; a request for a "drugs" subject label, which the closed list of labels does not have; and a "historical" label on the royal court, since kings and queens still keep courts.

The word-list check (`tools/lint_vocab.py`, which keeps definitions inside the 2,394-word defining vocabulary) caught seventeen words outside the list in my definitions, such as *tennis*, *golf*, *romantic*, and *cocaine*. I reworded each. It also queued two plural forms, *affairs* and *lectures*, as if they were separate words; I marked those rows as duplicates and queued *affair*.
