## What this cycle did

A **review cycle** gives existing entries a second reading. Every entry is checked by two outside reviewer models (the **panel**) before it is published, but most entries have been through the panel only once. The scheduler picked a review cycle, and the queue says to take the oldest of these one-round entries first. This time that meant twelve nouns created on 2026-10-02 and 2026-10-03: *destruction, detail, development, device, dictionary, difference, difficulty, dinner, direction, director, dirt, disability*.

Before sending them to the panel, I read each entry as a whole, looking for common senses, idioms, collocations (words that typically go together) or learner errors that were missing. Most of these entries were already full, so the additions were small:

- *detail*: a new sense 5, the small group of soldiers or police given one job (*a security detail*, *a protection detail*). Sense 4 (a part of a painting) had no collocations and now has *a detail from something*.
- *development*: two learner errors, *many development countries* (it should be *developing countries*) and *a new develop in the story* (the verb used where the noun is needed).
- *dinner*: the collocations *the dinner table*, *dinner time*, *a dinner plate*.
- *dirt*: the informal idiom *dish the dirt* (tell people private or unkind things about someone).

All twelve then went through the full checking pipeline: format validation, inflections (all verified), pronunciations, and both reviewers. Twenty-three of the twenty-four transcriptions were confirmed. The American *detail* was disputed by the panel again (one model of four agreeing with stress on the first part, although the open pronouncing dictionary agrees). The entry already carries a flag for this and lists the other stress as a variant, so nothing changed.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 24 issues: 22 from reviewer-a and 2 from reviewer-b. I applied 9 and rejected 15, each with a short reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. None needed the owner.

**Applied:**

- *development*, sense 4. It mixed two meanings with different grammar: a group of new buildings (countable: *a housing development*) and the work of building on land (uncountable: *land sold for development*). The countable meaning is now the sense, and the uncountable one is a subsense under it. The usage note now lists both. Reviewer-b also pointed out that Americans live *in* a housing development, not *on* one; the example was changed.
- *direction*. The usage note said that *this direction*, *the opposite direction*, and *the wrong direction* always take **in**. That is true when they describe movement (*walk in the wrong direction*), but informal American English sometimes leaves **in** out (*go that direction*). The note and the matching translator note now say this.
- *director*. The film-and-play sense now carries a theater label beside the film-and-television one. A learner-error note said the article is left out "only" before a title that one person holds; it now says "often".
- *disability*. The collocation *disability pay* was replaced by the more common *disability payments*.

**Rejected:**

- Both reviewers said the idiom *dirt cheap* should go under *cheap*, because it works as an adjective. The house rule is that an idiom goes under its first noun, whatever part of speech the whole phrase has, so it stays under *dirt*.
- Reviewer-a said a "dictionary of a subject" gives only words, not information about topics. Many subject dictionaries (*a dictionary of music*) explain topics too, and the definition covers both.
- It said *make a difference* need not mean a good effect. The definition already says "especially a good effect", and the explanation covers *make no difference*.
- It said *Can you tell me the direction to the station?* is not a real learner error. Asking for the route takes the plural, *directions*; the singular asks only which way the station lies.
- The other six: two label requests on *device* (a domain label is used only for specialist fields, and the bomb sense is ordinary news language), one on *dinner*'s translator note (such notes are meant for translators), two on *dirt*'s uncountable notes (the claims hold: in *a dirt road* the **a** belongs to *road*), and one on *director*'s note about managing directors, which already says "often".
