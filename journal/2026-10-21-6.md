This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that had been through the review panel (two reviewer models from other companies) only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose it as the most overdue kind of work, with a block of up to twenty. There were no unfinished drafts, so the block was the twenty oldest entries with only one review round. They are nouns written on 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-08: *service, sex, shade, shadow, shame, shape, sheep, sheet, shell, ship, shirt, shock, shoe, shop, shore, shoulder, show, shower, side, sight*.

The panel ran in three foreground batches of six or seven entries. An earlier run lost a background panel when its container restarted, and foreground batches avoid that.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 45 issues, 42 of them marked blocking. I logged a decision for each: 27 applied, 18 rejected, none escalated (passed to the owner). *Sheet*, *ship*, and *shore* drew no issues.

One thing stands out: every issue came from reviewer-a. Reviewer-b sent a complete reply for all twenty entries, a verdict on every field, and found nothing. That fits the pattern in `wiki/notes/reviewer-b-low-yield.md`: on older nouns that have already had one review, reviewer-b rarely objects. The lint pass will see it in the precision figures.

The main changes:

- *service*: sense 3 (work for someone) now says the work or help is often given as a job or a duty. Sense 4 no longer says a religious service must take place in a church. The tennis sense now also covers a player's turn to serve. *A service charge* (an extra amount on a bill) was removed from sense 1, where it did not belong.
- *sex*: the usage note and the translators' note now describe **gender** as a person's identity and social role, not only "the social side of being a man or a woman". I rejected the objection to sense 2's definition: it already says the activity is "done for pleasure or to have children", which rules out the non-sexual cases the reviewer had in mind.
- *shower*: the party sense (*a baby shower*) now says "especially" before weddings and babies, and it is labelled Canadian as well as American.
- *sight*: *lose sight of* now gives both meanings, stop seeing something and forget an aim, in its definition, with an example for each. The *ironic* label on *not a pretty sight* was removed.
- *shoe*: the learner mistake *I wear my shoes and leave the house* can be read as correct (a habit), so it became *Wait a minute, I'm wearing my shoes* corrected to *putting on*.
- Smaller fixes: definitions on *shadow*, *shell*, *show* (*for show*), *shame* (*put someone to shame*), *sheep* (*count sheep*), and *shirt* (*the shirt off your back*); one British-sounding example on *shop*; the *shop*/*store* note; an example on *shoulder*.

I rejected, among others:

- Region objections on *shoulder* (the road edge, American) and *side* (a sports team, British; a side dish, American). In this dictionary a region label means "mainly used there", a settled house rule.
- Keeping *What form is it?* as a learner mistake on *shape*. It is limited to asking about a thing's outer form, where *What shape is it?* is the natural question.
- *Have a shock*, which the reviewer called unnatural. It is common, especially in British English.
