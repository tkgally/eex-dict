# 2026-10-12, run at 16:43 UTC, cycle 3: closure, five nouns and seven verbs

## What this cycle did

This was a **closure** cycle. Closure means writing entries for words the dictionary already uses but does not yet define: words that appear in a definition (the "closure" rows of the queue) and words named in an entry's word family (the "family" rows). The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose it after this run's build cycle.

Only five closure rows were waiting: *decoration*, *extra* (the noun), *oval* (the noun), *ribs*, and *sperm*. The row *ribs* was a plural form picked up from the definition of *lung*. Entries are written for the base form, so I marked *ribs* as a duplicate in the queue and claimed *rib* instead. The other seven words came from the family rows: the verbs *average*, *belittle*, *blacken*, *blind*, *brave*, *brief*, and *brown*, each named by an existing adjective or noun entry.

All twelve went through the full pipeline and are now marked `reviewed`. All 24 pronunciations (American and British) were confirmed. The plural of *sperm* cannot be predicted by the rules (it is usually *sperm*, sometimes *sperms*), so I added it to the dictionary's own table of irregular forms, `schema/inflection-exceptions.json`.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 39 issues, 32 of them "blocking" (an entry cannot be marked as reviewed until each one has a decision). I applied 29 and rejected 10. None needed to go to the owner.

Applied (each fix written in my own words):

- *sperm*: I had written two senses, the cell and the liquid. reviewer-a pointed out that the liquid is *semen*, and that "sperm donor" and "frozen sperm" refer to the cells. The entry now has one sense, with those examples moved into it. *Semen* is listed as a word to compare, with the note "the liquid that carries sperm", and the usage note says that people sometimes call the liquid *sperm* in everyday speech.
- *decoration*: the British subsense had described a room's paint, paper, and furniture. In British English the word means the work of painting and papering, or its result, and it now says so. The example and the note pointing to *decor* were changed to match.
- *extra*: the film sense said an extra is paid "a small amount" and appears only in films. It now covers television too and leaves out the pay.
- *rib*: the third sense joined two different things, a curved support (the ribs of an umbrella) and a raised line (the ribs of a sweater). The raised line is now its own subsense.
- *blacken*: a note about *blackened fish* was about the adjective, not the verb, so it was removed. Both reviewers flagged it.
- *blind*: the definition "unable to see, for always" now reads "permanently unable to see". A learner error (*The light blinded my eyes*) was removed, because that sentence is not clearly wrong.
- *brief*, *brown*, *average*, *belittle*: a usage note on *brief* had wrongly suggested the verb is unrelated to the adjective; a cooking example of *brown* is now marked as passive; a weak collocation of *average* was replaced; and *criticize* was removed as a synonym of *belittle*.

Rejected:

- reviewer-b asked three times for an "intransitive" pattern on verbs such as *brown* and *blacken*. The dictionary's closed list of patterns has no such value; whether a verb can be used without an object is recorded in a separate field, which already says so.
- reviewer-a objected to the definition of *oval* (*like an egg, or like a circle that is longer than it is wide*). It matches the reviewed adjective *oval*, and both shapes are called oval.
- *blind someone with science* stays labelled British, since a region label here means "mainly used there".

The vocabulary check (which allows only words from the defining list, or words that have an entry) then flagged five definitions. I reworded them: *medal* and *honor* became "a small piece of metal or cloth given ... as a sign of praise", *occasion* became "a party or a holiday", *background* became "behind the main actors", *technical* became "difficult", and *knitted* was dropped.

The tools added seven back-links (the mirror cross-references that keep links two-way). Two were on the wrong sense and I moved them by hand: *extra* on *addition* now sits under "thing or person added", and *brave* on *avoid* under "stay away". The *sperm* link on *egg* landed correctly on the egg-cell sense.
