# 2026-10-20, cycle 1 of the 12:44 UTC run: second review of *request* to *routine*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that had been through the two outside reviewers (the review panel, two language models from other companies) only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose review because it was the most overdue kind of work, and the block is always the oldest such entries. This time the block was twenty nouns drafted on 2026-10-07: *request, respect, rest, restaurant, result, rhythm, rice, ride, right, ring, risk, river, road, rock, roof, room, root, rope, route, routine*.

Before starting, the usual checks found nothing stranded: no open pull request from an earlier run, the four leftover branches already fully merged and already listed for the owner to prune, and nothing in the inbox.

Each entry went to both reviewers again. I read every issue against the entry and either changed the entry (*applied*) or left it as it was (*rejected*, with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`). Nothing was escalated to the owner.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Seven entries drew no issues at all (*restaurant, result, rhythm, rice, ride, rock, routine*). The reviewers raised 35 issues, 31 of them blocking (meaning the entry should not stand without a decision). I applied 20 and rejected 15.

Applied:

- ***respect***: the idiom *pay your respects* now means a formal visit or going to a funeral to show respect, especially for someone who has died. The translator's note that said the feeling is uncountable now says so only of the feeling senses and adds that the *detail* sense is countable (*in many respects*).
- ***rest*** and ***ring***: each had a usage note that described the entry as covering only its first sense (*the noun about relaxing*, *the round object*), while the entry also holds the music rest and the secret group. Both notes now cover every sense. The *rest* learner-error note now says *have a rest*, *take a rest*, or *get some rest* (not *get a rest*).
- ***rope***: the definition now says *twisting or weaving*; *learn the ropes* moved out of the explanation of *know the ropes* into a phrase of its own with two examples; the translator's note on rope, cord, and string is softened, since the words overlap.
- ***route***: the sense used in road names (*Route 9*) had a function-word definition (*used before a number...*); both reviewers flagged it, and it is now a noun phrase: *a main road that has a number as its name*.
- ***right***: a learner-error correction, *Turn right*, used the adverb in the noun entry; it is now *Turn to the right*, with a note that bare *turn right* is the adverb.
- Smaller: *river* lost a *literary* label on *a river of people*, and its translator's note says English *usually* uses *river* for a large one; *risk* (danger sense) replaced *a big risk* with *a possible risk*; *roof* now says *a roof over his children's heads*; *room* got a clearer learner error (*Is there a room for one more person in your car?*).

Rejected, with the main reasons:

- ***rest***: reviewer-a wanted *come to rest*, *at rest*, and *lay someone to rest* moved to verb or adjective entries. The house keyword rule puts an idiom under its first noun, here *rest*.
- ***ring***, ***river***: reviewer-b said phrase definitions must start with *to*. House style is the bare base form.
- Region labels: *room and board* and *down the road* (future) keep *American*, and *by rights* stays unlabeled; a region label means *mainly used there*.
- ***root***: the word-part sense (*the basic part of a word ... from which other words are formed*) is accurate for learners even for roots that cannot stand alone.
- ***room*** and ***rope***: *Is there place for my bag?* and *I tied the present with a rope* are real, common learner errors and stay.
- ***ring***: a circus ring is a space where animals and people perform; the definition stands.
