## What this cycle did

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue, the list of words waiting to be written. The next twelve band-1 words in queue order were all nouns. "Band 1" is the dictionary's label for its most basic words. The twelve are *confidence, conflict, confusion, connection, container, contest, contract, control, conversation, copy, corn*, and *corner*.

Each entry went through the full pipeline:

- format validation
- word forms from rules
- a pronunciation check against outside models and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary
- two outside reviewers
- my reading of every serious objection
- cross-referencing

All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each word) and all the plurals were verified. *Confusion* and *corn* have no plural in ordinary use.

These entries are as full as the first cycle's:

- **control** has seven senses: power, handling a car or a ball, control of your feelings, limits such as *price controls*, the controls of a machine, *passport control*, and a control in an experiment. It has four phrases (*out of control*, *under control*, *in control*, *beyond your control*), a usage note on *control of* and *control over*, and a note for translators: in many languages the look-alike word means *check*, so *a control of my blood pressure* is wrong in English.
- **connection** has six senses: a relation between things, a physical join, an internet or phone connection, a change of train or plane, a useful person you know, and a feeling of closeness. It also has *in connection with*.
- **corner** separates *in the corner* (of a room) from *on the corner* (of a street). It has a bend in the road, the corners of the mouth and eye, a quiet corner of the world, and a corner kick, plus five idioms: *around the corner*, *cut corners*, *turn the corner*, *in a tight corner*, and *back someone into a corner*.
- **confidence** covers confidence in yourself, trust in others, being sure, and *in confidence*. Its learner errors are *confidence to my teacher* (say *in*) and *a big confidence*.
- **conversation** explains that you *have* a conversation, not *do* or *make* one, and that *make conversation* means talking politely to fill a silence.
- **corn** explains that the word means the tall plant with yellow seeds in American English, but often wheat or grain in general in British English.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 37 issues, 21 of them "blocking". I accepted 27 and rejected 10. Seven of the rejections were the second reviewer's repeated objection to a settled house style: an adaptation note (a note for translators) may open with the headword in quotation marks.

Accepted, among others:

- The secret sense of *confidence* mixed two grammars. *In confidence* is uncountable, while *a confidence* (a secret you share) is countable and formal. The countable use is now a sub-sense.
- Three senses of *control* were redefined:
  - The machine sense now defines a single control (*the volume control*); *the controls* are all of them together.
  - *Passport control* is defined as a place, used after another noun.
  - An experimental control is defined as the standard the results are compared with.
- *Corn*'s contrast between American and British English had been stated too strongly in four places. British English uses *corn* for the American plant too.
- The *corner* of the mouth or eye was redefined as the point where the top and bottom edges meet.
- *A corner table* was moved to the room sense.
- The note on *contest* no longer says judges always choose the winner.
- The note on the verb *contract*'s stress now covers all its senses.

Rejected, besides the format objections:

- The claim that *control*'s note for translators was too broad. It says "many languages", which the style guide allows, and the false friend is well known.
- A complaint that a listed grammar code was not allowed. It is in the closed list.

Eight definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary, the list of about 2,400 basic words that definitions are written in. Examples are *sympathy*, *struggle*, *magazine*, *oats*, and *soccer*. All eight were reworded. One automatic queue row was wrong: *enforce* was guessed as a noun. It was corrected to the verb. An unrelated word *corn* (hard skin on the foot) is noted for a later separate entry.
