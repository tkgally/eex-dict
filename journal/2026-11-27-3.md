# Run report, 2026-11-27 (third run of the day)

## Cycle 1: closure

**What this cycle did.** The tool that picks each cycle's job (the selector) chose *closure*: writing entries for words the dictionary already uses or points to but has no entry for yet. Its reason was that closure had the largest scheduling debt of the eligible jobs; the day's budget had US$13.86 of US$15 left. Closure takes rows in a fixed order of sources. The first source, words used inside definitions, is empty, so the rows came from the second, *family*: words that an existing entry lists as a related form (for example, **afford** lists **affordable**). Twelve were claimed, the cycle's limit, all band-2 adjectives (band 2 is the second-most-important frequency group).

The twelve new entries:

- **affordable**: not too expensive, so that most people can pay for it. A note says how it differs from **cheap**, which can suggest poor quality.
- **aged**: three senses: having a stated age (*children aged six to ten*), very old (formal, *her aged parents*), and food and drink kept for a long time to improve it (*aged cheese*). The "very old" sense has two syllables (*AY-jid*), which is recorded as a second pronunciation.
- **amazed**, **annoyed**, **annoying**: each with its prepositions (*amazed at*, *annoyed with someone*, *annoyed about something*) and, for **amazed** and **annoyed**, the common learner mistake of using the *-ing* form for the person who feels the emotion.
- **associated**: two senses, both used only before a noun (*the associated costs*; *an associated company*). A usage note explains that *the costs associated with moving* is the verb **associate** used in the passive and sends the reader there.
- **athletic**: strong and good at sports, and connected with sports (*athletic shoes*), with a note for translators that the British **athletics** usually means only running, jumping, and throwing events, and a note that the stress moves (*ATH-leet*, *ath-LET-ik*).
- **balanced**: having parts in the right amounts (*a balanced diet*), fair to all sides (*a balanced report*), and calm and not easily upset (of a person).
- **beneficial**: having a good effect, with *beneficial to* and *mutually beneficial*, and a stress note (*BEN-uh-fit*, *ben-uh-FISH-ul*).
- **bloody**: covered in blood, violent (*a bloody battle*), and the British and Australian informal word that adds force (*I've lost my bloody keys*), with an explanation that many people hear it as a mild swear word. The same word before an adjective (*bloody good*) is an adverb; it has been added to the queue as its own entry.
- **boiling**: of water at the point of turning to steam, and, informally, very hot (*It's boiling in here*).
- **bottom**: in the lowest position (*the bottom shelf*) and lowest in a list or level (*the bottom group*).

**Checks.** The word-form tool produced the forms from its rules; only **bloody** has its own comparative (*bloodier*, *bloodiest*). The pronunciation panel (three models plus an open pronouncing dictionary, consulted only to check) agreed with every transcription except **aged**: the panel preferred the two-syllable form, while the open dictionary agrees with the one-syllable form we use as the main one. Because the word really has both, the entry keeps the one-syllable form as the main one, lists the other as a variant for the "very old" sense, and carries a flag saying the pronunciation was disputed. One line in the curator file records this for the owner.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewer models raised 64 issues, 2 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry can be published). Both came from reviewer B on **associated**: my first draft treated *associated with* (*the risks associated with sport*) as the adjective, but it is the verb in the passive, and the dictionary keeps each part of speech in its own entry. I agreed and rebuilt the first sense around the uses before a noun. Of the logged decisions on minor issues, I applied 31 and rejected 10. Applied: clearer definitions for **affordable** and **aged**, the senses of **balanced** reordered so that *a balanced diet* comes first, more natural examples (*bloody footprints*, *fill out the form*, *lead a balanced life*), the house marks for words and quoted phrases in several translator notes, signposts that tell senses apart better, and a note in **bloody** that repeated its labels removed. Rejected: reviewer A's repeated claim that *comparative with more* is not a grammar code (it is one of the house codes, a settled question), a request for a "rude" attitude label (no such label exists; the explanation says it), and a request to cover *associated with a charity* (the passive verb again).

The cross-reference tool added the matching back-links it could place and left seven where it could not tell which sense to link; I placed those by hand (for example, **associated** now appears among the synonyms of **connected** and **related**).

**Spend.** US$0.34 for this cycle, almost all reviewer B and the pronunciation panel; US$1.48 of US$15 for the day.

**What did not work.** Nothing broke. The draft of **associated** shows a recurring risk with past-participle adjectives: it is easy to write the passive verb into the adjective entry.

**What is next.** The next cycle, if the clock allows, takes whatever the selector picks; more family rows remain for closure.
