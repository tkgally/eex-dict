## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models review every field of every entry. They raised 36 issues; I applied 22 and rejected 14. None needed the owner.

Applied, among others:

- *Enforce*, sense 2, had said "make people accept or do something by using power" and gave *enforce your opinions on other people* as an example. That use belongs to **impose**. The sense now covers keeping silence or order by using power. The example became *enforce discipline in the class*, and *impose* moved from "synonym" to "compare".
- *Signal*: the radio and phone sense no longer says "a set of waves", and now includes information as well as sound and pictures. The traffic sense now covers trains as well as drivers. *Traffic light* moved from "synonym" to "compare", because a traffic light is only one kind of signal.
- *Lack*: the explanation now gives the grammar rule and its exception together. The noun is uncountable, but it is often used with **a** (*a lack of money*). This follows style guide section 7.
- *Sleeve*: the "cover" sense now covers anything flat, not only records and photographs.
- *To death* (under *death*) is now defined as "extremely, or so much that you can hardly stand it" instead of "very much".
- *Amuse*: the learner error *I was very amusing at the show* could also be correct English, for someone who entertained others. It now reads *I was very amusing by his jokes*, which is clearly wrong and is corrected to **amused**.

Rejected:

- Reviewer-a said *skater* does not include skateboarders. In current use it often does, and the usage note also gives *skateboarder*.
- Reviewer-a wanted *force* removed from the *enforce* word family, and *sign* from the *signal* word family. Both words are the base the other is built on, and the link helps learners.
- Reviewer-b objected that a learner error and an adaptation note on *lack* (noun) mention the verb. They show the noun pattern (*lack of*) being carried over onto the verb, which is exactly where learners go wrong, so they stay.
- Reviewer-b asked for "singular or uncountable" on *lack*. That value is not in the closed vocabulary; the explanation now states the exception instead.
- Two reviewer-b comments withdrew themselves in their own text ("This is correct").
