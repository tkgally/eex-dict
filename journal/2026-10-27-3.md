## Cycle 1: closure

**What the cycle did.** The scheduler chose *closure*: writing entries for words the dictionary already uses or points to but has no entry for. The `closure` list (words used inside definitions) is empty, so the words came from the `family` list (related forms that other entries name in their word-family boxes). Twelve were claimed. Two of them, *run* and *set* as nouns, are large, so I released four lighter adjectives (*lost*, *middle*, *own*, *south*) back to the queue for a later cycle and wrote eight entries in full:

- **run** (noun): ten senses, from running as exercise through a trip, a series of wins or losses, an attempt to be elected, a play's time on stage, a point in baseball, a sudden demand (*a run on the bank*), a broken line in tights (American), a ski run, and a fenced area for animals. Seven idioms, including *in the long run*, *on the run*, and *give someone a run for their money*.
- **set** (noun): seven senses: a group of things that belong together, a television, part of a tennis match, a stage or filming set, a group of songs at a concert, a group of repeats of an exercise, and the mathematics sense.
- **back** (adjective): *the back door*, *back roads*, *back pay*.
- **better** and **best** (adjectives): the comparison forms of *good*, the "more/most sensible" uses (*it's better to ask*), *better* meaning less ill, and *best friend*; idioms *be better off*, *better late than never*, *better safe than sorry*, *better than nothing*, *best of all*.
- **boring**, **exciting**, **interesting** (adjectives), each with the learner error that mixes up the *-ing* and *-ed* forms (*I am interesting in music*).

Every entry went through the full pipeline: format check, the inflection tool, the pronunciation check (all sixteen transcriptions agreed with the pronunciation panel and with the open CMU pronouncing dictionary), the two-reviewer panel, my adjudication, and the vocabulary and cross-reference checks.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 82 issues, 11 of them blocking (must be settled before an entry is published). I applied 19 and rejected 8 with logged reasons.

- The most useful catch: I had marked *better* and *best* "not gradable" so that the inflection tool would not invent *betterer*, but both words take *much better*, *far better*, so the label was wrong. I removed it and instead added two lines to the inflection exceptions table, which now shows "already the comparative of good" and "already the superlative of good". This table is allowed to change in the same pull request as the entry that needs it.
- A volleyball set is made of points, not games; the definition now says "games or points".
- The core idea of *back* began "Something back is...", which is not English; reworded.
- An example for *make a run for it* (running to the car in the rain) was not an escape; replaced.
- Coverage: both reviewers noted missing senses. I added the election sense and the animal-pen sense to *run*, the exercise sense to *set*, and *be better off* to *better*. I did not add *the runs* (an illness word) or the print-run sense; they can come in a later review.
- Rejected: one reviewer wanted *best of all* moved to an adverb entry; the house rule puts an idiom under its first content word, whatever its part of speech. A flap symbol in the American transcription of *exciting* is not house practice. *Had better* belongs under the verb *have* by the same keyword rule, not under *better*; the `have-v` entry does not have it yet, so it is on the review list in `NEXT.md`.
- Several signposts (the short labels that help a reader pick a sense) were longer than the three-word limit; all shortened.

The vocabulary check refused seven words in definitions that have no entries (*cricket*, *stockings*, *slope*, *sled*, *tennis*, *volleyball*, *scenery*); each definition was reworded with plainer words.

## Cycle 2: lint

**What the cycle did.** The scheduler forced a *lint* pass, the routine health check of the whole dictionary that runs every ten runs. It has a mechanical half (tools that check caps, links, cross-references, and the queue) and a judgment half (reading the tools' reports and the wiki's own pages for contradictions). It costs almost nothing: the only paid step is the band vote below.

**Mechanical checks.** Word caps on the rule files and the wiki all pass; the wiki stands at 29,981 of its 30,000 words, so I condensed two old paragraphs of the reviewer-precision note before adding to it. Every relative link resolves. The eight references to "ruled-out" targets (words the owner ruled should not have entries, such as *look for* as a phrasal verb) are unchanged; they belong to the cross-reference audit already on the list.

**Cross-references.** When entry A names entry B as a synonym, antonym, or "compare" word, B should name A back. The tool wrote these mirror links in seven entries on its own. For ten more it could not tell which sense of B the link belongs to, so I placed them by hand: for example, *wonderful* lists *amazing* as a synonym, and the link back goes on sense 2 of *amazing* (very good), not sense 1 (surprising); *end* lists *start* as an antonym, so *start* sense 1 (the beginning) now names *end*.

**Reports.** The "promises" report (a note that says a word "has its own entry" when no entry or queue row exists) shows the same seven false alarms as last time. The circular-definition report lists 320 definitions that use their own headword; most are harmless (*all the people who live in a town*), and they are judged one by one in review. The missing-senses report lists 724 pairs for the cross-reference audit.

**Band vote.** Thirty-three queue rows added by tools since the last vote had no frequency band (a band is the editorial estimate of how common a word is, 1 to 5). The three word-list models voted (US$0.006). Three wide disagreements were settled at the lower middle vote. Two rows were plainly wrong and marked duplicates: *relieved* queued as a verb (it is a form of *relieve*) and *glad* as a noun (it is an adjective, and the right row exists). Both came from the known weakness of the vocabulary checker in guessing a word's part of speech. A third row had a capital letter (*Determinant*); I lowercased it and wrote a short note about it in the wiki so that a later run can fix the cause.

**Reviewer precision.** Precision is the share of a reviewer's issues that I end up applying. A family of issues that falls under 30 percent over twenty or more decisions is switched off for that reviewer. Reviewer A (the Claude model) stands at 0.80 over 1,563 decisions, reviewer B at 0.79 over 526. Reviewer B's spelling-and-format family is at 0.28 but stays on by the owner's ruling; reviewer A's inflection family (0.25) and reviewer B's phrase family (0.18) are under the line but have fewer than twenty decisions each. No switch-offs.

**Spend.** US$0.006; the day stands at about US$2.58 of US$15.

**What is next.** The next lint is due in ten runs. The queue and the review list in `NEXT.md` are unchanged apart from the counts.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new.

## Cycle 3: build

**What the cycle did.** The scheduler chose *build*: new entries taken from the queue in the order the owner set. The next rows were twelve band-1 function words, all of them uses that other entries already point to as "a separate entry":

- **less** and **same** as pronouns (*I eat less than I used to*; *I'll have the same*), with the reply *same to you* as a phrase;
- five prepositions of more than one word or a second part of speech: **because of**, **in front of**, **instead of**, **next to**, and **than** before a pronoun or an amount (*taller than me*, *more than an hour*);
- five conjunctions: **as if**, **as soon as**, **even if**, **even though**, and **like** before a clause (*like I said*).

Function words need an explanation more than a definition, so most senses carry a short note on use, for example that **as soon as** takes a present tense even about the future, or that **because of** cannot introduce a clause. Each entry has at most two learner errors, chosen from mistakes learners really make (*Even though it was late, but we stayed*; *She is taller then me*).

**Pronunciation.** For five of the multi-word headwords the pronunciation panel (three models that transcribe independently) disagreed with my first transcriptions. I asked the panel for its own answers: all three used weak forms of the small words, as most speakers do (*əz ˈɪf* for *as if*, *ˈnɛkst tə* for *next to*, *ˈi.vən ɪf* for *even if*). That matches house practice for *in spite of*, so I changed them, and all 24 transcriptions are now verified. One re-check of *next to* was needed because a panel member's answer varied between calls.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** 48 issues, 8 of them blocking; 18 applied and 7 rejected, all logged.

- Both reviewers caught a circular definition: *same* was defined with "the same thing". Redefined.
- *Next to* lacked its *almost* use (*next to impossible*, *next to no traffic*); added as a third sense.
- The definitions of *even if* and of *in front of* sense 2 were awkward ("where someone can see"); both rewritten.
- Several small duplications (the same rule in an explanation and a learner-error note) were removed, keeping one copy.
- Rejected: both reviewers wanted *rather than* and *other than* under *than*. By the house keyword rule an idiom goes under its first content word, so *rather than* belongs to *rather*, where it already is. *Other than* belongs to *other*, which lacks it; it is on the review list in `NEXT.md`. Also rejected: *same here* (already a phrase of the adjective *same*) and moving *As if!* to an interjection entry.

The cross-reference tool found five pairs where one entry calls a word a synonym and the other calls it only "compare". I matched the new entries to the existing ones, so the audit list does not grow. A queue row for *rather than* as a preposition, created by my own first draft, was marked a duplicate.

**Found along the way.** The conjunction *because* points to an entry `because-prep` that should be `because-of-prep`. This is noted in `NEXT.md` for its next review, not fixed here, because the cross-reference is part of an entry's reviewed content.

**Spend.** About US$0.33 for this cycle; the day stands at US$2.91 of US$15.

## Cycle 4: review

**What the cycle did.** The scheduler chose *review*: rereading entries that have already been published, both to fix errors and to fill gaps. There were no drafts or reader reports waiting, so I took six entries. Three came from earlier cycles of this run, which had each found something missing. The other three were the next entries in the coverage pass, the owner's 2026-10-24 instruction to reread the most important everyday words, the ones with the most senses first.

- **have** (verb): added *had better* (*You'd better hurry*) and *have got to* (*I've got to go*), both very common and both missing. Following the house rule that an idiom goes under its first verb, they belong here, not under *better* or *got*.
- **other** (pronoun): added *other than* (*nobody other than Sam*), *among others*, and *one after the other*. I also replaced an example that named two real cities; house policy allows no place names smaller than a continent.
- **because** (conjunction): the earlier cycle thought its pointer to `because-prep` was a mistake. It is not. It points to the newer informal use *because reasons*, which is analyzed as a preposition, and the note on the link says so. My `NEXT.md` line was wrong and is removed. I trimmed two notes that repeated other fields.
- **pick up** (phrasal verb): the owner's 2026-10-24 ruling adds a `separable` code to phrasal verbs whose object can go between the verb and the particle (*pick it up*), so the transitive senses now carry it. The core idea (the one-sentence summary at the top of an entry with many senses) had grown to a long list, and I cut it to one plain sentence. Three examples that did not fit their senses were replaced.
- **come out** (phrasal verb): added the sense *fall out* (*One of my teeth came out*) and gave flowers opening (*The roses are coming out*) its own sense. The definition of the sense about telling people you are gay was vague, and I rewrote it.
- **field** (noun): added *field of vision*, rewrote the core idea to cover all eight senses, and removed a usage note that repeated a cross-reference note.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** 92 issues, 11 of them blocking; 22 applied and 6 rejected. Both reviewers caught that *have* listed *get* as a synonym while also saying, in a note, that *get* means something different; *get* is now only under "compare". A definition of *pick up* used the word *notice* to define *notice*. Rejected: one reviewer called *not used in continuous tenses* "not a pattern", but it is on the closed list. Another wanted the hand marks removed from *others* in the pronoun's examples. Pronoun entries have no inflections table, though, so without the marks the site would not show *others* in bold. Most of the minor issues were notes repeating each other (the notes rules of 2026-10-24 say one fact, one field); I removed the copies.
