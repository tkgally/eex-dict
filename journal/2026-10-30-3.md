Some choices in these entries:
- **assure** separates its main use (telling someone something so they stop worrying, with the person as the object) from a formal use (*assure success*). It has a short note on choosing between **assure**, **ensure**, and **insure**. Its learner error is *I assure that...* without the person.
- **exhaust** and **exhibit** each carry a pronunciation note: the **h** is silent and the **x** sounds like *gz*.
- **exit** notes that American speakers usually say *EG-zit* and British speakers *EK-sit*, and that both are correct.
- **experience** warns translators that in some languages a look-alike word also means *do an experiment*.
- **face** has five senses: pointing toward something, dealing with a problem bravely, having a problem ahead, *can't face* doing something, and playing against someone in sport. It has two real learner errors: *can't face to tell him* and *face to the problem*.
- **fish** has the learner error *go to fishing*.
- **file** has a short usage note that points to the separate verb **file** meaning smoothing something with a metal tool. That verb is not in the queue yet.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 68 issues, 5 of them blocking (a blocking issue must be settled before the entry can be published). I applied 56 and rejected 13. One decision was logged twice, because I first applied a change and then reversed it.

- **Blocking issues:**
  - Three were about one **exhaust** example, *she was completely exhausted*. That sentence shows the adjective, not the verb. I replaced it with an active sentence.
  - The movie sense of **film** said "in a particular place". That is not part of the meaning, so I removed it.
  - A reviewer asked for *let's face it* to be added to **face**. I rejected this. The house keyword rule puts an idiom under its first noun, or under its first verb if it has no noun. That verb here is **let**, so the phrase belongs in **let**, beside *let's say*. It is now a to-do in `NEXT.md`.
- **Minor fixes worth knowing about:**
  - Signposts (the short labels that help a reader pick a sense) were shortened to the house limit of three words.
  - The definition of the first **file** sense no longer uses the noun **file**.
  - **exclude**'s "rule out" sense now reads "decide or show that something is not possible or not true".
  - **fish** now covers *fish the river*.
- **A decision I reversed.** At first I accepted a reviewer's point that **look** is not a synonym of **face**. Then I checked **look**: its own sense 4 is exactly this use (*the house looks south*). I put the synonym back with a short note and logged the reversal.
- **Other rejections:** extra verb patterns asked for where the closed list has none; *face the music*, which is already under **music**; and two requests to reorder or merge senses that differ in subject and collocations.

**Back-links placed by hand.** The cross-reference tool links entries both ways, but it no longer guesses which sense of the other entry to link from. Two new synonyms needed that: **ensure** now names **assure**, and **tire** now names **exhaust**, each in its first sense.
