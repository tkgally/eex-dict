## Cycle 1: build

**What this cycle did.** The selector is the script that picks each cycle's kind of work from the project's state. It chose **build**, which means writing new entries from the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written). It said this kind of work had fallen furthest behind, and it allowed up to 12 entries and US$3 of paid model calls. I wrote the next twelve common ("band 2") verbs in queue order: **assure**, **exchange**, **exclude**, **exhaust**, **exhibit**, **exit**, **experience**, **face**, **fade**, **file**, **film**, and **fish**. Every one went through the full pipeline:

- the schema check;
- verb forms generated by rule;
- the pronunciation panel (three models plus an open pronouncing dictionary, voting on my transcriptions);
- both reviewers on every field;
- my reading of every issue;
- the cross-reference check.

The dictionary now has 3,353 entries, all reviewed.

Before this cycle I checked for work an earlier run might have left behind. There were no open pull requests and no reader reports filed as issues. The five leftover `claude/` branches hold nothing that is not already on `main`, and each one is already listed for pruning in the curator file (the list of questions and chores for you).

Some choices in these entries:
- **assure** separates its main use (telling someone something so they stop worrying, with the person as the object) from a formal use (*assure success*). It has a short note on choosing between **assure**, **ensure**, and **insure**. Its learner error is *I assure that...* without the person.
- **exhaust** and **exhibit** each carry a pronunciation note: the **h** is silent and the **x** sounds like *gz*.
- **exit** notes that American speakers usually say *EG-zit* and British speakers *EK-sit*, and that both are correct.
- **experience** warns translators that in some languages a look-alike word also means *do an experiment*.
- **face** has five senses: pointing toward something, dealing with a problem bravely, having a problem ahead, *can't face* doing something, and playing against someone in sport. It has two real learner errors: *can't face to tell him* and *face to the problem*.
- **fish** has the learner error *go to fishing*.
- **file** has a short usage note that points to the separate verb **file** meaning smoothing something with a metal tool. That verb is not in the queue yet.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 68 issues, 5 of them blocking (a blocking issue must be settled before the entry can be published). I applied 56 and rejected 13. One decision was logged twice, because I first applied a change and then reversed it.

- **Blocking issues:**
  - Three were about one **exhaust** example, *she was completely exhausted*. That sentence shows the adjective, not the verb. I replaced it with an active sentence.
  - The movie sense of **film** said "in a particular place". That is not part of the meaning, so I removed it.
  - A reviewer asked for *let's face it* to be added to **face**. I rejected this. The house keyword rule puts an idiom under its first noun, or under its first verb if it has no noun. That verb here is **let**, so the phrase belongs in **let**, beside *let's say*. It is now a to-do in `NEXT.md`.
- **Minor fixes worth knowing about:**
  - Signposts (the short labels that help a reader pick a sense) were shortened to the house limit of three words.
  - The definition of the first **file** sense no longer uses the noun **file**.
  - **exclude**'s "rule out" sense now reads "decide or show that something is not possible or not true".
  - **fish** now covers *fish the river*.
- **A decision I reversed.** At first I accepted a reviewer's point that **look** is not a synonym of **face**. Then I checked **look**: its own sense 4 is exactly this use (*the house looks south*). I put the synonym back with a short note and logged the reversal.
- **Other rejections:** extra verb patterns asked for where the closed list has none; *face the music*, which is already under **music**; and two requests to reorder or merge senses that differ in subject and collocations.

**Back-links placed by hand.** The cross-reference tool links entries both ways, but it no longer guesses which sense of the other entry to link from. Two new synonyms needed that: **ensure** now names **assure**, and **tire** now names **exhaust**, each in its first sense.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. The reviewer tool needs an index number when one reviewer raises two issues on the same field, so a few decisions were logged a second time with it.

## Cycle 2: build

**What this cycle did.** The selector chose **build** again, with the same limits: 12 entries and US$3. I wrote the next twelve common verbs in queue order: **flee**, **flood**, **forecast**, **function**, **gain**, **glance**, **glue**, **grab**, **grade**, **graduate**, **grin**, and **grind**. Each went through the same full pipeline as in cycle 1. The panel verified all 24 pronunciations. Three verbs have irregular forms, and those come from the project's exceptions table: *fled*, *forecast* (with *forecasted* noted as also used), and *ground*. The dictionary now has 3,365 entries, all reviewed.

Some choices in these entries:
- **flee** has a usage note: the word is common in news and stories, while people usually say **run away** or **escape** in conversation.
- **gain** has the learner error *gain money* for *earn money*.
- **glance** has the learner error *glanced the clock* for *glanced at the clock*.
- **graduate** has a usage note on British and American use (*graduate high school* is American). It also has a pronunciation note: the verb ends like *late*, while the noun ends with a weak sound.
- **grin** holds the idiom *grin and bear it*. Its keyword is **grin** because the phrase has no noun and **grin** is its first verb.
- **grade** in the sense of marking schoolwork carries the American label, because British English mainly says **mark**.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** 56 issues, 16 of them blocking. I applied 34 and rejected 22, which is more rejections than usual.

- **Blocking issues I fixed:**
  - **flood**: the definition of the feeling-and-light sense covered only "fill something", but the examples (*relief flooded over him*, *memories came flooding back*) take no object. The definition now covers both uses.
  - **flood**: "of a river or a lake" sat at the front of a definition. It moved to the explanation, as the style guide requires.
  - **graduate**: the entry mentioned *graduate high school* but its grammar said the verb takes no object. Sense 1 now takes an object or none, with an example.
  - **grind**: *the gears ground as he changed speed* became *...as he changed gear*.
- **Blocking issues I rejected:**
  - Reviewer B said the irregular forms *fled*, *ground*, and *glued* must be bolded by hand in examples. The style guide says the site bolds every form listed in the entry's inflections, and these are listed.
  - *grind to a halt* belongs under **halt**, because the keyword rule picks the first noun.
  - *grind away*, *grind down*, and *grind out* are phrasal verbs, which get entries of their own. I added all three to the queue.
- **Other rejections:**
  - Reviewer B three times called *usually passive* a grammar code. It is a verb pattern in our closed list.
  - Two synonym objections (**bag** for **grab**, **correct** for **grade**). In each case the other entry's matching sense already lists the word, so the link stays.
  - *gain ground*, which belongs under **ground**.
- **Fixes worth knowing:**
  - **grab** gained a subsense for grabbing power or land, labeled disapproving.
  - **glue** no longer uses **glue** in its own definition.
  - **gain**'s core idea now covers *gain on someone*.

**Reviewer noise.** Reviewer B's precision this cycle was low (0.48, meaning about half its issues were accepted), mostly because of the hand-mark and *usually passive* objections. I recorded the pattern in `wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`. If it recurs, a later run can add a line to the reviewers' house rules.

**Back-link placed by hand:** **serve** sense 6 ("be useful") now names **function**.

**Spend.** US$0.31 this cycle; US$2.42 so far today.

**What is next.** Band-2 verbs continue in queue order.

**What needs you.** Nothing new.
