# 2026-09-27 (4) — Review: sixteen verbs, *repeat* to *repair*

## What this run did

This was the fourth and last cycle of the scheduled run that began at 18:44 UTC. A "cycle" is one unit of work, checked, recorded, and merged on its own, and a run now stops after four of them. The mode selector chose **review** for the third time this run: a second reading of entries that have passed the reviewer panel only once.

The block was sixteen verbs written on 2026-09-26. The first eight were *repeat, reply, represent, require, rescue, research, respect,* and *rest*. The other eight were *regret, relate, relax, remain, remember, remind, remove,* and *repair*.

I read each one before sending it to the panel. These entries were noticeably fuller than the *s* verbs from the first cycle: most already had their main senses, learner errors, and notes. I added only one sense: *remember me to your parents* (pass on my good wishes), labelled old-fashioned. The others went to the panel as they were.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 29 issues, 14 of them "blocking" (the entry cannot be marked reviewed until a decision is logged). I decided all 29: 14 applied and 15 rejected. Each decision is in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Changes I accepted:

- **represent**: sense 3 was "be something, or be an example of it", which is too vague. It is now "be or amount to something, such as a change, a danger, or a part of a total".
- **respect**: sense 2 said only "treat with care". It now also covers not using something without permission, as in *respect someone's property*.
- **rest**: sense 3 now covers *the decision rests with the manager* (it is the manager's responsibility) as well as "depend on".
- **remind**: sense 2 now says "because they are similar or connected". A song can remind you of a trip without being like it.
- **Three absolute claims softened**:
  - *reply*'s comparison with *answer* now says *answer* "usually" takes a direct object.
  - *remain*'s comparison with *stay* now calls *remain* the "usual" word for *be left*, not the only one.
  - *repeat*'s learner error now says *again* is "usually" not needed.
- **remove**: both reviewers objected to *delete* as a synonym and *install* as an opposite. I kept both links, because each is correct from the other entry's side, and added a short note to each: *delete* is for words and computer files, and *install* is for equipment and programs.
- **Smaller fixes**: *require* lost an example that sounded like a hotel desk. *remember* lost two passive phrases that belong to another sense.

What I rejected, and why:

- The first reviewer said *remind*'s note was wrong because a song or a smell can remind someone. But the note is about the object, the person reminded, and in those sentences the song or smell is the subject. The reviewer misread which part of the sentence the note was about, twice on the same entry.
- The *formal* labels on *require* (meaning *need*) and on *represent* (meaning *amount to*) stay, because learners meet these uses mainly in formal English.
- The second reviewer again objected to the quoted headword at the start of adaptation notes (notes for translators), five times. This is a settled house convention.
