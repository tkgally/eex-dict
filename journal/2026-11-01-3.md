# Run report, 2026-11-01 (third run of the day)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the script that picks each run's kind of work) chose *build*, which means writing new entries from the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written). It allowed up to 12 new entries and a spending ceiling of US$3.00. I claimed the next 12 rows in queue order, all band-2 verbs (band 2 is the second-most-common of five frequency bands the project assigns by model vote): *specialize*, *spin*, *spoil*, *spray*, *squeeze*, *stare*, *state*, *store*, *straighten*, *stress*, *strike*, and *struggle*. All twelve went through the full pipeline and are now `reviewed`, the status that lets an entry appear on the public site.

The entries range from one sense (*stare*, *state*) to eleven (*strike*: hit, stop work as a protest, happen suddenly, attack, an idea coming to mind, *strike someone as*, *be struck by*, a clock, a match, finding oil or water, and striking a deal). *Specialize* records the British spelling *specialise* as a variant. *Stare* has a short box that explains how to choose between *look*, *stare*, *gaze*, and *glare*. *Spoil* has the phrase *spoil someone rotten*, and *strike* has *strike it rich*. Learner-error notes went only where learners really make the mistake: *specialize on* for *specialize in*, *staring me* for *staring at me*, and *strikes me like* for *strikes me as*.

**Checks.** The inflection tool generated every verb form, from rules or from the existing exceptions table (*spun*, *struck*, and the note on British *spoilt*). The pronunciation panel (three outside models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary) agreed with all 24 American and British transcriptions.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Both reviewers, A (a Claude model) and B (a Google model), read every field. They raised 87 issues, 9 of them *blocking* (meaning the entry cannot be marked reviewed until I rule on them). I applied 61 and rejected 26, with a logged reason for each; none needed escalation to you.

Applied, among others:
- *spin* had no sense for making a ball spin in sports; I added it as a subsense.
- Three entry-level translation notes (*spin*, *spoil*, *strike*) were generic ("these senses are different words in other languages") and named no specific trap, which the house rules forbid; I removed them.
- An example in *stress* stated a rule about English word stress instead of showing the verb in use, and an example in *strike* belonged to a different sense; both were replaced.
- Several definitions were too narrow for their own examples (*squeeze* for budgets, *struggle* for "having difficulty", *straighten* for tidying a room) and were widened.
- *spray* and *squeeze* gained subsenses (things thrown out with force; squeezing money or information out of someone), and *stress* now covers the transitive informal use, *this noise is stressing me*.
- Four signposts (the short labels that help a reader pick a sense) were over the three-word limit and were shortened; an example about body fat was a science claim and was replaced.

Rejected, among others:
- Requests to add idioms whose first noun is another word: *strike a chord*, *strike a balance*, *strike a pose*, *strike fear into someone*, *stare into space*, *stare someone in the face*. The house keyword rule puts an idiom under its first noun. *Stare into space* is already under *space*. The others are noted in `NEXT.md` for their noun entries, and `chord-n` was added to the queue.
- Reviewer B asked eleven times for *struck* to be hand-marked in examples. The site marks every listed inflected form automatically, so this is not needed.
- Reviewer A said two closed-list verb patterns were not patterns. They are in the closed list.

**Cross-references.** The tool that mirrors synonym and compare links could not pick a sense in ten target entries (*treat*, *rot*, *press*, *look*, *declare*, *worry*, *attack*, *occur*, *impress*, *fight*), so I placed those back-links by hand. I dropped two weak synonym links from *strike*: *light* for striking a match and *make* for striking a deal.

**Spend.** US$0.43 this cycle (review panel and pronunciation panel); US$2.45 of the US$15 daily cap is spent so far today.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. I ran the pronunciation check on two entries a second time by mistake, which cost a few cents.

**What is next.** More band-2 verbs in queue order. The next lint pass (the periodic whole-dictionary check) is due in two runs.

**What needs you.** Nothing new from this cycle. The standing items are in `NEXT.md` under "For the owner".

## Cycle 2: review

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose *review*, which means rereading entries that are already published. There were no drafts and no reader reports open. The work therefore went to the coverage pass: a full reading of the most important band-1 entries (the most common words) to find senses, phrases, and patterns that are missing, taking the entries with the most senses first. I read three six-sense entries that nobody had read since 2026-10-24: *feeling* (noun), *change* (verb), and *world* (noun). That brings the coverage pass to 136 entries. I kept the block small so that each entry got a full reading.

**What I added before the reviewers saw the entries.**
- *feeling*: a use for strong emotion in the way you do something (*she sang with feeling*), and a new sense for a natural understanding or ability (*a real feeling for music*).
- *change*: the pattern *change something into something* (*change this sofa into a bed*) and the phrase *change for the better*.
- *world*: a grammar code saying it often goes before another noun (*world peace*, *a world record*). Two examples stated real-world facts, which house style forbids, and I replaced them.
- I removed a usage note on *world* that only repeated a learner-error note, and a grammar note on *feeling* that only repeated two senses.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The reviewers raised 33 issues, none blocking. I applied 28 and rejected 5, each with a logged reason.

Applied:
- *feeling*: the sense for feelings that can be hurt was reworded. A missing use, *bad feeling between people*, was added as a subsense. The sense about the ability to feel no longer uses *feel* in its own definition.
- *change*: the plural rule (*change trains*, *change jobs*) was stated in two places, and only the learner-error note keeps it now. A synonym note on *alter* repeated the box that explains the difference between similar words, so it was removed.
- *world*: new subsense *the real world* / *the outside world*; new phrases *mean the world to someone* and *do someone a world of good*. The nature sense was redefined to fit *the natural world*. Two generic translation notes were removed.

Rejected:
- Moving *change your mind* to the verb. The house keyword rule keeps it under *mind*, where it already is.
- Calling *turn something into something* a poor synonym for *change something into something*. It is a true synonym.
- Two requests to restructure senses whose grammar and translation are shared.

**What did not work.** Reviewer B's first reply on *world* had no verdicts in it. I re-ran that reviewer alone and it came back clean.

**Spend.** US$0.22 this cycle; US$2.67 of the US$15 daily cap is spent so far today.

**What is next.** The coverage pass continues with the unread six-sense entries (*wing*, *wide*, *weight*, *use*, and others), then the five-sense ones. The lint pass (the periodic whole-dictionary check) is due next run.

**What needs you.** Nothing new.
