## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: new entries written from the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written). The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose it after this run's review cycle. It is the third cycle of this run.

I claimed the next twelve band-1 words (band 1 is the most common words, the ones learners meet first) and wrote all twelve: *social*, *soft*, *solid*, *special*, *specific*, *spicy*, *spiritual*, *square*, *stiff*, *straight*, *strange*, and *strict*. All are adjectives. Each went through the full pipeline (format check, generated word forms, a pronunciation check, two outside reviewers, my decisions on what they found, and the final checks), and all twelve are now marked `reviewed`, so they will appear on the public site.

Some points from the entries:

- *square* explains the difference between *ten square meters* (an area) and *ten meters square* (a square ten meters long on each side, which is one hundred square meters), in a usage note and in a note for translators.
- *strange* warns learners from languages that use one word for *strange* and *foreign*. Its learner error shows *people from other countries* as the correction.
- *special* explains the difference between the adverbs *especially* and *specially*, and has the phrase *nothing special*.
- *spicy* explains why *hot soup* can be unclear in English (high temperature or a burning taste).
- *specific* warns that learners sometimes say it like *pacific*.

All 24 transcriptions (American and British) were confirmed on the first pass. Three words, *special*, *specific*, and *spiritual*, needed a grammar code on every sense before the word-form tool would decide that they make their comparatives with *more*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers (outside language models from two different companies, who check every field) raised 52 issues, 46 of them marked blocking. I accepted 45 and rejected 7. None needed to go to the owner. *spiritual* came back with no issues.

The main pattern this cycle: adverb uses had crept into adjective entries three times. *Sit up straight* and *stand up straight* use the adverb *straight*, *He's been acting strange* uses *strange* as an adverb, and the note on *bored stiff* described the adverb *stiff*. The project keeps one part of speech per entry, so I removed the examples and reduced the *stiff* note to a pointer to the adverb entry. I will watch for this in later adjective batches; it is noted in `NEXT.md`.

Other changes:

- ***straight*.** The sense meaning *not gay* had an *informal* label and the signpost *not gay*. The reviewer was right that this use is standard, not informal, so the label is gone and the signpost now says *opposite sex*.
- ***solid*.** *Solid gold* now means "made of the same material all the way through, not just on the outside", which is the real contrast (with gold-covered objects). The *solid evidence* sense now says "good and strong, and based on facts or skill".
- ***social*.** The *social life* sense had an example about social media, which is a different meaning (websites and apps). I replaced it. The *informal* label on *a very social person* was removed.
- ***stiff***, ***soft***. Two word combinations did not fit their definitions: *a stiff price* means a high price, and *a soft target* means something easy to attack. Both were removed.
- ***strict***, ***spicy***, ***specific***, ***square***. One definition each was made more exact.

What I rejected: a complaint that *stiff* (of a cooking mixture) should not have an explanation (the house rule puts a subject restriction there); a request to drop the British label on *soft option* (a region label means *mainly used there*); and three synonym complaints where the link was a good near-match (*square* and *cool*, *specific* and *exact*, *special care* and *particular care*).
