# 2026-10-21, cycle 4 of the 00:43 UTC run: second review of *carpet* to *server*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that had been through the two outside reviewers (the review panel, two language models from other companies) only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose it as the most overdue kind of work. The block is always the oldest such entries. This time it was twenty entries from 2026-10-07: the verbs *carpet, chance, characterize, clothe, club, coin, computerize, condition, confide, conflict, prepare*, and the nouns *season, seat, second, security, seed, seller, sense, sentence, server*. This is the last cycle of the run, which reached its limit of four.

Each entry went to both reviewers again. I read every issue against the entry and either changed the entry (*applied*) or left it as it was (*rejected*, with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`). Nothing was escalated to the owner.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Four entries drew no issues (*characterize, computerize, conflict, seller*). There were 42 issues in all; I applied 27 and rejected 15.

Applied:

- ***security***: one sense covered both the security staff and the place at an airport where people and bags are checked. The airport use is now its own subsense (a smaller sense under the main one), with *go through security*.
- ***coin***: the money sense now reads "make coins out of a material, usually metal", and an unnatural example about "small silver pieces" was replaced. A usage note that used the project's own jargon ("the house keyword rule") now just says where *to coin a phrase* is found.
- ***condition***: the opening summary sentence now covers the "shape how something develops" sense, and the note on *be conditioned to do something* no longer says it always means acting without thinking.
- ***chance*** and ***clothe***: the notes about formality were too strong. *Chance to see someone* is formal but not only written; *feed and clothe* and *fully clothed* are not formal at all.
- ***server***: the kitchen-tool sense said "a spoon, fork, or similar tool"; it now says "a large spoon or a flat knife", which fits salad servers and cake servers.
- Two synonym links were wrong: *confide in* is not *trust*, and *grain* is only the seed of a cereal plant. Both links were removed from both entries, so the cross-reference script will not put them back.
- Smaller: *carpet* (the leaves-on-the-ground sense is not literary), *club*, *confide*, *second*, *sentence*.

Rejected, with the main reasons:

- ***season***: reviewer-a said it is false that British English calls a set of television episodes a *series*. It is true, and the note already says *season* is now common in Britain too.
- ***club***: *club together* (share a cost) is mainly British; the note matches the house rule that a region label means "mainly used there".
- ***security***: reviewer-a said *Security asked us to leave* needs a plural verb. *Asked* has the same form for both, so the example shows nothing either way.
- ***sentence***: reviewer-a objected that a phrase can contain a verb (*for Kim to leave*). That is a clause in most grammars; the learner-level account of *phrase* stands, though the learner-error note now says a phrase is *usually* only part of a sentence.
- ***prepare***: *put together a plan* does mean prepare it, so the link from the new phrasal verb stays.
- Small style points that go against house rules (marks inside examples, the number of examples in a phrase).
