# 2026-10-03 (13): twelve nouns, enemy to example

This was the first cycle of the run that started at 12:44 UTC. A *cycle* is one unit of work that is drafted, checked, recorded, and merged on its own; a run holds at most four. The selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from what the dictionary most needs) chose **build**: draft new entries from the queue, the list of words still to be written, in order.

## What was done

Before any work, the run checked for unfinished business. No pull requests were open. One old branch from the previous run, `claude/wonderful-bardeen-ftblhl`, still exists on GitHub; the absorb tool found nothing on it that is not already in `main`, so it was only noted for deletion in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` (this tool set cannot delete branches). The inbox was empty.

The cycle then drafted twelve common nouns from the core vocabulary: *enemy, energy, engine, entrance, equality, equipment, error, escape, evening, event, evidence, example*. Together they hold 27 senses (numbered meanings) and 11 phrases, among them *be your own worst enemy*, *in error*, *see the error of your ways*, *good evening*, *in the event of something*, *in any event*, *in evidence*, *for example*, *make an example of someone*, and *lead by example*. Two entries carry a short paragraph on choosing between near-synonyms: *error* against *mistake*, and *evidence* against *proof*. There are 19 learner errors: common mistakes shown with their correction, such as *We bought new equipments*, *The police found many evidences*, *I did an error*, *by error* for *by mistake*, *in Friday evening*, and *as for example*.

Every entry then went through the standard pipeline. The inflection tool generated the plural forms. The pronunciation check confirmed all 24 American and British transcriptions. Two outside reviewer models then read every field of every entry.

## What the reviewers found and what was decided

The reviewers raised 46 issues, 21 of them marked blocking. Every one was read and decided by hand: 28 were applied and 18 rejected, each with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

The most useful findings came from the first reviewer:

- **escape** had a third sense, gas or liquid coming out of a pipe (*an escape of gas*). The reviewer pointed out that this is mainly a technical use and that *leak* is the ordinary word. The sense was removed, and the usage note was rewritten so that it talks about the noun (*an escape from prison*, *the escape of two prisoners*) rather than the verb.
- **enemy** had marked the war sense as used only in the singular, with *the enemy*, and the notes said the word could not mean one soldier. That was wrong: *an enemy* can be one person on the other side. The grammar and two notes were corrected.
- **error**'s choice-of-word paragraph said a computer never shows a *mistake*. That was too absolute and was softened.
- **example**: one sample sentence for *for example* lacked the comma its own explanation described; it was replaced.
- **event** had a note that *eventually* is a related word. A claim about where a word comes from belongs only in the etymology field, so the note was removed.
- **equality**: one example and one learner error were replaced with more natural ones.

The rejected issues were mostly settled house rules: the quoted headword at the start of an adaptation note, the American transcription of *evening* without a length mark, and the phrase *and so on*, which is the house replacement for *etc.*. One rejection is worth explaining. The second reviewer objected that *energy* is listed as having no plural while one subsense covers *energies* (*she put her energies into the garden*). The objection is fair, but the inflection tool cannot record a plural for a noun marked uncountable. This is a known gap, noted in `wiki/notes/inflect-uncountable-plural-gap.md`, and this case has been added to that note.

Two definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of simple words definitions are written from) and were reworded: *greet* in *good evening*, and *occasion* in *event*. The cross-reference tool added one back-link, *evening* on `afternoon-n`, which is placed correctly. The missing target words named in the new entries, such as *mistake*, *proof*, *night*, and *equal*, were added to the queue.
