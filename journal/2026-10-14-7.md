## What this cycle did

This was an **originality** cycle: a spot check that the dictionary's wording is its own and not copied from a published dictionary. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) forces one every tenth run, and this was run 430.

The tool `tools/originality_check.py` picked ten fields at random from the whole dictionary: definitions of senses and of idioms. For each one I ran an exact-phrase web search to see whether any published dictionary uses the same words. I also asked one of the two reviewer models, in a single question, whether each definition reads as copied, and to quote the published wording if it said yes. As in earlier checks, the search decides the verdict; the reviewer's answer is only a hint.

The ten fields and the verdicts:

- *criminal* (adjective) sense 2, *very wrong or very bad, though not always against the law*: no match. **Original.**
- *to boot* (in *boot*, noun), *as well; in addition*: two plain words that any dictionary would use; an online learner's dictionary has the same pair. **Generic overlap** (short and plain by necessity, not copying).
- *burn* (noun) sense 2, *a mark or hole made in something by fire or heat*: no match. **Original.**
- *throw* (verb) sense 3, *move your body or a part of it suddenly and with force*: no match. **Original.**
- An idiom in *gun* (noun) for someone hired for special skills: no match. The reviewer quoted a dictionary's wording, but the search could not find it. **Original.**
- *cook* (verb) sense 1, *make food ready to eat by heating it*: no exact match; learner's dictionaries use the same plain idea in their own words. **Generic overlap.**
- *lose your shirt* (in *shirt*): no match; one dictionary words it differently. **Original.**
- An idiom in *handle* (verb), *too difficult or dangerous for someone to deal with*: no match. The reviewer's quoted source was not found. **Original.**
- *as always* (in *always*, adverb), *in the way that is usual*: a learner's dictionary defines **usually** as *in the way that is usual or normal*. Ours is that wording with two words removed. **Rewrite.**
- *play* (noun) sense 4, the North American sports sense (*the best play of the game*), *a single action or move in a sport*: a learner's dictionary has *an action or move in a game* for the same sense. Ours followed that frame. **Rewrite.**

## The two rewrites

- *as always* is now *as happens every time*.
- *play* sense 4 is now *one thing that a player or a team does during a game, or a plan for doing it*. The second part covers the example *The coach called a new play*, which the old definition did not.

A rewrite counts as a review, so both entries went through the reviewers again. The first reviewer raised 7 issues (5 blocking); I accepted 5 and rejected 2. The second reviewer raised none.

- *play* sense 5 (*the play of light on the water*) was *a quick, changing movement of light or shadow*; the reviewer said it need not be quick, and it is now *a changing pattern of light or shadow*. The idiom *make a play for* now covers trying to win someone's support or love, not only trying to get something.
- *always* sense 2 (*I'll always love you*, *He has always lived here*) said *for all time, in the past or in the future*, which suggests all of past time. It is now *for as long as anyone can remember, or for all time in the future*. Sense 4 (*you can always take the bus*) said it is used with **can** or **could**; it now says *mainly*, and names **might** and **would** too.
- Rejected: an objection that *The title of the book is a play on words* is a weak example (it is a natural use), and an objection to the informal label on *make a play for*.

## About the reviewer's answers

The reviewer said *copied* seven times out of ten. Unlike earlier checks, it did not simply quote our own text back; it gave other wording. But the search found none of five of its quotes. For *as always* it named the right dictionary but the wrong headword, and for *play* its quote was close to a real frame. So it pointed at both real cases, among five false alarms. Search still decides. I added a line to the note that tracks this (`wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`).
