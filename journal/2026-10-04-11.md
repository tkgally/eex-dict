Some notes on the entries:

This entry holds only the noun uses: *a half*, *two halves*, the first and second half of a game, the number ½, *half past four*, and the British *a half* of beer. A usage note points to the separate entries for the determiner (*half an hour*), the pronoun (*I'll have half*), and the adverb (*half asleep*). A note for translators warns that informal British *half four* means 4:30, while a similar phrase in some languages means 3:30.

- **habit** has a synonym-choice paragraph that separates *habit* (one person), *custom* (a society), and *tradition* (passed down). It also warns translators that a word that looks like *habit* means clothes in some languages.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models review every field. We call them reviewer-a and reviewer-b, and neither is from Anthropic. They raised 47 issues, 34 of them "blocking" (they must be settled before an entry is published). I applied 37 and rejected 10, each with a logged reason.

The most useful catches:

- **A real error.** My pronunciation note for translators on *guest* said it does not rhyme with *quest*. It does. The note was corrected.
- *City hall* and *town hall* do not fit the sense "a large room for events". *Hall* now has a fifth sense for the building of a town's or city's government.
- The notes on *a group of students was / were* overstated the plural verb. They now say the singular is normal in careful American writing and the plural is common in speech and in British English.
- The medical sense of *growth* was too narrow (it said "small" and "hard"). The idea of *a hired gun* was too general. Compound tools such as *staple gun* did not fit the tool sense of *gun*. All three were rewritten.

Rejections:

- Two region labels: *by a hair* (mainly American; British English prefers *by a hair's breadth*) and *hall* for a passage (British English usually says *corridor*). A region label here means *mainly used there*, not *only used there*.
- Requests to capitalize definitions and to drop *and so on*, which go against house style.

The pronunciation check (three models plus an open American pronouncing dictionary) verified all 24 transcriptions.
