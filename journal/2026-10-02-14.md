# 2026-10-02 (16:43 UTC run, cycle 2): second review of twelve verbs, *invest* to *lead*

## What this run did

This was the second cycle of the run that started at 16:43 UTC. The first cycle drafted twelve new verbs and was merged as pull request #186. For this cycle the mode selector (the script that chooses each cycle's kind of work) picked **review**. A review is a second reading of entries that have already passed one round of the outside reviewers. It looks for missing senses, idioms, learner errors, and usage notes, and then sends the entries through the reviewers again.

There were no unreviewed drafts, so the rule was to take the oldest entries that had been read only once. These were twelve verbs drafted on 2026-09-26: *invest, invite, involve, join, judge, keep, kick, kill, kiss, know, lay,* and *lead*. Earlier notes said that the verbs from that period often lacked usage notes and learner errors. That was true of most of these.

Before the reviewers saw them, I added by hand:

- **Usage notes** (a short paragraph on a point of use) to *invest* (which preposition goes with the money and which with the business), *invite* (*invite someone to* an event, *for* a meal), *join*, *judge* (*judging by* and *judging from*), *kick*, *kill* (people *are killed in* an accident; *murder* is only for a crime), *know* (not used in continuous tenses; *get to know*), and *lead* (the past form is *led*; *lead to* takes a noun or an *-ing* form).
- **Learner errors**: *She invited me at her party* (use *to*); *I joined her birthday party*, said by a guest (use *went to*); *judge people with their clothes* (use *by*); *He kicked to the ball* (no *to*).
- **Nine idioms**, each placed under the keyword rule (an idiom goes under the first noun in it, or the first verb when it has no noun): *alive and kicking*, *kick someone when they're down*, *it won't kill you*, *kiss and make up*, *you never know*, *who knows*, *know better*, *not that I know of*, and *lead someone to believe something*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 52 issues. Twenty-two were *blocking*, meaning I had to decide each one before the entries could stay marked as reviewed. I applied 25 decisions and rejected 27, and none needed to be escalated to the owner. Every decision is logged with its reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

The most useful findings were about *judge*. Four of its five definitions were either too narrow or too broad. One said a competition judge picks "the winner", when judges often rank everyone. One said guessing an amount is done only "by looking". The court sense said a judge "decides the result of a case", which ignores juries. All four were rewritten, and the court sense got a better example and its own collocations. Other changes I applied:

- *kill* meaning "be very angry" (*Mom will kill me*) now includes the idea of punishing, and is described as "usually" an exaggeration rather than "never" a real threat.
- The notes in *lay* had said it always takes an object, but the egg-laying sense does not need one (*the hens are laying*). The notes now say so.
- In *lead*, the learner error *lead to increase prices* could be read two ways. It is now *lead to lose customers*.
- The informal sense of *invest* (*I finally invested in a good coat*) now carries the informal label that its usage note already mentioned.

Most of the rejections were points the house rules already settle: the quoted headword at the start of an adaptation note, a stress mark placed after a syllable break, and a limit on a word's subject written in the explanation. One reviewer asked me to change the American pronunciation of *lead* to /lid/, which is exactly what the entry already says.

The basic-vocabulary check caught one definition that used *criticize*, which has no entry yet. I reworded it.
