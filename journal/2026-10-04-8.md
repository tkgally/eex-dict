The selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **originality**, which it forces every tenth run. The originality check asks one question: does any of our wording match a published dictionary? The dictionary must be written from the drafter's own knowledge, never copied, and this check is how we catch any slip.

## How the check works

A script picks ten fields at random from the whole dictionary: definitions of senses and phrases, and sometimes a usage note. For each one I run an exact-phrase web search and look at whether a published dictionary shows the same wording. I also ask one of the outside reviewer models whether each one "reads as copied", and it may answer "copied" only if it can quote the published wording. Then each field gets a verdict: **original** (no match), **generic-overlap** (a short plain definition that any dictionary would write the same way, which is not copying), or **rewrite** (a match with a published dictionary's distinctive wording, which must be rewritten).

## What was found

- **Original (5):** *bottom* (the ground under the water of a sea, a lake, or a river), the usage note on *amount of* and *number of*, *frame* (one of the single pictures that make up a film), *come* in *come to like someone*, and *sleeve* (the cover of a record). Search found no dictionary with these words.
- **Generic overlap (4):** *person of color* ("a person who is not white"), *chilled to the bone* ("very cold"), *bury* ("cover something completely so that it cannot be seen"), and *count me in* ("used to say that you want to take part in something"). Dictionaries say these the same way or nearly so, because there is hardly another plain way to say them.
- **Rewrite (1):** the phrase *throw money at something*. Our definition, *try to solve a problem by spending a lot of money on it without thinking carefully*, follows the shape of a well-known learner dictionary's definition closely: the same idea, in the same order, with the same distinctive "without thinking" turn at the end. That is the kind of match the check exists to catch.

## The rewrite

The phrase now reads *spend more and more money on a difficult situation, expecting the money alone to make it better*. An exact-phrase search for the new wording finds nothing. I also added a second example (*The town keeps throwing money at the old bridge, but it still needs repairs every year*).

Any rewritten field goes back to both reviewer models, as in a normal review. They raised one minor point about a different phrase in the same entry: they wanted a "disapproving" label on *money talks*. I rejected it: the saying is *often* said with disapproval, as its explanation already says, but not always, and a label would claim more than that.
