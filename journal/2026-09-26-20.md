# 2026-09-26 (twentieth run): ten closure words, *gamble* to *ethnic*

## What this run did

This was the first cycle of a new scheduled session, and a **closure** run. A closure run writes entries for words that the dictionary's own definitions already use but that do not yet have entries of their own. Closing these gaps matters because a learner who clicks a word inside a definition should find an entry for it. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each run's kind of work) chose it as the most overdue.

Before starting, the run checked for unfinished work from earlier sessions. There were no open pull requests. The previous session's working branch, `claude/relaxed-bell-iujuq0`, was fully merged, with nothing left on it. Its name was added to the list of branches the owner can delete (`reviews/needs_curator.txt`), because the tools this run has cannot delete branches.

The ten words were:

- **gamble** (verb): betting money, and taking a risk in the hope of a good result.
- **axe** (noun): the tool; the informal *the axe*, meaning jobs or plans being cut; and the idiom *have an axe to grind*. The American spelling *ax* is recorded as a variant.
- **diaper** (noun): labelled American, with the British word *nappy* named as its equivalent.
- **gene** (noun): with the informal phrase *be in someone's genes*, and a note that *genes* sounds exactly like *jeans*.
- **organization** (noun): a group, the work of planning, and the way things are put in order.
- **resource** (noun): useful supplies such as money and time, natural resources, and a source of help such as a library.
- **attractive**, **biological**, **comparative**, **ethnic** (adjectives).

Each queue row's part of speech was checked first; all ten were right.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline (the fixed sequence of checks each entry must pass):

- **Format and inflections.** All ten passed the format check. The inflection tool generated the plurals and verb forms from its rules. It also recorded which adjectives are not gradable, meaning they have no *more* or *most* forms.
- **Pronunciation.** All twenty transcriptions, American and British, were confirmed by the panel of pronunciation models. The American ones were also checked against the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, an open word list consulted at run time and not stored in the repository.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 46 issues. Each one got a logged decision: 31 fixes applied and 15 rejected.

The main fixes:

- *Attractive* sense 1 was defined too narrowly, as if only people could be attractive, while its own examples described a town square. It is now simply "pleasant to look at".
- The note comparing *gamble* and *bet* wrongly said that only *gamble* is used for a risk that is not about money. People also say *I'm betting on a sunny day*. The note now says both are possible.
- *Ethnic* sense 2 (*ethnic food*, *ethnic music*) was reworded, and its usage note now says plainly that some people dislike this catch-all use. The collocations stayed, because the use is common and a learner will meet it.
- *Comparative* sense 2 (*comparative comfort*) is now defined as "true to some degree when compared", and its translator's note no longer uses a misleading contrast.
- The stress note on *biological* now names the stressed part, *LOJ*, correctly.

The rejected issues were mostly pronunciation objections from reviewer-a. It wanted a final *r* in the British *diaper*, a different vowel in the British *organisation*, and stress on the second part of the American *resource*. The panel and the CMU dictionary had already confirmed all three transcriptions as written, so they stay.

Six definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary, the list of about 2,400 basic words that definitions should keep to. Examples were *waist*, *charity*, *arrange*, and *culture*. Each was reworded into plain words.
