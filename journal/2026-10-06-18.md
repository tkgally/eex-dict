## What this cycle did

The scheduler chose **review**. A review is a second reading of entries that have been through only one round of the panel. The panel is two outside language models, called reviewer-a and reviewer-b, that check every field of an entry and report problems; I then decide on each problem. There are no unreviewed drafts, so the review took the twelve oldest one-round entries by creation date: *career, carpet, cartoon, case, cash, cat, cause, cave, ceiling, cell, center,* and *century*. All were written on 2026-10-01.

First I read each entry myself, looking for missing senses, idioms, notes, and learner errors. My additions:

- *career*: the common combination *pursue a career*. I also added a false-friend note (a warning for translators about a similar-looking word that means something else): in some languages a word like **career** means a university course of study.
- *case*: a learner error for using **in case** where **if** is meant.
- *cat*: the idiom *cat got your tongue?*, said to someone who has gone quiet. I also added a usage note: people often call their own cat **he** or **she**, and a cat they do not know **it**.
- *cause*: the phrase *cause and effect*.
- *case* and *cash*: two notes showed a wrong form in italics, against the house rule that wrong forms appear only in a learner-error box. I reworded both.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 38 issues, 25 of them marked blocking. I applied 20 and rejected 18. None needed to go to you. Every decision is logged with a reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Applied, among others:

- *career*: the comparison of **job**, **career**, and **profession** said a career is work you "move forward in". You can have a career without being promoted, so it now says "often with chances to move up". The correction in the learner error now names a subject (*I'm studying law*) instead of switching to *degree*.
- *cartoon*: the translators' note said English *cartoon* means "only a funny drawing". Political cartoons need not be funny, so the note now says "often funny or making a point".
- *case*: the grammar sense ("nominative", "accusative") was redefined around the word's job in a sentence, and marked countable only. My new *in case* learner error used a sentence that can be read correctly, so it now uses a clearer one (*In case you see Ana, tell her hello*).
- *cat*: the wild-animal sense said "a large wild animal", which leaves out small wild cats. It now says "large or small".
- *cause*: the explanation's *cause to do something* looked like the verb. It now reads *have cause to do something*.
- *center*: the town sense no longer says "most" stores are there. The sports sense now names a particular middle position. A duplicate of the phrase *the center of attention* was removed from a collocation list.
- *century*: the one-line summary at the top (the "core idea") said "a hundred of something". It now says "a hundred years, or a score of a hundred in one sport".

Rejected, among others:

- reviewer-b said *case* lacks the container sense (*suitcase*, *phone case*). That *case* is a different word with a different origin, a homograph. The house rule keeps it in its own entry (`case-n-2`), which still waits on a queue fix noted in `wiki/notes/homograph-queue-and-inflection.md`.
- reviewer-b twice said that two *carpet* phrase definitions must be base-form verb phrases. They already are.
- reviewer-b wanted *fat cat* as its own entry. Idiomatic compounds stay under their keyword, as *cash cow* does under *cash*.
- reviewer-a said a cricket *century* is exactly a hundred runs. A score of 150 is still a century, so "a hundred or more" stays. Its objection to the *century* usage note was also rejected: the note already gives both the everyday count (1900 to 1999) and the strict one (1901 to 2000).
- reviewer-a called the *career* false-friend note too broad. It is hedged with "in some languages", as the style guide asks, and it is true.

One slip of my own: two of my fixes used words outside the defining vocabulary (*basketball*, *cricket*). The vocabulary check caught them and I reworded both before finishing.

*Cave* and *ceiling* drew no issues at all. reviewer-b raised 7 of the 38 issues: 2 applied, 5 rejected.
