## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers ("reviewer-a" and "reviewer-b", two different language models) raised 35 issues, 23 of them marked blocking (meaning the entry should not stand until someone decides). I accepted 12 and rejected 23; none needed to go to the owner.

Changes made:

- **have** (auxiliary verb): the sense used after modal verbs (*should have told me*) listed exactly five verbs, which left out *may have*, *can't have*, and others. It now names four and adds "and similar verbs", and the explanation gives *can't have seen* (surely did not see). The pronunciation note was opened up the same way.
- **minister**: the usage note said *the Prime Minister* always takes capital letters. In American writing the title is capitalized before a name (*Prime Minister Kim*) but usually written in small letters otherwise (*the prime minister said*). Corrected.
- **moon**: the learner-error note said to use *the moon* "because there is only one", while sense 2 of the same entry says some planets have more than one moon. The note now says this applies to the moon that goes around the earth.
- **morning**: sense 1 said morning runs "from when the sun comes up", while its sub-sense (*three in the morning*) said "from midnight". Sense 1 now says *usually* from sunrise, and the sub-sense says "until the middle of the day", which avoids the unclear "twelve o'clock in the day". The collocation *all morning* was moved out of the "preposition + noun" list, where it did not belong.
- **message**: the sense *the main idea of a film or a speech* now reads "the main idea that a film, a book, or a speech wants people to understand", which is plainer than "is trying to show people".
- **metal** and **mood**: two small fixes to the boxes of related words.

Main rejections, with reasons logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`:

- On **metal**, reviewer-a objected that steel is an alloy, not a metal. In everyday English, which is what the definition describes, steel is a metal.
- On **minister**, reviewer-a raised eight issues, six of which argued that *minister* is not mainly a Protestant title and that not every head of an American government department is a *secretary*. Both claims in the entry are already hedged (*mainly*, *usually*) and are accurate.
- Reviewer-a wanted *good morning* moved out of **morning** into an entry of its own. Under the house keyword rule, a fixed phrase lives under its first noun, so it stays. (The queue does hold an old row for *good morning* as a phrase entry; see "What is next".)

My first rewording of *have* used the word *modal*, which is outside the defining vocabulary; the checker refused it, so it now says "and similar verbs".
