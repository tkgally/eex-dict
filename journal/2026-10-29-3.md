# Run report, 2026-10-29 (third run of the day)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work from how long each kind has waited) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue of headwords still to be written. Its reason was that build had the highest "debt", meaning it had waited longest relative to its share of the work. The defining vocabulary (milestone 1) is finished, so the queue offers band-2 words; band 2 is the second most important of five editorial frequency bands. The twelve rows at the top were all verbs that other entries already point to ("crossref" rows): *accuse*, *address*, *adjust*, *admit*, *advise*, *aid*, *announce*, *applaud*, *assist*, *attach*, *attempt*, and *attract*. Several fill obvious gaps: the dictionary had the noun *address*, *advice*, and *attempt* but not the verbs, and entries such as *help*, *try*, *charge*, and *clap* already named these verbs as synonyms.

All twelve went through the full pipeline: schema check, inflection check, the pronunciation panel, both reviewers, my decision on every issue, a second schema check, the defining-vocabulary lint (which checks that definitions use only the plain words of the defining list or words that have entries), and the cross-reference tool. All twelve are now `reviewed` and will appear on the site at the next build. The dictionary has 3,269 entries, none in draft.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers (reviewer A, a Claude model, and reviewer B, a Google model) raised 61 issues, 9 of them blocking. I applied 47 and rejected 14.

- **A circular definition.** The letter sense of *address* (*address an envelope*) was defined with the noun *address*. It now says "write on a letter or package the name of the person it is for and the place where it should go".
- **A definition that contradicted its own example.** I defined the "get used to" sense of *adjust* with *slowly*, then gave the example *Most children adjust quickly*. The word *slowly* is gone, and the definition now also covers the body (*your eyes adjust to the dark*).
- **Definitions with the wrong object.** In *attach importance to something*, the object of the verb is the quality (*importance*), not the thing judged; in *attract attention*, it is the reaction, not the person. Both definitions were rewritten.
- **Examples that did not fit.** *What attracted you to this job?* sat under a sense defined only for people. I widened that sense to "a person or thing" and kept the example. Two examples for the physical sense of *attract* (*A magnet attracts iron*) were science claims, which the examples policy forbids; they are now everyday scenes.
- **Added on review:** a fourth sense of *announce*, "be a sign that something is coming" (*a loud bell announced the start of the lesson*); *adjust for* (*adjusted for inflation*); the intransitive *the lens attaches to the camera*; *admit* with a *that*-clause.
- **Rejected,** with reasons logged: a request to merge the "agree something is true" and "admit a wrong act" senses of *admit* (they take different objects and are often translated differently); a request to mark *aiding* by hand in *aid and abet* (the site already marks listed forms); calls to drop *advise someone to do something* and *attempt to do something* as collocations (house practice keeps the full wording); *enclose* as a synonym of *attach* (the existing entry *enclose* already pairs them).

**Cross-references.** Three entries named one of the new verbs as a synonym while I had named that entry under "compare" (*charge*, *recommend*, *try*). I moved them to synonyms so the two sides agree. Seven back-links that the cross-reference tool could not place on a sense (it no longer guesses) were placed by hand, in *suggest*, *inform*, *praise*, *fasten*, *enclose*, *deal with*, and *alter*.

**Pronunciation and inflections.** All twenty-four transcriptions were verified by the panel and, for American English, by the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, consulted at run time and not stored. All inflections came from the rules, including the doubled consonant of *admitted*.

**Spend.** US$0.37 this cycle; US$3.22 of the US$15 daily cap so far today.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. The definition-word lint caught five words outside the defining vocabulary in my first drafts (*envelope*, *remark*, *complaint*, *reaction*, *sexual*); each definition was reworded with plainer words.
