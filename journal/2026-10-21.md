# 2026-10-21, run at 00:43 UTC, cycle 1: originality check

## What this run did

This was an **originality** cycle, the forty-first. Every tenth run the scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) forces a check that our wording has not drifted toward a published dictionary's; this was run 480. The tool (`tools/originality_check.py`) picked ten fields at random from finished entries: eight sense definitions and two usage notes (a usage note is a short paragraph on a point of use).

The ten fields were:

- *shape* (verb), sense 1: "give something a particular form"
- *abroad* (adverb), the usage note saying no preposition comes before it (*go abroad*, *visitors from abroad*)
- *above* (preposition), sense 2: "more than an amount or a level"
- *bunch* (noun), sense 4, the British hairstyle *bunches*: "two parts of long hair, each tied at one side of the head"
- *spring* (noun), sense 3: "a place where water comes up naturally from the ground"
- *pipe* (noun), sense 3: "a simple musical instrument made of a tube that you blow through, or one of the tubes that make the sound in an organ"
- *parent* (noun), sense 1: "a person's mother or father"
- *burn* (verb), the usage note on American *burned* and British *burned*/*burnt*
- *smart* (adjective), sense 4: "fashionable and usually expensive"
- *system* (noun), sense 4: computer equipment and its programs, or a network of computers

For each, I searched the web for the exact wording and, where a dictionary page came up, for that dictionary's frame of the sense. Nothing was copied from any source; only the verdicts are recorded, in `reviews/originality/2026-10-21.md`.

## What the searches found

No field matched a published dictionary word for word. Four are **original** (no similar wording found: *abroad*, *above*, *burn*, *system*). Three are **generic overlap**, meaning plain wording that any dictionary would write much the same way: *parent* ("a person's father or mother" is the only natural way to say it), *shape*, and *smart*.

Three needed a **rewrite**, because each followed the frame of a learner's dictionary closely even without matching it exactly:

- *bunch*: a learner's dictionary describes the hairstyle as hair separated into two sections and tied at each side of the head. Ours is now "two lengths of long hair, one on the left and one on the right, each held together with a band".
- *spring*: learner's dictionaries frame the water sense as a place where water comes naturally out of the ground. Ours is now "a small stream of water that flows out of the ground by itself, or the place where this happens".
- *pipe*: a learner's dictionary has a tube-shaped instrument played by blowing, and separately a tube that makes sound in an organ; ours had joined those two in the same order. Ours is now "a tube that makes musical notes when air is blown through it, played on its own or as one of many in an organ".

These are borderline calls; the house rule is to rewrite when a definition follows a published frame.

## The reviewer question

The routine also asks one outside reviewer model whether each field reads as copied. I asked reviewer-a, with its hidden reasoning turned off. It answered "copied" on eight of the ten, each time naming Oxford and giving our own text back as the "quote"; it answered "cannot tell" on the two usage notes. Searches did not confirm any of those quotes, so, as in earlier checks, the search results alone decided the verdicts. The pattern is recorded in `wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`.

## The three rewritten entries

A rewrite counts as a review of the entry, so the three went through the full pipeline: validation, the inflection and pronunciation checks (all forms and all transcriptions verified), and both reviewers on every field. The reviewers raised seven issues, none about the new definitions. I applied two: the *bunch* usage note now says that *a bunch of* takes a singular verb before an uncountable noun (*A bunch of work is still waiting*), and the idiom *put that in your pipe and smoke it* is now labelled old-fashioned. I rejected five: both reviewers objected to the American label on *a bunch of* meaning *a lot of*, but in this dictionary a region label means "mainly used there", a settled house rule; reviewer-a also doubted the collocation *play a pipe* and the learner error *the pipe of water*, both of which I kept. All three entries remain `reviewed`.
