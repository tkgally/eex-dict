# 2026-10-16, cycle 3 of the 00:44 UTC run: second review of *do* (auxiliary) and *motor* to *network*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that had been through the two outside reviewers (the review panel) only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) picks the oldest such entries first. This block was twenty entries drafted on 2026-10-05 and 2026-10-06: *do* as a helping verb (the auxiliary, as in *Do you know?*), and nineteen nouns in alphabetical order: *motor, move, movement, movie, mud, murder, muscle, museum, music, musician, name, nation, nature, neck, need, neighbor, nephew, nerve, network*.

Each entry went to both reviewers again. I read every issue they raised and decided whether to change the entry (logged as *applied*) or leave it (logged as *rejected*, with a reason). I also read each entry myself for anything missing: common idioms, learner errors, and the plain-English pronunciation notes that many entries of that period lack.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

reviewer-a raised 36 issues; reviewer-b raised 2, both on *motor*. Six entries drew no issues at all (*mud, muscle, museum, nature, neck, network*). I applied 21 and rejected 15 (the count includes reviewer-b's two, both rejected). No question needed the owner.

Applied, among others:

- ***do*** (helping verb): sense 4 (*"Do you like jazz?" "Yes, I do."*) said *do* stands for *a verb*. It really stands for the verb and the words after it, and the explanation already said so. The definition and the opening summary now match. A translator's note said many languages form questions *by voice*; it now says *by letting the voice rise*.
- ***motor***: the entry said **motor** is the word for electric machines and **engine** for cars, which is too neat. It now says an engine is the part that burns fuel, and that **motor** is usual for electric machines and is also used for small fuel engines, as on a boat.
- ***murder***: two notes said the verb with *murder* is **commit**, as if no other verb were possible (the entry itself lists *investigate a murder*, *solve a murder*). They now say **commit** is the usual verb for carrying one out, not **do**.
- ***need***, sense 3 (*families in need*): it read like any shortage. It now says *the state of being poor, or of not having the food, money, or help that you must have*. *If need be* now carries a *formal* label.
- ***name***: a translator's note said English forms *always* ask for a first and a last name. It now says *usually*, and names *given name* and *family name* as well.
- ***nation***: in the box that compares *country*, *nation*, and *state*, the definition of *state* no longer sounds like *government*.
- ***musician***: a "compare" link to the adjective *musical* was removed, from both entries. The two words are already linked as one word family, and learners do not confuse them.
- ***nerve***: the British slang *bottle* (courage) stays as a synonym, now marked *British, informal*.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- *The movies* (the theater) keeps its *American* label. In this dictionary a region label means *mainly used there*. British English mostly says *the cinema*.
- *Music teacher* and *music lesson* fit the sense *music as a school subject*.
- *Close neighbors* is a normal phrase for countries, so it stays under that sense of *neighbor*.
- *Which nation are you from?* stays as a learner error. It is grammatical, but in an everyday question it sounds strange, and the note explains why.
- reviewer-b wanted the use of *motor* before another noun (*a motor vehicle*) moved to an adjective entry. Here *motor* is a noun used before another noun, like *business* in *business meeting*.
