## What this run did

This was the second cycle of the scheduled run that began at 06:43 UTC. The first cycle, a review of twelve conjunctions, merged as pull request #130.

The mode selector forced a **lint** pass. A lint pass is a housekeeping check over the whole dictionary and its notes, due at least every five cycles. It makes no paid calls. It covers two kinds of check:

- **Mechanical checks**, run by tools: word caps on the instruction files, links inside the wiki, the unit tests, and the entry validator. All passed.
- **Judgment checks**, done by reading: whether the pages agree with each other, and whether the tools have put anything in the wrong place.

The main finding came from the **cross-reference tool**. When entry A lists entry B as a synonym, antonym, or "compare" word, the tool adds the matching link back from B to A. This is called a back-link. The tool has to guess which sense of B the link belongs on, and it often guesses wrong. This pass it added twelve back-links:

- **Six were right**:
  - *shrink* ↔ *expand*
  - *shine* ↔ *flash*
  - *show* ↔ *represent*
  - three word-family links (*discolor* from *color*, *foresee* from *see*, *foretell* from *tell*)
- **Six sat on the wrong sense**, and I moved them by hand:
  - Five were on *show*. All five had landed on its first sense, "let someone see something". *express* and *hide* (as in hiding your feelings) belong on the sense about showing a feeling. *reflect*, *say*, and *tell* (as in *the results tell us something*) belong on the sense "make it clear that something is true".
  - On *slide*, the link from *run* (as in running your hand along a surface) moved from "move smoothly" to "move something smoothly across a surface".

A second run of the tool made no further changes, so the fixes will stick.

Other checks:
