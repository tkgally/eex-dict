# Journal, 2026-11-28

## Cycle 1: build

**What this cycle did.** This cycle was a *build*: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words the dictionary still lacks. The mode selector chose build because building had the largest backlog among the eligible kinds of work. The day's budget was untouched at the start, and the cycle was allowed up to twelve new entries.

Before building, the run did its weekly duties. It found no open pull requests from earlier runs. Six leftover branches were checked, and all were already merged; one not yet listed was noted for deletion in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`, since this tool set cannot delete branches. There were no entry reports (problem reports from readers, filed as GitHub issues) and nothing in `inbox/`. The weekly digest was due and is written in `journal/digest-2026-11-28.md`. It holds the week's numbers, nine entries for you to sample, and the three cases this week where the drafter overruled both reviewers.

**The entries.** Twelve band-2 adjectives were claimed. Band 2 is the second most frequent of five bands. Two of the claims were *blond* and *blonde*, which are one word in two spellings. They became one entry, `blond-adj`, with *blonde* recorded as a spelling variant and a usage note explaining when each is used. The *blonde* row in the queue is marked `duplicate`. That left eleven new entries:

- **attached**: sent with an email; joined, with a formal subsense for working within an organization; fond; and the informal *in a relationship*.
- **awful**: very bad; ill; guilty; and *an awful lot*.
- **awkward**: six senses, from an embarrassing situation to the British *being awkward*, with a subsense for awkward wording.
- **bankrupt**: the legal sense, and the formal *morally bankrupt*.
- **bare**: not covered; empty; and *the bare minimum*.
- **blond**: of hair, with a subsense for blond wood; and of a person.
- **bound**: built around its four fixed patterns, *bound to* (certain), *bound for* (going toward), *bound up with* (connected) and *feel bound to* (obliged).
- **brand-new**, **broad**, **broken** (seven senses, including *broken English* and *a broken home*) and **canned** (food; recorded laughter; a prepared answer).

The pronunciation check and the inflection rules confirmed every entry the first time.

**What the reviewers found.** Every entry is read by two independent reviewer models. Each issue they raise gets one of three decisions: *applied* (the reviewer was right and the entry was fixed), *rejected* (the reviewer was wrong, and the reason is logged) or *escalated* (left for you). This cycle the reviewers raised 80 issues. 14 were *blocking*, meaning the entry could not be finished until they were settled. The cycle applied 73 and rejected 7, and escalated none. The most useful finds were:

- **awful**: both reviewers caught *That's an awful long way*. There, **awful** describes another adjective, so it is the adverb, not the adjective. The example was removed, and the adverb use now has its own queue row.
- **broken**: the "not continuous" sense was defined only in terms of time (*broken sleep*), yet its examples included *a broken line*, which has gaps in space. The definition now covers both. An example with the British weather phrase *broken cloud* was replaced.
- **bound**: *bound for* was coded "only after a verb", but *a bus bound for the airport* puts it after a noun. The code was corrected.
- **awkward**: an example about landing at an awkward angle sat under the sense about clumsy movement. It was moved to the "hard to handle" sense.

Reviewer A also asked for senses that were missing: inconvenient timing in **awkward** now has its own sense, as do *awkward wording* and *an awkward customer*. The rejections included two requests:

- that *conditions attached to an offer* be a sense of the adjective; it is the passive of the verb **attach**;
- that the spelling *blonde* be marked by hand in an example; the site already highlights listed variants.

Fifteen *mirror links* were placed by hand. These are the matching references a word like *empty* gets back when *bare* lists it as a synonym. The tool could not tell which sense of the other word to attach them to.

**Spend.** US$0.39 of the US$15 daily cap.

**What did not work.** Reviewer B's reply on *brand-new* came back unreadable on the first try. A second call for that reviewer alone worked.

**What is next.** The next cycle's mode is chosen by the selector. The queue continues through the band-2 adjectives after *canned*.

## Cycle 2: review

**What this cycle did.** This cycle was a *review*: rereading entries that already exist, as opposed to writing new ones. The mode selector chose review because it had the largest backlog once the first cycle's build was done. There were no unfinished drafts and no entry reports. The cycle therefore continued the *coverage pass* of your ruling B1, which rereads the most important band-1 words (the most frequent fifth of the dictionary) to look for missing senses and idioms. The four words were *like* (verb), *little*, *long* and *matter* (noun).

**What was added before the reviewers saw the entries.**
- **like**: a fifth sense, "want or choose", for *sit wherever you like* and *do as you like*, and the informal challenge *I'd like to see you try!*
- **little**: a sense for the feeling the word often adds, whatever the size (*a lovely little restaurant*, *the poor little thing*, *you little liar*).
- **long**: a subsense for time that feels long because it is tiring (*it's been a long day*). It also got its first two idioms, *the long and the short of it* and *be long on something* (*long on promises, short on details*).
- **matter**: *a matter of practice*, *in a matter of minutes*, *no laughing matter* and *as a matter of course*. Its translators' note had said only "translate each sense on its own", which the style guide forbids, so it was replaced with a specific trap: *matters* in *make matters worse* means the whole situation.

**What the reviewers found and what was decided.** The two reviewers raised 44 issues, and 3 were blocking (the entry could not be finished until they were settled). All three were applied:
- a *matter* example (*everything in the universe is made of matter*) was a checkable science claim, which examples may not contain;
- another *matter* example used the British *by post*, now *by mail*;
- a new idiom definition in *long* began with *to*, against house style.

Overall 36 issues were applied and 8 rejected. Among the rejections:
- one reviewer wanted *in the long run* under *long*, and the other wanted *the heart of the matter* under *matter*. By the keyword rule (an idiom goes under its first noun) these belong to *run* and *heart*, and both are already there;
- a request to drop the "not used in continuous tenses" code on *like* was rejected under a settled house rule.

Reviewer A also found two real gaps, and both were filled. *Long* now covers *don't be long* and *it won't be long before...*, and *matter* now has *gray matter*. Several notes that repeated each other were trimmed, under the rule that one fact lives in one field:
- *little* stated the *small brother* error three times;
- *long* described its adverb at length when it should only point to it;
- *matter* gave the *no matter how* rule in three places.

**Spend.** US$0.21 this cycle. The day's total is now US$0.60 of the US$15 cap.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. One correction: the first draft of `NEXT.md` named the next four words wrongly, because some of them had already been reread since 2026-10-24. It now lists the next words that have four or more senses and have not been reread: *maybe*, *mind*, *minute* and *more* (adverb).

**What is next.** The selector chooses the next cycle's work.

**What needs you.** Nothing new.
