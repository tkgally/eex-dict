# Run report, 2026-10-27 (scheduled run 20261027T004409Z-n9o1cm)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue of words still to be written. It gave a ceiling of twelve entries and a paid-call budget of US$3. The words came in the order the owner set for milestone 1 (the roughly 2,400 words the dictionary uses to write its own definitions, which must all have entries before other words). The twelve were: *thankful*, *yearly*, *scarcely*, *seldom*, *wrongly*, the phrasal verb *take apart*, and six prefixes: *anti-*, *de-*, *mis-*, *non-*, *post-*, *pre-*. Milestone 1 now has 18 words left.

Before the build, the pre-flight checks found no stranded pull requests, no open entry reports from readers, and nothing in the owner's inbox. Three old work branches remain on GitHub with nothing unmerged on them; each is already listed in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` for deletion, since the session's tools cannot delete branches.

Each entry was drafted from scratch and then went through the full pipeline: the schema check; the inflection tool (which writes forms like *took apart*); the pronunciation check, where three outside models vote on each IPA transcription and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary is consulted where it has the word; and the two-reviewer panel. Reviewer A is a Claude model and reviewer B is a Google model. They read every field and return a closed verdict on each.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The panel raised 106 issues, only one of them blocking. In the *grateful*/*thankful* comparison box for *thankful*, the example (*I'm thankful the train was late too*) made no sense on its own. I replaced it with one that does. Of the issues, 79 were applied and 14 rejected, each with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. None needed escalation. The main changes:

- *wrongly*: both reviewers saw that sense 2 mixed two ideas, *unfairly* (*wrongly accused*) and *mistakenly* (*wrongly believe*). The mistaken uses moved into sense 1, and sense 2 is now just the unfair one. The word is now marked as having no comparative form. A learner-error item that only repeated the usage note was removed.
- *anti-*: reviewer A pointed out that a real use was missing, *anti-* meaning the opposite (*anticlimax*, *antihero*, British *anticlockwise*). It is now sense 3. *anti-bullying* moved to the "prevents" sense, where it belongs.
- *pre-*: my note on hyphens was wrong for American English, which usually writes *preexisting* as one word. The note now gives the American and British habits separately. The stress note now admits that some nouns stress the prefix (*PREE-school*), and the British *pre-match* example became *pregame*.
- *scarcely*: the core idea and the third sense were reworded. The third sense is an understatement (*it is scarcely surprising*), not a flat "certainly not".
- Six entries had the same fact stated in two or three places, which the house rule "one fact, one field" forbids. The extra copies were removed.

Rejections included a reviewer calling *comparative with more* "not a grammar code" (it is one; this is a settled house rule), a request to mark *took apart* in an example (the site marks listed inflected forms itself), and two proposals to merge senses of *non-* and *pre-* that differ in grammar and in how they are usually translated.

The pronunciation panel agreed on every transcription except American *anti-*. I gave *AN-tee* first and *AN-tie* as a variant, and the panel leaned to the other form. Both are standard, so the entry carries a `pronunciation-disputed` flag and a line for the owner in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`. This is the same situation as *semi-* yesterday.

After review, the cross-reference tool listed nine places where a new entry points to an older one but the tool could not tell which sense of the older entry should point back. I placed all nine back-links by hand (for example, *beat* sense 2, "defeat", now lists *take apart* as a synonym). The compare links from *mis-* to *un-* and *dis-* were dropped rather than mirrored, because *mis-* ("badly, wrongly") is not a negative prefix.

**Spend.** US$0.36 of the day's US$15 cap, all of it on reviewer B and the pronunciation panel. Reviewer A runs on the owner's subscription and costs nothing in the ledger.
