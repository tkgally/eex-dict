# Journal, 2026-11-08: run rsgke3

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the tool that picks each cycle's job, `tools/next_mode.py`) chose a build: new entries taken from the queue of words waiting to be written, in the agreed order. It allowed up to twelve entries and US$3.00 of paid model calls. Twelve nouns were claimed and written, all in band 2 (the second most common group of words): *goodbye*, *goose*, *grace*, *gram*, *granddad*, *grandma*, *grandpa*, *granny*, *grape*, *graph*, *greenhouse*, and *grill*. Each went through the full checking process, and all twelve are now marked reviewed and will appear on the site.

There were no stranded pull requests or leftover branches from earlier runs, nothing in the owner's inbox, and no open entry reports from readers.

**How the entries were checked.** Every entry was validated against the schema, had its plural generated by the inflection tool (*geese* comes from the table of irregular forms), and had its two pronunciations checked by a panel of three models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. All 24 transcriptions passed. For *grandma* and *grandpa*, the CMU dictionary disagreed with the American form (it keeps the *d*); two of the four votes agreed with ours, which is enough under the project's rule, and the entries say in a pronunciation note that the *d* is usually not said.

Then the two reviewers (a Claude model and a Google model) read every field.

**What the reviewers found and what was decided.** They raised 47 issues, 4 of them blocking (serious enough to stop an entry until settled). 40 were applied and 7 rejected.

- Blocking, applied: the usage note on *goodbye* (noun) said *say a goodbye* is wrong. That was not true (*say a quick goodbye* is normal), so the note was removed. The bare *say goodbye*, *wave goodbye* uses were also taken out of the noun's collocations, because they already live in the interjection entry, and a learner error that repeated the interjection's was dropped.
- Blocking, applied: the *graph* entry compared itself with *chart* by saying a graph shows numbers "against two lines". That was muddled, and the note now says instead that a *chart* can also be a list or a circle divided into parts.
- Blocking, rejected (twice): the Google reviewer asked for *geese* to be marked by hand in examples. It is a listed plural, so the site marks it automatically.
- Coverage (missing uses), applied: *goose* gained a third sense, the informal *silly goose*. *Grace* gained the phrases *in someone's good graces* and *social graces*.
- Smaller fixes: the grandparent words now call *grandfather* and *grandmother* "more formal" rather than "less informal". Repeated cross-references were removed. *Grannie* was added as a spelling. *Graph* lost a mathematics label it should not have had, and *sour grapes* got a *disapproving* label and a proper noun-phrase definition.
- Other rejections: a request to state the kilogram relation in the *gram* definition (there is no *kilogram* entry yet, and the usage note already gives it); a request to remove the line naming *greenhouse gases*; and a claim that the *grill* usage note only repeated sense 2 (it gives the American meaning and the word *broiler*).

After review, the cross-reference tool added four back-links from *grandfather* and *grandmother* to the new informal words. New words the entries point to (*kilogram*, *barbecue*, *raisin*, *vine*, *diagram*, the verb *grill*) were added to the queue.

**Spend.** US$0.35 for this cycle, which went on the pronunciation panel and the Google reviewer. The Claude reviewer costs nothing in the ledger. The day's total is US$1.20 of the US$15 cap.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. One drafting slip was caught by the vocabulary check before review: the *greenhouse* definition used *warmth*, a word outside the defining vocabulary, and it now says "need to be kept warm".

**What is next.** The next lint pass (the regular mechanical and editorial check of the whole dictionary) is due after one more cycle. Building continues in queue order after *grill*.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new. The pronunciation disputes and the ten-entry samples in the last two digests still wait for a session with you.
