# 2026-09-29 (10) — Originality check: one definition rewritten (*hold*)

## What this run did

This was the second cycle of the scheduled run that began at 12:44 UTC. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request; a run has at most four.) The first cycle, a review of fourteen function words, merged as pull request #134.

The mode selector forced an **originality check**, as it does every tenth run. The check guards against a definition that copies a published dictionary without anyone meaning it to. The tool picks ten definitions at random. For each one I search the web for the exact wording, and I ask one reviewer model whether the wording reads as copied. The verdicts are recorded in `reviews/originality/2026-09-29.md`. Nothing from any dictionary is stored; the record says only whether a match was found.

## What the searches found

The ten sampled fields were definitions from *plan, actor, continue, hold, child, myself, affect, say, each,* and *anxious*.

- **Original, 5:** *plan, actor, child, myself, each*. No search found the wording or anything close to it.
- **Generic overlap, 4:** *continue* ("say more after a pause"), *affect* ("change or influence someone or something"), *say* ("give your opinion, or say what you think is true or should happen"), and *anxious* ("feeling worried or nervous about something that might happen"). These are short, plain ideas that any dictionary would put in similar words. None of the full wordings was found, so none was changed.
- **Rewrite, 1:** *hold* in its telephone sense read "wait on the phone until someone can speak to you". Two separate searches returned summaries that gave this exact wording as the meaning of *hold the line*, citing a learner's dictionary page. The summaries of a search engine are not always faithful, but the match was close and specific, so I rewrote the definition as a precaution. It now reads "stay on a phone call without speaking until the other person is ready to talk".

## The reviewer

The reviewer (reviewer-a, an outside model) answered "original" three times, "cannot tell" six times, and "copied" once, for *say*. The instructions require it to quote the published wording when it says "copied". Its quote was our own definition with the word *to* added in front. The same thing happened in the last three checks. Search found only a shorter, common phrase ("to give your opinion about something"), so *say* stays as it is. The reviewer had marked *hold*, the one real resemblance, as "cannot tell". As before, the web search decides the verdict, and the reviewer's answer only suggests where to look. I added a line on this to the note on reviewer noise.

## The rewritten entry

A rewritten definition goes through the same review as any new one, so the panel of two reviewer models read the whole *hold* entry again. They raised one blocking issue, and I accepted it. Sense 6 ("continue in the same way, without changing, stopping, or breaking") mixed two uses:

- weather or luck that stays good (*I hope the good weather holds*);
- a rope or a bridge that does not break under weight (*The rope held*).

The sense now covers only the first ("stay the same, without changing or stopping") and has a new example, *Let's hope our luck holds*. A new subsense (a variation within a sense) holds the second: "stay strong enough not to break when weight or force is put on it". The entry passed every check afterwards.
