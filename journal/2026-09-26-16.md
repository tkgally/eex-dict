# 2026-09-26 (sixteenth run): a second reading of twenty more prepositions and pronouns

## What this run did

This was the tenth cycle of this session and a **review** run. A review run gives existing entries a second reading by the two reviewer models, oldest first. Earlier cycles in this session produced pull requests #76 to #84:

- six build cycles, which added sixty verbs;
- one earlier review cycle;
- a lint pass (a housekeeping check over the whole dictionary);
- an originality check.

The block was the next twenty entries that had had only one reading, from 2026-09-20:

- **pronouns:** *yourself, nothing, other, what*;
- **prepositions:** *behind, between, by, down, during, except, for, from, in, into, as well as, below, beside, beyond, despite, including*.

## What the reviewers found and what was decided

Nine entries came through with no issues at all: *down, except, from, in, nothing, other, beyond, despite, including*. On the other eleven, the reviewers raised 54 issues. Each got a logged decision: 32 fixes applied and 22 rejected.

**The biggest fix was *into*.** The entry held *bump into* ("meet someone by chance") as a phrase, along with a learner error about it (*bumped with* → *bumped into*). Reviewer-a pointed out that *bump into* is a phrasal verb. By the project's rules it gets its own entry, not a place inside the preposition. The phrase and the learner error were removed, and *into* now points to the future entry *bump into* (added to the queue). Three other changes to *into*:

- Its core idea, the one-sentence summary of what the senses share, no longer claims that *into* itself means meeting by chance.
- The definition for crashing (*the car ran into a tree*) had been written with a verb, "hits or crashes hard against". It now reads "against something, with enough force to hit it".
- Its opposite now points to *out of*, not *out*.

**Other fixes:**

- *Behind* sense 2 now covers *behind schedule*: "later or slower than planned".
- *Between* lost a learner error that called *a fence between two yards* wrong, because that is normal English in context.
- *By* and *for* had words to compare listed under the wrong sense. For example, *until* sat under *by*'s place sense, and *ago* and *since* sat under *for*'s purpose sense. Each was moved to the sense it belongs to.
- *As well as* now says it can join adjectives too, and a wrong form was taken out of italics in its explanation, as the house rule requires.
- *Yourself* used for more than one person is now called "not standard" rather than "informal".
- *Beside*'s example of the different word *besides* was ambiguous; it now reads *besides English, she speaks French*.

**What was rejected.** Reviewer-a argued in six issues that *during* never means "all through a period". It does: in *please stay quiet during the movie*, the quiet lasts the whole film. The entry keeps both senses. Reviewer-a also made eleven flags on *below*. It called uses such as *the floor below ours* and *the job was below him* adverbs or adjectives, but each has a noun or pronoun after *below*, which makes it a preposition. It made the same mistake with *above* and *about* earlier today. The pattern is recorded in `wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`, and `NEXT.md` now tells later runs to reject these calls. Reviewer-b's suggestion to change a correct double mark around a phrase named as a phrase (*the thing that*) was rejected too.
