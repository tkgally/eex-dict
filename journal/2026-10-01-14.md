# 2026-10-01 (14): second reading of twelve verbs, *follow* to *guess*

*Second cycle of the run that started at 18:43 UTC. Mode: review.*

## What this run did

The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that have been through the review panel only once. The panel is two outside reviewer models, from labs other than the drafter's, that check every field of an entry and return closed verdicts. No entry is in draft, so the review took the oldest one-round entries by creation date. These are twelve verbs drafted on 2026-09-25: *follow, force, forget, forgive, form, freeze, fry, gather, give, govern, grow, guess*.

Earlier notes say verbs from that period often lack learner errors and usage notes. So I first read all twelve myself and made additions by hand before the panel saw them:

- **Learner errors.** *follow*: *The meeting was followed with lunch* (it should be *by*). *forget*: *I forgot my keys at home*, where English says *I left my keys at home*; *forget* is used when no place is named. This is one of the most common mistakes with the word. *freeze*: the wrong past form *freezed*, and *It's freeze outside* for *It's freezing outside*.
- **A usage note on *guess*.** *I guess* (meaning *I suppose*) is especially American.
- **A grammar fix.** *follow* sense 4 (*a speech followed the dinner*) was coded "usually passive" although most of its examples are active. The code came off.

The other entries already had the core senses, idioms and errors a learner needs, so I added nothing to them.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 44 issues, 21 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry counts as reviewed). I accepted 14 and rejected 30. Each decision is logged with a reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

**Accepted:**

- *crash* and *freeze* (of a computer) were listed as synonyms. A reviewer pointed out that a program that crashes closes, while one that freezes stays open but stops responding. They are now "compare" entries with a note saying exactly that, on both sides.
- *freeze over* is a phrasal verb, not a collocation. It moved to "see also" and was queued for its own entry.
- The example *"Freeze! Nobody move!"* is an order, not the fear-or-surprise sense it illustrated. It was replaced with *We all froze and listened*.
- *freeze* for a computer now also covers "nothing you do has any effect", not just a still screen.
- *fry* sense 2 now says "hot and uncomfortable, or burned", not just "hot".
- *grow* sense 3 no longer says you must plant seeds. You can grow vegetables from plants already in the ground.
- My new *guess* note had overstated the regional split. It now says British speakers use *I guess* too, but *I suppose* is more usual there.
- On *grow*, the *to*-infinitive pattern was left only on the sub-sense where it belongs (*grow to love*), and *grow tall* came out of the "become" sense.

**Rejected:**

- **Quoted headwords.** Nine flags by the second reviewer against the quoted headword at the start of adaptation notes. House rule allows it, and this flag recurs on almost every entry.
- **Grammar misreadings.** Three *form* examples (*form a new one*, *formed a circle*, *form a square*) were called intransitive, but each has a direct object. Pattern values such as "usually passive" were called "not patterns", though they are values of the closed pattern list.
- **Pronunciation.** The missing length mark in American *friz*: house practice drops it in American transcriptions.
- **A cross-reference.** Removing *lend* as a synonym of *give* sense 4. *lend support* and *lend a hand* do mean *give* help, and *lend* lists *give* in the matching senses.
- **A region label.** Removing the American label from *I guess*. A region label here means *mainly* used there, which is true.

Reviewer precision this cycle was low: 38 percent for reviewer-a and 20 percent for reviewer-b (the share of their flags I accepted). Most rejections were repeats of points the house rules already settle.
