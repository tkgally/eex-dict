## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 43 issues, 34 of them blocking (an entry with an unsettled blocking issue cannot be published). I read each against the entry and logged a decision for each in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`: 27 applied and 16 rejected. None went to the owner. *guide* and *guess* came back with no issues.

The main changes:

- **habit**: sense 2 had defined *a strong need to keep taking a drug, drinking alcohol, or smoking*, but its example (*gave up her smoking habit*) and its collocation *a smoking habit* are really the ordinary sense, a usual action. Sense 2 is now only the need for a drug, with a new example and collocations (*a drug habit*, *a heroin habit*, *an expensive habit*). The entry's notes had also said that **habit** is always followed by *of* and an -ing form; they now say so only of the pattern *a habit of doing something*. The note contrasting **custom** (a society's) with **habit** (a person's) now says *usually*. The phrase *make a habit of something* now covers doing something regularly as well as starting to.
- **half**: the example *I'll take the bigger half* was dropped (halves are equal by definition), replaced by *Fold the paper in half*. The British drink sense (*I'll just have a half*) was redefined; the reviewer was right that it is half a pint. Because the word *pint* has no entry yet and lies outside the defining vocabulary, the definition says *a small glass of beer or another drink, holding half the usual amount*, and the explanation names the pint.
- **gun**: the learner error (*He shot to the bird* → *shot at*) and the translator's note had stated a general rule about the grammar of **shoot** and **fire**. Both now describe only aiming at a target, and the note says that *shoot someone* means the shot hits, while *shoot at someone* means aiming and firing, hit or not.
- **hall**: the core idea now covers halls where students live and town halls, and *hall of fame* is defined as a group of people chosen for great success, or the building where they are remembered. A doubtful learner error (*hall* written as *hole*) was removed.
- Smaller fixes: *guest* (people invited to a museum event can be guests), *under guard* (watched to stop escape *or* to keep someone safe), *stand your ground* (*hold your ground* is not only British), and new collocations for the *online name* sense of *handle*.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Four region labels: *thin on the ground* and *go halves* (British), *by a hair* and *hall* meaning a passage (American). A region label in this dictionary means *mainly used there*, and each is true in that sense.
- A claim that `half-n` should not mention *half an hour*, *half of them*, or *half asleep*. Its usage note only points to the determiner, pronoun, and adverb entries, which is what the style guide asks for.
- A request to write the phrase as *make a habit of doing something*: the slot also takes a pronoun (*I don't make a habit of it*).
- reviewer-b's point that the *gun* learner error belongs to *shoot*: it is the mistake learners make when talking about guns, so it stays.
