# Run report, 2026-10-26 (second run of the day)

## Cycle 1: build

**What this cycle did.** The scheduler (the script that picks each cycle's job from the state of the dictionary) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue of words still missing. The queue is worked in the order the owner set on 2026-10-24: first the words the dictionary itself uses in its definitions ("milestone 1"). Twelve words were taken, the per-cycle limit: *specially*, *widely*, *billion*, the ordinal numbers *sixth*, *seventh*, *eighth*, *ninth*, and *tenth*, the interjection *shh*, the phrasal verb *start over*, and the prefixes *dis-* and *ex-*. Each was drafted in full, then passed through the usual checks: the schema validator, the inflection generator, the pronunciation check (three or four models and an open pronouncing dictionary must agree on each transcription; all twelve agreed), and two independent reviewers who read every field. All twelve entries are now published as `reviewed`. Ninety-six milestone-1 words remain.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The reviewers raised 107 issues, 27 of them marked blocking. I accepted 57 and rejected 50, each with a logged reason.

The useful catches:

- The date senses of the ordinals were written *the sixth day of a month*, which uses the word being defined. They now read *day 6 of a month, as a date*. The older entries *first* to *fifth* have the same flaw; that is now on the review list.
- The usage note of *eighth* said *A eighth* instead of *An eighth* (the wording came from a shared template). Fixed.
- The pronunciation note for *sixth* said "four sounds together" but listed three. Fixed, and a doubtful claim about native speech was removed.
- The spelling note for *eighth* named the wrong missing letter (learners leave out the **h** after **g**, writing *eigth*). Fixed.
- *Billion* had an example stating how many people use phones and one about stars: checkable facts, which the house rules forbid. Both replaced. Its definition of the "very many" sense now covers *I've told you a billion times*.
- *Disinfect* is not really *dis-* plus *infect*, so that example under *dis-* was replaced with *disassemble*.
- *Widely* lacked the use in *she smiled widely*; it now has a fourth sense for that.
- Phrases such as *specially made* and *widely used* were typed as "adverb + verb" collocations; the house rule types participle phrases as plain phrases.
- The American transcription of *start over* now uses the house symbol for the final *-er* sound.

The rejections were mostly familiar: reviewer A wanted the date use of ordinals (*on the sixth*) moved to a noun entry, and wanted the "in order" and "in a competition" senses merged. Both follow the pattern already settled for *first* to *fifth*, which `NEXT.md` lists as a fence (a settled question not to reopen). Requests for *sixth sense* and *in seventh heaven* were rejected because idioms are filed under their first noun (*sense*, *heaven*). A request to add *finished sixth* as a use was rejected under the same fence.

One cross-reference mismatch was fixed: *former* already listed *ex-* as a synonym, while *ex-* listed *former* only as a word to compare; *ex-* now matches.

**Spend.** US$0.35 for this cycle's paid calls (reviewer B and the pronunciation panel; reviewer A runs on the owner's subscription at no billed cost). The day stands at US$1.20 of the US$15 cap.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. The shared template for the five ordinals carried two errors (*A eighth*, the circular date definition) into every copy, which the reviewers caught. Writing similar entries from one template saves time but multiplies one slip; each copy still needs to be read on its own.

**What is next.** The next cycle's job is chosen by the scheduler. Milestone 1 continues with 96 words; the review list now includes rewording the date sense of *first* to *fifth*.

**For the owner.** Nothing new needs a decision from this cycle. The open items in `NEXT.md` ("For the owner") are unchanged.

## Cycle 2: originality

**What this cycle did.** The scheduler forced an originality check, which comes every twenty runs. The check guards against one risk: that a definition in this dictionary repeats the wording of a published, copyrighted dictionary. A script picked ten fields at random from the 2,948 entries (nine definitions and one usage note). I searched the web for each one as an exact phrase and looked only at whether a published dictionary or word list used the same words. I also asked reviewer B (the non-Anthropic reviewing model) whether each one read as copied. Nothing was copied from any source; the record keeps only the verdicts.

**What was found.** Five fields were original: no published wording matched (*realize*, the *come out in the wash* phrase, the police sense of *beat*, *countable*, and the usage note of *these*). Four were "generic overlap": short, plain definitions that any dictionary would write in much the same words, which the style guide says is not copying. These were *concert* (*a performance of music by...*), *create* in the sense *cause a situation to exist*, *save* in the sense *make it unnecessary*, and the phrase *a dead loss*, *a person or a thing that is useless*.

One needed rewriting. The first sense of *include* read *have someone or something as one of the parts of a group or total*. A learner's dictionary uses very nearly that wording, and *group or total* is distinctive enough to count. It now reads *have someone or something in it as one part among others*. A rewrite counts as a review, so the entry went through the full checks: validation and both reviewers. Both reviewers objected to *embody* being listed as a synonym of *include* (its main meaning is different). I removed it, and also removed the matching link back from *embody*, as the house rule requires. Reviewer A noticed that one example under the second sense (*Tax is included in the price*) really showed the first sense; it was replaced with *Could you include a short note with the gift?*

**About the reviewer's answers.** This was the first time reviewer B was asked the originality question; earlier checks asked reviewer A. Reviewer B answered *cannot tell* on six rows. Twice it named a learner's dictionary as a possible source but could not quote the matching wording. The checklist says the web search decides when no quotation is given, and it did here. Reviewer B's answer did not point to the one real match (*include*); it gave *cannot tell* there too.

**Spend.** About US$0.04 for this cycle (one reviewer-B question and one panel run). The day stands at about US$1.24 of US$15.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed.

**What is next.** The next originality check is due in twenty runs. The scheduler chooses the next cycle's job.

**For the owner.** Nothing new.
