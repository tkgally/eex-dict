## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 31 issues, 22 of them marked blocking. I applied 17 and rejected 14; none needed escalating to the owner. *hate*, *harm*, and *heart* came back with no issues. Almost all issues came from reviewer-a; reviewer-b raised one.

Applied:
- *group*: the usage note and the translator's note said careful American writing prefers *a group of students was waiting*. The reviewer pointed out that *were waiting* is also normal in American English; both notes now say both forms are common. *block* and *team* were removed as synonyms (a team is a narrower idea). Because cross-references must match on both sides, I also removed the mirror links in the `block-n` and `team-n` entries by hand.
- *hearing*: the "official meeting" sense was labelled *law*, but its own example is a public hearing about a new road, which is not a court matter. The label was removed.
- *help*: the notes said *a help* is used "only" after **be** (*You've been a big help*). *Is that a help?* and similar uses exist, so they now say "mostly".
- *hat*: *at the drop of a hat* is now defined as "immediately, without stopping to think" (the old definition added "willingly", which is not part of the meaning); two loose see-also links (*head*, *coat*) were removed.
- Smaller fixes: the note on *tumor* in `growth-n`, the wording of the *height* definition, the *heat* comparison box, an unnatural example (*at the holidays* is now *over the holidays*), and the *help* core idea, which now mentions websites.

Rejected:
- Moving *hard of hearing* to its own entry: the house rule keeps an idiom under its first noun.
- Removing the British label on *health and safety*: a region label means *mainly used there*.
- The claim that *a fear of height* is as normal as *a fear of heights*: the plural is the usual form.
- Two complaints about translator's notes that say "many languages": the style guide asks for exactly that kind of hedge.
- A claim that the currency label *old-fashioned* was in the wrong place (it is in the currency set).
- The learner error *My uncle is hard to hear* (meaning *hard of hearing*): the reviewer said the sentence is grammatical, which is true, but the error is a confusion of two meanings and the note explains both.
