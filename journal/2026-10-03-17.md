# 2026-10-03 (17): second reading of twelve verbs, solve to stir

This was the first cycle of the run that started at 16:43 UTC. A *cycle* is one unit of work that is checked, recorded, and merged on its own. Before any work, the run looked for stranded work: there was no open pull request, and the one leftover `claude/` branch had nothing that `main` lacked. The inbox was empty.

The selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **review**. A review is a second reading of entries that have been through the reviewer panel only once. The panel is two outside models that read every field of an entry and report problems, each marked *blocking* (must be settled before publication) or *minor*.

## What was reviewed

The block was the twelve oldest entries with only one panel round, all verbs written on 2026-09-29: *solve*, *sound*, *spell*, *spend*, *spill*, *split*, *stand*, *start*, *stay*, *steal*, *stick*, and *stir*.

I read each in full before the panel saw it. Several lacked common uses an advanced learner would expect. The additions, all written by hand:

- **stand**: a new sense for being in a situation (*As things stand...*; *He stands accused of...*), and collocations for four bare senses (*stand empty*, *can't stand the sight of something*).
- **stick**: a new sense for being unable to go on (*I'm stuck on the last question*), with a subsense for *be stuck with* something unwanted, and a usage note on *be stuck* and *get stuck*.
- **steal**: two new senses: taking something quickly before anyone notices (*steal a look*, *steal a kiss*) and the sports use (*steal the ball*).
- **start**: a sports sense (*He started every game last season*). **stay**: the phrase *stay tuned* and a usage note (a visitor is asked *Where are you staying?*, not *Where are you living?*). **spill**: a subsense for the literary *spill blood*.
- Smaller additions: a learner error on **sound** (*It sounds like interesting*), a note on **solution** in **solve**, the relationship patterns of **split**, and a pronunciation note on **stir**.

*spell* and *spend* were already full and were sent to the panel unchanged.

## What the panel found and what I decided

The panel raised 34 issues, 20 of them blocking. I applied 22 and rejected 12. Each decision has a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Real catches, applied:

- **spend**: the energy sense said you use energy *until little or none is left*. That is too strong; the definition now just says *use energy or effort on something*. The learner error about *spend money for* was softened: **on** is the usual preposition, but *for* is not impossible.
- **solve**: the note said **solve** is *only a verb*. An informal noun (*a quick solve*, in puzzle talk) does exist, so the note now says the usual noun is **solution**.
- **stick**: the definition of pushing a sharp point into something did not cover *A piece of glass stuck in his foot*. It now does. A collocation I had added (*make something stick*, meaning make a charge or a rule hold) was a different meaning and came out.
- **stand**: my explanation for the new situation sense claimed it was mainly used in writing and the news; that was not supportable and was removed. *know where you stand* is about your position with someone, not an opinion on a question, and was removed from the opinion sense. A note for translators no longer calls *can't stand to do* less common.
- **stay**: my new usage note said a stay is always short. It now says *a time away from home*.
- **start** and **steal**: two weak collocation sets were replaced, and an informal example (*Can I steal you for a minute?*) was dropped rather than labelling the whole sense informal.

Rejected: the British label on *stand for election* (a region label means *mainly used there*); objections to two values that are in the closed pattern list; subject restrictions in an explanation field (the style guide puts them there); a three-word signpost (allowed); and a claim that *stir* does not rhyme with *her* in British English (it does).

Two definitions failed the defining-vocabulary check at first (*puzzle* in *solve*, *baseball* in *steal*). Both were reworded with words from the list; baseball moved to the explanation.
