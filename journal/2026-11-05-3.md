## Cycle 2: build

**What this cycle did.** The selector chose a *build* cycle: drafting new entries from the build queue (the ordered list of words still to be written), with a ceiling of twelve entries and US$3 of paid calls. I took eight: six from the top of the queue — *chef*, *comedian*, *conference*, *consumption*, *cross* (noun), and *crossing* — and two "homographs" that earlier runs had asked for by hand. A homograph is a word with the same spelling and part of speech as another but a different meaning and origin, so it gets an entry of its own: `case-n-2` is *case* the container (a pencil case, a case of water, upper and lower case), separate from *case* meaning a situation or an example; `clip-v-2` is *clip* meaning to fasten something with a clip, separate from *clip* meaning to cut. The existing entries for *case*, *box*, and *clip* already pointed to these two, so readers following those links now find a page.

Each entry was drafted from my own knowledge and the house style guide, then sent through the full checking pipeline: a structure check, word forms generated from rules (*clipped*, *crosses*), pronunciation checked by a panel of models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary (all 16 American and British transcriptions agreed), and two independent model reviewers who read every field.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Over two rounds (the second only for the three entries that gained new senses in the first), the reviewers raised 39 issues, 4 of them "blocking" (must be settled before the entry can be published). I accepted 36 and rejected 3; none needed the owner.

- The main blocking issue, raised by both reviewers: in *conference*, the example about a *press conference* sat under the sense "a meeting at which a small number of people discuss something", which it does not fit. Press conferences are now a sense of their own ("a meeting at which a person or a group speaks to people from newspapers, radio, and television and answers their questions").
- In the second round, a new sub-sense for media use of *consumption* ("our consumption of news") was first defined as time spent; both reviewers said it is the taking in of the content, not the time, and the definition was changed.
- An example "Turn left at the next crossing" was called unnatural in American English, which says *intersection*; it was replaced.
- Other accepted points: the *crossing* definition used the word *cross* itself (circular); a soccer restriction was moved out of a definition into the explanation field; a few repeated facts were cut so each lives in one field; *container* was moved from "synonym" to "see also" because it is a broader word.
- Rejected: a request to label the "funny person" sense of *comedian* as ironic (the sense is neutral; only one example teases); a request to hand-mark the plural *crosses* in an example (the site marks listed forms automatically); and a suggestion to merge the British "suitcase" sense of *case* into the general container sense (it is regional and translates differently, so it passes the house test for a separate sense).

After review, six definitions that used words outside the dictionary's defining vocabulary (*suitcase*, *reporters*, *wooden*, *ancient*, *railroad*) were reworded with plainer words. The cross-reference tool adds a matching link back from each word an entry points to, but it could not decide which sense should carry six of them; I placed those by hand in *meeting*, *check*, *mixture*, *journey*, *fasten*, and *attach*.
