## What this cycle did

The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose a **closure** cycle. A closure cycle writes new entries for words the dictionary already uses but does not yet define: words that appear in definitions ("closure" rows), words named in another entry's word family ("family" rows), and words named in cross-references. The aim is that a reader who follows any word in the dictionary finds an entry for it.

There was one closure row: *south*, used in a definition in the adverb *down*. The dictionary had no entry for any of the four compass points. The other eleven came from word families, all verbs formed from existing entries: *busy* (*busy yourself with something*), *centralize*, *cheapen*, *commercialize*, *deaden*, *deafen*, *debrief*, *dirty*, *discount*, *distance* (*distance yourself from something*), and *doctor* (*doctor a photo*). Each was written from my own knowledge under the house style guide, then sent through the full checking pipeline. The dictionary now has 2,411 entries, all published.

All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel. *Discount* as a verb is said two ways in American English; the entry gives *DIS-count* as the main form and *dis-COUNT* as a variant, and British *dis-COUNT*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers ("reviewer-a" and "reviewer-b", two different language models) raised 48 issues, 34 of them blocking. I accepted 44 and rejected 4. That is a far higher rate of acceptance than in review cycles: new entries have more real faults than ones already read once. The main changes:

- **south**: both reviewers pointed out that a learner error (*fly to south* corrected to *fly south*) and part of the usage note taught the adverb, which belongs in its own entry under the house rule of one part of speech per entry. The error now shows a noun mistake (*ten miles to south* corrected to *to the south*), and the usage note only points to the adverb.
- **cheapen**: sense 2 said "make something cost less to make or to buy", with examples like *cheapen the price*. Both reviewers said this is not natural, and they were right: the verb is used about making things cheaper to produce, not about shop prices. The sense, its examples, and its collocations were rewritten.
- **deafen**: sense 1 was defined as "be so loud that...", which does not fit a verb whose object is the person who cannot hear. It is now "make someone unable to hear anything else, because a noise is very loud". An example in which an illness "deafened" a child was replaced.
- **debrief**: an example used the verb without an object (*Let's debrief tomorrow*), a use the definition did not cover. The example was replaced, and the entry is now transitive only.
- **doctor**: the note for translators said the verb never means "give medical treatment". It does, rarely and informally (*doctor a cut*); the note now says so. Sense 2 (adding something to food or drink) no longer insists the thing added is harmful.
- Smaller fixes: *centralize* lost a "politics" subject label that was too narrow; *deaden* now includes weakening an effect; collocation lists were corrected on *busy*, *discount*, and *distance*.

Rejections: twice, reviewer-a wanted *heavily discounted* and *heavily commercialized* filed as "adverb + past participle", a type that does not exist in the dictionary's closed list of collocation types; a past participle is a verb form, so "adverb + verb" stands. It also wanted *brief* removed from the word family of *debrief*; the two words share a stem, and *brief* already lists *debrief*.

After the review, the cross-reference tool added five links back to the new entries (in *deafen*, *brief*, *clean*, *dirt*, and *distant*). One of them duplicated a "see also" link in *deafen*, which I removed by hand.
