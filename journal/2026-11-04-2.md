# Journal, 2026-11-04 (second run of the day)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work from what is most overdue) chose **build**: new entries taken in order from the queue, the list of words the dictionary still needs. Its reason was the "highest scheduler debt", meaning building had gone longest relative to its share. It set a limit of 12 entries and a paid-call budget of US$3. The checks before the cycle found no unfinished pull requests, no leftover branches, no open entry reports from readers, and nothing in `inbox/`.

I wrote twelve common nouns from band 2. Bands are the dictionary's five frequency levels, and band 2 means "common". The words were *buyer*, *cab*, *cafe*, *cafeteria*, *calorie*, *campaign*, *can* (the container, not the verb *can*), *candidate*, *cap*, *capacity*, *car park* (labeled British, with *parking lot* and *parking garage* given as the American words) and *cart*. Six idioms were added as well: *a buyer's market*, *open a can of worms*, *in the can*, *kick the can down the road*, *put on your thinking cap* and *put the cart before the horse*.

**Checks.** The pronunciation panel is a set of models that transcribe each word independently, compared against the open CMU pronouncing dictionary where it has the word. It agreed with all 24 transcriptions (American and British for each entry). The plural forms were generated by rule, and all were regular.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Each entry goes to two reviewers from different companies, which judge it field by field. They raised 61 issues, 6 of them *blocking*, meaning the entry cannot be published until the issue is settled. All six were right and I fixed them:

- The pronunciation note for *cafeteria* said the stress falls on the fourth syllable. It falls on the third (*kaf-uh-TEER-ee-uh*), and the note's own respelling showed that. Both reviewers caught it.
- An example for the science sense of *calorie* stated a scientific definition. House rules ban checkable facts in examples, so I replaced it.
- *Buyer* was defined as someone buying "especially something large". It applies to any purchase, so the restriction is gone, and the definition no longer uses the word *buy*.

Of the 55 minor issues I applied most. The ones that changed the most:

- *Candidate*: the exam sense now also covers university degree students (*a PhD candidate*) and moved to last place. "Stood as a candidate" (British) became "ran as a candidate".
- *Can*: the "toilet" slang sense is now marked as always *the can*. Spray cans are covered.
- *Cab*: the driver's cab is no longer said to be "at the front", since a crane's isn't.
- Several notes repeated facts already given in another field, which breaks the "one fact, one field" rule. Those notes were removed.

I rejected four issues, each with a logged reason:

- *Upset the apple cart* belongs under *apple*, because idioms are filed under their first noun.
- A request to hand-mark *putting* in an idiom example contradicts the marking rules: the site marks the headword itself.
- A complaint that one adaptation note (a note for future translators) was generic. It names a specific trap: a soup can and a garbage can are often different words in other languages.
- A mushroom-top sense for *cap*. I added it at first, then removed it. *Mushroom* has no entry yet, and the defining-vocabulary gate (a check that definitions use only words the dictionary already explains) refuses it. *Mushroom* is now queued, and the sense is noted in `NEXT.md` for when that entry exists.

Four cross-references could not be mirrored automatically, because the tool could not tell which sense of the target entry they belonged to. I placed them by hand in *lid*, *limit*, *ability* and *role*.

**Spend.** US$0.36 for the reviewers and the pronunciation panel. Today's total is US$1.04 of the US$15 daily cap.

**What did not work.** Only the *mushroom* problem above. The reviewer suggestion and the vocabulary rule pulled in opposite directions, and the rule won.

**What is next.** More band-2 nouns in queue order from *cart* onward, or whatever the selector picks. The next lint pass (the periodic consistency check) is due in three runs.

**For the owner.** Nothing new. The open questions listed in `NEXT.md` still stand.

## Cycle 2: review

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose **review**, a second reading of entries that already exist, with a limit of 20 entries. It did two pieces of work.

The first was clearing the open items on the review list in `NEXT.md`, the file that tells each run what to do next. Five entries had a cross-reference that pointed at a phrase rather than a meaning:

- *also* and *too* pointed at *well*, meaning the phrase *as well*.
- *slightly* pointed at *bit*, meaning *a bit*.
- *whatever* pointed at *matter*, meaning *no matter what*.

A synonym link has to be mirrored on a matching sense of the other entry, and a phrase has no sense, so these links could never be completed. Each one is now an entry-level "see also" link with a short note (*for **as well***). See-also links do not need a mirror.

Two related links were fixed as well:

- The *look after* / *look for* comparison is now mirrored on the "search" sense of *look*.
- *Birthday* listed *anniversary* both as a synonym and as a see-also link. The duplicate is gone and the synonym is mirrored on *anniversary*.

The second piece was the coverage pass. This is the owner's request that the most important band-1 entries be re-read for meanings and uses they are missing. Three entries were read this cycle, making 184 so far:

- **Interest**:
  - added the meaning "a group with a shared aim" (*powerful business interests*, *special interests*)
  - added two idioms: *out of interest* (labeled British) and *have someone's interests at heart*
  - corrected the countability of the "wanting to know" sense, which takes *an* in *take an interest*
- **Hit**:
  - added "attack or rob a place"
  - added "go to or arrive at a place" (*hit the beach*, *the phone hits the stores next week*)
  - added "meet a problem on the way" (*we hit traffic*)
- **Green**:
  - the "not ripe" sense now also covers freshly cut wood

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The eight entries that changed went to both reviewers. They raised 75 issues, 4 of them blocking.

- *Also* compared sentence-opening *Also, ...* with *similarly*, which means something different. I replaced it with *besides* and removed the mirror link in *similarly*.
- Three blocking issues asked for idioms that the house rules file elsewhere: *hit the road*, *hit the nail on the head* and *too bad*. Idioms go under their first noun, or under *bad* in the last case, and all three are already there. I rejected them with that reason.

Most of the minor issues were the same complaint: a fact stated in two or three places. An example is the rule "use *either*, not *too*, in negative sentences", which appeared in an explanation, a comparison note and a learner error. I cut those duplicates, so each fact now lives in one field. I also replaced two learner errors that reviewers rightly called invented. *He hitted the ball* is gone. *I am slight tired* became *I have a slightly headache*, a mistake learners really make. 62 issues were applied and 13 rejected.

**Spend.** US$0.33. Today's total is US$1.37 of US$15.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. One new definition used *target*, which is not in the defining vocabulary, and the vocabulary check caught it; it now says *a thing*.

**What is next.** The coverage pass continues with 22 unread six-sense entries, starting from *go out*.

**For the owner.** Nothing new.
