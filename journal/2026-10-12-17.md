## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle. A review sends existing entries back to the two outside reviewer models (reviewer-a and reviewer-b, from two different companies) for a second reading, to catch what the first reading missed. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose it because review had the largest backlog of waiting work after the last run's build. No drafts were waiting, so I took the twelve oldest entries that had been through the panel only once. All are nouns written on 4 October: *increase*, *industry*, *infection*, *influence*, *injury*, *insect*, *instrument*, *intelligence*, *intention*, *interest*, *internet*, and *iron*. As the last few review cycles advised, I started the panel in the background so that the ten-minute limit on a single command could not cut it off. It finished in about nine minutes.

Before starting, I checked for unfinished work from earlier runs. There were no open pull requests (proposed changes waiting to be merged), and the three leftover `claude/*` branches had already been fully merged and are already listed for the owner to delete.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 25 issues, 20 of them "blocking" (an entry cannot stay marked as reviewed until each one has a decision). I applied 16 and rejected 9. None needed to go to the owner. *industry* and *insect* came back with no issues. Every decision is logged, with a short reason, in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Applied (each fix written in my own words):

- *iron*: the golf club had been mentioned only in the usage note (a short paragraph on a point of use). reviewer-b said it is a separate meaning, and it is: many languages translate it with a different word. It is now sense 3, labelled as a sport word, with its own examples (*a five iron*) and collocations (words that typically go with it). With three senses the entry now needs a core idea (one sentence on what the senses share), so I wrote one. The usage note is now empty.
- *infection*: sense 1 had said "an illness in part of the body", but an infection can affect the whole body. Sense 2 had said "getting a disease, or passing it to other people", which did not fit an example about keeping a wound clean. Both definitions were rewritten.
- *influence*: the usage note said **influence** takes **on** for the person affected, but the entry's own examples also show **of** for the source (*the influence of jazz*). The usage note and the note for translators now give both. The translators' note had also put a wrong form in italics, which house style does not allow; it now says the preposition **to** is not used.
- *instrument*: sense 2 is a tool for careful or exact work, but one example (*He was hit with a heavy instrument*) and the collocation *a blunt instrument* were about weapons. The example now shows a nurse handing a doctor an instrument, and the collocation was removed. Sense 3 had limited measuring instruments to cars and aircraft; it now says they measure or record things, with aircraft, cars, and laboratories as examples only.
- *intelligence*: the note for translators mentioned a plural, *intelligences*, while the entry's forms say there is no plural. Both reviewers flagged it. The note now says the word takes no **a** or **an** and, in ordinary use, has no plural.
- *intention*: *mind* was listed as a synonym (a word with the same meaning), which it is not. I removed it from both entries, since the dictionary keeps such links on both sides and a one-sided removal would come back. The usage note now says a to-infinitive is more common after **announce** and **state**, while **of** and an -ing form is also possible.

Rejected:

- reviewer-a said the *injury* definition "physical harm or damage..." is not a noun phrase. It is: *harm* is the noun at its head. It also said the translators' note on *injury* and *damage* conflicts with the note comparing the words. Both say "usually" and agree on the medical exception (*brain damage*).
- *under the influence* means drunk or affected by drugs, "especially while driving". "Especially" names the typical setting without limiting the meaning.
- The *interest* note that the money sense and the "wanting to know" sense are different words "in many languages" is hedged and true.
- *computer* is a fair "see also" for *internet*, and *have too many irons in the fire* does not need an informal label.
