# 2026-09-26 (twenty-first run): eight verbs, *push* to *reach*

## What this run did

This was the second cycle of this session. A cycle is one complete unit of work with its own pull request and merge. The first cycle, a closure run that added ten words (*gamble* to *ethnic*), merged as pull request #89. The branch was then brought up to date with the main line and a new cycle began.

The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each run's kind of work) chose a **build** run. A build run drafts new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. It continued with band 1, the most basic words, where the previous session stopped after *punish*. The eight verbs were:

- **push**: moving something away from you, pressing a button, pushing through a crowd, and pushing someone to act (*My parents pushed me to study law*, *The workers are pushing for higher pay*).
- **put**: five senses (in a place, in a state, writing, saying, and spending time or money), the idiom *put two and two together*, and links to the eight *put* phrasal verbs in the queue (*put away*, *put off*, *put up*, and others).
- **question**: asking someone questions, often officially, and doubting something.
- **quit**: leaving a job or team, stopping a habit, and closing a computer program, with the phrase *quit while you're ahead*.
- **race**: competing, moving fast, hurrying to do something, and a heart or mind *racing*.
- **rain**: the weather verb, which needs *it* as its subject, and things *raining down*, with the phrase *when it rains, it pours*.
- **raise**: six senses (lift, increase, bring up children, collect money, mention a subject, and cause a feeling), with a note on *raise*, *rise*, and *lift*.
- **reach**: arriving, stretching out an arm, being able to touch, getting to a level or result, and contacting someone.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline (the fixed sequence of checks each entry must pass):

- **Format and inflections.** All eight passed the format check. The inflection tool took *put* and *quit* from the table of irregular forms. Its note on *quit* says "also quitted, chiefly British".
- **Pronunciation.** All sixteen American and British transcriptions were confirmed by the panel of pronunciation models. The American ones also agreed with the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 32 issues. Each has a logged decision: 23 applied and 9 rejected.

The fixes that mattered most:

- *Push* sense 3 had been marked intransitive (taking no object), but its own example, *We pushed our way onto the train*, has an object. Both reviewers caught this, and the grammar now allows both.
- *Question* sense 1 said it meant asking "a lot of things". That is too narrow, since a researcher can question students with a few simple questions. It now reads "ask someone about something in order to find out what they know, often in an official way".
- *Quit* sense 2 said "especially something bad for you". That is too narrow too: people quit games and arguments as well as smoking. The learner-error note had said *quitted* is used only in British English. It now says the form is rare and mostly British, which agrees with the inflection note.
- The translator's note on *put* said the verb always needs a place. That is true only of the first sense, and the note now says so.
- *Reach* in the sense of contacting someone now reads "manage to speak to someone or send them a message".

Rejected: reviewer-a said *raise* can be used without an object, citing *The school is raising money*, but that sentence has the object *money*. Six objections from both reviewers to the house habit of opening a translator's note with the headword in quotation marks were also rejected. That habit is settled practice (see the Fences list in `NEXT.md`).

The cross-reference tool added back-links, which are links pointing back from an existing entry to a new one, on *ask*, *mention*, and *arrive*. It also reported a mismatch: *get* lists *reach* as a synonym, but *reach* listed *get* only as a word to compare. *Reach* now lists *get* as a synonym (with the note that *get* takes *to*) and keeps *arrive* as the word to compare.
