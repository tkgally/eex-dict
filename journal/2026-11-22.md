# Report for 2026-11-22

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work from what is most overdue) chose *build*: new entries taken from the queue of words waiting to be written, at most twelve. Twelve band-2 nouns were claimed (band 2 is the second most important of five editorial frequency groups) and all twelve were written and finished: *tale*, *target*, *teamwork*, *teaspoon*, *tech*, *technique*, *teen*, *telescope*, *tendency*, *tension*, *tent*, and *tenth*. The dictionary now has 4,655 entries, all reviewed.

Some highlights. *Target* has four senses (a result you try to reach, a thing you shoot at, someone who is attacked, and the group a product is meant for), the phrase *on target*, and a short note on choosing between *target*, *goal*, and *aim*. *Tension* has four senses, from nervous worry to the tightness of a rope, and a note for translators: in some languages the similar-looking word means blood pressure or electrical voltage, which English *tension* does not. *Teen* covers both the informal word for a teenager and *in your teens*, with a small subsense for temperatures *in the low teens*. *Tale* has two idioms, *tell tales* (mainly British) and *live to tell the tale*. *Technique* warns against the common learner form *technic*. *Tenth* follows the pattern of the existing fraction entries such as *sixth*.

One existing entry was changed on purpose: the instructions for the next sessions (`NEXT.md`) asked that the camping sense of the verb *pitch* say *put up a tent* once an entry for *tent* existed. It now does, and the whole *pitch* entry went through the reviewers again.

**Checks.** Every word form (here, the plurals) was generated by the inflection rules and verified. All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each entry) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, a group of models that vote on each transcription, and by an open pronouncing dictionary for American English. The vocabulary check found four definition words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words definitions should use): *imaginary*, *artistic*, *circus*, and *poles*. All four definitions were reworded in plainer words.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Two reviewer models from different companies read every field of the thirteen entries. They raised 38 issues; only one was blocking (serious enough to stop the entry until settled). That one said *teen* should not carry the grammar code *often before another noun* because a word before a noun (*teen magazines*) is an adjective. The project settled this question long ago: a noun used before another noun is still a noun, and the code exists for exactly that, so I rejected it. Of the 38 issues, 22 were applied and 16 rejected. Applied fixes included: *tell tales* gets a British label; a *humorous* label came off *live to tell the tale*; *on target* now defines both meanings (on schedule and exactly correct) instead of hiding one in an explanation; *teaspoon* loses an unneeded explanation (the abbreviation *tsp* moved to a usage note); a *tension* example about a deadline that really showed worry, not conflict between people, was replaced; a dangling clause in the *tendency* definition was finished; and the *pitch* entry gained a new subsense for *pitch the town into panic*, a clearer signpost, and better collocations. Most rejections were the same settled noun-modifier question raised again on *target* and *tech*, plus a request to split one *pitch* sense that shares one idea, and a request for a date sense on *tenth*, which lives in the number entry by house rule.

**Cross-references.** The cross-reference tool adds matching back-links, but it does not guess which sense of the other entry to use. Five such links were placed by hand: *aim*, *technology*, *skill*, and *stress* now point back to *target*, *tech*, *technique*, and *tension*. A link from *tenth* to *eleventh* was dropped because that queue row is a duplicate.
