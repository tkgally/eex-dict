# 2026-10-11: build — twelve adjectives, *following* to *funny*

## What this cycle did

A **build cycle** drafts new entries from the queue of words still to be written, taking the most important words (band 1) first. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose a build because it had the largest backlog among the modes allowed today. The claim tool handed out the next twelve band-1 adjectives in queue order: *following*, *foreign*, *formal*, *former*, *free*, *frequent*, *fresh*, *friendly*, *frozen*, *full*, *fun*, and *funny*. All twelve were drafted, checked, reviewed, and are now `reviewed` (published on the next site build).

Some highlights. *Fresh* has nine senses (food, not frozen or canned, new, recently made, air, clean and pleasant, water without salt, not tired, and British cool weather), with a subsense for *fresh from college* and a separate phrase for American *fresh out of milk*. *Full* has eight senses plus *in full*, *full of yourself*, and *to the full*. *Free* has seven (no cost, not controlled, not a prisoner, not busy, not being used, *free of* something bad, not fixed) and the phrases *for free* and *free and easy*. *Fun* and *funny* each carry a note that settles the most common learner confusion between them (*a fun party* is enjoyable; *a funny party* made people laugh, or was strange). *Former* explains *former* / *previous* / *ex-*. *Friendly* warns that it is an adjective even though it ends in **-ly** (*He talked to me in a friendly way*, not *talked friendly*).

## Pronunciation and word forms

The pronunciation panel (three outside models, plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary for American English, consulted at run time and not stored) verified all 24 transcriptions on the first pass. The word-form tool generated the comparative and superlative forms from the grammar codes: *freer*, *fresher*, *friendlier*, *fuller*, *funnier*; *more formal*, *more frequent*, *more fun*; none for *following*, *former*, and *frozen*, which are not compared. *Foreign* first came back undecided, because its sense *not familiar* had no gradability code; I added *comparative with more* (*more foreign*), and it then verified.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models (reviewer-a and reviewer-b) read every field. *Following* came back clean. The other eleven drew 58 issues, 51 of them marked blocking. I applied 42 and rejected 16, each logged with a reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. Applied, among others:

- *full*: sense 6 was defined as *busy* but its example was *a full and happy life*; it now reads *having a lot of activities or experiences*. *A full moon* moved out of the *rounded* sense into its own subsense under *complete*. *Full with water* is now called rare rather than wrong; *to the full* gained a British label, with American *to the fullest* named.
- *free*: *Children travel free* is an adverb use, so the example was replaced and the usage note now points to *free* (adverb), queued. *Free of charge* was moved off the *without something bad* sense.
- *frozen*: two examples in the money sense were passive verbs (*accounts were frozen by the court*), not adjectives; they were rewritten. The note contrasting *frozen* and *freezing* was softened: *freezing* is the usual word for weather, but a person can be *frozen*.
- *fun*: the entry-level *informal* label was removed (*a fun day* is now ordinary in speech), and the note on *funner* / *funnest* now says they are increasingly common but disliked by many.
- *funny*: *go funny* (stop working properly) moved from *slightly ill* to *strange*.

Rejected: reviewer-a called *the former* (*the first of two*) a pronoun; I kept it as a noun, queued as `former-n`, to match how the dictionary already treats *the following* (`following-n`). It also argued that *Skiing is fun* shows the adjective; the existing *fun* (noun) entry treats it as the noun, and I kept that for consistency. *Completely free* does not make *free* gradable (*completely* goes with ungradable adjectives). *Serious* stays as a contrast to *funny*, and *rich* stays as a synonym of *full* for a voice or flavor.

The vocabulary check found six definition words outside the defining vocabulary (*procedure*, *everyday*, *cage*, *replacing*, *cheerful*, *suspicious*); I reworded each definition. The cross-reference tool added seven return links on other entries. Three landed on the right sense; four were moved by hand: *full* on `busy-adj` to the *time* sense, *frozen* on `cold-adj` to *feeling cold*, *free* on `empty-adj` to *not being used*, and *fresh* on `old-adj` to *not new*. Twenty-six new words were queued (for example `free-adv`, `former-n`, `unfriendly-adj`, `freezing-adj`).
