## The judgmental finding: core verbs missing from the queue

The main result of this pass came from a clue in cycle 3. When I wrote *cry* (noun), the cross-reference tool found that *cry* (verb) had never been put in the queue of words to write, although it is one of the commonest verbs in English.

The cause: the core queue (band 1) was built on 16 September from three models' proposals, one row for each word and part of speech that at least two models proposed. The models were asked for words by topic. A word such as *laugh* was proposed under "feelings" as a noun, so only the noun was queued. The defining vocabulary is a plain list of words, so *laugh* counts as covered there, but the verb was never scheduled.

A scan found 926 words on the defining list that have a noun row but no verb row and no verb entry. Most of them are rightly nouns only (*aunt*, *bathroom*, *ceiling*). I went through the list and queued by hand the 43 whose verb use is common:

- at band 1 (to be written soon): *laugh, smile, shake, knock, smoke, plant, promise, report, sign, wave, escape, switch*; I also raised the existing rows for *cry* and *print* to band 1;
- at band 2: 31 more, from *frown* and *scratch* to *upload*, *download*, and *protest*.

Each new row carries a note saying it was added by hand and why. The finding is written up in `wiki/notes/queue-missing-verb-rows.md`, now listed in the wiki index. The same gap probably exists the other way round (verbs missing a noun row) and for adjectives. Checking those is listed for the next lint pass; choosing the words stays an editorial judgment, not a script's.
