## What this cycle did

The selector chose **review**: a second reading of entries the outside reviewers have seen only once. I did two things.

**1. The owner's ruling on auxiliary verbs, first part.** On 2026-10-05 the owner ruled that *be*, *have*, and *do* used as helping verbs ("auxiliary verbs") should be separate entries, one per review cycle. Until now the verb entry *be* held both uses. One was the main verb (*She is a doctor*, *The keys are on the table*, *There is a problem*). The other was the helping verb (*is raining*, *was built*, *you are to wait here*).

The new entry `be-aux` has three senses:

- the -ing tenses (*Yuki is studying*), including the present for fixed plans (*I'm meeting Sam tomorrow*);
- the passive (*The bridge was built many years ago*);
- *be to* for plans and orders (*You are to wait here*), labeled formal.

It also has:

- a usage note on questions, negatives, and short answers (*Is it raining? Yes, it is*), and on never using *do* with *be*;
- three learner errors: *She working now*, *I am knowing the answer*, and *The house was build last year*;
- a pronunciation note on the weak forms (*I'm going*, *wuz*);
- a note for translators on the -ing tenses and the passive.

`be-v` now keeps only its three main-verb senses. Its summary sentence, usage note, translator note, and "see also" list all point to the new entry, and the new entry points back.

**2. A second reading of nine entries:** *bee*, *beer*, *beginning*, *behavior*, *belief*, *belt*, *bench*, *benefit*, *berry*. These were the oldest entries with only one review round. They were already fairly full. I added what was missing:

- three idioms: *small beer* (British, "not important"), *belt and suspenders* (American, "very careful"), and *warm the bench* (American, "sit and not play");
- a learner error on *seat belt* (not *security belt*);
- collocations: *worker bee*, *queen bee*, *beer belly*, *behavior problems*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

I logged 25 decisions: 18 changes applied, 7 rejected, none sent to the owner. The useful catches:

- *berry* said every berry is "round" and grows "on a bush or a tree". That is too narrow, so it now says "on a bush or another plant".
- *bench*: its summary reduced the law sense to "the people who sit there". It now says the bench in law means the judges.
- *behavior*: "the way an animal or machine *usually* acts" lost the "usually".
- *benefit* had the British term *housing benefit* in an unlabeled sense. It is now *disability benefits*.
- *be-aux*: *be to* also covers events simply arranged to happen (*The two leaders are to meet next week*). The definition now says so.
- *beginning*: I had added *start* and *end* as cross-references, but they were already there. The doubles are removed.

The rejections:

- Reviewer-b wanted the forms of *be* listed in the new entry. Helping verbs carry no forms by design; the forms stay in `be-v`.
- I kept the note that *on benefits* is mainly British (American English says *on welfare*).
