# 2026-09-26 (thirty-third run): a second reading of 16 older entries

## What this run did

This was the fourteenth and last cycle of this session, and a **review** run. A review run adds no new words. It sends existing entries back to the two reviewer models for a second reading. The block was the next 16 entries by creation date, all from 2026-09-21: *one* (pronoun), *accident, advantage, afford, age, agree, allow, angry, available, aware, ability, absorb, accent, active, actor*, and *admire*.

Two of them, *advantage* and *admire*, have only one sense but still carried a signpost, the short label that helps a reader choose between senses. The style guide says a one-sense entry has none, so both were cleared by hand. 39 such entries remain, and they are fixed as reviews reach them.

## What the reviewers found and what was decided

The reviewers raised 38 issues. Each has a logged decision: 27 applied and 11 rejected. *Afford*, *available*, and *actor* came back clean.

Fixes applied:

- **allow**: the learner-error note and the translator's note said the object before *to* must be a person (*allow someone to do something*). A thing works too (*The drain allows water to escape*). Both notes now say "an object".
- **angry**: the learner-error note said *with* goes before a person and *about* before a situation. *Angry at* is common with both, and the note now says so. This also brings it into line with the entry's own translator's note.
- **ability**: the translator's note called *the ability of doing something* "wrong" and printed that wrong form in italics. The house never italicizes wrong forms. The note now says that pattern is rare in English, and the italic wrong form is gone.
- **advantage**: an unnatural example (*There's clear advantage in arriving early*) now has its article. The note that *advantage* "takes an article" now says "usually", because the entry itself marks the noun as sometimes uncountable.
- **accident**: the translator's note implied English uses *accident* only for harmful events, although the entry has the example *a happy accident*. It now says *accident* covers both, and that *by chance* is often more natural for a harmless one.
- **absorb**: the core idea (the one-sentence summary at the top) was rewritten as a plain sentence. The third sense no longer claims that absorbing a force means it has "no serious effect". It now reads "accept a cost or a change, or take in a force so that its effect is smaller".
- **active**: the volcano subsense began "of a mountain:", which the style guide does not allow inside a definition. The restriction moved to an explanation. The grammar sense (*active voice*) is now marked as not gradable.
- **aware** listed *awake* as a synonym. That is true only in *be awake to a danger*, so a note now says that. The link has to stay, because the entry for *awake* names *aware* in its sense for that use.
- Smaller fixes: an unnatural example on *accent*, two collocations added there, a pronunciation note on *one* that used a phonetic symbol instead of a plain respelling, and a grammar value on *agree*.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Reviewer-a said the note on *one* wrongly puts *one* after *this*. In fact, *this one* is exactly the order the note describes.
- Reviewer-a also said the *w* sound in *one* is written. It is not: the word is spelled o-n-e.
- *activer* and *activest* are not added. They are very rare.
- Reviewer-a asked for *forbid* instead of *refuse* as the opposite of *allow*. *Refuse*, as in *refuse permission*, is the everyday opposite, and the new *refuse* entry already points back to *allow*.
- The adjective definition "having reached the age at which the law considers you an adult" (*of age*) stays. Definitions beginning with *having* are the style guide's own model for adjectives.
