## What this cycle did

The scheduler chose **build**: new entries from the queue of words still to be written. The queue works through "band 1" (the most common words, the ones the dictionary's own definitions are written with) in order, and the nouns had reached *mouth*. *motor, move, movement, movie, mud, murder, muscle, museum, music, musician*.

I stopped at ten, not the allowed twelve, so that each entry could be as full as the earlier ones. Each has every sense a learner is likely to meet, examples, typical word combinations ("collocations"), and, where they help, idioms, a usage note, learner errors, and notes for translators ("adaptation notes"). Some highlights:

- **move**: four senses (an action taken to get something done, a change of home, a body movement, a turn in a game) and four idioms: *get a move on*, *make a move*, *make the first move*, *on the move*. The learner error is *a good move to do*.
- **movement**: moving, a group working for change (*the peace movement*), a change in a situation (*movement in prices*), and a part of a long piece of music.
- **movie**: the film itself, *the movies* as a place to go, and *the movies* as a business, with a note that British English often says *film* and *the cinema*.
- **murder**: the crime, and informal *murder* for something very hard (*The traffic was murder*), with the idioms *get away with murder* and *scream bloody murder* (British *blue murder*). The usage note separates *murder* from the general word *kill*.
- **muscle**: the body part and *muscle* as power (*financial muscle*), with *flex your muscles* and *not move a muscle*; the pronunciation note says the **c** is silent.
- **music**: the sounds, written music (*Can you read music?*), and the school subject, with *face the music* and *music to your ears*, and two learner errors (*a beautiful music*, *listening music*).
- **mud**, **museum**, **musician**, **motor** are shorter, as befits them; *clear as mud* and *a museum piece* are included.

All twenty pronunciations (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation check, which compares several models' transcriptions and an open pronouncing dictionary; the plural forms came from the rules.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models review every field of every entry. 30 issues needed a decision: 22 applied, 8 rejected, none escalated.

Applied, in my own words:

- **movie**: I had put *the movies* as a theater and *the movies* as a business into one sense; they are now two, and an example that sounded British (*What's on at the movies?*) became *What's playing at the movies?*
- **move**: *make a move* meaning *leave* is informal and mainly British; it moved from the definition to the explanation. Missing collocations were added (*one false move*, *your move*).
- **motor**: my notes said too firmly that *engine* is the word for cars; American English also says *motor*. The translator note now says "some languages" instead of "many".
- **murder**: the usage note now says it is killing that the law does not allow, and the note on *manslaughter* no longer says it always means killing without meaning to.
- **movement**: the music sense no longer says the piece is for a large group of players, and the usage note now says *movement* is the usual word, not the only word, for moving in general.

Rejected: four requests to open translator notes with the word in plain quotation marks instead of bold (the house style allows bold); a request to add "unlawful" to the definition of *murder* (the word *crime* already says it); and a request to treat *motor* before another noun (*motor racing*) as a separate sense.

The automatic back-link tool put *move* beside the wrong sense of *action*; I moved it to "a thing that someone does". The second reviewer again gave only partial answers on two entries, the known problem noted in earlier reports.
