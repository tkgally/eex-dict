## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field of every entry. They raised 28 issues, 13 of them marked blocking. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 22 applied, 6 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied:

- **Core ideas.** A "core idea" is the opening sentence of an entry with three or more senses, saying what the senses share. Three were reworded. *Drop*'s said every sense was sudden, which is not true of dropping a subject. *Eat*'s listed the senses instead of linking them, and it mentioned *eat at someone* although the entry shows *eat someone*. *Entertain*'s said the word means "receive" an audience.
- ***eat someone alive*** had two definitions joined by "or". It is now one meaning with examples of it: "treat someone very roughly, for example by beating them easily in a game or by punishing them angrily". My first rewording used *defeat*, *criticize*, and *severely*. Those words are outside the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words that definitions must use), so I changed them.
- ***enter*, sense 4.** The definition said "become part of ... a school", but its collocation *enter college* means starting to study there. It now reads "begin working in a profession or studying at a school or college".
- **Synonyms.** Both reviewers said *feed* is not a synonym of *eat*, and *pass* is not a synonym of *end*. Taken alone, they are right. But each link mirrors a real sense on the other side: *feed* means "eat" when it is said of animals, and *pass* means "come to an end" for a feeling or a storm. Removing only one side would make the next lint pass (the regular tidying run) put it back. So I kept both links and added a note limiting each one: "of animals and babies"; "of a feeling, a storm, or a time". On *drop*, the phrasal verb *drop off* moved from "compare" to "synonyms", with the note "more usual in everyday speech".
- ***enjoy***'s note for translators said the verb "is never used alone", which contradicted its own usage note on the informal *Enjoy!*. It now names *Enjoy!* as the one exception.
- Smaller fixes: *elect someone to something* became *elect someone to the council* and similar; *Police employed...* became *The police employed...*; two passive phrases on *employ* moved to the right collocation type; *entertain* sense 2 now says guests are "usually" in your home; quotation marks became the house bold mark in two notes.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- That the American transcription of *eat* needs a length mark. This is settled house practice (a "fence" in `NEXT.md`), and the CMU pronouncing dictionary agrees.
- That *drop dead*, meaning "die suddenly", should lose its informal label. It is informal; the rudeness of *Drop dead!* is already in its explanation.
- Three flags on collocation types (*eat at home*, *empty something down the sink*, *greatly encourage*). The types are right and the phrases are common.
- That *dry*'s explanation is unnecessary. It explains *dry your eyes*, "stop crying", which the definition does not cover.

**Cross-references.** No back-links were added or changed.
