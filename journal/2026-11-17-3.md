# Run report, 2026-11-17 (third run)

## Cycle 1: closure

**Why this cycle was a closure build.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose closure: writing entries for words the dictionary already uses or points to but does not yet define. It had the largest backlog of the eligible kinds of work. The limit was twelve new entries and US$3 of paid model calls. The first closure source, words used in definitions, is empty, so all twelve came from the second source, `family`: words named in another entry's word family (for example, *shout* the noun, named by *shout* the verb). All twelve are band 2, the second most important frequency group.

**Before the work.** No pull requests were left open by earlier runs. The two old working branches on GitHub (`claude/nice-hawking-93pz5c` and `claude/zealous-mayer-8da3yb`) hold nothing that is not already on `main`, and each already has a line in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` asking the owner to delete it. The inbox was empty and there were no open entry reports (problem reports filed by readers). No weekly digest is due until 2026-11-21.

**What was built.** Twelve nouns, from *self* to *single*:

- **self**: a person's usual character (*her usual cheerful self*) and the inner, aware part of a person (*a sense of self*), with the idiom *be your old self*.
- **servant**: in a home, and of the public (*a public servant*).
- **settlement**: four senses under a core idea (the one-sentence summary every entry with three or more senses carries): an agreement ending an argument, with a subsense for the money or property paid; a place where people have come to live; the process of moving into an area; and the formal payment of a debt. A note for translators warns that the agreement sense and the place sense are different words in many languages.
- **setup**: the way something is arranged, a set of equipment, making equipment ready, and an informal trick. It records the British spelling *set-up* and one real learner error: writing the verb as one word (*to setup* instead of *to set up*).
- **seventh**: the fraction (*a seventh of the cake*), built on the model of *ninth*.
- **shipping**: sending goods, with a subsense for the money paid (*free shipping*), and ships as a group.
- **shooting**: an attack with a gun, the sport, filming, and kicking or throwing at a goal.
- **shopper**, compared with *customer*.
- **shout**: a loud call, and the British and Australian informal *my shout* (my turn to buy drinks), with the idiom *give someone a shout*.
- **silver**: the metal, silver objects, the color, the second-place medal, and British coins.
- **singing**, with a subsense for birds.
- **single**: seven senses (a song, an unmarried person, a hotel room, a British one-way ticket, the game of *singles*, a baseball hit with a cricket subsense, and an American one-dollar bill).

**Checks.** All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. The inflection tool produced the plurals from its rules, including *selves*. Two definitions first used words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words definitions are written in): *targets* in *shooting* and *tennis* and *cricket* in *single*. They were rewritten; *tennis* moved to an example and *cricket* to an explanation.

**What the reviewers found.** Two reviewer models read every field. They raised 38 issues; one was blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry may be published): the baseball sense of *single* said *the first base*, but the term takes no article. That was fixed. Of the 38, I applied 34 and rejected 4, each with a logged reason:

- applied: the *settlement* payment subsense now covers property as well as money, since a divorce settlement can include a house; the *shipping* example *Shipping is free* moved to the cost subsense, where both reviewers said it belonged; *setup*'s computing label was dropped because making equipment ready is not only about computers; the *single* core idea was rewritten to cover every sense, and two explanations that only repeated a label or the grammar were removed; *servant*'s public sense no longer uses the word *serve*, which made it partly circular; the cricket *single* was added because a reviewer showed it was missing.
- rejected: an example comparing someone to a servant (it still shows the home sense); a translator's note said to be generic (it names a specific split, like the style guide's own *bank* example); the date use of *seventh* (it lives in the number entry by house rule); a request for a second bird example (a subsense may have one).

Cross-references: seven back-links (the matching link on the other entry) were placed by hand where the tool could not pick the sense, including on *customer*, *gold*, *album*, *double*, and *return*.

**Spend.** US$0.37 this cycle; US$1.46 of the US$15 daily cap so far today.

**What did not work.** I first stamped the entries with a creation time a few minutes ahead of the clock, and the validator refused it; the times were corrected to the real drafting time before anything was committed.
