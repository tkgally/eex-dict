# Run report, 2026-11-17 (third run)

## Cycle 1: closure

**Why this cycle was a closure build.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose closure: writing entries for words the dictionary already uses or points to but does not yet define. It had the largest backlog of the eligible kinds of work. The limit was twelve new entries and US$3 of paid model calls. The first closure source, words used in definitions, is empty, so all twelve came from the second source, `family`: words named in another entry's word family (for example, *shout* the noun, named by *shout* the verb). All twelve are band 2, the second most important frequency group.

**Before the work.** No pull requests were left open by earlier runs. The two old working branches on GitHub (`claude/nice-hawking-93pz5c` and `claude/zealous-mayer-8da3yb`) hold nothing that is not already on `main`, and each already has a line in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` asking the owner to delete it. The inbox was empty and there were no open entry reports (problem reports filed by readers). No weekly digest is due until 2026-11-21.

**What was built.** Twelve nouns, from *self* to *single*:

- **self**: a person's usual character (*her usual cheerful self*) and the inner, aware part of a person (*a sense of self*), with the idiom *be your old self*.
- **servant**: in a home, and of the public (*a public servant*).
- **settlement**: four senses under a core idea (the one-sentence summary every entry with three or more senses carries): an agreement ending an argument, with a subsense for the money or property paid; a place where people have come to live; the process of moving into an area; and the formal payment of a debt. A note for translators warns that the agreement sense and the place sense are different words in many languages.
- **setup**: the way something is arranged, a set of equipment, making equipment ready, and an informal trick. It records the British spelling *set-up* and one real learner error: writing the verb as one word (*to setup* instead of *to set up*).
- **seventh**: the fraction (*a seventh of the cake*), built on the model of *ninth*.
- **shipping**: sending goods, with a subsense for the money paid (*free shipping*), and ships as a group.
- **shooting**: an attack with a gun, the sport, filming, and kicking or throwing at a goal.
- **shopper**, compared with *customer*.
- **shout**: a loud call, and the British and Australian informal *my shout* (my turn to buy drinks), with the idiom *give someone a shout*.
- **silver**: the metal, silver objects, the color, the second-place medal, and British coins.
- **singing**, with a subsense for birds.
- **single**: seven senses (a song, an unmarried person, a hotel room, a British one-way ticket, the game of *singles*, a baseball hit with a cricket subsense, and an American one-dollar bill).

**Checks.** All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. The inflection tool produced the plurals from its rules, including *selves*. Two definitions first used words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words definitions are written in): *targets* in *shooting* and *tennis* and *cricket* in *single*. They were rewritten; *tennis* moved to an example and *cricket* to an explanation.

**What the reviewers found.** Two reviewer models read every field. They raised 38 issues; one was blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry may be published): the baseball sense of *single* said *the first base*, but the term takes no article. That was fixed. Of the 38, I applied 34 and rejected 4, each with a logged reason:

- applied: the *settlement* payment subsense now covers property as well as money, since a divorce settlement can include a house; the *shipping* example *Shipping is free* moved to the cost subsense, where both reviewers said it belonged; *setup*'s computing label was dropped because making equipment ready is not only about computers; the *single* core idea was rewritten to cover every sense, and two explanations that only repeated a label or the grammar were removed; *servant*'s public sense no longer uses the word *serve*, which made it partly circular; the cricket *single* was added because a reviewer showed it was missing.
- rejected: an example comparing someone to a servant (it still shows the home sense); a translator's note said to be generic (it names a specific split, like the style guide's own *bank* example); the date use of *seventh* (it lives in the number entry by house rule); a request for a second bird example (a subsense may have one).

Cross-references: seven back-links (the matching link on the other entry) were placed by hand where the tool could not pick the sense, including on *customer*, *gold*, *album*, *double*, and *return*.

**Spend.** US$0.37 this cycle; US$1.46 of the US$15 daily cap so far today.

**What did not work.** I first stamped the entries with a creation time a few minutes ahead of the clock, and the validator refused it; the times were corrected to the real drafting time before anything was committed.

**What is next.** The `family` rows continue after *single*; *sixth* and *tenth* as fractions are waiting and follow the same model. The next lint pass (the regular housekeeping check) is due after about five more cycles.

**For the owner.** Nothing new. The two old branches still need deleting by hand.

## Cycle 2: review

**Why this cycle was a review.** After the first cycle merged, the mode selector chose a review: rereading existing entries rather than writing new ones. There were no draft entries and no open entry reports, so the cycle continued the coverage pass. That pass is a second reading of the most important band-1 words (the most common group), most senses first, to make sure every common present-day use, and the common idioms, are in the entry. This cycle read four words with four senses each: *choice*, *circle*, *climb*, and *cloud*.

**What I changed before the reviewers saw the entries.**

- **choice**: the "range of things to choose from" sense listed *option* and *alternative* as synonyms, but they mean the chance to choose, not a range. *Alternative* moved to the right sense (*we had no alternative* is *we had no choice*). An example that repeated the idiom *by choice* was replaced, and the idiom *spoiled for choice* now notes that British English says *spoilt for choice*.
- **circle**: three common uses were missing. Dark circles under the eyes is now a subsense, the American traffic circle is a fifth sense (with a pointer to the British word *roundabout*, which has been queued as a future entry), and *square the circle* (try to do the impossible) is a new idiom. *An inner circle* joined the collocations.
- **climb**: two uses were missing: plants climbing a wall (a subsense) and rising in rank (*the song climbed the charts*, a fifth sense). The fact that the **b** is silent was stated twice, in the pronunciation note and in a note for translators; now it is in one place.
- **cloud**: *a mushroom cloud* was added to the smoke-and-dust sense.

**What the reviewers found.** The two reviewer models raised 25 issues, none blocking. I applied 20 and rejected 5.

- Applied: both reviewers noticed that *climb down* was listed as a collocation of a sense defined as "go up"; climbing down a ladder or a tree is now its own subsense. The "move with effort" sense said you use your hands and feet, which is not true of climbing into bed, so it now says "often slowly or with some effort". A reviewer pointed out the missing idiom *run circles around someone* (American, informal; the British say *run rings around someone*), which was added. The cloud of smoke sense did not cover its own example, *a cloud of flies*; the definition now includes a large group of insects. The note on when *the cloud* (the computing sense) takes an article was moved from an explanation, which is meant for function words, to a note on grammar. The phrase *of choice* was redefined without the formula "used after a noun to say", which is kept for function words.
- Rejected: a request to spell the label `theatre` the American way (it is a fixed value of the closed label list); a request to move *spoilt for choice* to a variant field (phrases have none); a claim that *preference* cannot mean the thing preferred (*my preference is tea*); and two requests for a "usually plural" code on the social-group sense of *circle*, which would be wrong for *a circle of friends*.

**Spend.** US$0.19 for the review panel; US$1.65 of the US$15 daily cap today.

**What did not work.** Two first attempts at the climbing definition used words outside the defining vocabulary (*slope*, *uphill*); the third, *lead up a hill*, passes.

**What is next.** The coverage pass has now read 365 entries. Next are *common* (adjective), *condition*, *control* (verb), and *copy* (verb).

**For the owner.** Nothing new.

## Cycle 3: build

**Why this cycle was a build.** After the second cycle merged, the mode selector chose a build: new entries from the queue (the ordered list of words still waiting for an entry). The limit was twelve; I took eight, so that this third cycle's checks and merge would have plenty of room.

**What was built.** Eight band-2 nouns, in queue order:

- **seafood**, with one real learner error: *seafoods*, since the word is uncountable.
- **search engine** and **seat belt** (the one-word spelling *seatbelt* is recorded as a variant).
- **secret**: hidden information (*keep a secret*, *an open secret*), the way to do something well (*the secret of success*), and something not yet understood (*the secrets of the ocean*), with the idioms *in secret* and *make no secret of something*.
- **secretary**: in an office, of a club, and in government (*the Secretary of State*), with a pointer to *minister*, the word many countries use, and a note for translators that the government sense is the head of a department, not an office helper.
- **segment**: a part (with a subsense for a line segment in mathematics), a piece of an orange, and a part of a television or radio show.
- **selection**: the act of choosing, the thing chosen, and a range to choose from, with *natural selection* as a phrase.
- **semester**, labeled American, with a pointer to *term*, since British schools usually have three terms.

**Checks.** The pronunciation panel (three models plus, for American English, the open CMU pronouncing dictionary) at first disputed my British transcription of *secretary*, which had four syllables. The usual British form has three (*SEK-ruh-tree*), so I made that the main British form and kept the four-syllable form as a careful variant; the panel then agreed (2 of 3). Everything else agreed at once. A first attempt to write the entries failed before any file was written, because my drafting helper did not turn the space in *search engine* and *seat belt* into a hyphen for the file name; fixed and rerun. One definition (*seafood*) first used *shrimp* and *crabs*, words outside the defining vocabulary; it now says "especially ones with shells".

**What the reviewers found.** 16 issues, none blocking, all applied: the core idea of *secretary* described the government secretary as someone who works for others, when sense 3 says a department head; the translators' note called that person a "top leader", which overstated it; an example named a month for the end of a semester (examples must not state checkable dates); *line segment* and *natural selection* were missing; one *secret* example could be read in two senses and was replaced; and a few collocations had the wrong type (*the fall semester* is noun + noun). Two back-links were placed by hand on *assistant* and *range*.

**Spend.** US$0.24 this cycle; US$1.88 of the US$15 daily cap today.

**What is next.** The build queue continues after *semester*. The coverage pass continues with *common*, *condition*, *control*, and *copy*.

**For the owner.** Nothing new.
