# 2026-10-03 (21): second reading of twelve verbs, stop to survive

This was the first cycle of the run that started at 20:43 UTC. A *cycle* is one unit of work that is checked, recorded, and merged on its own. Before any work, the run looked for stranded work: there was no open pull request, and the one leftover `claude/` branch had nothing that `main` lacked (it is already listed for pruning in the curator file). The inbox was empty.

The selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **review**. A review is a second reading of entries that have been through the reviewer panel only once. The panel is two outside models that read every field of an entry and report problems, each marked *blocking* (must be settled before publication) or *minor*.

## What was reviewed

The block was the twelve oldest entries with only one panel round, all verbs written on 2026-09-29: *stop*, *strengthen*, *stretch*, *strip*, *study*, *suffer*, *suggest*, *supply*, *support*, *suppose*, *surprise*, and *survive*.

I read each in full before the panel saw it. Most were sound but thin in places an advanced learner would notice. The additions, all written by hand:

- **stop**: a new British informal sense meaning *stay for a short time* (*Are you stopping for dinner?*), and a subsense for telling a bank not to pay a check.
- **stretch**: a subsense for time (*Their friendship stretches back to their school days*), and the *give someone work that tests them* use split out of the money sense into its own subsense, with *Our budget won't stretch to a new car* added.
- **strip**: a new sense for emptying a place or a vehicle (*Thieves stripped the car*), a subsense for stripping as a performance, `stripper-n` added to the word family and queued.
- **study**: a subsense for going over notes before a test (*I have to study tonight*) and a usage note on that use. **suffer**: a subsense for *suffer for* something you did or believe. **suggest**: a formal subsense for *suggest itself*. **supply**: a subsense for being the source of something the body or a machine needs.
- **support**: a synonym discrimination of *support*, *back*, and *help*.
- Usage notes on *strengthen*, *stretch*, *supply*, and *survive*; a cultural adaptation note on the death-notice phrase *is survived by*; collocations on every sense that had none; ten new learner errors (for example *We need to strength the team* and *I'm surprise that you came*).

*surprise* was the only entry the panel passed with no issues at all.

## What the panel found and what I decided

The panel raised 30 issues, 21 of them blocking. I applied 21 and rejected 9. Each decision has a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Real catches, applied:

- **suppose**: the entry said that *was supposed to* *usually means* the thing did not happen. That is too strong; it now says it *often suggests* so, in both the explanation and the note for translators.
- **stretch**: *stretch the truth* was defined as saying something untrue. It means making a fact seem bigger or better than it is, and the definition now says that. The money sense now covers *be enough to pay for something*, which my new example needed.
- **suffer**: a learner-error note I had added said *suffer from* suggests something serious, which contradicted the entry's own examples (*headaches*, *hay fever*). The note now says only that *have* is more natural for a cold.
- **support**: one of my new learner errors (*supporting me with the money*) was not really an error, and was removed. An older one used the British sports sense; it now uses *the plan*.
- **survive**: the sense *live longer than someone* was labelled formal as a whole, but only the passive *be survived by* is formal. The label became an explanation.
- Smaller fixes: a stiff example on *stop* (now *stopped payment on the checks*), *feed* and *break* removed as synonyms, *suggest itself* redefined as *seem to be a possible answer*.

Rejected: an objection to the core-idea sentence form (house style); `usually passive` called "not a pattern" (it is in the closed list); a claim that learners would not write *striped* for *stripped*; a claim that *I suggest that he waits* is not mainly British; and two synonym objections on *suppose* (*I expect so*, *Say you won...* are the same uses).

The British transcription of *strengthen* came back unverified on the first pronunciation check (one of three votes) and verified three of three on a second check; it is recorded as verified.
