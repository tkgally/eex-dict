# 2026-10-19, cycle 1 of the 00:43 UTC run: second review of *prayer* to *proof*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that had been through the two outside reviewers (the review panel, two language models from other companies) only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose review because it was the most overdue kind of work, and the block is always the oldest such entries. This time that meant twenty nouns drafted on 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-07, in alphabetical order: *prayer, pregnancy, present, president, price, pride, priest, print, prison, prisoner, prize, problem, process, product, profession, profit, program, progress, promise, proof*.

Each entry went to both reviewers again. I read every issue against the entry and either changed the entry (*applied*) or left it as it was (*rejected*, with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`). Nothing was escalated to the owner.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

reviewer-a raised 39 issues, 28 of them marked blocking (meaning the entry should not stand without a decision). reviewer-b raised 2, both minor, and gave a verdict on every field. Four entries drew no issues from either reviewer (*pregnancy, price, prisoner, prize*); most others had one to three. In all I applied 18 and rejected 23.

Applied:

- ***profession***: the job sense listed *the medical profession* and *the legal profession* as its collocations (typical word combinations), but those phrases mean the people in the job, which is sense 2. They now stay with sense 2, and sense 1 has *a respected profession*, *a well-paid profession*, *a demanding profession*. The example *She chose a profession in medicine* was stiff and is now *She chose medicine as her profession*. The opening summary (the *core idea*) said the third sense was a statement of *belief*; it now says *what you believe or feel*, which covers *professions of love*.
- ***problem***: the sense *an objection* (*I have no problem with that*) had an explanation that said the word is *usually in have a problem with*, which seemed to clash with sense 1 (*We have a problem with the car*). The explanation now says this applies to this sense and that a problem with a machine or a plan is sense 1.
- ***priest***: the comparison with *monk* said a monk *does not usually lead services*; some monks are priests, and the note now says so. The usage note on addressing a priest is softened to *do not usually say*.
- ***prison***: the translator's note said that with *the* the word means the building; it now says *with a or the*, matching the entry's own explanation.
- ***present***: the *present*/*gift* comparison now says *gift* is a little more formal and common in shops and advertising, not *the usual word* there.
- ***progress***: the translator's note put the wrong forms *a progress* and *progresses* in italics (house style keeps wrong forms out of prose) and seemed to deny the verb form *progresses*. It now says the noun has no *a* and no plural, and that *progresses* is a form of the verb.
- ***proof***: the printed-copy sense was coded *usually plural* but its own examples used *a proof*; the code is gone.
- Smaller fixes: `prayer` (the *time of praying* sense no longer requires a meeting); *in print* (`print`) now covers any printed text, not just books and newspapers; `president` uses the double mark for words named as words; `product` keeps *child* as a synonym in the *result* sense (*a child of its time*) but now says so in a note.

Rejected, with the main reasons:

- ***pride***: both reviewers wanted the animal sense to say *a group of lions* instead of *a family group of the large wild cats* plus the explanation *Used only of lions*. I made the change, then the vocabulary check (which keeps definitions inside a fixed list of plain words) showed that *lion* is not on the list and has no entry yet. I put the original back and logged the decision again as a rejection.
- *Get with the program* keeps its American label: a region label in this dictionary means *mainly used there*.
- *Today* stays a synonym of *the present*: the *today* entry has a sense for the present age (*the world of today*).
- *Prison* keeps one sense with an explanation for *in prison* versus *the prison*, as *school* and *church* do.
