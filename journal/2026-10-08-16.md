## What this cycle did

An **originality check** tests whether the dictionary's wording is its own. The rules say it must never copy from a published dictionary. The scheduler forces this check every tenth run, and this was run 320.

The check works like this:

1. A tool picks ten fields at random from across the dictionary: definitions, idiom definitions, and usage notes.
2. Each one is searched on the web as an exact phrase, to see whether any published dictionary uses the same or nearly the same words.
3. One outside reviewer model (reviewer-a, asked with its extended reasoning switched off) is asked whether each one reads as copied. If it says "copied", it must quote the published wording.
4. The search results decide the verdict, not the reviewer. In past checks the reviewer has often claimed copying with "quotes" that were really our own wording.

## Results

Six of the ten are **original**. Searches found nothing close for: the note in *race* about the separate homograph; the radio-signal sense of *signal*; the *foot*/*feet* usage note; the note in *match*; the *head* idiom "stay calm and able to think in a difficult situation" (*keep your head*); and the *cost* idiom (*at all costs*).

Four needed a **rewrite**, because search found published learner's dictionaries with wording very close to ours:

- *pants*, sense 1 (trousers). A learner's dictionary has almost the same frame: a piece of clothing from the waist to the ankle, with a separate part for each leg. It now reads *clothing worn over the hips and legs, usually reaching down to the shoes, with one tube of cloth for each leg*.
- *pants*, sense 2 (British, underwear). This was close to a learner's dictionary definition of *panties*. It now reads *underwear worn under other clothes over the bottom and the lowest part of the stomach*.
- *animal*, sense 2 (the scientific sense that includes people). This was close to a learner's dictionary's "any living creature, including humans". It now reads *any creature that moves and eats food, people included*.
- *flight*, sense 3 (stairs). A dictionary page has "a set of stairs between one floor or landing and the next", nearly the same as ours. It now reads *a series of steps that go up without a break from one level of a building to the next*.

The reviewer said "copied" for five items and "original" for five. For each of its five "copied" answers it named one dictionary and gave as its "quote" our own wording, which it cannot have checked. Even so, four of the five pointed at definitions where search did find a real near match. That is the best its "copied" calls have done so far. The new pattern is recorded in `wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`.

## The re-review

A rewrite counts as a review of that entry, so *pants*, *animal*, and *flight* went back through both reviewers. Reviewer-b found nothing. Reviewer-a raised 9 issues; I applied 5 and rejected 4.

- **Applied:** My first rewrite of *animal* was "any living thing that is not a plant, people included". Reviewer-a pointed out that this would also include fungi, which are not animals, so the definition changed again to the one given above. In *flight*, the idiom *take flight* is now defined as *run away, or start to fly*. In *pants*, *a pair of pants* is now listed as a phrase rather than a "noun + preposition" collocation.
- **Rejected:** Reviewer-a said the adaptation note in *animal* calls every living thing an animal. It does not; it says that in science the word covers people, birds, fish, and insects, which is true. It also asked for literary labels on *take flight* and *a flight of fancy*, which are both common in ordinary writing. And it objected to *flee* in the word family of *flight*. The escape sense of *flight* is the noun that goes with *flee*, so the link stays.
