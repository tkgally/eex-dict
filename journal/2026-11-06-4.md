# Journal, 2026-11-06 (run fwhuo1)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from what is most overdue) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be added. The cap for a cycle is twelve new entries, and this cycle wrote all twelve, all nouns in frequency band 2 (common words just outside the core vocabulary): *estate*, *estimate*, *ethics*, *ethnicity*, *European*, *evaluation*, *ex*, *ex-wife*, *examination*, *excess*, *executive*, and *exhibit*. Each went through the full pipeline: schema check, inflections from the spelling rules, a pronunciation check by a panel of models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, two independent model reviewers reading every field, my own decision on each point they raised, and the closing checks. All twelve are now marked `reviewed` and will appear on the site at the next build.

Some content worth knowing about:

- *estate* has four uses: land with a large house (with a subsense for a tea or coffee farm), everything a person owns at death, and the British *housing estate*. The British sense carries a short culture note on *council estate*.
- *estimate* has a note for translators on the noun ending (*ES-tuh-mit*) as against the verb (*ES-tuh-mayt*).
- *ethics* separates the rules a profession follows, the question of whether something is right (*the ethics of testing on animals*), and the academic subject, with a usage note on singular and plural verbs.
- *European* has a learner error, *an European*, corrected to *a European*, because the word starts with the sound of *you*.
- *examination* has four senses (looking closely, a doctor's check, a formal test, questioning in court) and the phrase *on closer examination*.
- *excess* has the phrases *in excess of* and *to excess*, and a British insurance sense pointing to the American *deductible*.
- *exhibit* has the museum object, the American sense of a whole show, the court sense, and the informal phrase *Exhibit A*.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 15 issues, none of them blocking. I applied 14 and rejected 1. The applied ones were small but real: an example for the British housing sense used the American word *highway*; one *evaluation* example belonged to the worker-review sense, not the general one; *a medical examination* was filed under the wrong collocation type; *excess* in its first sense is both countable and uncountable (*an excess of food*, *any excess*); the party or union *executive* is mainly British and now has that label; and *ethnicity* said the same thing about *nationality* in two fields, which breaks the one-fact-one-field rule. The rejection: reviewer B asked for the plural *ex-wives* to be marked by hand in an example, but the house rule is that the site marks every listed inflection itself.

Separately, the vocabulary check found ten words in definitions that are outside the defining vocabulary and have no entry yet (*property*, *culture*, *tradition*, *gallery*, *insurance*, *union*, *philosophy*, *grapes*, *extreme*). I rewrote those definitions in plainer words; the vocabulary check now passes.

**Pronunciation.** 23 of the 24 transcriptions were verified. The American *excess* (stress on the second part) got only one of four panel votes, though the CMU dictionary agrees with it. Both stress patterns are heard, so I kept the second-syllable stress as the main form, listed the first-syllable one as a variant, flagged the entry `pronunciation-disputed`, and added a line to `reviews/needs_curator.txt` for a session with you.

**Cross-references.** The back-link tool could not choose a sense in two older entries, so I placed them by hand: *executive* is now a "compare" on *manager* (sense 1), and *evaluation* a synonym on *assessment* (sense 1). One gap turned up: the existing *race* entry has only the competition senses. The people-group sense comes from a different origin, so by the house rule it needs its own homograph entry, `race-n-2`. *ethnicity* already points to that slug, and `NEXT.md` asks the next build to hand-claim it.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. One surprise: several everyday words (*property*, *culture*, *gallery*) still have no entries. They are on the queue and will come up in order.

## Cycle 2: review

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector chose *review*: rereading existing entries to raise their quality. No entries were waiting as drafts, so the cycle carried on with the B1 coverage pass. That is the owner's ruling to reread the most important everyday words, those with the most senses first, and add whatever a learner would miss. This cycle took the next four of the 111 five-sense band-1 entries not reread since 2026-10-24: *bring*, *burn*, *busy*, and *cheap*. I read each one against my own knowledge of the word before sending it to the two reviewers.

**What I added before the review.**

- *bring*: a new sense for the price something is sold for (*the car should bring two thousand dollars*) and the informal phrase *bring it on*.
- *burn*: a subsense for a light or fire that is burning (giving out light), a sense for copying files onto a CD, and the informal *get burned* (be cheated after trusting someone).
- *cheap*: a sense for something of little value because it is easy (*talk is cheap*).
- All four had notes that repeated another field, which breaks the house rule of one fact in one field. I cut the copies: for example, the *burned*/*burnt* note in *burn* repeated the inflection table, and the bring/take rule in *bring* appeared in three places.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The reviewers raised 43 issues, 3 of them blocking. I applied 28 and rejected 15.

- **Blocking.** Both reviewers caught the same fault in my new *burn* subsense: the definition started with "of a light or a fire:", and the house rule puts that kind of restriction in the explanation. I moved it there. Both also objected to an example in *busy*, "December is the busiest time of the year for shops", because it states a real-world fact tied to one culture's calendar. I replaced it.
- **Coverage gaps the reviewers found.** *Bring* lacked the use for giving a quality to a job (*she brings years of experience to the job*), now subsense 2a. *Cheap* lacked *feel cheap* (feel ashamed), now sense 7.
- **Other changes.** Definitions were corrected for the legal sense of *bring* (the object is the case or charges, not the person) and for the damage sense of *burn* (it can also be intransitive). The core ideas of *busy* and *cheap* were widened to cover all their senses, an example for one sense was moved to the sense it fits, and a "mainly British" note was added to *dear* as an opposite of *cheap*.
- **Rejected.** Ten of the rejections were reviewer B asking for every *brought* in an example to be marked by hand. The house rule is that the site marks listed inflections itself, so these were noise. I also rejected two idioms suggested for *burn*, because the keyword rule files *burn your bridges* under *bridge* and *burn the candle at both ends* under *candle*. Two proposed sense merges were rejected because the senses translate differently in many languages.

**One process slip, corrected.** I first logged the ten *brought* rejections against the same issue ten times. I removed those ten lines before committing and logged each rejection against its own issue. The decision ledger in this pull request is correct.

**Other.** The adverb *cheap* (*I got it cheap*) has no entry, so I queued it.

**Spend.** US$0.22 for the cycle. That brings today's total to US$2.74 of US$15.

**What is next.** The coverage pass continues with *check*, *cold*, *color*, and *comfortable*.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new.

## Cycle 3: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose *build* again. I wrote eight new entries, fewer than the twelve allowed, to keep each one full and to leave room for checking. The first, `race-n-2`, was the gap found in cycle 1: the existing *race* entry covers only contests of speed, and the sense of a group of people comes from a different origin, so the house rule gives it a separate entry, a homograph numbered 2. The other seven came from the queue in order: *exhibition*, *existence*, *expansion*, *expectation*, *expense*, *expertise*, and *explosion*.

Points of interest:

- *race* (people): a sense for the groups people are divided into by features such as skin color, a sense for kinds of creatures in stories and games (*an alien race*), and the phrase *the human race*. A short note for translators says that in some languages the matching word is avoided for people because of its history, while English still uses *race* in law and on forms.
- *exhibition*: the public show, a show of skill (*an exhibition of skill*), the sports sense (*an exhibition game*), and *make an exhibition of yourself*. A pronunciation note warns that the **h** is silent and the stress differs from *exhibit*.
- *expense*: the cost, a thing you must pay for, work expenses, and the phrases *at the expense of something* and *at someone's expense*.
- *expertise*: a learner error (*many expertises*), because the word is uncountable, and a stress note (*ek-sper-TEEZ*).
- *existence*: a learner error for the common misspelling *existance*.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The reviewers raised 17 issues, 3 of them blocking, and I applied all 17.

- **Blocking.** An *expansion* example stated a general law of physics ("Heat causes the expansion of the metal"), which the house rules forbid. I replaced it with a scene. My phrase *at someone's expense* mixed two unrelated meanings (being laughed at, and someone else paying). The phrase now keeps only the first; the second is an example under the cost sense. Sense 1 of *explosion* was marked countable or uncountable, but in ordinary use it is countable only.
- **Minor.** The sports sense of *exhibition* now covers practice games. The British phrase *make an exhibition of yourself* is now labeled British. *Expectation* no longer says "a strong belief", since *low expectations* exist. Two collocation lists were tidied.

**Something to watch.** The script that stamps each new entry with its frequency band and defining-vocabulary flag (it reads them from the queue) gave `race-n-2` the band and flag of the old `race-n` queue row: band 1, and in the defining vocabulary. That is plausible, since the defining vocabulary probably needs this sense of *race*, but no band vote has looked at it. House rules say bands come only from the vote, so I left the value as stamped and noted it in the log for the next band vote.

**Cross-references.** The back-link tool could not choose a sense in *cost*, so I placed the link to *expense* in sense 1 (price) by hand.
