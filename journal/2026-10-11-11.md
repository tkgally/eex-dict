## What this cycle did

A **build cycle** drafts new entries from the queue of words still to be written, taking the most important words (band 1) first. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose a build because it had the largest backlog among the modes allowed today. The claim tool handed out the next twelve band-1 adjectives in queue order: *false*, *familiar*, *fast*, *fat*, *female*, *final*, *financial*, *fine*, *first*, *fit*, *flat*, and *flexible*.

I did not draft *first*. Almost all its uses (*the first day*, *first prize*) are those of an ordinal number, and the queue already has a separate number entry for *first* waiting to be written. Drafting the adjective first would put those uses in the wrong entry. I set the row to `deferred` with a note to decide, once the number entry exists, whether any true adjective use is left.

The other eleven were drafted, checked, reviewed, and are now `reviewed`. Some highlights: *flat* has nine senses (level ground, low shoes, a flat tire, a flat drink, a flat battery, a flat voice, a flat rate, a flat refusal, and a note sung too low); *fine* has seven, from *That's fine* and *I'm fine* to *fine sand* and *a fine day*; *false* separates *not true*, *not real* (*false teeth*), *meant to trick* (*a false name*), *giving a wrong idea* (*false hope*), and *not sincere* (*a false smile*). Several entries have notes that settle a common choice: *false* / *wrong* / *untrue*, *fast* / *quick* / *rapid*, *final* / *last*, and *fat* / *overweight* / *plump*.

## Pronunciation

The pronunciation panel (three outside models, plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary for American English, consulted at run time and not stored) confirmed 21 of 22 transcriptions at once. The British *false* was disputed: only one of three panel members agreed with my `fɔːls`. Both forms are heard in Britain, so I made the panel's preferred `fɒls` the main British form and kept `fɔːls` as a listed variant. The re-check then verified it.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models review every field. *Final* came back clean; the rest drew 48 issues. I applied 41 and rejected 7, each logged with a reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. Most of the applied issues were real:

- *fast* had a rare sense, *firmly fixed* (*made fast*), that was poorly labeled; I removed it. Its learner error about *fastly* belongs to the adverb *fast*, which is queued, so it was removed from the adjective.
- *fat*: the body sense now carries the label *disapproving*, matching its notes; *heavy* was taken out of the advice on polite words, because it is not reliably polite; the learner error *I became fat*, which is not actually wrong, was replaced with a real one (*He is very fatty*).
- *flat*: the second sense had joined unrelated uses (a flat dish, flat shoes, flat feet) under one wrong definition, so it was redefined as *having little height or depth*, and flat feet were dropped. *Fall flat* was removed: it is an idiom that belongs under *fall*.
- *financial* and *fit* (meaning *suitable*) had wrong position codes; both can stand after a verb as well as before a noun.

Rejected: the British label stays on *a fine day* (the project's rule is that a region label means *mainly* used there); *female* still defines the sex as a class (*the sex that can have babies or produce eggs*), because the definition does not claim every individual can; *plants* stays as the domain label, because *botany* is not in the closed list; and *comparative with more* on *flexible* is a valid grammar code.

The cross-reference tool added six return links. Five landed on the right sense; *dead* had *flat* attached to *no longer alive*, so I moved it by hand to *not working* (a dead battery).
