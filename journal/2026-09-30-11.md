# 2026-09-30 (11) — Closure: *amaze, avoid, behave, drawing, painting* and seven family verbs

## What this run did

This was the third cycle of the run that began at 12:43 UTC on 2026-09-30. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request, the proposed change that is checked automatically and then merged. The first two cycles merged as #150 and #151.) The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **closure**. That means drafting words the dictionary already uses, either in its definitions (the "closure" source) or as related words named by existing entries (the "family" source).

**The closure rows were wrong.** The five rows the queue offered from the closure source all had a wrongly guessed part of speech or form: *amazed* as a verb, *avoid* and *behave* as nouns, and the plurals *drawings* and *paintings*. The tool that adds these rows guesses the part of speech when a word in a definition has no entry, and here it guessed badly. This is a known weakness. I marked those five rows as duplicates or declined, each with a note, and added one line to `reviews/needs_curator.txt` (the owner's to-do list). Then I drafted the real words: the verbs *amaze*, *avoid* (which was not in the queue at all, so I added it), and *behave*, and the nouns *drawing* and *painting*.

**Seven family words.** To make up the twelve, I took the next seven rows from the family source, all verbs: *alphabetize*, *anger*, *angle*, *attend*, *author*, *authorize*, and *baby* (*stop babying him*).

Some points worth knowing:

- *avoid* has three senses and the classic learner error *avoid to eat* (it takes *eating*), plus *the fence avoids the dog from running away* (that should be *stops* or *prevents*).
- *attend* covers *attend a meeting* and *attend to a problem*, with learner errors for *attended to the meeting* and for using *attend* to mean *wait*.
- *angle* holds only the direction senses (*angle the lamp toward the desk*; *a program angled at teenagers*). *Angle* meaning *fish with a hook*, and *angle for something*, come from a different word. They need a separate entry numbered as a second homograph (same spelling and part of speech, unrelated meaning). The queue cannot yet hold that numbering, so I added it to the existing note on this problem.
- *drawing* has a mainly American sense, the event where winning tickets are chosen (British English says *draw*).

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The pronunciation check agreed with all 24 transcriptions. The two outside reviewers raised 32 issues, 17 of them marked blocking. I decided each one myself: 18 applied, 14 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied, among others:

- *avoid*: my notes said the word is only about keeping things away from yourself. reviewer-a pointed out that *avoid upsetting her* is normal. All four notes now say it is about what you do or what happens to you, and that stopping someone else is *prevent* or *stop*.
- *angle*: the usage note said the fishing verb has "a different origin". House rules keep claims about origin in the etymology field only, and both reviewers flagged it. The note now just says the fishing verb is a separate headword. The first sense also lost the words "not straight".
- *drawing*: the comparison between American and British English moved from the explanation to a usage note, *win a drawing* became *enter a drawing*, and the learner-error correction now reads *I drew a picture with a pencil*.
- *author* (verb): the note that people "usually" say *write* in speech was softened to "more common".
- *baby* (verb): a note for translators claimed that many languages have no such verb. There was no support for that, so it was removed.

Rejected, with reasons logged: that *drawing* in the lottery sense should not carry an *American* label (a region label means *mainly* used there, as the project has settled); that *attention* and *attentive* do not belong in *attend*'s word family; a British pronunciation objection to *alphabetize* (the pronunciation panel agreed with ours); and six flags on notes for translators that open with the headword in quotation marks, which house style allows.

**Cross-references.** The tool that keeps links matching in both directions reported one mismatch. *surprise* listed *amaze* as a synonym, but *amaze* listed *surprise* only as a word to compare. I made them agree as synonyms. It also added four back-links. Three were right (*prevent*, *write*, *allow*). The fourth put *attend* as a synonym under the "move or travel" sense of *go*, which is wrong, so I removed *go* from *attend* and removed the mirror link too.
