## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two reviewer models from other companies read every field of every entry. They raised 52 issues, 43 of them marked blocking (meaning they must be decided before the entry can be published). I read each one and applied 43 and rejected 9.

The most important changes:

- **brown**: one reviewer said the meaning used for people with naturally brown skin needed its own meaning, not just a usage note. I agreed, because *black* already has a matching meaning, and added it with a careful explanation. The other reviewer pointed out that *go brown* in the sun is mainly British (Americans say *get a tan*), so that meaning is now labeled British. The meaning for brown bread, rice, and sugar was redefined, because brown sugar is not a whole-grain food.
- **central**: *central heating* does not fit the meaning "controlled from one place for a whole country or organization", so it now has a sub-meaning of its own (heat coming from one place to every part of a building).
- **bright**: *a bright idea* moved out of the "intelligent" meaning into its own sub-meaning, which also says the phrase is often said ironically.
- **born**: the meaning used in compounds (*foreign-born*, *first-born*) was redefined so it covers the order of birth too.
- **busy** and **careful**: learner-error notes were corrected so they no longer contradict correct patterns (*too busy to talk*; *careful with a knife* versus *careful of the dog*).

Rejected: the telephone meaning of *busy* stays labeled American, because the project's rule is that a region label means "mainly used there", and British English usually says *engaged*; one reviewer called the code *comparative with more* invalid, but it is in the project's closed list; *brave for you* stays as a learner error, since *brave of you* is the standard pattern.

After the fixes, the cross-reference tool added three back-links (the matching link in the other direction): *black* points to *brown*, *main* to *central*, and *business* to *busy*. I checked all three. I also added by hand a link from *bright* to *bold*, because *bold* already named *bright* as a synonym.
