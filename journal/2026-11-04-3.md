# Run report, 2026-11-04 (third run of the day)

## Cycle 1: lint

**What a lint is.** Every ten runs the mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) forces a "lint": a housekeeping pass over the whole dictionary and its knowledge base (the `wiki/` folder of notes and rules), rather than writing or reviewing entries. Ten runs had passed since the last one on 2026-11-03, so this cycle was a lint. It was the hundred-and-first such pass.

**Before starting.** There were no stranded pull requests from earlier runs, no leftover `claude/` branches, nothing in `inbox/` (the folder where the owner leaves instructions), and no open "Entry report" issues from readers. The weekly digest is not due until 2026-11-07.

**Mechanical checks.** The size caps on the rule files, the internal links in the wiki, and the entry validator were all clean (3,703 entries, all reviewed, no errors).

The cross-reference tool mirrors links between entries: if entry A names entry B as a synonym, B should name A back. It added seven such back-links to entries built yesterday (*bookstore*, *breakdown*, *breakthrough*, *cafeteria*, *cart*, *cashier*, *celebration*). Three more could not be placed automatically because the tool no longer guesses which sense of the target the link belongs to, so I placed them by hand after reading the senses: *can* (the metal container sense) now compares itself with *bottle*; *campaign* (the organized-effort sense) lists *drive* as a synonym; *celebration* (the special-event sense) lists *festival* as a synonym. These are mirror links only; no definitions or examples were touched.

Five claim files from finished runs were removed (a claim file reserves headwords for a run so two runs do not draft the same word).

**Band vote.** Every word in the build queue carries a frequency band from 1 (most common) to 5. Forty-four words had been queued recently without a band, so three models voted on them as usual, at a cost of US$0.007. Only one split by more than one band: *buy* as a noun (*a good buy*), voted 4, 2 and 3. I settled it at 3, the middle vote: the noun is common but far less so than the verb.

**Two small tool repairs.** Two faults in the band-vote tool had been written up in earlier notes with the fix left for a later run, which is the project's rule for changing tools:

- The tool stamped every hand-settled word "band vote 2026-10-24" whatever day the vote was held. It now takes the date from the vote's folder name. The three rows that showed a wrong date (*kid*, *real* as an adverb, and today's *buy*) were corrected.
- A second vote in one session could reuse the first vote's saved replies for different words, because the saved replies were filed by chunk number only. They are now reused only when they answered exactly the same words.

Each fix has a unit test, and the full test suite passes. While correcting the dates I noticed a third, smaller fault: applying the same vote twice adds the settled note to a row a second time. That is recorded in the note for a later run, as the rules require.

**Reviewer precision.** Each lint measures how often each automated reviewer's objections are accepted, by kind of objection, and switches off any kind accepted less than 30 percent of the time over twenty or more decisions. Nothing new crossed the line. Reviewer A (a Claude model) stands at 84 percent overall; reviewer B (a Gemini model) at 81 percent. Reviewer B's spelling-and-format objections sit at 29 percent but stay on by the owner's earlier ruling; its pronunciation objections, at 38 percent over 29 decisions, are the nearest to the line.

**Reports for later review runs.** 329 definitions use their own headword (most harmless, such as *water* in "give an animal water to drink"; each is judged when its entry is next reviewed), and 931 cross-reference pairs may point at the wrong sense. Six usage-note promises point to entries that do not exist yet; all six were already known.

**Knowledge base.** The index lists every page and every listed page exists; no duplicate lines in the curator list; the open questions are still open. One thing to know: the wiki now stands at 29,992 of its 30,000-word cap, so the next run that adds a page must trim another first.

**Spend.** US$0.007 this cycle; US$1.39 of the US$15 daily budget used so far today.

**What is next.** The selector will pick the next unit; the queue in `NEXT.md` has the cross-reference audit, band-2 noun building, and the band-1 coverage review waiting.

## Cycle 2: build

**What a build is.** A build cycle writes new dictionary entries from the queue of words still missing, at most twelve per cycle, and puts every one through the full checking pipeline before it is published. The selector chose a build because building had the most catching up to do among the modes allowed.

**What was built.** Eleven entries, all now reviewed and published at the next site build: *can* (the verb: keeping food in cans or jars, firing someone, cancelling a plan, and the rude phrase *can it*), *interview* (noun), *bond* (verb), *boost* (verb), *category*, *celebrity*, *cereal*, *ceremony* (with the phrase *stand on ceremony*), *challenge* (noun, with *rise to the challenge*), *championship*, and *channel* (noun).

A twelfth word, *CD*, was claimed but released unbuilt. The queue lists it as a noun, but the dictionary already treats *TV* as an abbreviation entry (`tv-abbr`), so building *CD* as a noun would make the two disagree. Its queue row now records the question so the next build can settle it, most likely as `cd-abbr`.

**Checks before review.** The inflection tool generated every form from its rules (*canned*, *canning*, *categories*). The pronunciation panel (three models plus an open pronouncing list) agreed with every transcription except my British form of *channel*, which I had written in a shorter style than the dictionary uses for words like *travel* and *level*; I changed it to the house form and the panel then agreed.

**What the reviewers found.** Two automated reviewers read every field. They raised 61 issues in all, 5 of them blocking. I logged a decision on each: 49 accepted, 12 rejected. The most useful catches:

- Both reviewers pointed out that my learner-error example for *can* the verb, *Can you can your own fruit?*, is in fact correct English (the first *can* is the ordinary *be able to* word). I removed it; the usage note still explains that this verb makes questions with *do*.
- *Challenge*, sense 3, mixed two things: inviting someone to compete, and trying to take a title from its holder. The second is now a subsense of its own, and an unnatural example (*make a challenge for the title*) was replaced with the usual *mount a challenge*.
- *Channel* gained a subsense for a way of expressing feelings or energy (*painting gave her a channel for her anger*), which the reviewer rightly said was missing.
- Several collocation lists were cleaned up (*a famous celebrity* says the same thing twice; *help boost* is not an adverb pattern), and four translator notes that only repeated a definition were removed.

The rejections were mostly reviewer B asking for syllable dots and a different symbol in the transcriptions, which the dictionary's house style does not use, and one request to add *master of ceremonies*, which belongs under *master* by the rule that a phrase is filed under its first noun.

**Cross-references.** Twelve links that point back to the new entries (for example, *fire* now lists *can* as a synonym in its job sense) could not be placed automatically, because the tool no longer guesses which sense a link belongs to. I placed all twelve by hand after reading the senses.

**Spend.** US$0.33 this cycle, almost all of it the second reviewer; US$1.72 of US$15 used today.

**What is next.** The build continues through the band-2 nouns in queue order. *CD* needs its part-of-speech question settled first.

**For the owner.** Nothing new beyond the *CD* question, which the next build can settle by following the *TV* precedent unless you prefer otherwise.
