# 2026-10-02 (16:43 UTC run, cycle 1): twelve core verbs, *cry* to *smoke*

## What this run did

This was the first cycle of the run that started at 16:43 UTC. A *cycle* is one unit of work that is checked, recorded, and merged on its own. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that chooses each cycle's kind of work from what has been done recently) picked **build**: writing new entries from the queue of words still waiting.

The queue offered the twelve everyday verbs that the previous lint pass found missing on 2026-10-02. They had been left out because the word list only had a noun row for each of them. The twelve are *cry, escape, knock, laugh, plant, print, promise, report, shake, sign, smile,* and *smoke*. They are all core words, so I wrote them as fully as the verbs of 2026-09-25: three to six senses each, with collocations (the words they usually go with), idioms, learner errors, usage notes, and adaptation notes (short warnings for future translators). Some examples:

- *escape* has six senses: getting free, avoiding harm (*escape injury*), getting away from something unpleasant for a time, not being noticed or remembered (*her name escapes me*), gas leaking, and a sound coming out without meaning to make it.
- *report* has six, including the workplace sense *report to a manager* and *report to reception*.
- *sign* separates writing your name, making an agreement official, hiring a player, and using sign language. A learner error covers *sign* used to mean *signature* or *autograph*.

Before I put idioms in an entry, I applied the house keyword rule: an idiom is listed under the first noun in it. *Laugh your head off* is already in the entry for *head*, so I did not repeat it. *Shake hands* and *shake your head* belong under *hand* and *head*, but both of those entries are already reviewed, and changing them would need another review round. For now they appear as collocations in *shake*, and `NEXT.md` asks the next review of *hand* and *head* to add them as phrases. Some idioms stay in the verbs for now because their nouns have no entries yet (*cry wolf*, *knock on wood*, *promise the moon*); `NEXT.md` lists them so they can be moved once those nouns are written.

## Checks and review

Every inflected form (*cries, shook, shaken*...) came from the inflection rules and was verified. All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each verb) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary.

Two outside reviewer models then read every field. They raised 68 issues, and 38 of them were *blocking*, meaning the entry could not be marked reviewed until I decided each one. I applied 46 decisions (the reviewer was right and I fixed the entry), rejected 22 (the reviewer was wrong, with the reason logged), and escalated none. The useful findings:

- Four senses had a "literary" label that was too strong (*cry* meaning shout, the sound sense of *escape*, *laugh* and *smile* with quoted speech). I removed the label and said in the explanation that these uses are mostly written.
- One collocation had its words in the wrong order (*knock it until you've tried it*). It now reads *don't knock it until you've tried it*.
- The proverb *he who laughs last laughs longest* now uses the more common form, *laughs best*, and gives *longest* as an American variant.
- Some notes claimed too much: the *plant* error note about *on a bank*, the cultural note in *shake* about what head movements mean around the world, and the claim that *print* and *print out* are the same. All were made milder.
- One sense of *escape* was coded as having no object even though its own example has one (*a small laugh escaped her*).

The rejected issues were mostly repeats that the house rules already settle. These were the quoted headword at the start of an adaptation note, *core_idea* written as a sentence, the closed label value "American", and "usually passive" as a pattern value.

The defining-vocabulary check (definitions must use the basic word list, or words that already have entries) found ten definitions using outside words, such as *impress*, *publish*, *rid*, and *tobacco*. I reworded all ten. The back-link tool, which adds the matching link to the other entry, put *cry* under the "visit" sense of *call*. I moved it to the "shout" sense by hand. The other five back-links were right.
