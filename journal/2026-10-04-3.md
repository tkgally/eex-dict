All twelve were drafted, checked, and published as `reviewed`.

Some highlights:

- **front** has eight senses (the forward side, the forward position, a false manner, a business that hides a crime, the fighting area in a war, an area of activity such as *the jobs front*, a weather front, and the British seafront) and five phrases (*in front*, *out front*, *up front*, *front and center*, *present a united front*). Its usage note and learner errors deal with a very common mistake: saying *in front of the station* for a building across the street, where English says *opposite*. The preposition *in front of* is queued as its own entry, as house rules require.
- **game** has six senses and seven idioms (*the name of the game*, *give the game away*, *ahead of the game*, *play games*, *beat someone at their own game*, *fair game*, *game over*), plus a note on choosing between *game*, *match*, and *sport*.
- **fun** has a note separating *fun* (enjoyable) from *funny* (makes you laugh), with the learner error *The party was very funny*.
- **furniture** and **fruit** carry the classic countability errors (*furnitures*, *three fruits*).
- **future** includes the British *in future* (meaning "from now on") and the learner error that confuses it with *in the future*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewer models raised 46 issues, 32 of them blocking (must be settled before publishing). I applied 43 and rejected 3.

Applied, among others:
- *game*: I had included a sense "a large sports event with many sports", written as *the games*. The reviewer was right that this only works for named events, which our examples may not name, so the sense was removed. The "secret plan" sense became "what someone is really trying to do" (*What's your game?*), and the usage note no longer says you can never *make* a game, since children *make up* games.
- *fruit*: the definition said fruit is "soft". Many fruits are not, so it now says the part that contains the seeds, often sweet.
- *future*: the grammar sense said "the form of a verb", but English has several future forms. It now says "the tense or verb forms", which matches the entry's own translator note.
- *garage*: the repair-shop sense is now labeled British. The learner-error note and the translator note no longer suggest that a garage always belongs to a house.
- *fun*: the usage note had banned *a fun* while also mentioning *a fun day*. It now says the noun has no plural and points to the adjective entry.
- Smaller fixes on *gain* (*ill-gotten gains* is disapproving, not humorous), *fur*, *furniture*, and *fuel*.

Rejected: an objection that *rhymes* is a technical word (it is plain English, and not on the banned list); an objection that adaptation notes should not address translators (that is what they are for); and a learner-error complaint that misread a plural noun as an adjective.

The vocabulary check found nine definition words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of about 2,400 plain words definitions should use), such as *furthest*, *occasion*, *tennis*, *hunt*, and *criticize*. Those definitions were rewritten.
