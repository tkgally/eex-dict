One decision about word division. English has two unrelated words spelled *case*. One means a situation or a matter for a court; the other is a container, such as *a pencil case*. The house rule gives unrelated words separate entries, so *case* (noun) holds only the first word. The container waits for its own entry, `case-n-2`. That needs a hand claim, because the queue has no way to hold a second entry with the same spelling. The note on this problem now lists it with the four similar words found earlier.

Where learners of many languages make the same mistake, the entry shows it with a correction. Examples: *in case it will rain* for *in case it rains*; *in case of it gets cold* for *in case it gets cold*; *take care about* for *take care of*; *pay by cash* for *pay in cash*; *I came here with car* for *by car*; *the capitol of the country* for *capital*; *studying my career* for *studying for my degree*. *Case* also has a usage note on *in case* and *if*. You take an umbrella *in case* it rains, which means you take it now to be ready. That is not the same as *if* it rains. Translator notes cover false friends too: in some languages a word like *career* means a university course, and a word like *cartoon* means thick paper.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

All 24 pronunciations passed the check at the first try. Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field. They raised 35 issues, 22 of them marked blocking. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 25 applied, 10 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied (the main ones):

- ***card***: the ID sense was defined as a card "with information about a person". That did not cover a library card or a membership card, which the examples used. It now says "that shows who you are or that you may use a service". The computer sense now includes phones, so *SIM card* fits.
- ***cash***: sense 2 had said "money of any kind", which would include money in a bank. It is now "money that is available to spend now". *Cash cow* no longer says the money comes "easily".
- ***career***: the translator's note said the word means "working life only". That contradicted sense 1, the kind of work you do. The note now gives both.
- ***candy*** is no longer limited to food "eaten between meals". ***cause*** sense 2 now names its fixed phrases (*cause for concern*, *cause for alarm*). ***care***'s British sense now covers any arrangement in which the state looks after children, not only homes.
- The notes on compared words (*sweets*, *carriage*, *rug*, *change*, *reason*) now mark the words they name in bold, as the house style requires.
- A few labels came off: *formal* on *in case of* and *be the case*, *informal* on *cash* sense 2, *literary* on *a carpet of leaves*.

Rejected (the main ones):

- Moving *take care* and *take care of* to the verb *care*. The house rule puts a phrase under its first noun, and here that is *care*. Four linked flags fell with this one.
- A British label on *sweep something under the carpet*. Both varieties use it; the explanation gives the American *under the rug*.
- Calling *capital investment* an adjective use. It is the noun used before another noun, as in *capital gains*. The reviewer was right about *a capital letter*, so it came off the letter sense.
