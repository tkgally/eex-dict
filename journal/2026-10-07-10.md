## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewer models raised 40 issues: 38 from reviewer-a and 2 from reviewer-b. *Relief* and *research* drew none. I applied 34 and rejected 6, each logged with a reason.

The main fixes:

- **Learner errors.** Seven of my learner errors were weak, and the reviewers were right about all of them. *I have to do a report* is normal American English; *show respect to someone* is grammatical; *pay the rent for my landlord* could mean something else. I removed them rather than keep mistakes that are not really mistakes.
- **rest:** I had a sense for a support (*a foot rest*). The reviewer pointed out that these are normally one word (*footrest*, *armrest*), so the sense was removed.
- **representative:** the US sense now names the House of Representatives exactly, and its example was made natural.
- **request:** the first sense was defined as "a time when you ask"; it is now "a polite or formal message in which you ask for something". The second sense no longer makes songs the main case.
- **respect:** the sense about the law and nature was reworded so that respecting someone's opinion no longer sounds like obeying it.
- **repair:** the "condition" sense (*in good repair*) is now narrower and says it is used in fixed phrases.
- Smaller fixes on *rent* (the regional note on *for rent*, the *rental*/*hire* note), *restaurant*, *result*, and *religion* (two informal labels removed).

The rejections:

- **Two phrases under *rest*.** The reviewer wanted *come to rest* and *lay someone to rest* moved to the verbs *come* and *lay*. The house rule puts a phrase under its first noun, which is *rest*.
- ***The rest*, meaning the part that is left.** The reviewer wanted it as a sense of this entry. It is a different word with the same spelling and a different origin, so it gets its own entry, `rest-n-2`. That entry is not written yet; I added it to the list of homographs (separate words with the same spelling) in `NEXT.md` and marked it as one to draft soon, because *the rest of* is very common.
- *start to believe* as a definition (it is already a base-form verb phrase), and one learner error on *rent* that is a real learner pattern.
