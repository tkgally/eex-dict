## Cycle 4: originality

**What this cycle did.** Every twentieth run the scheduler does an originality check, and this was one. The check makes sure our wording has not drifted toward the wording of published dictionaries, which the drafters are never allowed to open. The tool picked ten definitions and notes at random. For each one I ran a web search for the exact phrase, to see whether any published dictionary shows the same words. I also asked one outside reviewer whether each one reads as copied.

**What the searches found.** None of the ten matched a published dictionary word for word, except where the definition is so short and plain that any dictionary would write it the same way. Those are *get something by paying money for it* (**buy**), *refuse to allow something* (a phrase under **hear**), and *have something in your hand or hands, or in your arms* (**hold**); I marked them as generic overlap. Six were clearly our own: for example the usage note on **truck** and the definitions of the phrases *make conversation* and *talk shop*.

One needed rewriting. Sense 1 of **reason** read *a fact or situation that explains why something happens or why someone does something*. That is not a copy, but it follows the frame a well-known learner's dictionary uses for *explanation* and *reason* too closely. It now reads *something that explains an event or a person's action; the answer to the question* **why**. My first rewrite said *something that makes an event happen*, but that describes a cause, and the entry's own note explains that a cause and a reason are different, so I replaced it before recording. The rewritten entry went through both reviewers. One of them pointed out that the common phrase *within reason* was missing, so I added it. I rejected three other points: one misread the grammar code, and the other two asked for a rare idiom and for a verb use that belongs in the verb entry.

**The reviewer's answers.** As in earlier checks, the reviewer said "copied from Cambridge" for nine of the ten, and each time it "quoted" our own wording back as Cambridge's. The searches did not support any of those claims, so the searches decided the verdicts, as the house practice already says. My first call to it, with its reasoning switched on, came back empty because it used all its allowance on hidden reasoning; the second call, with reasoning off, worked. I added a line to `NEXT.md` so later checks use the setting that works.

**Spend.** US$0.13, slightly over this cycle's US$0.10 ceiling because of the wasted first call. For the whole day: about US$3.36 of US$15.
