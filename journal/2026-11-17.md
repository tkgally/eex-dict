# Run report, 2026-11-17

## Cycle 1: lint

**Why this cycle was a lint.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose a lint pass because ten cycles had passed since the last one on 2026-11-15. A lint is the dictionary's housekeeping: it runs every mechanical check over the whole collection of 4,479 entries and then reads the project's own notes for contradictions and gaps. It writes no new entries, so its budget for paid model calls is nil apart from one small vote.

**Before the work.** No pull requests were left open by earlier runs. Two old working branches remain on GitHub; both were checked and hold nothing that is not already on `main`. One of them already had a line asking the owner to delete it; this run added the same line for the other (`claude/nice-hawking-93pz5c`), because the tools available to a run cannot delete branches. No entry reports (problem reports filed by readers through the site) were open, and the inbox was empty.

**The mechanical checks.** Word caps on the project's rule files and wiki were all within limits, and every relative link in the wiki resolves. The cross-reference tool (`crossref.py`), which makes sure that when entry A points to entry B, entry B points back to A, added four missing return links on its own: `compliment-n` now compares `praise-n`, `roommate-n` lists `room-n` in its word family, and `rumor-n` and `rush-n` each gained one. It left eight return links for a person to place, because it could not tell which sense of the target entry they belong to. I placed them by hand:

- `argument-n` listed **row** as a British synonym. That is the word that rhymes with *cow* and means a noisy quarrel, which is a different word from the existing `row-n` (a line of things). I pointed the synonym at `row-n-2`, the planned entry for the quarrel word, rather than add a wrong return link to the line word. `row-n-2` is already on the build list.
- `bank-n`'s sense "a set of machines or lights placed together" lists **row** as a synonym; the return link went on the first sense of `row-n`, with a short note.
- `reward-n` gained links back to `fruit-n` (*the fruits of your work*), `award-n`, and `prize-n` on its first sense.
- `resident-n` now compares `citizen-n`, and `beat-v`'s "defeat" sense lists `overcome-v`.

Six finished claim files (records of which words a past run had reserved) were cleared away.

**The band vote.** Fifty-two rows in the building queue had no frequency band yet (bands 1 to 5 rank how important a word is for a learner). Three language models voted on each, at a cost of under one US cent. Forty-nine were settled automatically; three had votes more than one band apart, and I settled them at the middle vote as earlier lints have done: *attention* as a spoken command (band 3), *boy* as an exclamation, as in *Oh boy!* (band 2), and *rubbish* as a British adjective meaning "bad" (band 3). All 52 are now ready to build.

**Reports read.** The list of possible missing senses (an entry links to another entry on only one sense when another sense may also fit) stands at 1,039 pairs; those are worked through in review cycles. The list of definitions that use their own headword stands at 314; most are harmless (for example "an illness caused by a virus" under *virus*). Six "promises" remain, where an entry's note says another word has its own entry and none exists yet; they are the same six as last time. Three (*storey*, the foot sense of *corn*, the tool sense of *file*) are named in the build plan; the other three are phrasal verbs or phrases (*butter someone up*, the *double* phrasal verbs, *used to*) that will be built in their turn.

**Reviewer precision.** Precision is the share of a reviewer's flags that I accepted. The first reviewer stands at 85 percent overall and the second at 83 percent. No new kind of flag fell below the 30 percent line that would switch it off.

**Spend.** About US$0.007, all on the band vote.

**What did not work.** The band-vote tool's `settle` and `apply` steps looked for a default vote file name and failed until I named the file explicitly; this was a small nuisance, not a fault in the data.

**What is next.** The next cycle goes back to the selector, most likely a build. The next lint is due after ten more cycles.

**For the owner.** Please delete the two old branches named in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` when convenient. Nothing else needs you from this cycle.

## Cycle 2: review

**What this cycle was.** The selector chose a review cycle: a second reading of entries that already exist, in the order the owner set on 2026-10-24. There were no unfinished drafts and no open reader reports, so the cycle continued the coverage pass. The coverage pass rereads the most important everyday words (band 1, the top frequency band) and asks one question of each: does the entry cover every common present-day use, including idioms? This cycle took the next four words with four senses each: *brother* (noun), *brown* (adjective), *build* (verb), and *button* (noun). NEXT.md also listed `lord-v` (*lord it over someone*) for review, but that entry does not exist yet; it is a low-priority row waiting in the building queue, so I removed it from the review list.

**What I added before the reviewers saw the entries.** *Brother* gained two idioms, *brothers in arms* and *Big Brother* (a government or organization that watches everyone). *Build* gained the use for body shape (*He was tall and strongly built*), a pronunciation note that the **u** is silent so that *build* sounds like *billed*, and *build a website* among its collocations. *Button* gained the British idiom *bright as a button* (clever and lively). *Brown* gained *brown paper*.

**What the reviewers found.** Two reviewing models from different companies read every field of the four entries and raised 47 issues, 4 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry can count as reviewed). All four blocking issues were right, and I fixed each one:

- I had written that *Big Brother* is used "with or without *the*". It is not; it takes no article (*Big Brother is watching*).
- *Brown*, sense 4 (about people), was defined as "having skin that is naturally brown", which uses the word to define itself. It now says what the word names: one of the groups of people whose skin is darker than that of white people but who are not usually called Black.
- One example for *build* said "Pressure builds before a storm", a weather claim that is doubtful and that the house rules forbid in any case. It now reads "Pressure was building as the deadline came closer."
- A learner-error note warned against *builded*. The house rules forbid such invented regular forms, so it is gone.

Of the 43 minor issues I applied 33 and rejected 10. Among the changes: *brother* used to address a man (*Thanks, brother*) was defined as "a male friend", which both reviewers rightly said was wrong, so it is now defined as a form of address. One reviewer pointed out a common missing use, *brother* meaning a Black man (informal, American), which is now sense 5. The other pointed out *Oh, brother!*; that is an interjection, which is a separate part of speech, so I queued a new entry for it and pointed to it. The body-shape use of *build* became its own sense rather than a subsense, because it differs in both meaning and grammar. *Button* now uses the American *fasten a button* where it had the British *do up*. Several notes said the same thing twice and were trimmed. The rejections were mostly reviewer B asking four times to hand-mark the word *built* in examples. The site already does this for every form listed in the entry's table of forms, so hand marks would be wrong. I also rejected a request to add *build bridges* to *build*: it already lives under *bridge*, because idioms are kept under their first noun.

**Spend.** About US$0.17 on the reviewers, bringing the day to about US$0.17 of the US$15 daily cap.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. One adjudication command was mistyped and logged a duplicate line, which I removed by hand before committing.

**What is next.** The coverage pass now stands at 357 entries. The next four are *care* (noun and verb), *certain*, and *child*.

**For the owner.** The new *brother* sense ("a Black man", informal, used mainly by Black people) and the rewritten *brown* sense about people are sensitive. Both were written with care, but you may want to look at them yourself.
