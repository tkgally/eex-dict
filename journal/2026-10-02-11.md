## What this run did

This was the third cycle of the run that started at 12:43 UTC. (A "cycle" is one complete unit of work with its own pull request, a proposed change that is checked automatically and then merged. Cycle 1 added the nouns *cost* to *crack*; cycle 2 gave twelve older entries a second review.) The mode selector chose **build** again: write new entries from the queue of words still to be written.

I wrote the next twelve nouns in the core list (band 1): *crash, creature, credit, crime, criminal, crop, crowd, cry, cup, curtain, custom*, and *customer*. Each has its senses with short labels (signposts), examples, typical word combinations, grammar, and, where they help, idioms, learner errors, and notes for translators. Some highlights:

- *credit* separates paying later (*on credit*), money in an account, praise (*take the credit*), college credits, and the credits of a film, with *to someone's credit* and *credit where credit's due*.
- *crime* separates one act (*commit a crime*), crime in general (*crime is falling*), and the loose sense *it's a crime to waste food*, and tells *crime*, *offense*, and *sin* apart.
- *custom* tells *custom*, *habit*, and *tradition* apart and points to *customs* (the border control) as a separate word.
- *customer* tells *customer*, *client*, *shopper*, and *consumer* apart, with a note for translators whose languages use a *client*-like word for shop customers.
- *cup* has *not my cup of tea* and tells *cup*, *mug*, and *glass* apart.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

All 24 pronunciations passed the panel check. The two reviewer models raised 37 issues, 21 of them blocking. I applied 22 and rejected 15, each with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`; none needed the owner. This time reviewer-a was right on most points (84 percent of its issues accepted). Several of my notes said too much:

- *creature*: I wrote that it "always" needs an adjective when it means a person, but the entry itself has *a creature of habit*. It now says "usually".
- *cup*: I wrote that cups are "mostly" for hot drinks; paper and plastic cups are common for cold ones. The note and the comparison with *mug* and *glass* now say "especially".
- *curtain*: I wrote that *draw the curtains* can mean open or close; it usually means close, and *draw back the curtains* means open. I also removed a learner error that called the singular *curtain* wrong, since one curtain is often fine.
- *credit*, *crop*, *custom*: definitions and core ideas (the one-sentence summary that opens an entry) were reworded so that each covers every sense exactly.
- *crash*: the comparison said an accident is "anything bad that happens by chance"; it now says "something bad that happens without anyone meaning it to".

Rejected: the second reviewer again flagged notes that open with the headword in quotation marks (house style allows this); labels the first reviewer wanted removed where I judged the use conversational (*credit where credit's due*, *a tough customer*, *it's a crime to waste food*).

The cross-reference tool reported that *audience* lists *crowd* as a synonym while my *crowd* listed *audience* only as a word to compare. I made both sides agree (synonym, with a note on the difference).
