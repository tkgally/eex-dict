The selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **build**: new entries from the queue, at most twelve. The claim tool took the next twelve band 1 nouns in queue order. *Band 1* is the dictionary's most frequent group of words, the ones a learner meets first.

## What was written

*garden*, *gas*, *gate*, *gear*, *gender*, *generation*, *girl*, *glass*, *glasses*, *glove*, *glue*, and *goal*. Each has its senses with signposts (the short labels that help a reader pick a sense), grammar, examples, collocations, and, where they help, a usage note, learner errors, and adaptation notes (short notes for translators). Some points of interest:

- **Phrases** (idioms filed under their key noun): *lead someone down the garden path*, *step on the gas*, *run out of gas*, *shift gears*, *kick into high gear*, *the girl next door*, *raise a glass to someone*, *fit like a glove*, *the gloves are off*, and *an own goal*.
- **Variety differences** are recorded where learners need them: *garden* means all the land by a house in British English but only the planted part in American English (the rest is the *yard*); *gas* is the American word for car fuel (British *petrol*); *shift gears* against British *change gear*.
- **glasses** (the thing you wear to see) is its own entry, always plural, with the learner errors *I can't find my glass* and *My glasses is broken*. The *glass* entry points to it.
- **gender** was written with care to be neutral and accurate: the first sense is now *the group, such as man or woman, that a person belongs to or feels they belong to, especially as it affects how society treats them*, and a second sense covers the noun groups of grammar.

## What the panel found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 41 issues, 26 of them marked blocking. I applied 34 and rejected 7; every decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Real catches, applied:

- **gate**: the sense for the entrance to a park or a sports ground defined *gate* using the word *gates*, which is circular; it is now *the way in and out of a large place ... which can be opened and closed*. A synonym link to *entrance* was removed, since an entrance is not the same thing.
- **gear**: the definition said a gear lets a vehicle go faster or slower "for the same effort", which is loose; it now says gears *control how fast the wheels turn and how much force they have*.
- **gender**: my first definition and the notes around it put "another identity" side by side with man and woman in an awkward way, and the usage note overstated the difference between *gender* and *sex*. All were reworded; the learner-error note now says *use an adjective such as* male *or* female, which does not limit the categories.
- **generation**: I had listed *the young generation* as a learner error. It is less usual than *the younger generation* but not wrong, so the box was removed.
- **goal**: *in goal* (playing as the player who guards the goal) is mainly British, and now says so; *an own goal* in its sports meaning is used everywhere, and only its figurative meaning (something that harms yourself) is marked British.
- Smaller fixes: an unnatural airport example, a learner-error sentence nobody would say (*Close the door of the garden*), wrong marks in two notes.

Rejected: reviewer-a's claim that *lead someone up the garden path* is the standard American form (American English usually says *down the garden path*; the British *up* form is named in the phrase's explanation); a request for a "figurative" label, which is not one of the dictionary's label values; and two minor link and label points.
