## What the reviewers found, and what I decided

The reviewers raised 20 issues, 16 of them "blocking" (an entry cannot stay marked as reviewed until each one has a decision). I applied 10 and rejected 10, and none needed to go to the owner. Five entries came back with no issues: *apple*, *bicycle*, *bill*, *setting*, and *tube*. Every decision is logged, with a short reason, in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

What I changed:

- ***island*.** The road sense said "a small raised area". Not every traffic island is raised, so it now says "a small area, often raised". Its first example said only *island*, which was unclear on its own, and now says *traffic island*. The kitchen sense called a kitchen island "a work surface or a cupboard". It is really a piece of furniture with both, so it is now "a piece of furniture with cupboards below and a flat top for working on, standing on its own in the middle of a kitchen".
- ***blanket*** (verb). The definition said "with a thick layer", but the entry's own examples include fallen leaves and flowers, which are not thick. *thick* is gone. The collocation box listed *be blanketed with fog*, which clashed with the usage note: *in* is the usual word with fog. It now lists *be blanketed in fog*, and keeps *with* for flowers.
- ***block*** (verb). Sense 1 mixed "be in a road" with "put something there". It now reads as one transitive phrase: "stop people or things from getting through a road, path, door, or pipe by being in it or by putting something in it".
- ***visitor***. The website sense said "someone who looks at a website". It is now "someone who goes to a website and looks at its pages", and the core idea (the one-sentence summary at the top of the entry) was changed to match.

What I rejected, and why:

- A new collocation type, "noun + of + noun", for *an island of calm*. Collocation types come from a fixed list, and "noun + preposition" already fits.
- A British label on the road sense of *island*. *Traffic island* is used in American English too.
- Two issues on *bike*. Its first sense is defined as riding a bicycle, so the adaptation note that says so is consistent with it. The motorcycle use stays in sense 2.
- On *rake someone over the coals*: dropping the *informal* label. The phrase is informal in general use.
- A reviewer-b flag on *coal* and one on *banana* that both said a definition broke the base-form rule. Both definitions already follow it; on *banana* the reviewer's own comment said the wording was correct.
- Two minor notes saying *according to*'s usage note repeats its explanation. The two overlap, but the note adds a point and an example.
- A minor note calling "usually passive" not a real verb pattern. It is on the fixed pattern list.
