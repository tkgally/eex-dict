## Back-links: one of four was wrong

When entry A names entry B as a synonym or a word to compare, `tools/crossref.py` adds the mirror link on B. It adds that back-link to the sense of B it thinks fits best. It often picks the wrong sense, so each lint checks them by hand. This pass the tool added four, all to nouns written in yesterday's two builds:

- *fun* as a synonym on *pleasure*, sense 1 (a feeling of being happy): right.
- *exercise* as a word to compare on *practice*, sense 1 (doing something again and again to get better): right.
- *fee* as a word to compare on *price*, sense 1 (the money you pay for something): right.
- *time* as a synonym on *point*, sense 2 ("the most important part of what someone says"): **wrong**. *Time* matches sense 4, "a particular moment or stage" (*at that point*). I moved it there by hand and re-ran the tool. It made no further changes.

The tool also added *please* to *pleasure*'s word family. The two words are related, so that stays. No type mismatches were reported (a type mismatch is when one side calls the link a synonym and the other calls it a comparison).

## Reviewer precision

Each entry is read by two outside reviewer models, called reviewer-a and reviewer-b. The adjudicator then decides whether to apply or reject each issue they raise. A reviewer's *precision* in an issue family (for example, "label" or "pronunciation") is the share of its issues that were applied. The rule is this: a family under 30 percent precision, over twenty or more decisions, is switched off for that reviewer.

Nothing new crossed the line this pass. Seven pairs stay switched off. Overall, reviewer-a is at 0.71 and reviewer-b at 0.54. The owner chose to keep reviewer-b's spelling-and-format family live, at 0.28. It had no new decisions. reviewer-b's phrase family is at 0.33 over 39 decisions, just above the line. Since reviewer-b moved to the stronger model on 2026-10-05, it has had 49 of 100 issues applied.
