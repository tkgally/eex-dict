# 2026-10-02 (5) — Ten words the dictionary already uses, *ankle* to *shelf*

*First cycle of the run that began at 06:44 UTC. A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request; a run does at most four.*

## What this cycle did

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **closure**. A closure cycle writes entries for words that existing definitions already use but that have no entry of their own yet: the definition of *boot* mentions an *ankle*, *bone* mentions a *frame*, *cloth* mentions *fibers*. Closing these gaps means a reader who clicks a word in a definition always finds an entry.

The tool that collects these words is rough. Of the twelve rows it handed this cycle, six were not real headwords: plurals (*bricks*, *christians*, *fibers*, *oars*, *shelves*) and *explode* guessed as a noun. Each was marked as a duplicate in the queue (the list of words waiting to be written), and the real word was written instead: *brick*, *fiber*, *oar*, *shelf*, and the verb *explode*. The row *gray* was guessed as a noun; the definition of *cloud* uses the adjective, so the adjective was written and the noun left in the queue for later.

The ten new entries:

- **ankle**, **oar**, and **propeller**: one sense each, with pronunciation help (*ANG-kul*; *oar* sounds like *or*), the British idiom *put your oar in*, and a learner error on the common misspelling *propellor*.
- **brick**: the countable block (*lay bricks*) and the uncountable material (*made of brick*), with *bricks and mortar* and the learner error *made by bricks*.
- **cream**: from milk, for the skin, the color, and *the cream of* a group, with *the cream of the crop*.
- **explode**: bombs, sudden anger (*explode with anger*, *explode at someone*), fast growth, and the formal *explode a myth*. A short note sets out when to say *explode*, *blow up*, or *burst*.
- **fiber**: threads for cloth, fibers in muscles and plants, fiber in food (uncountable: *a lot of fiber*, not *many fibers*), and *moral fiber*.
- **frame**: around a picture, the structure of a bike or bed, a person's body (*a small frame*), the frames of glasses, and a frame of film, with *frame of mind* and *frame of reference*.
- **gray**: the color, gray hair (*go gray*), gray weather, a gray face, and the "dull" sense, with a note for translators that gray hair is called *white* in many languages.
- **shelf**: the board, a shelf of rock, and the phrases *off the shelf* and *fly off the shelves*. The plural *shelves* is in the learner-error box.

All 20 pronunciations (American and British) were verified by the outside panel and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, and all the word forms were checked.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models reviewed every field. Counting the withdrawn *Christian* entry, they raised 39 issues; 21 were "blocking". I accepted 25 and rejected 14.

Accepted, among others:

- The phrase *on the shelf* meaning "postponed" is not well established (people say *shelved*), so it was removed.
- The rock sense of *shelf* had said "especially under the sea"; it now describes rock sticking out from a mountain side.
- The *glasses* sense of *frame* wrongly said the frame holds "the glass"; it now says it holds the two pieces of glass or plastic.
- The *cream* usage note had claimed American *heavy cream* and *light cream* match British *double* and *single cream*. They do not match exactly, so the note now says only that the names differ.
- An example with the British *sun cream* was replaced.
- The *blow up* note now says it is used with or without an object.
- Two weak learner errors were dropped: *become gray* and *my glasses' frame*.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Six objections from the second reviewer to the house style of opening an adaptation note (a note for translators) with the headword in quotation marks. The project settled this earlier.
- The claim that `blow-up-phrv` is a malformed name for *blow up*. It is the project's standard name for that phrasal verb.

The second reviewer's reply on *shelf* came back cut off and was run again.

## What did not work

*Christian* was written and reviewed, then withdrawn before merging. The church entry defines a church as a place where *Christians* pray, so the word was due. But no accurate definition avoids the name *Jesus Christ*. The defining-vocabulary check, a gate that every changed entry must pass, accepts only words on the basic list or words that have entries, and a person's name can never have an entry. Rather than write an inaccurate definition, I put the row back in the queue with a note and recorded the problem in `wiki/notes/lint-vocab-proper-names.md`. The proposed fix is a short, agreed list of proper names allowed in definitions. Words for other religions will need it too.
