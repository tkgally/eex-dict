# Journal, 2026-11-06 (second run)

## Cycle 1: closure

**What this cycle was.** The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's job from the state of the dictionary) chose *closure*: writing entries for words the dictionary already uses or points to but has no entry for yet. Its reason was that closure had the most "scheduler debt", meaning it was the job most overdue relative to its share of the runs. It allowed up to twelve new entries and a paid-call ceiling of US$3. The first closure source (words used in definitions) was empty, so the twelve came from the `family` source: words that existing entries list in their word family (related forms such as *drummer* from *drum*). No pull requests from earlier runs were open, no reader reports were waiting, and the inbox (where the owner leaves instructions) was empty. One old branch from an earlier run had nothing left that was not already in `main`, and it was already on the curator list for deletion.

**What was written.** Twelve nouns, all in frequency band 2 (the second most common of five bands): *driving*, *drop*, *drugstore*, *drummer*, *earnings*, *ease*, *economics*, *economist*, *editor*, *eighth*, *employment*, and *ending*. Points worth noting:

- *drop* has six senses: a drop of liquid, a fall in amount (*a sharp drop in prices*), a small amount of a drink, a distance down, eye or ear drops, and a small hard candy (*a cough drop*). It has the phrases *at the drop of a hat* and *a drop in the bucket*. It also records a real learner error, *a drop of prices* for *a drop in prices*.
- *ease* has the phrases *at ease* and *ill at ease*.
- *economics* explains when it takes a singular verb (the subject of study) and when it takes a plural one (*the economics of the plan are not good*). It warns against using it where *economy* is meant.
- *eighth* is the fraction (*an eighth of an inch*). The date and order uses stay in the existing number entry, as a standing project rule requires.
- *employment* warns against *an employment*. For one position the word is *a job*.
- *ending* is compared with *end*: *the end* is the last moment, *the ending* is the way a story finishes.

**The checks.** Every entry went through the full pipeline. Plurals came from the spelling rules. The pronunciation check compares each transcription with three other models, and the American one with the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. It confirmed all 24.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Two reviewers from different AI labs read every field. They raised 21 issues, none "blocking" (a blocking issue must be decided before an entry can be marked reviewed). I accepted 17 and rejected 4, and each decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. I accepted the cough-drop sense that was missing. The "fall in amount" sense of *drop* moved up to second place, because it is far more common than "a small amount of a drink". An example about a wall with a drop to a river was unnatural and was replaced. I removed a "mathematics" label from *eighth*, because cutting a pizza into eighths is everyday English. I rejected a request to add *march to the beat of a different drummer* under *drummer*. By the house rule an idiom goes under its first noun, here *beat*, so `NEXT.md` now asks for it at that entry's next review. I also rejected a call to give *eighth* a date sense (the number rule above), and a claim that a translator's note on *drop* was generic. The note names a specific split, the same kind as the style guide's own example for *bank*.

After the review, the vocabulary check found four definition words outside the defining vocabulary (*downward*, *sore*, *everyday*, *version*), and I reworded them. The cross-reference tool added matching links in ten existing entries. For five of them it could not tell which sense to use, so I placed them by hand (for example, *earnings* as a synonym in the money sense of *profit*). One small slip: I gave the metrics script 17 as the count of changed entries. The true count is 22 (12 new and 10 that got links), and the log says so.
