# Run report, 2026-10-28 (scheduled run 20261028T004414Z-e7cpi8)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each run's kind of work from the dictionary's state) chose a *build*: new entries from the queue, at most twelve. Milestone 1, the core defining vocabulary, finished yesterday, so the queue now hands out *closure* rows: words the dictionary already uses or points to but has no entry for. This cycle took ten verbs that other entries point to (the `crossref` source) and two nouns: **mind**, **miss**, **offer**, **point**, **prefer**, **recommend**, **rent**, **search**, **shoot**, and **spread** as verbs, and **check** and **chocolate** as nouns. All twelve are band 1, the most important words.

Each entry was written from scratch with every field filled: senses with plain definitions, grammar, two to four examples per sense, collocations (the words a word typically goes with), cross-references, and the notes a learner needs. Some examples of what the entries say: **mind** explains why *Would you mind...?* is answered with *No, not at all* when you agree; **search** warns that *search the room* names the place, and the thing you want comes after **for** (*search for my keys*); **rent** has a short guide to choosing between *rent*, *hire*, *let*, *borrow*, and *lend*; **shoot** notes that *shoot someone* means the bullet hit, while *shoot at someone* means you tried.

**Checks.** Every entry passed the schema check (0 errors), got its inflected forms from the rules table (**shoot** → *shot*, **spread** → *spread*, **prefer** → *preferred*), and had both its American and British pronunciations confirmed by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU dictionary: 24 of 24 verified.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers (a Claude model and a Google model) read every field of every entry. They raised 127 issues, 17 of them blocking. I applied 115 and rejected 12; nothing needed the owner. The useful catches:

- **shoot**: the example *Prices have shot up* sat in the "move very fast" sense, but it means "rise quickly", which is the phrasal verb *shoot up*. All five blocking flags on this entry were about it. I replaced the example with a literal one and pointed to `shoot-up-phrv`, which is now queued.
- **rent**: sense 2 (the owner lets someone use something) covered only houses and land. Shops that rent out bikes and cars were left out. The sense now covers them too. I also dropped an example with two objects (*rented us her house*) that sounds unnatural in American English.
- **search**: one sense was marked intransitive but had a transitive example (*searched his memory for her name*). The example moved to the sense it really belongs to.
- **prefer**: *prefer charges* (a legal phrase) was a separate sense but only ever occurs in that one phrase, so it is now a phrase.
- **check**: one grammar code was wrong, the compound *checkbook* was spelled as two words, and the chess sense said the king "can be taken", which is not how chess works. All fixed. The idiom *a blank check* was added.
- Smaller fixes: *movie* instead of *film* and *in line* instead of *in the line* (American English comes first), new phrases (*never mind* meaning "let alone", *spread yourself too thin*, *Search me*), and notes that said the same thing twice cut down to one.

I rejected requests that go against the house rules. Some idioms belong under their first noun, not under these verbs: *mind your own business*, *point the finger at someone*, *shoot yourself in the foot*, *spread like wildfire*. Phrasal verbs (*spread out*, *shoot up*) get their own entries rather than becoming senses, and *Shoot!* meaning "go ahead, say it" is an interjection, not a verb sense. I also rejected a request to put the "feel sad" sense of **miss** before "fail to hit": the core meaning that the other senses build on comes first.

Cross-references were made two-way. The linking tool places most back-links itself. Where it could not tell which sense to attach them to, I placed them by hand in five existing entries: **provide**, **suggest**, **extend**, **hit**, and **avoid**.
