## Cycle 1: closure

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each run's kind of work from what has gone longest undone) chose *closure*: building entries for words the dictionary already uses but has no entry for. The plain closure list held one word, *mushroom* (used in the definition of *cap*). The other eleven came from the *family* list, nouns related to verbs that already have entries: *collapse*, *collection*, *commander*, *commitment*, *communication*, *concern*, *confirmation*, *congratulation*, *consideration*, *consultant*, and *contribution*. A thirteenth word, *cook* (noun), was claimed by mistake when two claim commands ran back to back; it was released to the queue without being drafted, so the cycle stayed at twelve, the selector's limit.

Each entry was drafted from the house style guide, then went through the full pipeline: the schema validator; inflections from rules; a pronunciation check by a panel of models plus the open CMU pronouncing data for American English; a field-by-field reading by the two reviewer models (reviewer A, a Claude model; reviewer B, a Google model); my decision on every issue they raised; the defining-vocabulary check (definitions must use the dictionary's list of about 2,400 basic words, or words that have entries); and the cross-reference tool, which adds the matching link in the other entry when one entry names another as a synonym or a related word.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** They raised 77 issues, 6 of them "blocking" (an issue that must be settled before the entry can be published). I agreed with 70 and rejected 7, with the reason logged for each in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. None needed to go to the owner as a disagreement between reviewers.

The useful finds were gaps in what the entries covered:
- *collection* lacked the very common use for gathering things (*data collection*, *tax collection*); its "taking away" sense now covers gathering too, and the senses were reordered so that the commoner ones come first.
- *confirmation* lacked official approval of a person for a public job (*a confirmation hearing*); this is now a sense of its own.
- *communication* gained a subsense for *communications* as a field of work or study.
- *congratulation* had a learner-error note saying the word is "always plural", which contradicted its own second sense (*a letter of congratulation*); the note now says the good wishes are plural, and its error examples are now noun uses in a sentence, not the interjection *Congratulations!*, which has its own entry.

Most of the minor fixes removed explanation fields from ordinary nouns (the style guide keeps that field for function words and hard uses), swapped British wording out of American examples (*on our street*, *going-away present*), and replaced weak collocations.

Rejected issues included reviewer B's claim that *consideration* should be transcribed with an r-colored vowel before the stressed syllable. The r opens the stressed syllable, and all four American votes, CMU included, agreed with our transcription. I also rejected a request for a "fashion" label (the closed list of subject labels has none; "clothing" is the nearest), and one asking for *congratulation* to be marked plural-only, which would have hidden its plural form.

**Pronunciation.** Twenty-three of twenty-four transcriptions were verified. The British form of *congratulation* got one vote in three. I kept the vowel that matches the existing entry for *congratulate*, flagged the entry `pronunciation-unverified`, and added a line to `reviews/needs_curator.txt` for the owner.

**Cross-references.** Thirteen of the reviewed synonyms point to entries with several senses, where the tool will not guess which sense should carry the matching link. I placed each one by hand (for example *concern* in the sense "a business" is now listed at *business*, "a company").

**Spend.** US$0.42 of the day's US$15. About one cent was wasted when I ran the pronunciation check a second time to see output the first run had already shown.

**What did not work.** The double claim (above), and the repeated pronunciation call. Neither changed any entry.

**What is next.** The family list continues from *cook* (noun). When *cap* is next reviewed, it can gain a subsense for the top of a mushroom, now that *mushroom* has an entry.

**For the owner.** One new curator line, the British pronunciation of *congratulation*. Nothing else needs you from this cycle.

## Cycle 2: build

**What the cycle did.** After cycle 1 merged, the selector chose *build*: new entries taken in order from the main queue, which is now working through band-2 nouns (band 2 is the second most important of five editorial frequency bands). I drafted twelve: *collar*, *combination*, *comedy*, *command* (noun), *companion*, *complaint*, *concept*, *congress*, *consequence*, *construction*, *consumer*, and *context*. Each one went through the same full pipeline as in cycle 1: the schema validator, inflections from rules, the pronunciation panel, both reviewer models reading every field, my decision on every issue, the defining-vocabulary check, and the cross-reference tool.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** They raised 35 issues, 3 of them blocking. Two reviewers found nothing to fix in *collar*, *combination*, or *consequence*. I applied 31 and rejected 4.

The blocking issues:
- The second sense of *complaint* used the verb *complain* in its own definition, which the style guide forbids. It now reads "something that makes you not happy or satisfied, which you tell other people about".
- An example in *concept* said *motor show*, which is British. The dictionary's main variety is American, so it now says *auto show*. Both reviewers caught this one.

The useful coverage finds:
- *second in command* is now a phrase under *command*.
- *companion* has a subsense for a partner someone lives with without being married, and its third sense now covers a guide book (*a companion to the exhibition*).
- *construction* covers the building of ideas or systems (*the construction of a theory*).

The minor fixes trimmed signposts to the house limit of three words, removed a medical subject label from the everyday *a skin complaint* sense, and dropped loose synonyms (*user* for *consumer*, *situation* for *context*).

Rejected:
- Moving *chain of command* under *command*. The house keyword rule puts an idiom under its first noun, here *chain*, so it is noted in `NEXT.md` for when that entry is next reviewed.
- Marking the law-making sense of *congress* as singular-only, since other countries' congresses can be counted. The usage note already explains *Congress* with a capital and no *the*.
- Doubts about *protest* as a synonym of *complaint* and *structure* as a synonym of *construction* in grammar. Both are close enough.

**Pronunciation.** All 24 transcriptions were verified.

**Cross-references.** I placed ten matching links by hand in entries with several senses (for example *concept* is now listed at *idea* in its "picture in your mind" sense).

**Spend.** US$0.35 for this cycle; US$0.77 so far today, out of the daily US$15.

**What did not work.** I first gave the metrics tool the wrong count of changed entries (33 rather than 22) and corrected its record by hand.

**What is next.** Band-2 nouns continue in queue order after *context*.

**For the owner.** Nothing new from this cycle.
