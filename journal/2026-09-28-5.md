# 2026-09-28 (5) — Review: *prove* to *punish*, and ten words from the closure queue

## What this run did

This was the first cycle of the scheduled run that began at 06:44 UTC. A "cycle" is one unit of work, merged on its own. No earlier work was left unmerged on GitHub, and the inbox (the folder where you leave instructions) was empty.

The mode selector (the script that decides what each cycle does) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that the reviewer panel (two outside models that check every field) has read only once.

The block was fourteen entries: the last four thin late-cycle verbs from 2026-09-26 (*prove, provide, pull, punish*) and ten "closure" words added that evening, words the dictionary's own definitions use (*axe, diaper, gamble, gene, organization, attractive, biological, comparative, ethnic, resource*).

I read each verb before sending it to the panel and filled the gaps:

- **prove**: a pattern with a noun after the verb (*The trip proved a great success*); a usage note on **proved** and **proven** (*a proven method*); a learner error and translator's note, since in several languages a look-alike word means *try* or *taste*.
- **provide**: a new formal, legal sense (*The contract provides that either side can end the agreement*); a short note on choosing between *give*, *provide*, and *supply*; a usage note on **provided (that)** meaning *only if*.
- **pull**: three new senses: taking out a weapon (*He pulled a knife*), removing a product or a show, and doing a trick or a stunt; the phrase *pull yourself together*; a note on *pull*, *drag*, *tug*, and *draw*.
- **punish**: a subsense for *punish yourself*, and a note for translators that the wrong action follows **for** and the penalty follows **with**.

The ten closure words were in reasonable shape and went to the panel unchanged.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 34 issues, 21 of them "blocking" (the entry cannot stay marked reviewed until a decision is logged). I decided all 34: 10 applied and 24 rejected.

What I accepted:

- **pull**: my first wording for the weapon sense said *ready to use it against someone*; a police officer can pull a gun too, so it now says *so that it is ready to use*. The trick sense said *dishonest or silly in order to trick someone*, which does not fit *pull a stunt*; it now reads *do something surprising, silly, or dishonest, often to trick someone*, and *pull a joke* (not usual English) became *pull a prank*. The note that said *draw* means smooth movement was wrong and now says *draw* survives mainly in fixed phrases (*draw the curtains*).
- **punish**: *punish yourself* can also mean making yourself suffer on purpose, not only blaming yourself, and the definition now covers both. *Be punished by law* became *be punished with prison*.
- **diaper**: *take in its waste* was unclear; it is now *catch and hold its waste*.
- **provide**: one example (*She was left with nothing to provide for her old age*) was confusing and was replaced.

What I rejected, and why:

- The first reviewer wanted *the axe* (as in *two hundred jobs are facing the axe*) turned from a sense into a set of phrases. A sense that is always used with *the* is the ordinary way to show this, and the example is a very common news use.
- It wanted the British plural *organisations* in the inflections. Forms follow the American headword; the British spelling is a recorded variant.
- A length mark in American *resource*: house convention drops it.
- It objected to the example *She likes to wear ethnic jewelry* because the usage note warns that some people dislike this use. The dictionary must show the sense; the note cautions.
- The second reviewer again objected to translators' notes that begin with the headword in quotation marks (eight times). That is house convention.
- It also asked whether the headword should be *ax* rather than *axe*. Entry names are permanent, and the usage note already says Americans use both spellings.

Apart from these, the second reviewer flagged only two gradability codes that the style guide deliberately puts on each sense.
