# 2026-10-17, cycle 1 of the 12:44 UTC run: second review of *paper* to *payment*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that had been through the two outside reviewers (the review panel, two language models from other companies) only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose review because it was the most overdue kind of work, and the block is always the oldest such entries. This time that meant twenty nouns drafted on 2026-10-06, in alphabetical order: *paper, paragraph, parent, park, parking, parliament, part, partner, party, passenger, passion, password, past, path, patience, patient, pattern, pause, pay, payment*.

Each entry went to both reviewers again. I read every issue and either changed the entry (*applied*) or left it as it was (*rejected*, with a logged reason). I also read all twenty entries myself before seeing the reviews.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

reviewer-a raised 46 issues, 30 of them marked blocking (meaning the entry should not stand without a decision). reviewer-b raised 4 and gave a verdict on every field, so its replies were complete. Seven entries drew no issues from either reviewer (*parking, partner, party, past, patient, pattern, payment*). In all I applied 18 and rejected 32; nothing was escalated to the owner.

Applied:

- ***paper***: the newspaper sense was defined only as *a newspaper*, which leans on a word built from *paper* itself. It is now defined in plain words (printed pages with news and stories, sold every day or every week). The exam sense is labeled British, yet its example said *The math paper*, an American word; it now says *The history paper*.
- ***park***: the ballgame sense now reads *a field for a ball game, with seats for the people who watch*, with a short explanation that it is used mainly for baseball. The old explanation spelled *ballpark* as two words; that is gone. *Garden* is no longer listed as a synonym of the town park (it stays under *see also*).
- ***parliament***: the usage note said some countries *call their lawmakers* Congress; Congress is the name of the body, not of the people, so it now says some countries give the group another name. Two collocations now capitalize *Parliament*.
- ***pause***: the second sense was only *the button*; it now also covers the state of being stopped, with a new example (*The movie is on pause*). The learner error now corrects *Let's make a pause* to *Let's pause for a moment*.
- ***pay***: *payday* came out of the collocations (both reviewers: it is one word, not a collocation), and the learner error's correction is now *He gets good pay*.
- Weak synonyms removed: *slice* and *unit* from *part*, *fire* from *passion*.
- ***path***: an example for *cross someone's path* used the different form *our paths crossed*; it was replaced.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- *Take part* stays under *part*: the house rule files an idiom under its first noun.
- *Passenger* meaning a team member who does no work is real British usage (*we can't carry passengers*); the sense stays.
- Region labels stand where reviewer-a objected that the word is also used elsewhere: the exam sense of *paper* (British), *park* for a ball ground (American), *off the beaten path* (American; British usually says *off the beaten track*). In this dictionary a region label means *mainly used there*.
- *On the park* stays a learner error, *Member of Parliament* stays the phrase taught, and *the pause button* stays a collocation.
- reviewer-b said the definition of *cross someone's path* was not a base-form verb phrase; it is (*meet someone, usually by chance*).

## My own fixes

- ***parking***: sense 1 began *the act of leaving a car*, a padding phrase the style guide forbids; it now reads *leaving a car or another vehicle in a place for a time*.
- ***party***: a learner-error note showed the wrong form *the party of Ana* in italics, which the house rules do not allow in prose; the wrong form now appears only in the error's *incorrect* field.

No new cross-links were created, and every definition word has an entry. One rewrite first used *baseball* in a definition, which has no entry yet; the word moved to the explanation.
