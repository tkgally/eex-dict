## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers raised 47 issues, 29 of them blocking. I applied 39 and rejected 8.

What I accepted:

- **A reversed definition.** I had defined *spell* in *spell trouble* as "have something as a result", which is backwards. It now reads "be a sign that something, usually something bad, will happen".
- **Overstatements.** I had said that *smell* is never used in continuous tenses, that *starting raining* is wrong, and that only *start* can be used for creating a business. Each is too strong. The notes now say "usually", or name the exception: *I'm smelling the milk* is fine when you do it on purpose.
- **Definitions that muddled "make something do this"** in three senses of *split*. They now read "divide something..., or become divided in this way".
- **Extensions the definitions did not cover**: light and sound spilling out of a room, a band splitting up, *steal a look* under an intransitive sense. Each was removed.
- **A vague note** about false friends on *stay*. (A false friend is a word that looks like a word in another language but means something different.) It was replaced with a concrete grammar note.

What I rejected:

- *The mountains snowed overnight* as proof that *snow* takes subjects other than *it*. That sentence is not standard English.
- Two objections to grammar values that are on the house's closed list of patterns.
- Moving *spill the beans* under *spill*. The house rule files an idiom under its first noun, *beans*.
- The claim that *We spent a good time* is not a common learner error. It is a very common word-for-word translation.

After the reviews, the vocabulary check found ten words in definitions that are outside the defining vocabulary (the plain list of words that definitions must use), such as *puzzle*, *upright*, *visitor*, and *deceive*. I rewrote those definitions in plainer words.

The cross-reference tool adds the matching link back when one entry links to another. It added eleven of these. Two were on the wrong sense and I moved them: the link from *spell* to *mean* now sits on "result in", and the link from *stay* to *leave* on "go away". Two links had different types on the two sides (a synonym on one side, a "compare" on the other). I settled both.
