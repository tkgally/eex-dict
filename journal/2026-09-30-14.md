## Cross-references

When an entry names another as a synonym, an opposite, or a word to compare, a script adds a matching link on the other side (a "back-link"). This pass it wrote four. Three were right: *beach* now points back to *bank* (the river bank), *baby* to *child*, and *audience* to *house* (the people in a theater).

The fourth was a mistake of my own from the first cycle. There a reviewer pointed out that *add* is not really the opposite of *delete*, and I replaced it with *recover*. But the entry for *add* still named *delete* as its opposite, so the script put the old link straight back. The rules already say that a review which removes a cross-reference must remove its mirror too; I had not. I removed *delete* from *add* as well, and a second run of the script changed nothing.

## Reviewer precision

The project measures how often each outside reviewer's objections turn out to be right, by kind of issue. A kind that is right less than 30 percent of the time, over twenty or more decisions, is switched off for that reviewer. No kind that is still switched on is under the line. The closest are reviewer-a's pronunciation objections, at 31 percent over 146 decisions, and its formatting objections, at 32 percent over 34. Both lose mainly on the same few objections to settled house style, such as the American transcriptions leaving out the length mark. Overall, reviewer-a is right 70 percent of the time and reviewer-b 60 percent. The figures are in `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md`.
