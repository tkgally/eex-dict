# 2026-10-18, cycle 1 of the 12:43 UTC run: the thirty-ninth originality check

## What this run did

This was an **originality** cycle. Every tenth run, the scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) forces a check that our wording has not drifted toward a published dictionary's. This was run 460, so the check was due. The tool (`tools/originality_check.py`) picked ten fields at random from finished entries: eight definitions, one phrase definition, and one usage note. For each, I searched the web for the exact wording and looked at whether any published dictionary or word list shows the same or nearly the same text. I also asked one outside reviewer model (reviewer-a) whether each field reads as copied. The verdicts are in `reviews/originality/2026-10-18.md`.

Before the check, the usual start-of-run sweep found no open pull requests and no leftover work on old branches; the four old `claude/*` branches are already listed for pruning in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`. The inbox was empty.

## What the searches found

Three fields are **original**: no published wording was close. These are *copy* in the sense *one of many books ... that are exactly the same*, the usage note on *on the beach* and *at the beach*, and *on* the preposition for buses, trains, planes, and horses.

Two are **generic overlaps**: short, plain definitions that any dictionary would write in much the same way. *Shoulder* as *meat from the top of an animal's front leg* is the only natural way to name that cut. The *party* idiom (*a person who loves going to parties and enjoys them very much*) shares the plain idea "loves parties" with other dictionaries but not their wording.

Five needed a **rewrite**, the most in any check so far:

- ***access***, sense 2, read *a way of entering or reaching a place*. A learner's dictionary has exactly these words. It now reads *the way or ways by which people get into a place or get to it*. (The new wording also drops the article *a*, because this sense is uncountable and the house style defines uncountable nouns without one.)
- ***access***, sense 1, read *the right or the chance to use something or to see someone*. The same learner's dictionary has *the opportunity or right to use something or to see somebody/something*: ours was the same frame with one word swapped. It now reads *being allowed or able to use something or to meet someone*.
- ***or else*** (a phrase in the entry *else*) read *used to say what will happen if something is not done*. A general dictionary has exactly this wording. It now reads *used before the bad result that will follow if someone does not do what was just said*.
- ***lift***, sense 3, read *a feeling of being happier or more hopeful*. A learner's dictionary has *a feeling of being happier or more confident than before*. Ours now reads *something that makes you feel happier or more hopeful for a time*, which also fits the examples better (*Winning the game was a real lift*: the lift is the thing, not the feeling).
- ***employer*** read *a person or an organization that pays people to work for them*. A learner's dictionary has *a person or company that pays people to work for them*. It now reads *a person, company, or other group that gives people jobs and pays them for their work*.

All five were rewritten by hand, field by field. No script touched them.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

A rewrite counts as a review of the entry, so the four changed entries went to both reviewers again (the "panel"). They raised 7 issues, 4 of them marked blocking. I applied 1 and rejected 6.

Applied: reviewer-a said the advice at the end of the culture note on *lift* (*Choose the word for the readers*) was vague. It now says *Use the word your readers or listeners will expect.*

Rejected:
- reviewer-b said *lift* meaning *a free ride in a car* is fully standard in American English and should not be labelled British. Our region labels mean *mainly used there*, and Americans usually say *ride*; this is a settled house rule.
- reviewer-a said the "way in" sense of *access* can be countable, with a plural *accesses*. In this sense *access* is uncountable; the article in the new definition was removed so the entry agrees with itself.
- reviewer-a wanted an informal label on the whole phrase *or else*. Only the bare threat *or else!* is informal, and the explanation already says so.

## What reviewer-a said about copying

reviewer-a answered *copied* for eight of the ten fields, each time naming the same learner's dictionary and "quoting" our own wording back, once with *bicycle* for our *bike*. Three of those quotes did match real wording, but in other dictionaries than the one it named; the rest were not found. This continues the pattern recorded in `wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md` (updated with this check): its quotes echo the input, and the web search alone decides the verdicts.
