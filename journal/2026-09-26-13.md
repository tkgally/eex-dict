This cycle drafted the next ten verbs in band 1, the most basic band of the word list: **need, notice, obey, observe, occur, open, operate, order, owe, own**.

- *Observe* and *open* are the largest, with five senses (separate meanings) each.
- *Need* explains two ways of saying a thing needs doing: *the car needs washing* and *the car needs to be washed*. It also notes that the modal *needn't* is mainly British.
- *Occur* notes that it has no passive (*the accident occurred*, not *was occurred*) and that in *it occurred to me*, the person comes after **to**.

The learner-error boxes show a common wrong sentence beside the correct one. This cycle's include *open the light* → *turn on the light*, *operated her* → *operated on her*, *obey to the rules* → *obey the rules*, and *I owe to you ten dollars* → *I owe you ten dollars*.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline:

- **Format and inflections.** All ten passed the format check. The inflection tool doubled the **r** in *occurred* and *occurring* by rule, as it should.
- **Pronunciation.** All twenty American and British transcriptions were verified.
- **Reviewers.** Three reviewer replies each left out a verdict on one field. Every other field in those replies was read, so the reviews were kept and not run again, and the tool recorded the gaps. The reviewers raised 27 issues, 16 of them blocking (they must be settled before publication). Each got a logged decision: 19 fixes applied and 8 rejected.

**What was fixed.** Reviewer-a's best finds were in *open*. The "start a new business" sense had included buildings, but a building is not "started"; the sense now says a new building or place is made ready for people to use. The computer sense now says a file is shown so you can use it, not just made "ready". The "for customers" sense now describes becoming ready at the start of the day. Other fixes:

- *operate* in the medical sense now says "in order to treat an injury or an illness" instead of "repair or remove a part".
- *owe* in *you owe me an apology* now ties the duty to what was done.
- *own*'s usage note no longer claims that owning is always legal.

A tool check found that *see* listed *notice* as a word to compare, while *notice* listed *see* as a synonym. The pair is now a compare pair on both sides, with a note that **see** can also mean *look at* while **notice** only means *become aware of*.

**What was rejected.** Reviewer-a again asked for a length mark in the American transcription of *need*. The house rule leaves it out, and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary agrees. It also called the notes that *need* and *own* are "not normally" used in the continuous form too absolute, but the notes already say "normally". It called *observe my class* intransitive, although *my class* is its object.
