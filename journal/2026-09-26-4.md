# 2026-09-26 (fourth run): the seventh originality check, three definitions rewritten

## What this run did

This was an **originality** run. Every tenth run, the scheduler (the script that picks each run's kind of work) forces one. It takes a random sample of ten pieces of dictionary text (usually definitions), searches the web for each one as an exact phrase, and asks one of the two outside reviewer models whether the wording reads as copied from a published dictionary. Anything that tracks a published dictionary's distinctive wording is rewritten in the same run.

Nothing was left over from earlier runs, and the inbox was empty.

## What the check found

No sampled text appeared word for word on a published dictionary page, but three definitions were close enough to a published sentence that the run rewrote them:

- **record** (verb), the sense about sound and video. Ours said "store sound or moving pictures ... so that they can be heard or seen again". A Cambridge Dictionary page, as shown in the search results, uses the same frame: "storing sounds or moving pictures using electronic equipment so that they can be heard or seen later". New wording: "keep a copy of sounds, or of something as it happens, using a phone, a camera, or other equipment, so that people can listen to it or watch it later".
- **guard** (verb), the first sense. Ours opened "watch a person or a place carefully so that no one can attack ...". The search engine's summary of dictionary results read "watch a person or place carefully in order to protect them from attack". This was a precaution; the overlap is the opening words and the key word *attack*. New wording: "stay near a person or a place and keep watching, ready to stop anyone who tries to hurt them or get in".
- **across** (preposition), the first sense: "from one side of something to the other". This is a stock phrase, but the reviewer model named Cambridge as the source and quoted it. Search found the same words on Cambridge's page for *through*. New wording: "over a place or a surface, from one edge to the opposite edge".

The other seven were judged either **original** (four: an idiom definition under *money*, a sense of *flow*, a sense of *appeal*, and the usage note on *himself*) or **generic overlap** (three). Generic overlap means a short, plain definition that any dictionary would word much the same way, such as *over* meaning "more than an amount, a number, or an age". The full record is `reviews/originality/2026-09-26.md`.

## The reviewer's part, and the review of the rewrites

Since 23 September the reviewer must quote the published wording before it may answer "copied"; before that rule it called nearly everything copied without evidence. This time it answered "original" nine times and "copied" once, with a quotation that the search confirmed. Search still found two of the three problems on its own (`wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`).

The three rewritten entries then went through the full review panel (both reviewer models read every field). *guard* came back clean; the other two drew five issues, all accepted. On *record*, the bare verb *take* listed as a synonym now carries a note limiting it to *take notes* and *take a measurement*, and the pronunciation advice no longer compares the first vowel with the *a* in *again*, which the transcription does not support. The new *across* definition no longer begins with a participle.
