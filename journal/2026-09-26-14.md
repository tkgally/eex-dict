# 2026-09-26 (fourteenth run): the eighth originality check

## What this run did

This was the eighth cycle of this session and an **originality** run. Every tenth run, the scheduler forces this check. It tests whether the dictionary's wording has drifted toward copying published dictionaries. The project's charter forbids consulting copyrighted dictionaries while drafting, and this check is the safeguard that looks for accidental resemblance afterward. Earlier cycles in this session built fifty verbs, reviewed twenty older entries, and ran a lint pass (pull requests #76 to #82).

A tool (`originality_check.py`) picked ten fields at random from across the dictionary. Each was checked in three steps:

1. **Web search.** The run searched the web for the exact wording, looking only at whether a published dictionary or word list shows the same words.
2. **Reviewer question.** The run asked one reviewer model whether the wording reads as copied. The reviewer may answer "copied" only if it can quote the published source exactly.
3. **Verdict.** Each field got one of three verdicts. **Original** means no resemblance. **Generic-overlap** means a short, plain wording that any dictionary would write much the same way; this is not copying. **Rewrite** means the wording follows a published dictionary's distinctive phrasing closely enough that it should be changed.

## What was found

None of the ten searches found an exact match.

- **Original (six):** *draw* ("come nearer in time"), a phrase under *be*, *behind*, *at*, *close* (the computer sense), and the usage note for *as well as*.
- **Generic-overlap (three):** the definition of *role model*, which is phrased much like other learner dictionaries but is not the same; a short sense of *fill*; and the usage note on *onto*. The *onto* note resembles a sentence on a grammar website, not a dictionary.
- **Rewrite (one):** *bend*, the sense about moving your body. The draft read "move part of your body, such as your back or your knees, so that it is not straight". The search results summarized Cambridge's entry for *bend* as "move your body or part of your body so that it is not straight". That is the same frame and the same ending. It is a plain idea, but earlier checks treated a match this close as a reason to rewrite, so this one was rewritten as a precaution.

The reviewer model, asked in a single combined question, called eight fields original and said it could not tell for two, the usage notes. It called none copied. As in the previous check, the web search found the close match and the reviewer did not. This supports keeping the web search as the main test.

## The rewrite

*Bend* sense 2 now reads: "put your back, your neck, a leg, or another part of your body out of a straight line, or lean your whole body one way". The changed entry then went through a review round, as the rules require for any rewritten entry. Both of reviewer-a's issues were accepted.

- The first rewrite had limited the sense to leaning forward and to arms and legs. The final wording above now covers any part of the body and leaning in any direction.
- Sense 1 said "go into a different shape instead of staying straight", which did not fit its example *the road bends sharply to the left*, because a road does not change shape. It now reads "be or become curved instead of straight, or make something do this".

The entry passed validation and the definition-vocabulary check. The full record of the check is in `reviews/originality/2026-09-26-2.md`.
