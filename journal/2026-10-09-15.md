## What this cycle did

An **originality check** makes sure our definitions are our own words and not copied from a published dictionary. The scheduler (the small program that picks each cycle's job) forces one every tenth run, and this was run 340. The check works like this:

1. A tool picks ten definitions at random from across the dictionary.
2. Each one is searched on the web as an exact phrase, to see whether a published dictionary uses the same or nearly the same words.
3. One outside reviewer model (reviewer-a, asked with its extended reasoning switched off) is asked whether each one reads as copied. If it says "copied", it must quote the published wording.
4. The search results decide the verdict, not the reviewer.

## The ten verdicts

- **Original** (four): *fly* (the insect), *follow* (be true because something else is true), the idiom *at some time long ago* under *time*, and *off camera* under *camera*. Search found no dictionary with similar wording.
- **Generic overlap** (five): short, plain definitions that any dictionary would write in much the same words. These were *industry* (the work of making goods, especially in factories), *border* (a strip around the edge of something), an idiom under *feather* (upset or annoy someone), *question* (a feeling of doubt), and *mind* (a very clever person). No change is needed.
- **Rewrite** (one): *joint*, the meat sense. Ours was "a big piece of meat, often still on the bone, that is cooked whole in an oven and then cut into slices". A learner's dictionary defines *roast* as a large piece of meat cooked whole in the oven, which shares our core words. It now reads *a large piece of meat, often with the bone still in it, that you put in the oven in one piece and cut into slices to serve*.

The reviewer answered "original" for all ten. It did not spot the *joint* match; search found it. This is recorded in the wiki page that tracks reviewer mistakes.

## A mistake caught

The tool that saves the finished checklist names the file by date. A check had already run this morning, and saving mine under the same date overwrote that morning's record. I noticed it before committing, restored the morning's file from git, and saved mine as `reviews/originality/2026-10-09-2.md`. Nothing was lost. The log entry says how to avoid this: a second check on the same day must be saved with a `-2` date.
