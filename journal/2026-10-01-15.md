# 2026-10-01 (15): twelve new nouns, *cheese* to *client*

*Third cycle of the run that started at 18:43 UTC. Mode: build.*

## What this run did

The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that chooses each cycle's kind of work) chose a **build**: write new entries from the headword queue. The next twelve band-1 words in queue order were all nouns: *cheese, chest, chicken, choice, church, circle, citizen, city, claim, class, clay, client*. Band 1 is the most basic frequency band.

I drafted each entry from the style guide and my own knowledge, filling every field that applies. Some points worth knowing:

- **Countable and uncountable uses.** *cheese* and *chicken* are uncountable as food (*some cheese*, *chicken for dinner*) but countable for kinds or whole items (*two French cheeses*, *a chicken* as the bird). Both entries have a learner error and a translator's note for this, because many languages count these words freely.
- **No article for the activity.** *church* takes no article when it means going to pray (*go to church*, *after church*), as *school* does. Its usage note and learner error (*We go to the church every Sunday*) cover this.
- **False friends.** *claim* is not a complaint, and *client* is not a shop customer. In some languages the similar-looking word has those meanings. Each entry has a learner error and an adaptation note (a note for a future translator) on the trap.
- **Choosing between near-synonyms.** Short paragraphs explain *choice* against *option* and *selection*; *village*, *town* and *city*; *lesson*, *class* and *course*; and *client* against *customer*.
- **Idioms under their first noun.** Fourteen in all, including *get something off your chest*, *don't count your chickens*, *a vicious circle*, *senior citizen*, *claim to fame* and *in a class of its own*.

Fourteen definitions first used words outside the defining vocabulary, the list of about 2,400 words that definitions are written from. Each was reworded. One case was awkward. *Christian* is not on the list, and the first sense of a list word is the only place where an outside word is allowed. So *church* names Christians only in sense 1. The "group of believers" sense keeps a plain definition, with an explanation that it is used only for Christian groups. The core idea (the one-sentence summary at the top of the entry) refers back to sense 1. This keeps the vocabulary rule and still answers a reviewer who said the entry had become too general.

## Checks and what the reviewers found

All 24 transcriptions were confirmed by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. Two outside reviewer models then read every field. They raised 25 issues, 14 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry counts as reviewed). I accepted 17 and rejected 8, each with a logged reason.

**Accepted:**

- *church* had compared itself with *hospital*, but American English says *in the hospital* with an article, so the comparison was wrong for the dictionary's main variety. Only *school* remains as the parallel.
- My learner error *I live in Osaka city* was a poor choice, because *Osaka City* is an official name. The error now uses a city with no such name.
- *play chicken* now also covers its use for arguments between two sides.
- Two *class* examples did not use *class* as a noun in the sense shown (*a working-class family*, *we traveled first class*) and were replaced.
- Small fixes: *a close circle* became *a small circle*, and *claim to fame* lost its "humorous" label.

**Rejected:**

- A claim that *I ate a chicken for lunch* is correct. It means the whole bird, and the note already says so.
- A request to replace *stomach* with a medical word in the *chest* definition. *Stomach* is the plain word a B1 reader knows.
- Five repeat flags against the quoted headword at the start of adaptation notes, which house rules allow.

The cross-reference tool added one back-link, from *box* to *chest*, on the right sense. Missing targets such as *option*, *customer*, *resident*, *lesson* and *coward* were queued.
