## Back-links: the main work

Cross-references in this dictionary are meant to be two-way: if *bill* says "compare *receipt*", then *receipt* should say "compare *bill*". A script (`tools/crossref.py`) adds the missing return links, but it must guess which sense of the target to attach them to, and it guesses wrong often enough that every lint checks each one by hand.

This pass the script added fourteen return links, mostly to the words drafted in the last two cycles. Eight were in the right place. Five were attached to the wrong sense and I moved them: *child* (in *a child of its time*) belongs to the "result" sense of *product*, not the "thing sold" sense; *emotion* contrasts with *reason* as the power to think, not with *reason* meaning "why"; *ground* (a good reason for doing something) belongs to the "good cause" sense of *reason*; *meaning* (what makes life worth living) belongs to the "aim in life" sense of *purpose*; and *paragraph* belongs to the general "part of something" sense of *section*, not the newspaper sense.

The fourteenth was wrong in a different way. The entry *color*, in its sense about skin color, said "compare *race*". Until this run, *race* had no entry; this run's *race* covers only the contest, because *race* meaning a group of people is an unrelated word with its own entry still to come (`race-n-2`). So the script attached the skin-color link to the running race. I removed it there and pointed *color* at `race-n-2` instead, so the link will work once that entry is written.

One pair disagreed in kind: *part* listed *section* as a word to compare, while the new *section* listed *part* as a synonym. By the standing practice I followed the older entry and made both "compare".
