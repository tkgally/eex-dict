# Run report, 2026-10-31 (fourth run)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the tool that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to write. Its reason was that build had the largest "debt" of the eligible kinds of work; the day's budget had US$12.90 left and no entries were waiting in draft. It claimed twelve band-2 verbs (band 2 is the second most important frequency group) in queue order: *recycle*, *reform*, *refund*, *regard*, *register*, *reject*, *release*, *rely*, *request*, *resemble*, *reserve*, and *resign*. All twelve were drafted from scratch, checked, reviewed by both reviewers, corrected, and marked `reviewed`, which means they will appear on the public site.

Pre-flight found no open pull requests, no entry reports from the public, and nothing in the owner's inbox. The five old branches with no unmerged work are still there; each already has a line in the curator file asking for it to be deleted, since this tool set cannot delete branches.

**Checks before review.** The inflection tool generated every verb form from its spelling rules (*relies*, *relied*, *relying*). The pronunciation check, a panel of models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary consulted at run time, accepted all 24 transcriptions (American and British for each verb). For *release* the CMU dictionary preferred a different first vowel, but three of four models agreed with ours, which meets the agreement rule.

**What the reviewers found.** The two reviewers (reviewer A, a Claude model; reviewer B, a Google model) raised 57 issues, 12 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry can be marked reviewed). Every one was decided by hand: 45 applied, 12 rejected, none escalated to you. The main changes:

- *reform*: an example (*a reformed smoker*) and two collocations showed *reformed* used as an adjective before a noun, which belongs to an adjective entry, not the verb. They were replaced with a plain verb use (*he finally reformed*), and `reformed-adj` was added to the word family so it is queued.
- *reject*: an example about teenagers rejecting their parents' values was filed under the sense "not give someone love", but it means "not accept an idea". It was replaced with one about rejecting a person. *shops* became *stores* (American wording).
- *register*: the "notice" sense (*it didn't register*) wrongly said the noticing happens "after a short time"; it now says "notice something or take it in". The "make a complaint officially" sense had a grammar pattern it does not take. An example about registering with a doctor, mainly British, was replaced.
- *reserve*: *reserve judgment* was redefined so that the definition takes an object, as the verb does.
- Coverage: *release* gained a sense for letting out a feeling (*release stress*) and a subsense for freeing someone from a contract or promise. *request* now also covers asking someone to do something (*Guests are requested not to smoke*).
- Smaller fixes: *recycle* now covers sorting waste as well as treating it; *regard* "look at" is labelled literary rather than formal; *rely* lost an explanation that repeated a learner-error note.

**What was rejected, and why.** Both reviewers asked for *reserve the right to do something*. Under the house keyword rule an idiom goes under its first noun, *right*, so it was rejected here and noted in `NEXT.md` for the next review of *right*. Three objections to short explanations stating a form restriction (*resign yourself to*, *it didn't register*) were rejected because house rule 15 puts such restrictions in the explanation. Two requests for a pattern for bare intransitive use were rejected because the closed pattern list has none.

**Cross-references.** The cross-reference tool adds a back-link in the other entry when it can tell which sense to attach it to. In eight cases it could not, and all eight were placed by hand: *improve* lists *reform*, *hold* lists *release* as an opposite, *issue* lists *release*, *trust* lists *rely*, *decline* lists *reject*, *accept* lists *resign*, and *return* and *retire* carry short notes telling them apart from *refund* and *resign*. *quit* already compared itself with *resign*, so *resign* now compares *quit* too, with a note, instead of calling it a synonym. Nineteen missing words named in the new entries were added to the queue, among them *registration*, *reliable*, *resemblance*, and *resignation*.

**Spend.** US$0.36 of the day's US$15 budget (reviewer B and the pronunciation panel; reviewer A runs at no charge). The day's total is US$2.46.

**What did not work.** Three definitions first used words outside the defining vocabulary (*aside*, *complaint*, *tension*); all were reworded. Nothing else failed.

**What is next.** More band-2 verbs in queue order, or whatever the selector picks; the next whole-dictionary lint pass is due in two runs.

**What needs you.** Nothing new from this cycle. The standing items are in `NEXT.md` under "For the owner".

## Cycle 2: closure

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose *closure*: building entries for words the dictionary already uses in its own definitions, word families, or cross-references but has no entry for yet. The day's budget had US$12.54 left. The `closure` list (words used inside definitions) held only one row, *needles*. That is a plural, so it was marked `duplicate` in the queue and its singular, *needle*, was claimed by hand instead. The other eleven came from the `family` list (words named in another entry's word family), in queue order: *acting*, *adjustment*, *advance*, *advertisement*, *adviser*, *airline*, *airplane*, *apology*, *application*, *assembly*, and *assistant*. All twelve are nouns, all were drafted from scratch, and all are now `reviewed`.

**Checks before review.** The inflection tool gave every noun its plural (*apologies*, *assemblies*) and left *acting* with none, as an uncountable noun. All 24 transcriptions passed the pronunciation check. For *adviser* only two of four models agreed with the American transcription and the CMU dictionary differed, but two votes is enough under the agreement rule. *advertisement* records the real difference between American English (stress on *-tise-*) and British English (stress on *-ver-*), with an adaptation note for translators.

**What the reviewers found.** The two reviewers raised 33 issues, 6 of them blocking. All were decided by hand: 32 applied, 1 rejected, none escalated to you. Examples first:

- *needle*: one example said that *searching* for a name "is a needle in a haystack". The idiom names the thing that is hard to find, not the search, so the example was replaced (*Her lost earring was a needle in a haystack somewhere in the sand*).
- *airplane*: an example about children making paper airplanes did not fit the definition (a vehicle with an engine), and it was replaced. *aircraft* moved from "synonym" to "compare", with a note that it also covers helicopters. The British *aeroplane* is now recorded as a different form with three syllables, not just a spelling.
- *assistant*: *assistant manager* means a rank, not a helper. It now has its own subsense (one level below the job named), with an explanation that it is used before a job title.

Other changes: *assembly* no longer says that assemblies are always elected or that school assemblies happen at the start of the day (both are mainly British facts), and its "coming together" sense is now countable too (*a large assembly of guests*). *apology* gained the polite phrase *my apologies*. *advance* lost a military label that was too narrow, and its core idea now covers the money and romantic senses. *advertisement* now covers radio and online advertising. Two core ideas were reworded so that every sense fits under them. Several collocations were corrected (*run an advertisement*, *get an advance*, *legislative assembly*).

**What was rejected.** Reviewer A suggested a sense of *apology* meaning "a very poor example" (*an apology for a meal*). It is rare and literary, too far down the list for a band-2 learner's entry, so it was rejected with that reason.

**Cross-references.** In four cases the tool could not tell which sense a back-link belonged to, and all four were placed by hand: *pin* now compares *needle*, *change* lists *adjustment*, *progress* (in society and science) lists *advance*, and *program* (computer) lists *application*. *act* and *ad* gained family links on their own. Five missing words were queued.

**Spend.** US$0.35. The day's total is US$2.82 of US$15.

**What did not work.** The closure list is now almost empty: it gave one row, and that row was a plural that should never have been queued. Five definition words fell outside the defining vocabulary (*sexual*, *pine*, *steady*, *rank*, *coach*), and all were reworded.

**What is next.** The selector's choice. The whole-dictionary lint pass is due in one run, and the `family` list continues after *assistant*.

**What needs you.** Nothing new from this cycle.
