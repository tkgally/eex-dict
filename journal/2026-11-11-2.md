**What was added.**

- *pick*: two subsenses under the "remove" sense, one for picking your nose or teeth and one for *pick at* (*don't pick at that cut*); a new sense for playing a stringed instrument with the fingers (*pick the banjo*); the "eat slowly" sense now says people do it when worried or unwell, not only when not hungry.
- *picture*: a subsense for the general state of things (*the overall picture*, *the jobs picture*); the idea sense now names a picture in your mind; *a picture frame*, *a picture book*; the saying *a picture is worth a thousand words*; and a learner error that is very common: *the man on the picture* for *in the picture*.
- *play*: a new sense for the small free movement of a part (*too much play in the steering wheel*), a new sense for the button that starts a video (*press play*), and the phrases *foul play* (a crime, or unfair actions in sport) and *bring something into play*.
- *pot*: a British sense for a small storing container (*a pot of paint*, *a yogurt pot*), the sayings *a watched pot never boils* and *pots of money* (British, informal), and a pointer to *melting pot*, which has been queued as a future entry.

**What the reviewers found and what was decided.** Two reviewer models read each changed entry field by field. They raised 47 issues, 5 of them "blocking" (must be settled before the entry stays published). 40 were accepted and fixed, 7 rejected.

- Two blocking issues on *pick* asked for *pick someone's pocket* and *pick a fight*. Both were rejected: the dictionary files an idiom under its first noun, and both are already in the entries for *pocket* and *fight*.
- Three blocking issues on *play* said the new *foul play* phrase was defined only as a crime while one example showed its sports meaning. They were right: the definition now covers both meanings.
- Among the accepted minor points: the *play* entry's translator note was generic and was replaced by a specific trap (the stage sense is countable, the fun sense is not); the *picture* translator note repeated a fact from the box comparing *picture*, *photo*, and *image*, and now says only what is new; the British phrase *put someone in the picture* got its British label; the new *pot* sense was labeled British, which both reviewers asked for.
- Rejected: a request to move the drug sense of *pot* earlier (the shared-money sense is at least as useful to a learner); a request to write the saying's definition as *used to say that...* (that formula is kept for function words); a claim that *the play of football* is an invented learner error (many languages use one word for *play* and *game*).
