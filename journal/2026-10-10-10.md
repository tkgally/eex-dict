## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 22 issues, 16 of them marked blocking. I applied 10 and rejected 12. Five entries (*face, fact, factory, farmer, fashion*) drew no issues at all.

Applied:

- *farm*: the second meaning combined fish and animal farms with wind and solar farms. They differ in meaning and word partners, so I split them: meaning 2 is now animals or fish, and the new meaning 3 is a large area of machines that make electricity from the wind or the sun. With three meanings, the entry now opens with a one-sentence summary: a farm is a place used to produce something.
- *faith*: *in bad faith* now means "dishonestly, without really meaning to do what you say you will do". *Keep faith with someone* is now "stay loyal to someone and do what you promised them". Each idiom gained a second example.
- *eye*: *with the naked eye* had said "without using anything, such as glasses". Ordinary glasses count as the naked eye, so it now says "without using any equipment that makes things look bigger or closer". The needle sense now says *thread* instead of *string*.
- *fall*: the waterfall meaning is now "a place where the water of a river falls straight down over a steep edge of rock".
- *fabric*: the usage note said *fabric* and *cloth* "mean the same in most uses". It now says they overlap a lot. It had also said *fabric* "never" means a factory; it now says "in modern English".

Rejected: the claim that *fan* the supporter is a meaning of this entry (it is a separate word); objections to the American label on *take the fall* and *bet the farm* (a region label here means *mainly* used there); the claim that *fabric* still means a factory in British English (long obsolete); an attitude label for *a failure* (the meaning covers things too, and the usage note already warns that the word can hurt); the claim that formal *older than we* is not standard; and both reviewers' objection to *fabric*'s one-sentence summary, which house style requires.
