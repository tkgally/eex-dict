# 2026-09-26 (twenty-seventh run): a second reading of 16 older entries

## What this run did

This was the eighth cycle of this session, and a **review** run. A review run adds no new words. It sends existing entries back to the two reviewer models for a second reading. Entries from the dictionary's first week were read only once, and a second round often finds what the first missed. The block was the next 16 entries by creation date, from *bad*, *off*, *out*, and *per* (2026-09-20) to *surround* (2026-09-21).

## What the reviewers found and what was decided

The reviewers raised 39 issues. Each has a logged decision: 31 applied and 8 rejected. Seven entries came back clean: *bad, arrangement, contact, lean, pressure, role*, and the adjective *concerning*, apart from one rejected point.

**The big one: *out* (preposition).** Almost all of this entry was really about *out of*: *out of the house*, *out of milk*, *made out of wood*, *out of kindness*. It also said three things that are wrong:

- that *out from the house* is always wrong (*out from under the bed* is normal English)
- that *out* is "never" used alone before a noun
- that *out the house* is an error in every case

Both reviewers objected. Reviewer-b pointed out that in American English, the dictionary's main variety, bare *out* is standard before *door* and *window*: *She looked out the window*, *He ran out the door*.

The entry now has a new first sense, "through a door, a window, or another opening", labelled American, with three examples. The *out of* senses stay. The usage note, the learner error, and the translator's note were rewritten to match. The learner error is now *He ran out the house* (corrected to *out of the house*), because bare *out* is used mainly with doors and windows. Reviewer-a also wanted *out of milk* treated as an adjective. That was rejected: *out of* plus a noun is the compound preposition the entry is about.

**Other fixes:**

- ***off***: the example *She was off school with a cold* and the phrase *off school* are British. They were replaced with *off duty*. A pronunciation note said *off* has the same vowel as *dog* and *long*, which is true only in some American accents. It now just explains how *off* differs from *of*. The "stay off coffee" subsense now includes medical reasons.
- ***per***, sense 2 (*per your request*), was defined as "in the way stated by", which is too vague. It now reads "in the way that a rule, a plan, or a request says". A note referred to "Sense 2" by number and now names the sense.
- ***concerning*** (preposition): the definition joined two definitions with a semicolon, and the explanation called *about* "too casual" for formal writing. Both were fixed.
- ***surround*** in *the mystery surrounding her disappearance* was defined as making something "seem more uncertain or interesting", which is not always true. It is now "be present around an event, a situation, or a person, as feelings, questions, or talk about it". Its core idea and signpost were simplified.
- ***enclosed*** had two senses but a signpost (the short label that helps a reader pick a sense) on only one. The missing one was added.
- ***feature***, ***opposed***: small definition fixes.
- ***opposition*** and ***contact***: the American transcriptions had a length mark on the vowel of *hot*. The house leaves length marks out of American transcriptions, so they were removed, and both were checked again and verified.

**Rejected:** reviewer-a's request for a plural of *opposition*, since both senses in the entry are uncountable or singular only. Also rejected: a request to repeat the *formal* label on a sense when the whole entry already carries it.

While running the cross-reference tool, the run also applied eight back-links from older entries to this session's verbs *remove*, *remain*, and *require*. Back-links are links pointing back from one entry to another that already names it. Examples are *add* and *install* as opposites of *remove*, and *keep* and *continue* as synonyms of *remain*. All eight were checked and are on the right senses.
