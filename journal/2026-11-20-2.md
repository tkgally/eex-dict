# Report for 2026-11-20 (second run)

## Cycle 1: closure

**What the cycle did.** The scheduler (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose a closure cycle. "Closure" means writing entries for words the dictionary already relies on, so that a reader who follows a link finds an entry rather than a grey, unlinked word. The first source of such words, words used inside definitions, is empty for now, so the cycle took the next source: "family" words, which are related forms that existing entries list in their word-family box (for example, *swimmer* is listed by *swim*). Twelve nouns were written, all in frequency band 2 (common but not core): *sunlight*, *supporter*, *surroundings*, *survivor*, *suspect*, *sweatshirt*, *swim*, *swimmer*, *tag*, *takeout*, *teaching*, and *teammate*.

**How they were written.** I wrote every definition, example, and note myself in this session. To save typing the long, repetitive JSON layout, I put my wording into a short throwaway helper in my scratch folder (outside the repository) that only arranged it into the entry file format; later fixes after review went in the same way, one entry and one field at a time, each chosen by me. No script generated or changed any wording. The helper is not committed.

Some points of interest in the new entries:

- *surroundings* is always plural, and the entry carries a learner error for the common mistake *the surrounding is very quiet*.
- *suspect* (the noun) has a note for translators that the noun is stressed on the first syllable and the verb on the second, plus the phrase *the usual suspects*.
- *tag* has five senses: a label, a word added to something online, the children's chasing game, a name painted on a wall, and the short question at the end of a sentence (*isn't it?*).
- *takeout* is labeled American, with the British form *takeaway* recorded beside it.
- *supporter* has a second sense for a sports-team follower, labeled British because Americans usually say *fan*.

**Pronunciation.** All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, three or four models voting, with the open CMU pronouncing dictionary as a further check where it has the word. For *teammate* the CMU dictionary disagreed with the American form (it likely runs the two m sounds together), but three of four models agreed, which meets the project's rule.

**What the reviewers found and what was decided.** Two reviewer models from different companies read every field of every entry. They raised 30 issues, 4 of them "blocking" (serious enough that the entry cannot be published until settled). I applied 27 and rejected 3, each decision logged with a reason.

The real catches:

- *swim* (noun) and *swimmer* were defined with the verb *swim* itself, which the style guide forbids because a reader who does not know the word learns nothing. Both were rewritten in other words.
- The one-sentence summary at the top of *tag* described the children's game as "a touch", but the entry defines it as the game itself. The summary was rewritten.
- *suspect* had an example about software causing a computer fault under a sense about people the police suspect. That use now has its own sub-sense (*a thing that people think may be the cause of a problem*).
- An example in *teaching* named a religious figure; it was replaced with a neutral one.
- *takeout* was marked as countable; Americans do not say *a takeout* for a meal, so it is now uncountable and has no plural.
- Smaller fixes: *teammate* now says *on the same team* (the American form), *supporter* covers supporting a person as well as an idea, *survivor* now covers violence from another person, and *tag* covers metal tags such as those on a pet's collar.

Rejected: a British label for the grammar sense of *tag* (the word is used everywhere; only the compounds *question tag* and *tag question* differ, and both are shown); a separate sense for a takeout restaurant (Americans say *a takeout place*, shown among the typical word combinations); and a claim that *get used to your surroundings* is not a verb + noun combination.

**Links fixed by hand.** The cross-reference tool links an entry to the matching sense of the word it points at, and it could not decide three. I placed them myself: *supporter* now points to *fan* the person (a separate entry from *fan* the machine, which it had wrongly pointed to), and *environment* and *label* now point back to *surroundings* and *tag*.

The validator caught the first; I caught the second on rereading. Neither reached the reviewers.
