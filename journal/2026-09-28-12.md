# 2026-09-28 (12) — Review: ten early verbs, *assume* to *board*

## What this run did

This was the fourth and last cycle of the scheduled run that began at 12:44 UTC. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request, merged before the next begins; a run may have at most four.) The first three cycles, #118 to #120, had already merged.

The mode selector chose **review** again: a second reading by the reviewer panel (two outside language models that check every field) of entries read only once. The next oldest were ten verbs written on 2026-09-22: *assume, attack, bake, beat, believe, belong, bite, bleed, blow,* and *board*.

Like the previous cycle's verbs, these were thin, and several had real mistakes. Before the panel, I changed by hand:

- **Wrong cross-references.** *beat* listed *break* as a synonym, and in its "defeat" sense it listed *destroy*. Both are gone. *belong* had *go* as a synonym of "be owned by". It now sits under the sense "be in the right place" (*the plates go in the top cupboard*).
- **Missing senses.** These are uses a learner meets often:
  - *assume* a form or a false name
  - *attack* in sport, and *attack* a piece of work
  - *bake* for being very hot (*I'm baking in here!*)
  - *beat the traffic*
  - *believe in* something as good (*I believe in hard work*)
  - *belong with*
  - *bite* for a fish taking the food
  - *bleed* a radiator
  - *blow* for being moved by the wind, and for explosions
  - *board* for *your flight is now boarding*, and for looking after animals for pay
- **New phrases.** *beat it*, *it beats me*, *beat someone to it*, *you can't beat something*, *believe it or not*, *believe me*, and *I don't believe it!*
- **Learner errors.** These are common mistakes:
  - *We won the other team* (you **beat** a team)
  - *I baked some rice* (rice is **cooked**)
  - *The wind blowed*
  - *He beated me*
  - *A dog attacked to my son*
  - *This bag is belonging to Sam*
- **House-style fixes.** Several definitions began with a subject restriction such as "(of a tire)". House style puts that in the explanation field, so it moved there. Several notes showed a wrong form in italics, and those were rewritten. *assume*'s British pronunciation lacked its length mark; I corrected it, and the pronunciation check verified the new transcription.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 63 issues, almost all from the first reviewer, and this time most were right. I applied 49 and rejected 14.

What I accepted:

- **Definitions that did not cover their own examples.** *bake* "become hard" did not cover *bake clay hard*. *blow* "send air at something" did not cover the wind as subject or *blowing bubbles*, which now has its own subsense. *blow* money "by making a mistake" did not fit *she blew her savings on a car*. *bleed* "a pipe or a heating system" did not fit the brakes example. *board* "be ready for passengers" misread *now boarding*. All are rewritten.
- **bite.** The fish sense and the informal use (*I offered a discount, and they bit*) are now split, and the second carries the label "informal". The phrase *what's biting you?* was removed. The usual idiom is *what's eating you?*, and the reviewer was right that ours is marginal. *bite someone's head off* no longer claims that the person did nothing wrong.
- **Smaller fixes**: grammar-pattern labels on passive examples, and two phrase labels.

What I rejected:

- Two claims that the American transcriptions of *beat* and *bleed* (written with *i*) are wrong. House style drops the length mark in American transcriptions.
- A claim that you blow on something only to cool it. Blowing on cold hands warms them, and the entry has that example.
- Removing the "informal" label from idioms such as *bite the bullet*. They would sound out of place in a formal report.
