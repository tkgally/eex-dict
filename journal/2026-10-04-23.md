# 2026-10-04 (23): twelve new nouns, *issue* to *juice*

## What this cycle did

The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue of words still to be written. The next twelve core nouns in queue order were *issue, item, jacket, jail, job, joint, joke, journey, joy, judge, judgment*, and *juice*. All twelve went through the full pipeline (format check, word forms, pronunciation check, review by two outside language models, my reading of every objection, a check of the words used in definitions, a check of links) and are marked `reviewed`. The dictionary now has 1,324 entries.

The fullest is *job*. It has five senses (paid work, a task, a duty, a difficult thing to do, and informally a crime such as *a bank job*) and six phrases (*good job*, *on the job*, *out of a job*, *it's a good job*, *just the job*, *make the best of a bad job*). It also has a note on choosing between *job*, *work*, and *task*, and two learner errors: *I'm looking for a work* and *make your job*. *Issue* has four senses and four phrases (*at issue*, *take issue with*, *make an issue of*, *force the issue*), with a warning for translators that a similar-looking word in some languages means *exit*. *Judgment* records the British spelling *judgement*, and *jail* the old British spelling *gaol*. *Jail* also explains the American difference between a jail (short stays) and a prison (long ones).

Before review, five definitions had to change because they used words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of about 2,400 plain words that definitions must use, unless the word has its own entry): *magazine* and *stamps* in *issue*, *cigarette* in *joint*, and *critical* and *criticize* in two phrases of *judgment*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 37 points, 26 marked blocking. I accepted 25 and rejected 12; each reason is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. Among the useful corrections:

- *Issue*: my newspaper sense said "the copies that come out at the same time"; an issue is one particular printing (*this week's issue*), and the definition now says so. The summary sentence at the top of the entry was also rewritten so that it fits every sense.
- *Jail*: the note on *in jail* said it always means punishment; people are also held there while waiting to go to court. Fixed in two places.
- *Joint*: the informal "place" sense no longer says the place must be cheap. The drug sense now names cannabis in its explanation; the definition itself cannot, because the word has no entry.
- *Judge*: the court sense implied every judge sentences criminals; punishment is now only an example.

Two disputes were settled by open data rather than by me. Reviewer-a said *jacket* is not the skin of a potato, and that informal *juice* does not mean car fuel. The open dictionary Wiktionary, checked at run time, lists both: *jacket* as "the tough outer skin of a baked potato", and *juice* as "petrol; gasoline" beside "electricity". Both senses stay. Two label objections were rejected under a standing house rule: a region label means *mainly* used there, so *good job* (as praise) stays American and *have a job doing something* stays British, though both are heard elsewhere.
