The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **build**: new entries taken from the queue in order.

I claimed and drafted the next twelve band-1 words (band 1 is the most basic level of the dictionary's word list). All are nouns: *ball, band, basis, basket, bath, bathroom, battery, battle, beach, bean, beard,* and *beat*. I aimed to make them as full as the first cycle's entries: every current sense, collocations (words that typically go together), idioms, and learner errors (mistakes learners of many languages make, each with a correction and a one-line reason) where they help.

Some points worth knowing:

- *ball* has five idioms, including *on the ball*, *get the ball rolling*, and *the ball is in your court*. Following the project's rule on offensive words, its two vulgar senses (the body part, and *balls* meaning courage) are included and labeled *vulgar*. *Ball* meaning a formal dance party, with *have a ball*, comes from a different word. It needs a separate entry numbered as a second homograph (same spelling and part of speech, unrelated meaning). The queue cannot yet hold that numbering, so I added it to the existing note on this problem.
- *band* is one entry covering the music group, a group of people, a strip, a line of color, and a range. The senses share an old root, and splitting them would have needed the homograph numbering the queue cannot yet hold.
- *basis* uses the exceptions table (the list of irregular forms) for its plural *bases*. Its notes explain that this sounds different from the plural of *base*.
- *bath* and *bathroom* deal with the American and British differences: *take a bath* against *have a bath*, a *bath* against a *bathtub*, and *bathroom* as the polite American word for a toilet.
- *beach* has a note for translators that its vowel must stay long, because a short vowel makes a rude word.
- *battle* has a comparison of *battle*, *war*, and *fight*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The pronunciation check agreed with all 24 transcriptions. The two outside reviewers raised 41 issues, 27 of them marked blocking. One of reviewer-b's replies (on *bath*) could not be read by the tool, so I ran that review again. I decided each issue myself: 29 applied, 12 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied, among others:

- *basket*: both reviewers said the online-shop sense (*add it to your basket*) is mainly British, where American sites say *cart*. It now has a British label. The basketball sense now separates the ring from the point scored. The economics sense is now "a fixed group of goods whose total price is compared".
- *battery*: the core idea (the one-sentence summary of what the senses share) claimed every sense names a group, which was not true of the crime or the farming senses. It now names each kind. The legal sense is now touching someone in a way that hurts or upsets them without their agreement, rather than only violent contact. The farming sense now describes chickens kept in small wire boxes.
- *band*: the strip, line, and marching-band definitions were each too narrow; each was widened to fit its examples, such as *a band of rain*.
- *bathroom*: the American *toilet* sense is now defined as a room with a toilet even when there is no bath, which shows why it is kept as a separate sense.
- *beard* now includes the cheeks, *bean* names the coffee plant, and *drumbeat* is written as one word.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- That the American transcriptions of *beach* and *beat* show the wrong vowel. House style drops the length mark in American English. The project settled this long ago, and both transcriptions agree with the pronunciation panel and the open pronunciation data.
- That *drop the ball* should not carry an *American* label. A region label means *mainly* used there.
- That the American *toilet* sense of *bathroom* should be merged into the first sense. Learners translate it with a different word, which is a reason in house style for a separate sense.
