## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 53 issues, 45 of them marked blocking (meaning the entry cannot be published until I decide). I agreed with 46 and fixed the entries myself, and rejected 7 with a logged reason. None needed the owner. Reviewer-a raised almost all of them; reviewer-b raised only two.

The most useful corrections:

- *university*: I had written *go to university* and *at university*, with no article, as general English. Reviewer-a pointed out that this is British; Americans say *go to college* or *at the university*. The whole entry was rewritten so the examples, collocations, usage note, and translator notes all present the American use first and mark the article-free use as British. The second sense is now the university as an organization, not "its students and teachers".
- *value*: the same problem on a smaller scale. *The meals are good value* is British; American English says *a good value*. Both forms are now shown and labelled.
- *video*: sense 3 had mixed the old tape and the machine that played it. It is now the tape only, and the explanation names the machine (*video recorder*, *VCR*). *Video call* and *video game* were removed from the "method" sense, where they did not fit.
- *vehicle*: the definition no longer says a vehicle must have wheels or an engine.
- *use* and *user*: two usage notes overstated a rule (*any use* does not take *-ing*; *a user of the phone* is fine with an article). Both were corrected.

What I rejected: reviewer-a wanted an Australian label on *unit* meaning an apartment. In American English *rental unit* and *a building with forty units* are ordinary housing language, so the sense stays unlabelled, with an explanation that it belongs to renting and selling homes. I also rejected two style and grammar objections that went against house style.
