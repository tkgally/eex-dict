## What this cycle did

The scheduler chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue (the list of words still to be written). It took the next twelve band-1 nouns in queue order. Band 1 is the dictionary's "very common" group, the words every learner meets first. Some notes on the drafting:

- *point* is the biggest of the twelve. It has nine senses (an idea, the main idea, purpose, a moment, a place, a unit of score, a sharp end, a quality, and the decimal point) and nine idioms, among them *point of view*, *to the point*, *up to a point*, and *point taken*. Its learner errors cover *no point to wait* and writing *2,5* for *2.5*. A note for translators explains that English writes a point where many languages write a comma.
- *beside the point* was stored under the preposition *beside*. The house keyword rule files an idiom under its first noun, here *point*, so I moved it there.
- *pool* holds only the water senses: a swimming pool, and a pool of liquid on a surface. The *pool* of shared people or money (*a pool of drivers*) and the table game come from a different word with a different origin. The house rule gives such a word its own entry (a *homograph*, here `pool-n-2`). The queue cannot list homographs yet, so it joins the others waiting on that fix in `NEXT.md`.
- *policy* and *politics* each carry a false-friend warning for translators: in many languages one word covers both.
- *possibility* compares itself with *chance* and *opportunity*, and has a learner error for *I had the possibility to study abroad*.

All twenty-four transcriptions (American and British for each word) passed the pronunciation check, and the open pronunciation dictionary agreed with every American one. The plural forms came from the rules. *politics* is marked as having no plural.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 34 issues, 26 of them blocking. I applied 25 and rejected 9. None needed to go to you.

Applied, among others:

- *player*: both reviewers said *drum player* is unnatural (people say *drummer*). It is now *bass player*.
- *pocket*: the money sense (*out of your own pocket*) is reworded. A mention of *pocket money* that did not belong there is gone. The idiom *out of pocket* (having lost money) now carries a British label, and its note says that in American English it often means "away and impossible to contact". My learner error used a sentence that is actually correct, so it is replaced by a real one: *She put her hands in the pockets* for *in her pockets*.
- *possibility*: my note said the word is never followed by a to-infinitive. That is too strong, so the rule now applies only to the "may happen" sense.
- *plug*: the bath-plug sense no longer says the plug is always round and made of rubber or plastic.
- *pool*: the "liquid on a surface" sense no longer says the pool is always small.
- *point*: two grammar explanations were loosened, and an idiom example was rewritten in plainer tense.
- *port*: the saying *any port in a storm* is now defined in the house pattern for sayings (*used to say that...*).

Rejected, among others:

- reviewer-b asked for a separate figurative sense of *poison* (*Jealousy is poison to a friendship*). The style guide does not split a use that any reader understands from the first sense; it stays in the usage note.
- reviewer-b asked for the shared-group sense in *pool*. That is the unrelated homograph described above.
- reviewer-b again said a phrase definition (*pull the plug on something*) must be a base-form verb phrase. It already is.
- reviewer-a wanted the *formal* label removed from the job and opinion senses of *position*. I kept it: *position* for a job is more formal than *job*.

The back-link tool then added five matching links on existing entries (for example *position* as a synonym on *job*). I checked each one; all fit their senses.
