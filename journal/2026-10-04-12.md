## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 35 issues, 19 of them "blocking" (they must be settled before an entry stays published). I applied 14 and rejected 21, each with a logged reason.

What I changed:

- **weigh**: the entry said this verb is not used in continuous tenses when it means *be a particular weight*. Reviewer-a pointed out that the continuous is possible when the weight is changing (*the baby is weighing more each week*). The usage note, the learner-error note, and the translator note now all say so. The basic rule (*I weigh sixty kilos*, not *I am weighing sixty kilos*) stays.
- **work**: sense 8 mixed two meanings, shaping a material such as clay and farming land. They are now a sense and a sub-sense. Two synonyms for "a machine works" were removed, *go* and *run*, which fit only some machines.
- **wonder**: *wonder at something* was defined as thinking that something is very good. It now means being very surprised, often with admiration, since people also wonder at strange or bad things.
- **whisper**, **wipe**, **wear**: the sense of spreading a story secretly, the sense of removing something from your memory, and one learner-error example were all made more accurate.

When a review removes a cross-reference, the house rule says its mirror must go too, or the next lint pass re-creates it. So removing *go* and *run* from *work* and *take* from *win* also meant editing *go*, *run*, and *take*.

What I rejected:

- **Eleven** flags from reviewer-b objected to the quoted headword that opens a translator note. House style allows it; this is a standing rejection, and that kind of flag is waiting on your ruling.
- A claim that British *work* does not share the vowel of *bird* and *her*. It does.
- A claim that *everything she could wish for* is a different grammar pattern from *wish for something*. It is the same verb and preposition, with the preposition left at the end.
- A request to move the greeting *good afternoon* to its own entry. Greetings stay as phrases of their noun, as *good evening* already does.
