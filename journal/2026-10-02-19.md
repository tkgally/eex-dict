# 2026-10-02 (20:44 UTC run, cycle 3): twelve new nouns, *death* to *democracy*

## What this run did

This was the third cycle of the run that started at 20:44 UTC. (A "cycle" is one complete unit of work with its own pull request, a proposed change that is checked automatically and then merged. Cycle 1 reviewed twelve verbs again (#190); cycle 2 was a lint pass, a check of the whole dictionary and its notes (#191).) The mode selector (the script that chooses each cycle's kind of work) picked **build**: writing new entries from the queue of words still to be written.

The queue gave the next twelve core nouns in order: *death, debate, debt, decade, decision, decrease, defense, definition, degree, delay, demand,* and *democracy*. Each entry has its senses with definitions, examples, grammar, and typical word combinations, plus learner errors, a usage note, and notes for translators where they help. Together they hold sixteen idioms, placed by the keyword rule (an idiom goes under the first noun in it). Among them: *bored to death*, *at death's door*, *be the death of someone*, *open to debate*, *be in someone's debt*, *on the decrease*, *come to someone's defense*, *in someone's defense*, *by definition*, *give someone the third degree*, *without delay*, *in demand*, and *on demand*.

Some points a learner needs that the entries now make:

- *death* is the noun, *dead* the adjective, and *die* the verb (*my grandfather is dead*, not *is death*).
- The *b* in *debt* is silent. You *run up* debts; you do not *make* them.
- *since a decade* should be *for a decade*.
- In American English you *make* a decision; *take a decision* is also British.
- A university degree is *in* a subject (*a degree in economics*).
- *democracy* is a noun; before a noun use *democratic* (*a democratic country*).
- *demand* looks like words in some other languages that just mean *ask*, but in English it is much stronger.

One idiom, *sign your own death warrant*, stays in the entry for *sign*. An earlier run decided to move it under *warrant* when that entry is written, and I kept to that.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 43 issues. Twenty-three were *blocking*, meaning each had to be decided before the entries could be marked as reviewed. I applied 31 and rejected 12, and none went to the owner. Every decision is in `reviews/decisions.jsonl` with its reason.

The most important correction was to *delay*. I had written that a person who arrives late should never say *sorry for my delay*, and I gave it as a learner error. The reviewer pointed out that *sorry for the delay* is normal English, for example when answering a message slowly. I removed the learner error. The usage note and the note for translators now say that *delay* is used *mainly* for trains, flights, plans, and replies, and that a person who arrives after the agreed time is usually *late*.

Other changes I applied:

- *degree*: the university sense now puts the title first and the course of study second. I also took out *do a degree*, which is mainly British.
- *defense*: the military sense now means the protection of a country, not just its soldiers and ships.
- *decade*: the second sense now refers to the named decades (*the nineties*) instead of a disputed rule about which year a decade starts.
- *democracy* now says the people hold power, usually by voting, not only that they vote.
- *debate*: a formal debate no longer has to "end with a vote".
- The definitions of two idioms, *open to debate* and *by definition*, were made more exact.

Most rejections were the second reviewer's repeated objection to a settled house rule: a note for translators may open with the headword in quotation marks.

One automatic back-link was put in the wrong place. A back-link is a cross-reference the tools add so that both entries point to each other. This one listed *debate* as a synonym of *argument* in the sense of an angry quarrel; I moved it to the sense of disagreement about what is true.
