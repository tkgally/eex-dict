# 2026-10-22, cycle 3 of the 00:44 UTC run: the forty-second originality check

## What this cycle did

This was an **originality** cycle. Every tenth run, the scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) forces a check that our definitions are our own and not copied from a published dictionary. The tool `tools/originality_check.py` picked ten fields at random from the 2,687 entries. For each, I ran an exact-phrase web search and looked only at whether a published dictionary shows the same wording. I also asked one of the outside reviewer models (reviewer-a) whether each definition reads as copied. The completed checklist is in `reviews/originality/2026-10-22.md`.

## Results

- **Original (6):** the *human*/*humane* usage note on `human-adj`; `stone-n` (a stone in the body); `set-up-phrv` (give someone what they need to start out); `club-v` (go out dancing); the phrase *take years off someone* in `year-n`; and `rubber-n`. The searches found nothing close: health pages, charity pages, nightlife guides, and so on.
- **Generic overlap (1):** `nor-conj`, *and also not*. A children's dictionary gives *and not*. This is the shortest possible gloss and any dictionary would write it that way, so it stays.
- **Rewrite (3):** none of these matched word for word, but each followed the shape of a learner's dictionary definition too closely, so I rewrote them in my own words:
  - `simply-adv` sense 3 (*explain it simply*, *simply furnished*) followed a learner's dictionary's pairing of *easy to understand* with *plain*. Now: *in a plain way that is clear to understand, or with only the things that are really needed*.
  - `race-n` sense 1 followed a learner's dictionary's frame of a competition between people, animals, and vehicles to see which is fastest. Now: *an event in which people, animals, or vehicles try to be the first to reach the end of a set distance*.
  - `drunk-adj` sense 2 (*drunk with power*) had no web match, but its pairing of *a feeling* and *power* is close to the usual frame for this sense, so I rewrote it as a precaution.

A rewrite counts as a review of the entry, so the three entries went through the reviewer panel again. It raised three issues. I applied one: reviewer-a said my first rewrite of *drunk with power* (*made too proud or too happy... so that you stop thinking clearly*) was too narrow, so it is now *full of a strong, happy feeling such as pride or pleasure, because of power, success, or something similar*. I rejected two that did not concern the rewrites: that *a race against time* conflicts with the grammar label *singular only* (it does not: the label allows *a*), and that *used to make a statement stronger* in `simply-adv` sense 2 is padding (that formula is allowed for words like intensifiers).

## The reviewer's answers

reviewer-a answered *cannot tell* on all ten, without naming any source. That is better than in recent checks, when it often answered *copied* and "quoted" our own wording back as if it were Oxford's. Its first reply, with its hidden reasoning switched on, used up all its allowed length on reasoning and returned no text; the second call, with reasoning off, worked. As before, the web search alone decides the verdicts. The pattern is recorded in `wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`.
