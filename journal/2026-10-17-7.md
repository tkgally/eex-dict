# 2026-10-17, cycle 3 of the 12:44 UTC run: *confirm* and eleven verbs, *document* to *educate*

## What this cycle did

This was a **closure** cycle. Closure means writing entries for words the dictionary already uses but does not yet define, so that a reader can follow every link. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose it as the most overdue kind of work.

The first source of closure words gave only one row: *confirm*, used in the definition of *indeed*. The queue had guessed it was a noun, but *confirm* has no noun use. I marked that row as a duplicate, queued the verb, and wrote it. The second source, family words (forms related to an existing entry), gave eleven verbs in queue order: *document, dog, double, drug, drum, duck, dull, ease, economize, edge, educate*. Each has a noun or adjective entry already, such as *dog* (the animal), *edge*, and *education*.

Every entry went through the full pipeline:

- the format check;
- inflections from the rules (all regular, with doubled consonants in *dogged*, *drugged*, and *drummed*);
- the pronunciation check;
- both outside reviewers;
- my decisions on each issue;
- the vocabulary and cross-reference checks.

All twelve are now `reviewed`. *Economize* lists the British spelling *economise* as a variant. *Educate* warns about a common confusion: in many languages the same word means bringing up a child with good manners, which in English is *bring up* or *raise*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

reviewer-a raised 24 issues and reviewer-b 10. Two entries drew none (*dog*, *economize*). I applied 25 and rejected 9.

Applied, among others:

- My *duck* entry said the verb was not related in meaning to the bird. Both reviewers said this was wrong, and they were right; the sentence is gone. It was also a claim about origins, which belongs only in the etymology field.
- Phrases like *well documented* and *well educated* had been listed as *adverb + verb* collocations. The house rule puts a participle phrase under the phrase type (*be well documented*), so they moved.
- The church sense of *confirm* now reads *accept someone as an adult member of a Christian church at a special religious service*. The draft had said *full member*, which is not true of every church.
- *Drum* is no longer limited to a regular sound made with fingers or sticks. *Drum up* is now glossed as getting support *by making an effort*, not *by working hard*.
- An example that used *documented* as an adjective was replaced. The note on how the verb and the noun *document* sound different now says *usually*, not *always*.
- *Double*: the definition now covers *twice as many* for the transitive use. A learner error that was not really an error came out.

Rejected:

- reviewer-b asked four times for a bare intransitive pattern on verbs that can take an object or none. The closed list of patterns has no such value; the transitivity field already says it.
- reviewer-b also called two patterns from the closed list invalid.
- On *drum*, the remark that the subject is usually rain or feet stays in the explanation, where the house rules put subject restrictions.
- *Double as* can have a person as subject (*the coach doubles as the driver*).
- Two objections on *edge* were rejected: one contradicted itself, and the other was to the house style that a core idea is a full sentence.

## My own checks

Three definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary (*definite*, *ceremony*, *slip*), so I reworded them. Twelve back-links were added to existing entries. One landed on the wrong sense: *duck* went under *bend* meaning *change shape*. I moved it by hand to *bend* meaning *move your body*. The queue also guessed *sideways* as a noun from the *edge* definition; it is an adverb and already queued, so that row is marked as a duplicate.

## Pronunciation

Twenty-two of the twenty-four transcriptions were verified. The British forms of *document* (verb) and *educate* did not reach agreement. My first drafts used a weak middle vowel. When I changed them to match the existing entries *document* (noun) and *education*, one panel model agreed and the other two gave different answers. I kept the forms that match the existing entries. Both are flagged `pronunciation-unverified`, and the question is in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`.
