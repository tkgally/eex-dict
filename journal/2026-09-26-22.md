# 2026-09-26 (twenty-second run): a second reading of 18 older entries

## What this run did

This was the third cycle of this session, and a **review** run. A review run writes no new entries. It sends existing entries back to the two reviewer models for a second reading. Entries written in the dictionary's first week were read by the reviewers only once, and a second round often finds problems the first missed. About 390 entries still have only one round.

The block was the next 18 one-round entries by creation date:

- nine prepositions from 2026-09-20: *inside, like, minus, near, onto, outside, past, plus, since*
- nine band-1 words from the same evening: *able, accept, account, actual, add, adult, anxious, apologize, awake*

## What the reviewers found and what was decided

The reviewers raised 37 issues. Each has a logged decision: 21 fixes applied and 16 rejected. Five entries (*near, able, accept, awake*, and most of *past*) came back with nothing or almost nothing to fix.

Fixes applied:

- **account** (noun), the online sense, was defined as "a way to use a website". It now reads "an arrangement with a website or a computer service that lets you use it as a named user, usually with a password".
- **apologize** was defined as saying sorry "for something wrong or unkind that you have done". That does not fit one of its own examples, *The company apologized for the delay*. It now covers things that caused a problem too.
- **since** (preposition): its explanation of tenses now covers the past perfect correctly. The period can last up to now, or up to a later point in the past (*The team had not lost a game since the spring*). Its link to **ago** now carries a note on the difference.
- **onto**: the usage note had called *on to*, written as two words, a mainly British spelling. The real rule is different: two words are used when *on* belongs to the verb, as in *she went on to win* or *hold on to the rail*. The note now says so. The sense *be onto someone* (know their secret) gained a short explanation of its pattern.
- **add** listed *lend* and *calculate* as synonyms of its first sense, "put something together with another thing". Both reviewers objected. *Lend* is not a synonym and was removed, both from *add* and from the matching link in *lend*. *Calculate* was moved to the arithmetic sense.
- **like** and **such as** each listed the other twice, as a synonym and as a word to compare. The duplicate was removed on both sides, and the useful note was kept on the synonym link.
- **outside**: *within* moved from the physical sense to the sense about limits (*outside business hours*), where it is the true opposite. That sense's signpost, the short label that helps a reader pick a sense, was shortened to "beyond a limit".
- **actual**: the learner error *What is the actual population of the city?* is in fact natural English, since it can contrast the real number with an estimate. It was replaced by a sentence where *current* is clearly meant.
- Smaller fixes: a more natural example for *anxious* (*They gave the darkening sky an anxious look*), a softer usage note on *like* before a clause, and the biology label removed from *adult* (animal).

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Reviewer-a said four examples of *plus* and *outside* were not prepositions. Each one has a noun phrase after the word (*outside the theater*, *two plus two*), which is exactly the preposition use. The dictionary's *minus* entry treats arithmetic the same way.
- Reviewer-a said the *since* notes were too categorical, citing *It is years since I saw him*. That *since* is a conjunction, which has its own entry, and the notes already say "usually".
- Reviewer-b asked for a "below zero" sense of *minus* (*minus ten degrees*). That use is an adjective, not this preposition entry.
