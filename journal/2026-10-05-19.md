## What this cycle did

The scheduler chose **review**: a second reading of entries that the outside reviewers have seen only once. I did two things.

**1. The owner's ruling on helping verbs, second part.** On 2026-10-05 the owner ruled that *be*, *have*, and *do* used as helping verbs ("auxiliary verbs") should each get their own entry, one per review cycle. *Be* was done this afternoon. This cycle did *have*.

The new entry `have-aux` has three senses:

- the perfect tenses (*I have finished*, *They had already left*, *Have you ever seen snow?*). The explanation says that the present perfect links the past with now, so it is not used with a finished time such as *yesterday*.
- after *could*, *should*, *would*, *must*, *might*, or *to*, talking about the past (*You should have told me*, *She must have missed the bus*, *I'm sorry to have kept you waiting*).
- the formal use at the start of a clause instead of *if* (*Had I known you were coming, I would have cooked more*), labeled formal.

It also has:

- a usage note on its forms, questions, negatives, and short answers, and on the short forms *'s* and *'d*, which can also stand for *is* and *would*;
- three learner errors: *I have seen him yesterday*, *You should of told me*, and *I have lived here since five years*;
- a pronunciation note on the weak forms;
- a note for translators on the present perfect.

The verb entry `have-v` no longer has its perfect-tense sense. Its summary sentence, usage note, translator notes, pronunciation note, and "see also" list now point to the new entry, and the new entry points back. No other entry depended on the removed sense. `do-aux` is next, in a later review cycle.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

I logged 44 decisions: 32 changes applied, 12 rejected, none sent to the owner.

Useful catches:

- *bill*: three fields said Americans ask for "the check" in a restaurant and British speakers for "the bill", as if each side used only one. Americans also often say *bill*, so all three now say so. The law sense now covers a change to an existing law as well as a new one.
- *birthday*: the entry treated a company's or a school's "birthday" as an odd, friendly use and pushed readers to *anniversary*. It is ordinary English, so the definition, the usage note, and the translator note now allow it, and *anniversary* is described as the more formal word.
- *bit*: *love someone to bits* is mainly British and is now labeled so. A sentence about where *champing at the bit* comes from was moved out of the explanation, since the house rule keeps origins in the etymology field only.
- *birth*: the translator note had a wrong form in italics, which the house rule does not allow. It was rewritten, and the learner-error note now says the verb *birth* exists but belongs mainly to medical or formal writing.
- *bird*: the learner error *a bird crying* was not really an error, so I replaced it with a common one: birds fly *in* the sky, not *on* it.
- *have-v*: two back-links (from *make* and *take*, about *have a party* and *have a shower*) were on the "own" sense. I moved them to the "do or experience" sense.
- *have-aux*: reviewer B was right that the plain form *have* in examples does not need a hand mark (the website marks it); *has* and *had* keep theirs.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Reviewer A said that *to have met* and *having finished* are not uses of the helping verb. They are: they are the same perfect *have* in its non-finite forms.
- Reviewer B asked for the forms of *have-aux* in the inflections table. The inflection tool writes no forms for helping verbs, as with *be-aux*; the usage note lists them.
- Reviewer B asked to move *a bit* (meaning *slightly*) to an adverb entry. The house rule keeps idioms under their key word whatever their part of speech.
- Reviewer A said *the best bit* is not British. The region label means *mainly used there*, and American speakers usually say *part*.

Reviewer B again returned only part of its answer on five of the ten entries (*have-aux*, *bike*, *birthday*, *bit*, *blade*). *Have-aux* was still partial after one re-run. I worked on the issues it did send.
