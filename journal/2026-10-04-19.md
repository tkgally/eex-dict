# 2026-10-04 (19): second reading of twelve entries, *answer* to *attitude*

*Third cycle of the run that started at 16:44 UTC on 4 October 2026.*

## What this cycle did

The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that have been through the reviewers only once. The queue for this work runs oldest first, and the oldest such entries were twelve nouns written on 30 September: *answer, appearance, area, argument, arm, army, arrest, art, artist, attempt, attention*, and *attitude*.

Before sending them back to the two outside reviewing models, I read each one looking for what a learner would miss: a common sense, a fixed phrase, a typical mistake. Most were already full (*art* has five senses, *arm* four idioms such as *cost an arm and a leg*). I added two phrases by hand: *in answer to something* (as in *I'm writing in answer to your letter*) to *answer*, and *arm in arm* to *arm*. I also added *to all appearances* to *appearance*, but a reviewer pointed out that the entry already shows it under its sense *the way something seems*, with an example and in the list of common word combinations; it was a duplicate, so I took it out again.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 23 points, 10 of them marked blocking. I accepted 12 and rejected 11; each decision and its reason is in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Accepted, among others:

- *Attention*: the entry said the noun is "never" countable. The reviewer noted the plural *attentions* (kind or romantic acts toward someone, as in *his unwanted attentions*). It is rare and formal, but it exists, so the usage note and the note for translators now say "uncountable in ordinary use" and name the plural.
- *Attitude*: the one-sentence summary of the senses at the top of the entry (the "core idea") described an attitude as "a way of holding yourself toward the world", which is a figure of speech a learner may not follow. It now says plainly: the way you think and feel about something, a rude way of behaving, or the position of your body. A note for translators that made a claim about how other languages translate the word was rewritten to say only what the sense means.
- *Area*: the phrase *in the area of* (*in the area of two hundred guests*) was defined as "about; close to a number", which is a list of synonyms rather than a definition. It now reads *close to a particular number or amount, a little more or a little less*.
- *Art*: the skill sense said "an activity that needs such a skill", which was too broad; it now says "an activity that needs special skill". *Artist* gained word combinations for its "skilled person" sense (*an artist with words*, *a real artist*). *Attempt* now lists *survive an attempt on someone's life* instead of the vaguer *survive an attempt*.

Rejected, among others: six objections to notes that begin with the headword in quotation marks, which the house style allows; a claim that *often before another noun* is not an allowed grammar code (it is on the closed list); a claim that *in the area of* always means "somewhat more" than the number (it means roughly, either way); and the suggestion that *cardiac arrest* should not be explained as the main use of the medical sense of *arrest*.
