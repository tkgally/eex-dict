## What was built

Twelve nouns from the first frequency band (the most common words): *fee*, *feeling*, *fence*, *festival*, *fever*, *field*, *fight*, *figure*, *file*, *film*, *finance*, and *finger*. Each was written by hand to the style guide, as full as the entries of the first cycle. Some highlights:

- **field** has eight senses, from farm land and sports grounds to a subject of study (*an expert in her field*), an oil field, a space on a form, all the runners in a race, and work *in the field*. Its idioms include *a level playing field* and *out of left field*.
- **feeling** has six senses and now holds the idioms *hurt someone's feelings* and *no hard feelings*. `NEXT.md` had asked for *hurt someone's feelings* to live here, because the house rule puts an idiom under its first noun.
- **figure** separates a number, a written digit, a person (*a public figure*), body shape, a numbered picture in a book, a small model, and a shape in mathematics. A note warns translators that in some languages a look-alike word means the face.
- **fee** has a note on choosing between **fee**, **price**, **charge**, **fare**, and **cost**, a common problem for learners.
- **finger** has five idioms, from *keep your fingers crossed* to *wrap someone around your little finger*.

**file** has the same problem as **fan** in the second cycle: *file* the tool for making nails smooth is an unrelated word with the same spelling. It needs its own entry (`file-n-2`), and was added to the list in `wiki/notes/homograph-queue-and-inflection.md` of words waiting for a hand claim.

All 24 pronunciations were confirmed by the pronunciation panel (three outside models compared with each other and with the open CMU pronouncing dictionary).

## What the panel found and what I decided

The reviewer panel (two outside models that read every field) raised 34 issues, 22 of them blocking. I applied 17 and rejected 17, each logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Applied, among others:

- **finger**: *fish fingers* is British; the American food is *fish sticks*. The example and collocation now use *fingers of toast*, which work everywhere. The main definition now leaves out the thumb, and the usage note explains that the thumb is sometimes counted.
- **figure**: *a six-figure salary* uses *six-figure* as an adjective, so it was the wrong example for the noun sense *a written digit*. It was replaced.
- **film**: camera film was described as belonging to *older cameras*; some cameras still use it, and the definition now says so.
- **fee**: a *charge* is the amount asked, not always money already paid.
- **field**: the computing label came off the *space on a form* sense, since paper forms have fields too.

Rejected:

- Six flags from one reviewer on **file**, all from one claim: that a file is never a box or other container for papers. Standard use includes this meaning, so the definition stands; only the collocation *a file folder* came out.
- Three flags on **finance** saying the entry should list the plural *finances*. The reviewers are right about English, but the inflection tool (`tools/inflect.py`) cannot record a plural when the first sense is uncountable. This is a known gap, recorded in `wiki/notes/inflect-uncountable-plural-gap.md`; this case was added to it. The examples mark *finances* by hand so the site shows it.
- Two flags claiming that **festival**'s definitions began with a capital letter (they did not); on **fight**, an objection that *a boxing match* is circular (it is not), and one that misread the purpose of the translator notes.

A cross-reference check found that *battle* names *fight* as a synonym only in its *effort* sense; the extra compare link from the *hitting* sense of *fight* was removed so the two entries agree. Back-links were added from *cost* to *fee* and from *record* to *file*.
