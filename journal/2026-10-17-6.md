# 2026-10-17, cycle 2 of the 12:44 UTC run: twelve adverbs, *soon* to *there*

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: new entries drafted from the queue (the ordered list of words still to write). The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose build because it had become the most overdue kind of work after the review cycle before it. The queue's next words in the most common frequency band (band 1) were twelve adverbs: *soon, still, straight, strangely, strongly, suddenly, sure, surely, terribly, the, then, there*. The adverb *the* is the one in *the more, the better*; the determiner *the* already has its own entry.

I drafted each entry from my own knowledge and the style guide, then ran the full pipeline:

- the format check;
- the inflection tool, which writes forms such as *sooner* from rules and a table of exceptions;
- the pronunciation check, a panel of models plus an open pronouncing dictionary;
- both outside reviewers;
- my decisions on every issue;
- the vocabulary and cross-reference checks.

All twelve entries are now `reviewed`.

Some choices worth knowing:

- ***there***: the entry includes the *there is / there are* use as one of its senses. The entry for *be* already sends readers to the adverb *there* for this pattern, so the two entries agree.
- ***soon***: the entry carries the phrases *sooner or later*, *as soon as possible*, *the sooner the better*, *would sooner*, and *no sooner ... than*. *As soon as* before a clause is a conjunction, so it was queued as its own entry.
- ***still***: the entry separates *continuing* (*It's still raining*), *even so*, and *even more* (*colder still*), and explains the choice between *still*, *yet*, and *already*. *Still* meaning *not moving* is an adjective and is already queued.
- ***sure*** and ***surely***: the entries keep apart the informal *Sure* (yes; American *It sure is cold*) and *surely*. *Surely* often asks the listener to agree (*Surely you know him?*) rather than meaning *certainly*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

reviewer-a raised 27 issues and reviewer-b 2. Only *the* drew none. I applied 25 and rejected 4.

Applied, among others:

- Three definitions written as two words joined by a semicolon were rewritten as single phrases. On *then*, *after that; next* became *after that, or next in order*. *Sure* meaning *really* is now *used to make a statement stronger*, with a clearer note on where the word goes.
- On *straight*, the sense *with no break* (*three nights straight*) lost its American label: it is common in British English too. The notes no longer say that *straightly* does not exist; they now say it is very rare.
- Doubtful cross-references were removed: *running* as a synonym of *straight*, *weakly* as an antonym of *strongly*, and *almost surely* as a collocation.
- On *there*, a learner error said *It has a bank near my house* is wrong. That sentence is fine when *it* means a town, so the error now reads *Near my house has a bank*.

Rejected:

- reviewer-b said the *there is* use belongs in a separate pronoun entry. I kept it in the adverb, for the reason above. It is a fair question of grammar, but changing it would also mean changing *be*, and the current treatment is consistent.
- reviewer-a said *suddenly* cannot take *more*. *It stopped more suddenly than I expected* is normal English.
- *Even* stays listed beside *still* (*colder still* means *even colder*), now as a word to compare rather than a synonym, which matches the *even* entry.
- reviewer-b objected to a closed-list grammar code on *strangely*.

My own fixes after the checks: two definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary (*prefer*, *upright*), so I reworded them. Ten entries gained matching back-links, such as *straight* listed under *directly* and *terribly* under *badly*. I checked that each landed on the right sense.

## Pronunciation

Twenty-two of the twenty-four transcriptions were verified. The British forms of *sure* and *surely* (said like *shore*) drew one vote in three: the other panel models gave the form that rhymes with *poor*. I kept the *shore* form to match the existing adjective *sure*, which passed with two votes in three, and listed the other as a variant. Both entries carry a `pronunciation-disputed` flag, and there is a line for the owner in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`.
