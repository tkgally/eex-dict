Some points about what went in:

- *place* is one of the most common nouns in English, and it got eight senses, from "a particular area" through a seat, a person's home, and a position in a race, to "the point you have reached in a book". It also holds eleven idioms whose main noun is *place*: *take place*, *in place*, *out of place*, *in the first place*, *fall into place*, *go places*, and others. Two common learner errors are included: *no more place in my bag* (English says *room*) and *take part* when *take place* (happen) is meant.
- *play* holds the theater sense, children's play, play in a sport, and the idioms *fair play* and *a play on words*. Its learner error is *the play of football*; a sports event is a *game* or a *match*.
- *plane* and *plain* sound the same, and the entry says so. Its learner errors match those already in *car*: *by plane*, not *with plane*, and *get on* a plane, not *in*.
- *plan* includes the common error *Do you have any plan for tonight?* (English uses the plural, *plans*).
- *pilot*, *plastic*, and *pet* (from the previous cycle) all have uses before another noun (*a pilot project*, *a plastic bag*, *a pet project*) that count as adjectives. Each noun entry points to its adjective, and the new adjective words were put in the queue.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 35 issues, 25 of them marked blocking. I applied 23 and rejected 12; none needed to go to you.

Applied, among others:

- *pill*: I had defined the contraceptive sense as a pill taken "every day"; not every kind is daily, so it now says "regularly". Its grammar was corrected to *singular only*, since it is always *the pill*.
- *pilot*: the television sense now says "a single show made to test an idea for a new series", which is more accurate than "the first show of a planned series".
- *plane*: the mathematics sense now says the flat surface is imagined as going on forever. The usage note no longer suggests that *airplane* is a more careful word in American English. Both words are ordinary there.
- *plan*: a cross-reference calling a building plan a "map" was removed. The paying-service sense (*a phone plan*) was made broader.
- *plant*: the learner error *give water to the plants* was removed. It is wordy but not wrong.
- *play*: *a play on words* can also use words that sound alike, and the definition now says so. Typical combinations were added for two senses.

Rejected, among others:

- Several label objections that went against the dictionary's closed lists of labels: *old-fashioned* is listed under how current a word is; there is no "woodworking" topic and no "impolite" attitude label to use.
- Objections to the region labels on *more's the pity* (British) and *a play* meaning one move in a sport (American). In this dictionary a region label means *mainly* used there.
- That *driver* for the person who flies a plane is not a real learner error. Speakers of languages with one verb for driving and flying do make it.
