## What this cycle did

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that have been through only one round of outside review. I took the twelve oldest:

- the verbs *hug* and *hurt*
- ten words written on 2026-09-26 to fill gaps in other entries' definitions: *achievement, border, bullet, commit, criticism, ladder, pen, pencil, eager*, and *package*

Entries written in the later cycles of long runs came out thinner, so before sending them to the reviewers I checked each one for missing senses, idioms, and learner errors, and added what was missing by hand:

- **hug** gained two senses: holding something against your body (*hugging her knees*), and clothes fitting closely (*the dress hugs her figure*). It also gained a core idea (the one-sentence summary of what the senses share), a usage note comparing *hug*, *embrace*, and *give someone a hug*, and the learner error *hugged to her mother*.
- **hurt** gained the phrase *it won't hurt to ask* (also *it wouldn't hurt you to say thank you*). It also gained a learner error about tense: *my stomach hurts since this morning* should be *has been hurting*.
- **achievement** gained *no mean achievement* and two learner errors: *a big achieve*, and *made many achievements* (say *achieved a lot*).
- **border** gained a note on choosing between *border*, *boundary*, and *frontier*.
- **criticism** gained the learner error *criticism to the plan* (say *of*).
- **pen** gained the saying *the pen is mightier than the sword*. **pencil** gained a usage note on *in pencil* and *in pen*.
- **ladder** gained a note for translators: some English speakers think walking under a ladder brings bad luck.

The idiom *bite the bullet* was listed under both *bite* and *bullet*. The house keyword rule files an idiom under its first noun, so it now stays only under *bullet*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 25 issues, 13 of them "blocking". I accepted 21 and rejected 4.

Accepted, among others:

- *Hurt* sense 2 (*my leg hurts*) was defined as "give you a feeling of pain", which is not a plain verb phrase. It is now "be painful". The example *It doesn't hurt to ask* moved out of the "harm" sense, since the new phrase covers it.
- *Criticism*'s usage note and translator note said criticism is "almost always" negative. They now say "usually" and allow balanced criticism in formal discussion.
- *Pen* was defined as having "colored liquid" inside. It now says the tool leaves a line of black, blue, or other colored liquid.
- *Pencil* had been marked uncountable because of the phrase *in pencil*. It is now countable only, and the phrase is explained in the usage note.
- The *commit* sense about sending someone to prison or hospital no longer says "usually without their agreement", and now includes sending someone to court for trial.
- My own additions had put duplicate see-also links on *pen* and *pencil*. Both reviewers caught them, and the duplicates were removed.
- The notes on *eager*/*anxious to* and *package*/*packet* were made less absolute.

Rejected:

- Two flags against the quoted headword at the start of a translator's note, on *hurt* and *criticism* (a settled house style).
- A suggestion to write the region label as "US"; the house value is "American".
- A claim that the law label does not fit the hospital sense of *commit*. That process is also legal.
