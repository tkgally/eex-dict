The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that have been through only one round of outside review. Following the plan in `NEXT.md`, I took the twelve oldest such entries by creation date, all verbs written on 2026-09-24: *count, cover, crash, create, cross, crush, cure, cut, damage, dance, date,* and *decide*.

Before sending them to the reviewers I read each one myself, looking for missing common senses, idioms, and learner errors (mistakes learners of many languages make, each shown with a correction and a one-line reason). The entries were mostly sound. What I added by hand:

- Three new senses. *Cover* now has "protect yourself" (*keep a copy of every email, just to cover yourself*). *Cut* now has "stop" (*the driver cut the engine*; *our electricity was cut*). *Dance* now has "not talk about something directly" (*stop dancing around the question*). Each entry's core idea, the one-sentence summary of what the senses share, was widened to include the new sense.
- Five new learner errors: *Does a phone call count a visit?* (needs *as*); *She created a cake* (everyday things are *made*); *We walked cross the street* (the preposition is *across*); *I cut my hair at the hairdresser's* (if someone else did it, *I had my hair cut*); and *This house is dating from the last century* (*date from* is not used in the continuous).

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field of every entry. They raised 32 issues, 15 of them marked blocking. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 12 applied, 20 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied:

- *crash*, sense 1: the definition only covered a car hitting something. It now also covers a driver crashing the car (*he crashed his father's car*).
- *cross*: the breeding sense now reads "breed two different kinds of plant or animal together", which matches the example *crossed two kinds of apple tree*, and a strange example about a dog crossed with a wolf was replaced. The sense in *don't cross him* now says "act against someone or against what they want, often making them angry"; before, it said the point was making them angry.
- *cure*: the comparison with *heal* and *treat* said *heal* is used for a wound getting better by itself. It now also shows *heal* with an object (*this cream helps heal small cuts*).
- *decide*: it pointed to the phrasal verb *make up* as a word to compare. The real expression is the idiom *make up your mind*, which belongs under the noun *mind*, so the pointer now goes there.
- Smaller fixes: *date back* with a length of time (*dates back hundreds of years*) is now named in the explanation; the collocations *cut sharply* and *cut drastically* now show the object (*cut something sharply*); an unclear example for *decide* ("Her experience decided it") was replaced; a passive example for *crush* is now marked as passive; *count for a lot* is filed as verb + preposition, not adverb + verb; the new *dance around* sense lost its *informal* label.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Two objections that a core idea should not be a sentence. House style says it is one.
- *counter* as part of *count*'s word family. Both come from the same word for counting.
- That *freeze* and *hang* are not synonyms of *crash* for computers, and *chop* not a synonym of *cut* for reducing costs. In everyday use they are close, and each of those entries already lists *crash* or *cut* as a synonym in the same sense.
- That *make something new exist* is ungrammatical. It follows the same pattern as *make it happen*.
- That *We danced with the music* is not really an error. In ordinary use it is.
- Two objections to where *cover*'s syllable break falls. The transcription itself was checked earlier by the pronunciation panel.
