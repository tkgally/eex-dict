## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Four entries drew no objections: *string*, *structure*, *substance*, and *suit*. On the other sixteen, the reviewers raised 40 issues. I accepted 21 and rejected 19. Each decision and its reason is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Accepted, with my own rewording:
- **stroke**: the illness sense now names bleeding in the brain as a second cause. The swimming and rowing sense no longer says the movements are made only with the arms, since rowing uses an oar. An example under the phrase *not do a stroke of work* now actually uses the phrase.
- **student**: the note said British English calls schoolchildren *pupils* and keeps *student* for college. That was too strong. The note now says younger children are *often* called pupils, though *student* is also used. A second copy of the same point in the sense explanation was removed.
- **study**: the sense for subject names (*media studies*) was limited to colleges. Schools teach *social studies* too, so it now says "especially at a college or university".
- **style**: *in style* was defined as "in an expensive way". It now means "in a way that people notice and admire, often with a lot of comfort or money".
- **subject**: the grammar sense now says the subject "does the action of the verb or is described by it", which also fits passive and linking sentences. The sense for a monarch's subject no longer adds "a duty to obey".
- **sugar**: the sense for the sugars found in food now also covers sugars added to food.
- **surgery**: the countable sense is now "a single operation", not only cutting the body open.
- **supply**: *bank* was listed as a synonym and *demand* as an opposite. Neither is right. A link of that kind has to be removed from both entries, so I also edited `bank-n` and `demand-n`. The two words now point to each other under "see also", with the phrase *supply and demand*.
- **success** and **suggestion**: one learner-error note and one translator note made claims that were too broad, and both are corrected.

Rejected:
- Two objections to region labels on **sugar**. In this dictionary a region label means "mainly used there", a rule settled earlier. Calling someone *sugar* is mainly American, even if it is heard elsewhere.
- That *in surgery* (*my father is still in surgery*) belongs to a different sense. It uses the uncountable noun, which is the sense where it sits.
- That *stuffy* is not in the word family of *stuff*. It is formed from *stuff* plus *-y*.
- That *a support group* is out of place under the "kindness" sense. A support group exists to give exactly that kind of support.
- That the signpost "doing what you want" on **success** breaks the three-word limit. It has three words.
- That *catch the sun* (British) only means getting sunburned. It covers going a little red or brown.
