# Report to the owner: 4 November 2026, evening run

## Cycle 1: build

**What this cycle did.** The automatic selector, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work by weighing how long each kind has waited, chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue of words still missing. It handed out twelve common nouns from band 2 (the second of five frequency bands, "common: words most intermediate learners know or need soon"), and all twelve were written, checked, and published: *chip*, *cigarette*, *cinema*, *circuit*, *circumstance*, *citizenship*, *clap*, *classmate*, *cleaner*, *cleaning*, *clerk*, and *click*. The dictionary now has 3,734 entries, every one of them reviewed. Before starting, the cycle checked for unfinished work from earlier runs (open pull requests, leftover branches, messages in the owner's inbox, and "Entry report" issues filed by readers) and found none.

**How each entry was checked.** Each entry went through the full pipeline. The validator checked the file's shape and house rules; the inflection tool generated the plural forms from rules; the pronunciation panel, three outside models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, agreed with all 24 American and British transcriptions; and two reviewers from different companies (reviewer A, a Claude model, and reviewer B, a Google model) read every field. The vocabulary check confirmed that every definition uses only words from the dictionary's defining vocabulary or words that already have entries. Three first drafts failed that check and were reworded: *cookies* in a definition of *chip*, *source* in *circuit*, and *dentist* in *cleaning*.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The reviewers raised 60 issues in all. I applied 47, rejected 13, and sent none to you. Only two were "blocking", meaning the entry could not be published until they were settled, and both were the same problem: in the computer sense of *chip*, defined as a small part inside a machine, one example described a chip put under a dog's skin. The reviewer was right. I replaced it with an example about chips made for cars and phones.

The most useful minor findings:
- *chip*: the American sense was defined as a slice of potato, but the examples showed tortilla chips. The definition now covers other foods too. The reviewer also spotted that *bargaining chip* was missing, so it is now a subsense under the gambling sense.
- *circuit*: a sense for a court district, and the regular trip a judge makes around it, was missing and has been added.
- *cleaning*: *a good cleaning* (one time, countable) was not covered. The definitions also no longer use the word *clean*, which made them partly circular.
- *clerk*: *paper work* is now avoided altogether, and the American shop sense says *store*.
- *citizenship*: an example that described a school subject, not good behavior in a community, was replaced.

What I rejected, and why: reviewer B wanted *cigarette* defined with the word *tobacco*. *Tobacco* has no entry yet, so the vocabulary gate would refuse it; *dried leaves* is plain and accurate, and the entry links to *tobacco* for later. Reviewer B also asked for single rather than double asterisks around expressions such as **go to the movies**. The style guide uses double asterisks for any expression named as a word, so that was rejected. Reviewer A said the American/British usage note on *chip* only repeated the region labels. I kept the note, because the labels alone do not tell a learner that the two varieties swap the words (*chips* versus *crisps* and *French fries*).

**Links between entries.** The cross-reference tool adds a reverse link whenever a new entry names an existing one, but it could not decide which sense to attach three of them to, so I placed them by hand: *cinema* on *film* (the art and business sense), *circuit* on *track* (the racing sense), and *circumstance* on *condition* (the living-conditions sense). The tool also noticed that *assistant* lists *clerk* as a word to compare while *clerk* listed *assistant* as a synonym. I changed *clerk*'s link to "compare" so the two agree. Nine words named in the new entries have no entries yet (among them *crisp*, *French fry*, *tobacco*, *cigar*, *applause*, and *nationality*). They are now in the queue without a band, waiting for the next band vote.
