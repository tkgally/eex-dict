## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewer models raised 53 issues, 50 of them from reviewer-a. I agreed with 48 and fixed the entries in my own words. The main fixes:

- *sand*: I had given the plural *sands* (as in *golden sands*) a sense of its own. Reviewer-b pointed out that this contradicted the entry's own forms table, which says the word has no plural. That table is written by a script that cannot record a plural for an uncountable noun; this is a known gap, recorded in `wiki/notes/inflect-uncountable-plural-gap.md`. I used the existing workaround: the plural is now described in the usage note as a named exception.
- *science*: the second and third senses were redefined, and a note that said English *science* means only the natural sciences was softened.
- *school*: the college sense is no longer labeled informal, since it is standard American English. *Faculty* was dropped as a synonym.
- *sea*: **all at sea** is now its own phrase, not a note inside **at sea**.
- *sauce*: the phrase is now **be on the sauce**, so that its definition can be a verb phrase.
- *score*: a remark on the old meaning "twenty" was removed. Reviewer-a was right that history belongs only in the etymology field.

I rejected five issues, each for a logged reason. Two examples: *built on sand* is an established idiom, and in American English a *grade* is a normal word for a test result.
