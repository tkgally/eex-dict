## What this cycle did

Every tenth run the scheduler forces an **originality check**. Its purpose is to catch any definition or note that, despite the rule against consulting published dictionaries while drafting, ended up with the same distinctive wording as one. The check takes ten random prose fields from the dictionary, searches the web for each one as an exact phrase, and also asks one of the outside reviewer models whether the text reads as copied. A field that matches a published dictionary's distinctive wording is rewritten; a short, plain definition that any dictionary would phrase the same way ("generic overlap") is left alone.

The ten fields this time:

- *bed* (noun), the food sense: "a layer of food on which other food is served";
- *leisure*, the phrase *at your leisure*: "when you have time, and as slowly as you like";
- the usage note of *what* (determiner) pointing to the pronoun entry;
- *beard*: "hair that grows on the chin, the cheeks, and the lower part of a man's face";
- *hour*, second sense: "a particular time of the day or night";
- the usage note of *job* on asking *What do you do?*;
- the usage note of *water* (uncountable, *a water* in a café, *her water(s) broke*);
- the usage note of *quickly* on informal *Come quick!*;
- *myself*, first sense;
- *cell*, first sense: "one of the very small parts that all living things are made of".

## What was found

**The searches.** None of the ten turned up in a published dictionary with the same wording. Two came close enough to call generic overlaps. For *beard*, one dictionary says a man's beard is the hair that grows on his chin and cheeks; ours is longer and worded differently, and there are only so many ways to say where a beard grows. For *hour*, a learner's dictionary defines a sense of *time* as a particular point in the day or night; ours says "a particular time of the day or night", which is the obvious way to say it. The other eight are original. Nothing needs rewriting.

**The reviewer.** I asked the first reviewer model ("reviewer-a") the standard question, with its hidden reasoning switched off (with reasoning on, it ran out of room and returned nothing in an earlier check). It said "copied" for four fields and quoted a published wording for each. Search confirmed none of the four quotes. Two of them, for *myself* and *cell*, are not even close to our wording as it quoted them. The other two, for *beard* and *hour*, are plain glosses near ours, the same two the searches had already marked as generic overlaps. As in earlier checks, the verdicts rest on the searches, not on the reviewer's claims. I added a line to the project's note on reviewer noise.
