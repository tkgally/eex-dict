# 2026-10-01 (13): twelve new nouns, *cave* to *charge*

*First cycle of the run that started at 18:43 UTC. Mode: build.*

## What this run did

The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work from the run history and the budget) chose a **build**: write new entries from the headword queue. It allowed twelve. The next twelve band-1 words in queue order were all nouns: *cave, ceiling, cell, center, century, chain, chair, chance, change, chapter, character, charge*. Band 1 is the most basic frequency band, and every one of these is also on the defining vocabulary, the list of about 2,400 words that definitions are written from.

Each entry was drafted from the style guide and my own knowledge of the words, with every field filled where it applies: senses ordered by usefulness, grammar for each sense, examples, collocations (the words a word typically goes with), idioms, learner errors, and adaptation notes (short notes for a future translator about where the word behaves differently in other languages). A few points of shape:

- **Idioms under their keyword.** By house rule an idiom lives in the entry for its first noun. So *hit the ceiling*, *take center stage*, *a chain reaction*, *chapter and verse*, *out of character*, *in charge*, *free of charge* and *press charges* each sit under the noun they contain. *Chance* holds six (*take a chance*, *chances are*, *stand a chance*, *by any chance*, *on the off chance*, *fat chance*) and *change* three (*for a change*, *a change of heart*, *a change of scene*).
- **Where learners go wrong.** The learner errors target common mistakes made by learners from many languages: *the roof of my bedroom* for *ceiling*; *in 19 century*; *the possibility to study abroad* and *Good chance!* for *Good luck!*; *Do you have a change?* for coins; *a bad character* for *a bad temper*; *in charge for* for *in charge of*.
- **Comparisons that matter.** Short "synonym discrimination" paragraphs explain how to choose between *center* and *middle*, between *chance*, *opportunity* and *possibility*, between *character* and *personality*, and between *charge*, *price*, *fee* and *cost*.

Twelve definitions first used words outside the defining vocabulary (*cliff*, *cricket*, *professor*, *extreme*, and others). Each was reworded in plainer words. Where the plain wording lost something, the exact term went into the explanation instead: the cricket sense of *century* now says "used in cricket, where the points are called runs".

## Checks and what the reviewers found

All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary.

Two outside reviewer models then read every field. Reviewer-b's reply could not be parsed for *cave* and *century*, so I re-ran it for those two and both came back clean. Altogether there were 30 issues, 23 of them blocking. I accepted 23 and rejected 7, and logged a reason for each decision:

- **Accepted.** *On charge* (a phone left charging) is mainly British, so it came out of the American-first entry; both reviewers flagged it. The crime sense of *charge* no longer says the police make the statement, since prosecutors do too. A cricket *century* is scored in one turn at batting, not in "one game", because a game can last several days. The *center* and *middle* note had said *middle* is the only word for times; it now says *middle* is the normal one. *Cell* sense 1 now covers all living things, not only plants and animals. The *century* usage note now separates everyday counting (1900 to 1999) from strict counting (1901 to 2000) instead of saying "mostly".
- **Rejected.** A call to drop the American label on *chapter* meaning a local branch: in this dictionary a region label means *mainly used there*, and British English usually says *branch*. A claim that *Do you have a change?* is natural for coins: it is not standard. A claim that bare *on the off chance* is unnatural: it is common, and the entry shows both forms.

Back-links: the cross-reference tool added mirrors on *branch*, *responsibility*, *care*, and *battery*, and placed each on the right sense. Thirteen missing targets, such as *opportunity* and *price*, were queued.
