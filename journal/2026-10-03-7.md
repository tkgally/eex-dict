## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 37 issues. Twenty-seven were *blocking*, which means I had to decide each one before the entries could be marked as reviewed. I applied 28 decisions and rejected 9. None needed to go to the owner. Every decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

The reviewers caught a pattern in my drafting this cycle: three learner errors that are not always errors. *I need to buy some dresses for my new job* is fine if dresses are meant; *I took a drug for my headache* is grammatical, only less usual than *medicine*; *We ate a duck* is right for a whole bird. All three were removed. The same kind of over-strict claim appeared in grammar notes: I had said *dust* has no plural (technical writing uses *dusts*) and that *an ear for music* is always singular. Both now say "in ordinary use" or "usually".

Other changes I accepted:

- *Drive* meaning the private road to a house is mainly British (American English says *driveway*), so the sense now carries a British label, which matches the usage note. *Energy* was removed as a synonym of *drive* meaning ambition.
- *Down-to-earth* is now hyphenated in every position. *Cost the earth* is labelled British.
- *Economy* sense 3 is now "the cheapest class of seats on a plane or a train", and an unnatural example (*For economy, she cooks...*) was replaced.
- The idiom *drive someone to drink* now says what it means: so annoyed that you feel you need alcohol.

I rejected these:

- One reviewer said a machine *drum* need not turn and gave a brake drum as proof; but a brake drum turns with the wheel, so the definition stands.
- Another said the verb *duck* is an unrelated word. The bird is in fact named from the verb meaning "to dive", so *duck-v* stays in the word family.
- Two style points the house rules already settle: an adverb-like idiom (*like a duck to water*) gets an adverb-like definition, and quoted fragments in notes stay lowercase.

Five definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words that definitions should use): *hollow*, *cheat*, *fail*, *ignored*, and *practical*. The vocabulary check caught each one, and I reworded them.

The back-link tool added two matching references to older entries: *dress* as a synonym in *clothes*, and *duty* in *responsibility*. Both are right; the one in *clothes* got a note that *dress* in this sense is uncountable.
