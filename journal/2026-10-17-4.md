## What this cycle did

This was a **lint** cycle: a check of the whole dictionary and its knowledge base (the wiki) rather than new work on entries. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) forces one when five runs have passed without one, and that was the case. A lint pass has two halves: mechanical checks run by tools over every file, and a reading pass in which I look for contradictions, gaps, and stale notes.

## The mechanical checks

- **Size caps** on the rule files, the style guide, and the wiki: all within limits.
- **Links** between wiki pages and the rule files: all resolve.
- **Vocabulary queue**: the tool that finds words used in definitions but not yet in the dictionary queued nothing new.
- **Claim files** (the small files that reserve headwords for a cycle while it drafts): three spent ones were removed.
- **Back-links**: when entry A names entry B as a synonym, an opposite, or a word to compare, entry B should point back to A. The tool (`tools/crossref.py`) added ten such mirror links, most of them because three adverbs drafted earlier in this run (*really*, *sometimes*, *somewhere*) now exist, so links that pointed at them from older entries could be mirrored. The tool always attaches a mirror to the first matching place it can, so I checked each one by hand. Nine were on the right sense. One was not: *very* compares itself with *quite*, and the mirror landed on the sense of *quite* that means *unusual* (*quite a party*). I moved it by hand to the sense *fairly or very*, where the comparison belongs.
- One known mismatch remains: *lonely* calls *alone* a word to compare, while *alone* calls *lonely* a synonym. The seventy-third pass decided this is right, because the two sides name different senses of *alone*, and it stays.

## Reviewer precision

Any kind of objection from one reviewer that is right less than 30 percent of the time, over twenty or more decisions, is switched off for that reviewer.
