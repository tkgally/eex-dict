## Cycle 1: review

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the tool that picks each cycle's job, `tools/next_mode.py`) chose a review. There were no draft entries and no open entry reports, so the cycle continued the coverage pass. That is the second reading the owner asked for in October (ruling B1). It goes through the most common words (band 1) with the most senses and asks of each entry: is any common meaning, phrase, or use missing? This cycle read the next four five-sense entries in alphabetical order: *divide* (verb), *end* (noun), *enter* (verb), and *expect* (verb).

Before the reviewers saw them, I added what I judged to be missing:

- *divide*: a new subsense for dividing your time, attention, or effort between places or activities (*she divides her time between the city and the farm*), and the common *opinion is divided*.
- *end*: five new phrases. These are *the end of an era* (noted in the baton file, `NEXT.md`), *end of story*, *be on the receiving end*, *end to end*, and the informal American *go off the deep end*. Also the collocation *be at an end*.
- *enter*: a theatre subsense (coming onto a stage, including *Enter two soldiers* in a play's directions), a legal subsense (*enter a plea of not guilty*), and a new sense for a feeling coming into something (*a note of worry entered her voice*).
- *expect*: two phrases, *what do you expect?* and *when you least expect it*.

**What the reviewers found.** Every changed entry goes to two reviewer models: reviewer A (a Claude model) and reviewer B (a Google model). They raised 46 issues, 5 of them blocking (issues that must be settled before an entry can stand). I applied 38 and rejected 8. None needed the owner. The blocking ones:

- *divide*'s note for translators said the larger number comes first in *divide 12 by 3*. That is wrong (*divide 3 by 12* is just as possible). The note also repeated the learner-error notes, so I removed it.
- One example in the new *divide* subsense (*working parents feel divided between home and work*) showed feeling torn, not sharing out time. I replaced it.
- The new theatre subsense of *enter* said the name comes first in *Enter two soldiers*. The verb comes first. I corrected it.
- The new phrase *when you least expect it* was defined with *not expected*, which defines the word by itself. I rewrote it.

Among the minor issues, the most useful find was a missing sense of *end*: a small piece left over (*cigarette ends*, *the ends of the bread*). It is now sense 6. Both reviewers also caught the same fact stated in two or three places in all four entries. The house rule is "one fact, one field", so I removed four notes: the usage note on *end*, the notes for translators on *divide*, *enter*, and *expect*, and part of the usage note on *enter*. I also removed a repeated sentence from one of *expect*'s explanations.

The rejections, each with its reason in the decision log (`reviews/decisions.jsonl`):

- Two requests for a grammar pattern on bare intransitive uses (*please knock before you enter*). No pattern in our closed list names that use, and the entry already marks it as transitive or intransitive.
- *birth* kept as the opposite of *end*. It mirrors a valid link in the *birth* entry.
- *think* kept as a near-synonym of *expect*.
- Three requests to split or merge senses where one definition serves both uses.

**Spend.** US$0.25 for reviewer B. Reviewer A runs on the owner's subscription and costs nothing in the ledger. The day's cap is US$15.

**What did not work.** Nothing broke. In the decision tool, `--quote` matches the text a reviewer quoted, not the reviewer's reason. My first attempts quoted the reasons and matched nothing. I used `--index` for those six decisions instead.

**What is next.** The coverage pass continues from *fail* (verb) with the remaining five-sense band-1 entries. Pronunciations were not touched, so they were not re-checked.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new from this cycle.

## Cycle 2: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose a build: new entries taken from the queue (the list of words still to be written) in its set order. The claim tool took twelve rows. One of them, *gloves*, is just the plural of *glove*, which already has an entry, so I marked that row as a duplicate and did not write it. I wrote the other eleven, all band-2 nouns (band 2 is the second most common group of words): *gardener*, *gardening*, *garlic*, *gas station*, *gasoline*, *gathering*, *genius*, *gentleman*, *gesture*, *ghost*, and *glance*. Each one has full definitions, examples, grammar, collocations (the words that typically go with it), and cross-references. Notes appear only where a learner would really need one. Examples: *gas station* and *gasoline* carry the American label and a note on the British words (*petrol station*, *petrol*); *gentleman* has four senses, including *ladies and gentlemen* and the historical man of high social position, and the phrase *a gentleman's agreement*; *ghost* has the phrases *give up the ghost* and *ghost town*.

**Checks before the reviewers.** The inflection tool (it works out plurals from rules) gave *gentlemen* and *geniuses*, and no plural for *garlic*, *gasoline*, or *gardening*. The pronunciation check (three other models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, consulted only at run time) disagreed with my transcriptions of *gardener* and *gardening*. I had cut them as two syllables plus an ending; the dictionary has three syllables (*GAR-den-er*). I rewrote both, and the second check verified them. Every other transcription was verified the first time.

**What the reviewers found.** 37 issues, 3 blocking. I applied 35, rejected 2, and escalated none. All three blocking issues were the same fault: I had given sense 2 of *gathering* (collecting) the grammar code *often before another noun*, but in *information gathering* the word comes after the other noun. The code is gone. Other useful fixes:

- *Keen gardener* is mainly British, so it became *avid gardener*.
- *Give up the ghost* now covers its main meaning, *die*, as well as machines that stop working.
- The *ghost* senses were reordered so the common *ghosts of the past* comes before the rarer *the ghost of a smile*, and the *literary* label came off that sense.
- *Gathering* is now defined as the occasion, which fits *hold a gathering*.
- A learner error about *do the garden* was dropped, because that is real British use.
- *Ingenious* was removed from the word family of *genius*: the two are not plainly related for a learner.

I rejected a request to drop the *often before another noun* code from *gardening* (*gardening gloves* is exactly what the code is for). I also rejected a request to mark *gentlemen* by hand: it is the listed plural, and the site marks it itself.

Five definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of about 2,400 words that definitions are written in): *onion*, *occasion*, *intelligent*, *rank*, *faint*. I rewrote all five. The cross-reference tool adds the matching link in each linked entry, but it could not tell which sense to attach eight of them to, so I placed those by hand (in *meeting*, *party*, *talent*, *gift*, *lady* twice, *signal*, and *hint*).

**Spend.** US$0.32 this cycle; US$0.57 for the day so far.

**What did not work.** Nothing broke. The duplicate *gloves* row is another example of the queue holding a plural form as if it were a separate word, a problem already noted in the wiki.

**What is next.** More building in queue order, or whatever the selector picks.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new.
