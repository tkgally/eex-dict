# 2026-10-02 (16:43 UTC run, cycle 4): twelve closure words, *amuse* to *slightly*

## What this run did

This was the fourth and last cycle of the run that started at 16:43 UTC. The first three cycles were merged as pull requests #186, #187 and #188. The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **closure**. A closure cycle drafts words that the dictionary already uses in its own definitions but that have no entry yet, so that a reader can always look up a word they meet in a definition.

The closure queue gave twelve rows. As earlier notes warned, the script that adds these rows sometimes guesses the part of speech or queues a plural form. I checked each row before drafting:

- Five rows were not real headwords: *amused* queued as a verb, *signals*, *singers*, *sleeves*, and *slightly* queued as a noun. I marked them as duplicates in the queue and drafted the real words instead: the verb *amuse*, the adjective *amused*, and *signal*, *singer*, *sleeve*, and the adverb *slightly*.
- *Christian* came back again. I released it unwritten, because an accurate definition needs a proper name (*Jesus Christ*), and the house tools do not yet allow proper names in definitions. That is open question 7 for the owner.
- The rest were real words: *enforce*, *affair*, *gray* (the color as a noun), *lack*, *skate*, and *skater*.

So the twelve entries were *amuse, amused, enforce, affair, gray, lack, signal, singer, skate, skater, sleeve,* and *slightly*. They are less central than the core verbs of the earlier cycles, so most have one to four senses. Each still has collocations (the words a word usually goes with), usage notes, and learner errors where a real error exists. For example, *amused* versus *amusing*, *lack of* versus the verb *lack* with no *of*, and *slight tired* corrected to *slightly tired*.

## Checks and review

All inflected forms were verified. One pronunciation was disputed. I had written the American *enforce* with the vowel of *in*, but the open CMU pronouncing dictionary and most of the pronunciation panel give the vowel of *end*. I corrected it, and the check then verified it with three votes out of four.

The two reviewer models raised 44 issues, and 23 of them were *blocking*, meaning I had to decide each one before the entries could be marked reviewed. I applied 30 decisions, rejected 14, and escalated none. The useful findings:

- *gray* had no record of the British spelling *grey*, although its usage note mentioned it. The variant is now recorded.
- The phone and radio sense of *signal* and the traffic-light sense were too narrow, and both were redefined.
- *skater* now covers people on skateboards, as its usage note already said.
- The note that *amuse* is "not used in continuous tenses" was wrong (*what's amusing you?*), and I removed it.
- Two learner errors were weak, one in *signal* and one in *singer*, and I removed them.

I rejected, among others, a claim that *finger* has no hard **g** sound (it does, and that is the point of the comparison with *singer*). I also rejected the usual objections already settled by house rules: the American length mark and the quoted headword at the start of adaptation notes.

The basic-vocabulary check made me reword eight definitions, for example ones that used *sexual*, *electrical*, *railroad*, and *plenty*.

One idiom, *wear your heart on your sleeve*, has *heart* as its first noun, so under the house keyword rule it belongs in *heart*. That entry does not exist yet, so the idiom sits in *sleeve* for now. `NEXT.md` lists it with the other idioms waiting to move.
