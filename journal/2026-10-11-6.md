# 2026-10-11: originality — the thirtieth check, three definitions rewritten

*Cycle 2 of the run that started at 04:43 UTC. Run id `20261011T050430Z-3wfw6d`.*

## What this cycle did

An **originality check** tests whether any of our definitions copy a published dictionary. The scheduler forces one every tenth run, and this was run 370. The tool `tools/originality_check.py` picked ten fields at random from the published entries: seven definitions of senses, two definitions of phrases (idioms), and one usage note. For each, I ran an exact-phrase web search and looked only at whether a published dictionary or word list used the same wording. I also put one question to a reviewer model: does this read as copied, or as an original plain-English definition?

## What the checks found

Six fields were original: no dictionary had the same wording. One was a **generic overlap**, meaning a short plain definition that any dictionary would write the same way: *boil*, "cook food in boiling water".

Three needed a **rewrite**, because each followed the frame of a published learner's dictionary closely, even though the words were not identical:

- *egg*, sense 1, was "a rounded object with a hard shell, produced by a female bird, from which a young bird comes out". A learner's dictionary uses the same frame ("object", "hard shell", "produced by a female bird", the young bird inside). It now reads "an oval thing with a hard outer covering that a female bird lays, and from which a baby bird can come out".
- *weekend*, sense 1, was "Saturday and Sunday, or the time from Friday evening until Sunday night, when many people do not work or go to school". A learner's dictionary has almost the same sentence. It now reads "the end of the week, usually Saturday and Sunday and often Friday evening too, when many people have free time away from work and school".
- *confuse*, sense 1, was "make someone unable to understand something or to think clearly". A learner's dictionary has the same wording with its two halves the other way round. It now reads "cause someone to feel unsure about what something means or what to do".

The reviewer model answered "original" for nine fields and "cannot tell" for one, so it missed all three matches the searches found. As in earlier checks, the verdicts rest on the searches and my own reading, and the model's answer counts as weak evidence.

## The rewrites went back through review

A rewritten field gets a full review, like any change to an entry. Both reviewers read all three entries again.

- *weekend* came back with no issues.
- *egg* drew eight issues. I accepted four: the new definition said there was a baby bird inside, which is not true of the eggs people eat, so it now says a baby bird "can" come out; the summary sentence got the same change; the sub-sense for insects, fish, and snakes now adds "or other animal"; and the definition of the egg cell no longer says it "starts a baby". I rejected four, with reasons: *There's egg on your chin* is natural English (egg as food is uncountable there); the learner error *I ate egg for breakfast* is a real error when one whole egg is meant; and the "see also" links to *bird* and *chicken* follow house practice.
- *confuse* drew four issues. I accepted one: sense 3 (making a situation harder to understand) no longer says this is "often by adding things that do not matter". I rejected three: *mix up* is a phrasal verb with its own entry, a point the project has already settled; and the note and learner error on *confused* versus *confusing* stay, because misusing the verb's *-ing* form is a common mistake with this very word.
