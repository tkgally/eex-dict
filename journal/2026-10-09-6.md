## What this cycle did

A **lint pass** is the regular tidy-up of the whole project. Scripts check what they can check: word caps on the guidance files, links between wiki pages, the links between entries, and the words that definitions use. Then the session reads the results and the wiki by hand, looking for what a script cannot see. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) forces a lint pass once five cycles have gone by without one, and that point had been reached.

## The mechanical checks

The size caps, the wiki links, the entry validator, and the unit tests all passed with nothing to fix.

The **cross-reference tool** (`tools/crossref.py`) adds **back-links**: when entry A names entry B as a synonym or as a word to compare, B gets a matching link back to A. This pass added nine back-links across seven entries. A back-link is only as good as the forward link it mirrors, and the tool always attaches it to the first sense of the target, so I checked each one by hand:

- Eight are correct: *teenager* now points to *child*, *tea* to *coffee*, *team* to *club* (the sports-club sense), *surface* to *face* (the side of a mountain or building), *system* to *network*, and *television* to *tube* (informal "TV") and twice to *TV* (as a synonym and in the word family).
- One was wrong. The entry *experiment* named *test* as a synonym. The build cycle at 01:37 today had deliberately removed *experiment* as a synonym of *test*, after a reviewer pointed out that an experiment is not the same thing, but the forward link from *experiment* was left in place. So the tool put the back-link on the first sense of *test*, which is a test of knowledge (an exam). That is the wrong sense, and it would have undone the reviewer's fix. I removed *test* from the synonyms of *experiment* by hand. The back-link no longer appears.

The **vocabulary lint** (`tools/lint_vocab.py`) changed the queue notes for two words, *expression* and *magazine*, by repeating text that was already there. This is a known small fault, recorded earlier in `wiki/notes/lint-vocab-queue-note-duplication.md`, and it does no harm. Three spent claim files (lists of words reserved by earlier cycles, all now drafted) were deleted by the pruning tool.

## Reviewer precision

Each reviewer's issues fall into "families" (pronunciation, collocation, label, and so on). The rule is that a family where fewer than 30 percent of a reviewer's issues have been accepted, over twenty or more decisions, is switched off for that reviewer. No new family crossed that line. The seven pairs already switched off stay off.

One thing did need writing down. **Reviewer-b**, the second outside reviewer, has almost stopped finding problems. Over the 50 entries reviewed since the last lint pass it raised only 5 issues, and 3 were accepted: 0.06 accepted issues per entry. Before the previous lint pass the figure was 0.07, and in early October it was about 0.24. Its replies are complete (it gives a verdict for every field), so this is not the earlier fault where it answered only part of an entry. Over the same entries, reviewer-a raised many issues and about 70 percent were accepted, often on fields reviewer-b had passed. The previous lint pass said that if the figure stayed under 0.10, it should be written up. It is now in `wiki/notes/reviewer-b-low-yield.md`, with possible causes and remedies. Nothing has been changed yet; the project's rule is that an observed problem is written down first and fixed in a later run with a recorded reason.
