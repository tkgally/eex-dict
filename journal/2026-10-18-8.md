# 2026-10-18, cycle 4 of the 12:43 UTC run: the seventy-eighth lint pass

## What this run did

This was a **lint** cycle: a check of the whole dictionary and its knowledge base (the `wiki/` folder of rules, notes, and decisions) for problems that build up over time. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) forces one every five runs; this was the fifth run since the last pass. A lint cycle makes no paid calls and writes no new entries.

It has two halves. The mechanical half runs the project's checking tools over everything. The judgmental half reads the knowledge base for contradictions, gaps, and stale items that no script can see.

## The mechanical checks

- **Word caps** (`tools/check_caps.py`): every capped file is within its limit.
- **Links** (`tools/check_links.py`): every link inside the knowledge base points to a file that exists.
- **Vocabulary** (`tools/lint_vocab.py --all --queue`): no definition anywhere uses a word outside the defining vocabulary that lacks an entry. One queue row's note was updated; nothing new was queued.
- **Claims** (`tools/claim.py --prune`): three old claim files (the lists a cycle writes so that two runs never draft the same word) belonged to cycles whose entries have all merged, and were removed.
- **Cross-references** (`tools/crossref.py --all --apply`): this tool adds the "back-link" when one entry lists another as a synonym or a family member and the other does not list it back. It proposed six back-links in four entries. Past passes showed that it sometimes attaches a back-link to the wrong sense, so I checked each one. All are right: *upward* as a synonym of *up* in its first sense (*from a lower place to a higher place*); *mostly* beside *usually*; *okay* beside *well* in *in a good, right, or skillful way*; and *good* in the word families of *goodbye* and *well*. *Goodbye* is listed as a relative of *good* by form, which is what a word family records, so it stays.
