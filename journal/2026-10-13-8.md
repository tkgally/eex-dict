# 2026-10-13, 04:44 UTC run, cycle 4: closure, *lump*, *curve*, and three family verbs

## What this cycle did

This cycle was a **closure** cycle. Closure writes entries for words the dictionary already uses, in its definitions or in its word-family boxes, that have no entry of their own yet. The aim is that a reader who meets a word in a definition can always look it up. The selector, the script that picks each cycle's job, chose closure because it was the most overdue job. This was the run's fourth and last cycle.

The closure source, the list of words found inside definitions, had only two rows. Both were inflected forms, not dictionary headwords: *lumps* (used in the definition of *smooth*) and *curving* (used in *straight*). As earlier runs have done, I marked both rows as duplicates. I then queued and wrote the base words instead: **lump** (noun), **curve** (verb), and **curve** (noun), since the noun is at least as common as the verb.

The cycle then took three verbs from the family source, words named in the word-family boxes of existing entries: **complete**, **correct**, and **bunch**. Six entries in all, which is fewer than the twelve allowed. The rules prefer fewer, fuller entries, and this was the last cycle of a long run.

The entries:
- *lump*: a solid piece (with *a sugar lump* as a subsense) and a raised area on the skin. Two phrases: *a lump in your throat*, and the American *take your lumps*.
- *curve* (noun): a bending line, and a line on a graph. Two phrases: *ahead of the curve* and the American *throw someone a curve*.
- *curve* (verb): one sense, *The road curves to the left*.
- *complete* (verb): finish, make whole, and fill in a form. It has a short guide to *complete* and *finish*, and the learner error *I completed reading the book* (it should be *finished reading*).
- *correct* (verb): make right (with a subsense for eyesight), tell someone they are wrong, and mark schoolwork. It has the learner error *correct me my essay*.
- *bunch* (verb): come together in a tight group, and form tight folds in cloth.

## Checks

- **Pronunciation.** All twelve transcriptions were confirmed on the first pass.
- **Inflections** (*curves*, *curved*, *bunches*...) all came from the spelling rules.
- **Review panel.** The two outside reviewer models raised 11 issues, 8 of them blocking. I applied 7 of the blocking issues and rejected 1; nothing needed the owner. *Curve* (verb) and *correct* came back clean.
  - In *curve* (noun), the example and collocations for *a learning curve* had sat under the graph sense. They were removed from it, and a usage note now explains the phrase. The antonym *straight* was dropped.
  - Two examples were tagged *usually passive*, a pattern their sense does not list. Each now carries a plain *passive* note instead.
  - Rejected: a claim that the definition of *throw someone a curve* was not a base-form verb phrase. It already was one.

Two mirror links that the cross-reference tool added were on the wrong sense and were moved by hand: *lump* on `piece-n` (to sense 1, *part*) and *correct* on `fix-v` (to sense 2, *solve*).
