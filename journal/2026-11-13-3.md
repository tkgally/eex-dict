## Cycle 1: site

**What the cycle was for.** Each cycle of a scheduled run does one job, chosen by the mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, a script that picks whichever kind of work is most overdue). This time it chose *site*: rebuild the public website from the dictionary's entry files and check it in a real browser. A site cycle writes no entries and makes no paid model calls. Its selector reason was "highest scheduler debt among eligible modes", which means that of all the jobs allowed to run, the site check had waited longest relative to how often it is meant to run. The last site check was on 2026-11-12; since then three runs have added or changed entries (twelve family entries from *nursery* to *paste*, ten queue entries from *occasion* to *overtime*, and a coverage review of *single*, *sister*, *slow*, and *smell*), so a fresh check was due.

**Housekeeping before the work.** Every run first looks for work a previous run left unfinished. There were no open pull requests (a pull request is a proposed batch of changes waiting to be merged into the published `main` branch) and no open entry reports from readers. One old working branch, `claude/friendly-mayer-mdy32d`, still exists on GitHub; the branch-checking tool confirmed that everything on it is already in `main`, and it was already listed in `reviews/needs_curator.txt` for deletion (the run's tools cannot delete branches). **What was done.** The build script (`tools/build_site.py`) turned all 4,281 entries into 3,577 headword pages (a headword page groups every entry for the same spelling, such as a noun and a verb, on one page). No entries were held back as unfinished drafts, because every entry in the dictionary is now marked `reviewed`, and there are no redirects (pages that send an old address to a renamed entry). In all, 7,840 files were written to the `docs/` folder, which is never committed to the repository: GitHub builds its own copy for publishing.

The browser check (`tools/site_check.py`) then opened the site in a headless browser at phone width (360 pixels) and desktop width and ran its 79 checks. All 79 passed. Among other things, they confirm that:

- no page scrolls sideways at phone width, checked on every entry page and the key pages (3,583 pages);
- the search box finds a phrase from its main words (*a catch in voice* finds *a catch in someone's voice*) and offers "Did you mean" for a misspelling (*abadnon* finds *abandon*);
- the "not found" page searches for the word in the address;
- the jump menu on long entries (five senses or more) lists the senses and moves to the chosen one;
- a cross-reference to a word that has no entry yet appears grey and unlinked, with a note explaining the grey;
- dark mode shows a dark background, and no page raises a JavaScript error.

I also checked by hand that the pages for words added or changed today exist: *nursery*, *passage*, *occasion*, *outlet*, and *smell* are all there.

**What the reviewers found.** Nothing: a site cycle does not use the reviewer models, so there were no review issues to decide.

## Cycle 2: lint

**What the cycle was for.** The mode selector forced a *lint* pass because ten runs (each cycle counts as one) had passed since the last one on 2026-11-12. A lint pass is a whole-dictionary consistency check: it does not write new entries, but it tidies the links between entries, clears leftover bookkeeping, and checks that the project's rule pages and records still agree with each other. This was the hundred-and-eleventh such pass.

**Mechanical checks.** The size limits on the project's rule files and wiki were all respected, and every relative link in the wiki and rule pages pointed to a real file.

The cross-reference tool (`tools/crossref.py`) checks that when one entry names another as a synonym, an opposite, a word to compare, or a member of the same word family, the other entry names it back. It wrote three of these mirror links automatically: **occasion** (its first sense, "a time when something happens") now lists **time** as a synonym, because **time** already listed **occasion**; **overtime** now lists **time** in its word family; and **outcome** now lists the phrasal verb **come out** in its word family. Three more pairs the tool could not place, because the target entry has several senses and none clearly matched. I placed them by hand, each on the first sense: **option** now lists **possibility** as a synonym (the "something you can choose" sense), **mix** lists **cross** (the "combination of different things" sense), and **sick** lists **funny** (the "having an illness" sense, matching *funny* in the sense of slightly ill). These links are the only entry changes in this cycle; six entries changed in all.

Six old claim files were removed. A claim file is the list of words a run reserves before drafting so that two runs do not write the same entry; once the entries are merged, the file is no longer needed.

**Band vote.** Every word in the build queue carries a frequency band from 1 (the most important words) to 5. Thirty-seven words had been added to the queue since the last lint without a band. Three word-list models each voted a band for them, at a cost of less than one US cent (US$0.006). Where the three votes are close, the lower middle vote stands. One word was split widely: **May**, the month, got votes of 4, 1, and 3. I settled it at band 1, because **January**, **February**, **April**, and **June** are all band 1. All 37 words are now ready for building.

**Reports.** Three reports list things for later review cycles to look at; none needs action now:

- 1,013 pairs where one entry names another as a synonym or opposite but the other entry may lack a matching sense (up from 1,008, as new entries were built);
- 317 definitions that use their own headword (most are harmless, such as "give an animal **water** to drink" for the verb **water**);
- six usage notes that mention a separate entry that does not exist yet and is not in the queue. These are the same six as last time; most are false alarms caused by homographs and phrasal verbs whose queue names differ from what the tool expects.

**Reviewer precision.** Precision is the share of a reviewer model's objections that the adjudicating session accepts. Reviewer A stands at 0.85 and reviewer B at 0.83. The rule turns off any kind of objection that falls below 30 percent for a reviewer over twenty or more decisions. The three that are below the line (reviewer A on inflections, reviewer B on phrases and on pronunciation) were already turned off in October and November, so nothing new was switched off. Reviewer B's spelling-and-format objections stay on at 0.37, by the owner's earlier ruling.

**Records.** The wiki index matches the wiki's pages; the curator file has no duplicate lines; the open-questions page has no open questions; and every run since the last lint has its log entry.

**Spend.** US$0.006 for the band vote. The day's total is US$1.69 of the US$15 daily budget.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. One reminder for later runs: the band-vote `settle` and `apply` steps need `--votes votes-unbanded.tsv` when the vote was on unbanded rows only (this is already in the log from October).

**What is next.** The next lint is due after ten more runs. The selector will most likely choose building, closure, or review next; the coverage review continues from **smooth** and **space**, and the weekly digest is due tomorrow, 2026-11-14.

## Cycle 3: build

**What the cycle was for.** The mode selector chose *build*: writing new entries from the main queue, the ordered list of words the dictionary still needs. Its limit was twelve new entries and US$3 of paid model calls. The twelve words at the top of the queue were claimed (reserved so no other run writes them) and drafted from scratch, following the style guide. Two are band 1 (the most important words): **May** and **mile**. The other ten are band 2: **motivate**, **outline** (the verb; the noun was built earlier today), **alternative** (the noun), **kindergarten**, **luxury**, **motivation**, **pace**, **packet**, **painkiller**, and **pajamas**.

**What was built.** All twelve are now marked `reviewed` and will appear on the site:

- **May**: the month, with the phrase *May Day* and two learner errors (the capital letter; *in May* but *on May 3*).
- **mile**: the unit of distance (with a sub-sense for the nautical mile used at sea and in the air) and the informal plural *miles*, a long way (*The nearest hospital is miles away*). Six phrases: *go the extra mile*, *by a mile*, *be miles away*, *a mile a minute*, *miles from anywhere*, and the British *stick out a mile*. A learner error covers *a two-mile walk* (not *two-miles*).
- **motivate**: make someone want to do something, and be the reason for something (*The attack was motivated by anger*).
- **outline** (verb): give the main points of something, and draw or show the edge of something.
- **alternative**: something you can choose instead, with the phrase *have no alternative but to do something* and a short note on choosing between **alternative**, **option**, and **choice**.
- **kindergarten**: the American first school year for five-year-olds, and the wider meaning of a school for very young children, with a note for translators that school systems differ.
- **luxury**: comfort from expensive things, an expensive thing you do not need, and a rare pleasant chance (*the luxury of time*), with a warning that a similar-looking word in some languages means sexual desire.
- **motivation**: the wish and energy to do something, and a reason for doing it.
- **pace**: speed of moving, speed of change, and a single step, with *keep pace with*, *set the pace*, *pick up the pace*, and *put someone through their paces*.
- **packet**: a small container, an American set of papers (*a welcome packet*), a unit of computer data, and the British informal *cost a packet*.
- **painkiller** and **pajamas** (plural only, British spelling **pyjamas**, with the note that the form **pajama** is used before another noun).

**Pronunciation and word forms.** The plurals and verb forms came from the regular rules; **pajamas** has no plural form because it is already plural. All 24 transcriptions (American and British) were agreed by the pronunciation panel, after one change: my British transcription of **alternative** starting with the vowel of *olive* got only one of three votes. Two panel members gave the other common British form (starting like *all*), so I made that the main form and kept the first as a listed variant.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewer models raised 38 issues, 6 of them blocking (a blocking issue must be settled before an entry can be published). All six were right, and in each case both reviewers found the same problem:

- A note for translators in **May** said the capital letter tells the month from the verb **may** "at the start of a sentence". That is false: both begin with a capital there. I removed the note; the learner-error note already says the true part.
- **pace** in *the pace of life* was coded uncountable, but its own examples say *a slower pace of life*. It is now countable, usually singular.

Of the other issues I applied 26 and rejected 6. The main changes: **mile** gained *by a mile* and *stick out a mile*, and lost two collocations that belonged to the other sense; *politically motivated* moved to the phrase type of collocation, as house rules require; a health claim in an **alternative** example (that brown rice is healthier) was replaced, since examples must not state checkable facts; and a British-sounding **packet** example was replaced with *ketchup packets*. I rejected requests to put *a change of pace* and *at a snail's pace* under **pace** (idioms go under their first noun, so they belong to **change** and **snail**), a call to treat *usually passive* as a code rather than a verb pattern (a settled house rule), and a claim that a sub-sense needs two examples (one is allowed).

The cross-reference tool wrote two mirror links (**summarize** now names **outline** as a synonym, and **motive** compares **motivation**), and I placed five more by hand where the target had several senses (for example, **step** sense 1 now lists **pace** as a synonym). Eight related words were queued for later building, including the verb **pace**, the adjective **alternative**, and **inspire**.

**Spend.** US$0.37 for the review panel and pronunciation checks. The day's total is US$2.06 of US$15.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. One definition used *honoring*, which is outside the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words definitions are written in); it now says *show respect for*.

**What is next.** The build queue continues after **pajamas**; the coverage review continues from **smooth** and **space**.

## Cycle 4: review

**What the cycle was for.** The mode selector chose *review*: rereading entries that are already published to make them fuller and more accurate. There are no unfinished drafts and no open entry reports from readers, so the cycle continued the *coverage pass*, the owner's 2026-10-24 ruling that the most important (band 1) words be reread to check that every common meaning and idiom is there and that every example fits its sense. The pass takes words with the most senses first; this cycle read the next four, each with five senses: **smooth** (adjective), **space**, **spring**, and **stage** (nouns). That brings the pass to 319 words.

**What I changed before the reviewers saw the entries.**

- **smooth** gained a sub-sense for sound (*a deep, smooth voice*, *smooth music*), and its core idea now mentions sound.
- **space** gained *personal space*, the area around a person that others should not come too close to.
- **spring** gained the idiom *be no spring chicken* (be no longer young). A note for translators that ended in generic advice was shortened, and a usage note that repeated the two learner errors was cut down to the one fact they do not cover: the season is written with a small **s**.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewer models raised 27 issues, one of them blocking. I applied 18 and rejected 9.

The blocking issue, raised by both reviewers, said that **lap** is not a synonym of **stage** in the sense "one part of a long journey or race", because a lap is one trip around a track. I rejected it: this dictionary's own **lap** entry has a sense "one part of a long trip or a long process" (*the last lap of the journey*), so the link is correct. But the reviewers had a point that a closer word exists, so I added **leg** (whose fifth sense is "one part of a journey, a race, or a competition") as the first synonym and added the matching link in **leg** by hand.

The main changes I accepted:

- **space** now lists its senses in order of how often a learner will meet them: an empty area, space beyond the earth, a gap in writing, freedom to be alone, and a period of time. *Personal space* moved under the first sense, because it is a physical area, not freedom. The *astronomy* label came off the outer-space sense, since it is everyday English. A learner error about *room* (not *a room*) was removed, because it does not involve the word **space** and the **room** entry already has it.
- **stage** sense 3 now covers writing for the theater as well as acting, so its example about writing plays fits. A short explanation that only repeated the grammar was removed, and a weak see-also link to **level** was dropped.
- **spring** sense 4 was reworded so that *its* refers to something (*the ability of something to go back to its shape*), and **fall** was added beside **autumn**, since American English is the dictionary's main variety.
- **smooth** lost a note for translators that repeated the pronunciation note word for word.

The rejections: *smooth sailing* belongs under **sailing** (idioms go under their first noun); **flat**, **soft**, and **platform** stay as near-synonyms because those entries define the matching sense with **smooth** or as a raised place to perform; *humorous* is a valid register label; the restriction notes on two **space** senses (used mainly in *in the space of*; usually in *personal space*) stay, because the style guide puts such restrictions in the explanation; and *The meeting went very smooth* stays as a learner error, since the standard form is *smoothly*.
