# 2026-10-02 (4) — Twelve new nouns, *cold* to *condition*

*Fourth and last cycle of the run that began at 00:43 UTC. A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request; a run does at most four.*

## What this cycle did

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **build** again: writing new entries from the queue. The next twelve band-1 words in queue order were all nouns: *cold, colleague, college, comb, comfort, community, company, comparison, competition, computer, concert*, and *condition*. "Band 1" is the dictionary's own label for its most basic words.

Each entry went through the full pipeline: format validation, plural forms, a pronunciation check against outside models and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, two outside reviewers, my reading of every serious objection, and cross-referencing. All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each word) and all twelve plurals were verified.

These entries are as full as the first cycle's:

- **company** has five senses (a business, being with people, guests, a group of performers, a unit of soldiers). It also has three phrases (*part company*, *two's company, three's a crowd*, *present company excepted*) and a note on choosing between *company*, *firm*, and *business*.
- **condition** has four senses (the state of something, *working conditions*, a condition you must meet, and an illness) and three phrases (*on condition that*, *in no condition to drive*, the British *out of condition*).
- **comfort** separates the physical sense (*shoes made for comfort*) from comfort when someone is sad, *a comfort to her*, and *modern comforts*. It also has the idioms *too close for comfort*, *cold comfort*, and *take comfort in something*.
- **college** explains the American use (*in college*, *go to college*) and compares *college*, *university*, and *school*.
- **cold** separates the low temperature from the illness. Its learner error targets a mistake many learners make: *I have cold* (meant as "I feel cold"). English says *I'm cold*; *I have a cold* means you are ill.

Other learner errors:

- *my college* for *my colleague*
- *This sofa has a lot of comfort* (say *is very comfortable*)
- *keep me a company* (no article)
- *made a singing competition* (say *held*)
- *playing computer* (say *playing computer games*)
- *in comparison of* (say *with* or *to*)
- *the weather condition* (say *weather conditions*)

Three definitions in *company* used words outside the basic defining vocabulary of about 2,400 words (*dancers*, *singers*, *prefer*). They were reworded.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers raised 35 issues, 24 marked blocking. I accepted 21 and rejected 14, with a reason logged for each in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Most of the accepted fixes were on *college*, which reviewer-a went through closely:

- Sense 2 had said a college is a place for job skills. That was too narrow, since many colleges teach academic courses too, so the sense was widened.
- *At college*, mainly British, was dropped from the American sense.
- My learner error (*I'm studying in the college*) could be correct when one particular college is meant, so I replaced it with a clearer case.
- The comparison with *university* no longer treats research as part of the definition of a university.

Other accepted fixes:

- *Coworker* is not only American.
- A medical *condition* need not be long-term.
- *Computer* is now marked countable; the article-less *on computer* is explained in the usage note.
- *a wide-tooth comb*, not *a wide comb*.
- *a close-knit community*.

Rejected:

- reviewer-a said *concert* lacks a sense meaning a piece of music for a solo instrument. That word is *concerto*, so I rejected the claim.
- Its objection to *I've been over the contract*, which is the normal perfect form of *go over*.
- Four objections (three from reviewer-b, one from reviewer-a) to the quoted headword at the start of adaptation notes, which is settled house style.

One automatic back-link, from *comb* to *brush*, landed on the right sense of *brush*.
