## Cycle 2: build

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue of headwords still to be written. Its reason was that build had the highest "debt", meaning it had waited longest relative to its share of the work. The rows at the top of the queue were twelve band-2 verbs. Band 2 is the second of five editorial frequency bands: words most intermediate learners know or need soon. These were "crossref" rows, words that existing entries already name as synonyms or relatives but that had no entry of their own. The twelve are *bless*, *bother*, *broadcast*, *bump*, *burst*, *button*, *cancel*, *capture*, *challenge*, *chat*, *cite*, and *clarify*. Several close visible gaps. For example, *call off*, *talk*, *explain*, *annoy*, and *take* all named one of these verbs as a synonym, and the site showed those links grey.

All twelve went through the full pipeline:

- a schema check;
- the inflection tool (rules, or the exceptions table for *broadcast* and *burst*, which keep the same form in the past);
- the pronunciation panel, which verified all twenty-four transcriptions;
- both reviewers, and my decision on every issue they raised;
- a second schema check;
- the defining-vocabulary lint, which checks that definitions use only plain listed words or words that have entries;
- the cross-reference tool.

All twelve are now `reviewed`. The dictionary has 3,293 entries, none in draft.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers (reviewer A, a Claude model; reviewer B, a Google model) raised 102 issues, 6 of them blocking. I applied 84 and rejected 18.

- **An example that did not fit its definition.** *My back has been bothering me* sat under "make someone worried, upset, or annoyed". Pain is a different meaning, so it now has its own subsense, "cause someone some pain".
- **A confusing definition.** I had defined *don't bother to call* as "make the effort to do something, even though it is not much trouble". It now reads "take the time or trouble to do something, especially something that does not seem worth it".
- **An unnatural example.** *Tickets will be paid back* became *everyone will get their money back*.
- **Overstated grammar.** I had marked eight senses "usually passive" (for example *cancel* and *capture*) where the active is just as common. All eight were removed, and `NEXT.md` now carries a one-line warning for later builds.
- **Added on review:**
  - *bless* a plan (approve it), and the warm remark *bless him*;
  - the newer informal *cancel someone* (stop supporting them in public), labeled informal and new;
  - *capture data* and *capture the screen*, as a subsense;
  - *burst open*;
  - the British *I'm not bothered*.
- **Rejected,** with reasons logged:
  - Moving *bless her heart*, *God bless*, and *bursting at the seams* into these entries. Idioms go under their first noun.
  - Calling *quote* a wrong synonym of *cite*. The link points to the "give as an example" sense of *quote*, which matches.
  - Dropping the informal label on *chat*. The entry *talk* already calls *chat* informal, and the two should agree.
  - A request to mark up the automatic inflection note on *cancel*. That note is written by a tool, not by hand.

**Cross-references.** The cross-reference tool no longer guesses which sense of the other entry a back-link belongs to, so seven back-links waited for me. I placed them by hand in *worry*, *hit*, *fasten*, *catch*, *arrest*, *win*, and *question*. I also added the missing mirror links from *run* (the broadcast sense) and *eat* (*what's eating you?*). The phrasal verb *bump up* was added to the queue.

**Spend.** US$0.41 this cycle. So far today, US$4.24 of the US$15 daily cap has been spent.

## Cycle 3: review

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector chose **review**: a second careful reading of entries that are already published. No entries were in draft and no entry reports were open, so the cycle continued the **coverage pass**. This is the owner's ruling B1 of 2026-10-24: a reading of the most important band-1 entries (the very common words), asking for each one whether a sense, phrase, or common use is missing. Entries with the most senses come first. This cycle read the next four in that order: *raise* (verb), *private* (adjective), *order* (noun), and *open* (verb). Each went through the same pipeline as a new entry, so both reviewers read it again after my changes.

**What I added.** These are my own additions, made before the reviewers saw the entries:

- *raise*: the card-game use (*I'll raise you ten dollars*), and the collocation *raise an objection*.
- *order*: two senses and three phrases.
  - The senses are "the way a society is organized" (*the old order*) and the group of animals or plants larger than a family (labeled technical, biology).
  - The phrases are *on the order of* (about, used before an amount), *call something to order*, and *of the highest order*.
- *open*: three additions.
  - A new sense, *open onto* or *into*, for a door or room that leads to a place.
  - The use for markets and prices (*prices opened higher*).
  - Collocations for computer files, apps, and links.
- *private*: the collocations *private joke* and *private detective*.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The reviewers raised 43 issues, 1 of them blocking. I applied 35 and rejected 8.

- **Blocking: a doubtful opposite.** *Private* listed *commercial* as an opposite in its general sense, "for one person or group only". The two are opposites only for planes and pilots (*a private jet*, *a commercial flight*). The *commercial* entry names *private* in exactly that sense, so I kept the link and added a note limiting it to planes and pilots.
- **A sense that said too much.** *Private* meaning "secret" now reads "meant only for the people involved, not for others to know about or see". *Secret* moved from synonym to "compare" in both entries, because the entry's own note explains how the two differ.
- **A claim my own addition broke.** Three notes in *raise* said the verb "always" takes an object. The card-game use I added (*She raised, and everyone else gave up*) has none. The word "always" is gone, and the card-game use has its own grammar.
- **Repeated facts.** Notes are null by default, and each fact belongs in one field. On that rule I removed a usage note in *order* that repeated three other fields, and two adaptation notes (*raise*, *open*) that repeated a learner-error note or a pronunciation note.
- **Smaller fixes:**
  - *Open* now covers opening a box or a letter, not just doors and lids.
  - The American *open your books to page ten* replaces *at page ten*.
  - *Open* can take an object in the shop-hours sense (*Who opens the store?*).
  - *Out of order* now also means "not acceptable" in informal use generally, not only in British English.
- **Rejected:**
  - Idioms whose first noun is another word: *raise the alarm*, *raise the bar*, and *private parts* go under *alarm*, *bar*, and *part*.
  - Hand marks in phrase examples. The site marks the headword itself.
  - Reordering the senses of *private*. The current order is defensible, and moving senses risks breaking links.
