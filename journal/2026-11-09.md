**What the reviewers found.** Every entry goes to two automated reviewers from different companies, who give each field a verdict of "ok" or "issue". This time they raised 31 issues. Only 3 were blocking (serious enough to stop an entry from being published until settled), and all three were the same mistake: one example for the tree-substance sense of *gum* said *a thick gum*, treating as countable a sense the entry itself marks uncountable. Both reviewers caught it; I rewrote the example as *Thick gum was coming out of a cut in the tree.*

Of the 31 issues I applied 24 and rejected 7, each with a logged reason. The useful fixes:
- I had used *sweet* to mean a piece of candy, which is the British word; the dictionary's main variety is American, so the *gum* definition now reads *a soft, sweet substance that you chew*.
- *Grown-up* is now defined simply as *an adult*; the fact that children use the word, and adults use it when they talk to children, moved to the explanation field, where the style guide puts limits on who uses a sense.
- *Guidance* now covers official advice from an organization or a government, not only personal help, with a new example.
- The shop sense of *hairdresser* used the headword in its own definition (*a shop where a hairdresser works*); it now reads *a shop where people go to have their hair cut*.
- Small tidying: collocation types corrected on *grown-up* and *half hour*, a repeated fact removed from *half hour*, a misleading note on the link from *count* to *lord* corrected.

The rejections: one reviewer argued that *play guitar*, with no article, makes *guitar* uncountable; it does not, as instruments commonly drop the article after *play*. Both reviewers wanted the note that the shop sense is usually said *the hairdresser's* moved out of the explanation; the style guide says that a limit to one form belongs exactly there. I also kept a learner-error note on *haircut* (*I cut my hair at the hairdresser's* when someone else did it), because it is a real mistake learners make when talking about haircuts.
