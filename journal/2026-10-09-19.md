## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 30 issues, 24 of them marked blocking. I agreed with 22 and fixed the entries myself, and rejected 8 with a logged reason. None needed the owner.

Fixes I agreed with:

- *earth*: *down-to-earth* (practical and sensible) was listed as a phrase of the noun, but it is an adjective, so both reviewers objected. It was removed and added to the queue as an entry of its own. The entry also told learners that *travel around the earth* is wrong, which it is not; the learner error now shows a real mistake (*the best singer on the earth*, for *in the world*), and the translator note agrees.
- *earth*, again: my new sense is countable (*an earth*), but the forms field still said "no plural". That field belongs to the inflection tool, so I re-ran the tool, and it now gives **earths**.
- *egg*: the sense "a cell in a woman's body" said the cell can develop into a baby. Reviewer-a pointed out that it must first join with a cell from a male. The definition and one example were corrected.
- *economy*: the example *We flew economy* uses the word like an adverb, which belongs in a different entry. It now reads *We bought two seats in economy*.
- *edge*: my new phrase's definition mentioned mental illness, which the idiom does not need to imply. It now says only "lose control of their feelings".
- *email*: the notes said that *a mail* can never mean one message. They now say more exactly that *mail* is uncountable in American and British English, so one message is *an email*. *Mail* moved from the word family to "see also", because *email* is not formed from it in today's English.
- *dust*: a synonym, *powder*, moved to the powder sense, and the usage note no longer explains the verb at length.

What I rejected: reviewer-b again asked for verb definitions that begin with *to*, against house style, and for collocation items without articles. Reviewer-a called *make a big effort* unidiomatic; it is common. Reviewer-a also objected that the forms field says *dust* has no plural while the usage note mentions the rare plural *dusts*; the style guide asks for exactly this: the ordinary rule plus the exception named as one.

The vocabulary check found no definition word outside the defining vocabulary, and the cross-reference tool added no back-links.
