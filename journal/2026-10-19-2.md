# 2026-10-19, cycle 2 of the 00:43 UTC run: eleven interjections and *break down*

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: new entries taken from the queue (the list of words still to write) in its order, band 1 first (band 1 is the commonest words). The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose build because it was the most overdue kind of work after the morning's review cycle. The twelve words were the last band-1 interjections, *oh, okay, oops, ouch, pardon, sorry, thanks, uh, um, welcome, wow*, and the phrasal verb *break down* (an entry of its own, `break-down-phrv`, because a verb plus a particle often means something its parts do not).

Each entry was drafted by hand from the style guide, then run through the full pipeline: the format check, the inflection tool (which writes *breaks down, broke down, broken down*), the pronunciation check (three outside models plus, for American English, an open pronouncing dictionary consulted at run time), the two outside reviewers, my decisions on what they found, and the cross-reference tool.

Some choices worth knowing:

- *Sorry*, *thanks*, *okay*, and *welcome* already had entries as other parts of speech, or will. The interjection entries hold only the word used on its own (*Sorry?*, *Thanks!*, *Okay, let's go*, *Welcome back!*) and point to the others: *I'm sorry* and *You're welcome* are the adjectives.
- *Sorry* has four senses: apologizing, asking someone to repeat, correcting yourself (*seven, sorry, eight o'clock*), and softening a refusal (*Sorry, we're closed*).
- *Break down* has seven senses: a machine stops, talks fail, a person starts crying, a task is divided, a door is forced, barriers are removed, and food or leaves turn into simpler parts.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

reviewer-a raised 31 issues, most of them blocking; reviewer-b raised 4, all minor. Six entries drew no issues at all (*oh, oops, ouch, pardon, thanks, wow*). In all I applied 22 and rejected 13.

Applied:

- ***okay*** and ***sorry***: some of my notes on register (how formal a word is) and politeness were too absolute. *Okay* no longer claims that *okay* and *OK* are equally common or that *certainly* can replace it; *sorry* no longer says it is mainly British as a request to repeat, no longer says that *sorry* is never an admission of fault, and its note now covers *sorry* in the middle of a sentence.
- ***uh*** and ***um***: the notes said British *er* is the same sound as *uh*; it is a similar sound, and the notes now say so. "Every language has its own sound for a pause" became "many languages".
- ***welcome***: the note said **to** follows it; it now also shows a name following it (*Welcome, Ravi*).
- ***break down***: the door sense now reads *force a door or a wall to fall or open, usually by hitting it*; two senses gained collocations; a learner error was made more natural (*We had a break down on the highway* corrected to *breakdown*); the separability note now says the *barriers* sense usually keeps the object after **down**.

Rejected, with the main reasons:

- The *informal* label on *okay* stays: it matches the existing *okay* (adverb) entry, and formal writing avoids the word.
- reviewer-b wanted the grammar pattern of intransitive phrasal verbs written *verb + adverb*; the house pattern, used in *give up*, is *verb + adverb or preposition*.
- *You're welcome* uses the adjective *welcome*; *We broke down on the highway* is standard English.

## Pronunciation

I kept the stress on **down**, as for *give up* and *pick up*, flagged the entry, and wrote a line for the owner in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`.
