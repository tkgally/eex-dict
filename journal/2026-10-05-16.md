The selector chose a build cycle again, with a limit of twelve new entries. The automatic claim tool would have picked the auxiliary verbs *have* and *do* first. Under the owner's ruling, those wait for review cycles. So I claimed the next twelve nouns in queue order by hand:

*match, matter, mayor, meal, meaning, measure, measurement, meat, medicine, member, memory, menu*.

Each entry has its senses, grammar, collocations (typical word combinations), idioms where the word has them, learner errors, and notes for translators. Some highlights:

- *matter* has seven idioms (*what's the matter?*, *as a matter of fact*, *no matter what*, *for that matter*, and others). It also has two common errors: *No matter he is tired* and *What is the matter of you?*
- *measure* and *measurement* each carry the error learners make with the other word: *took measurements to stop crime* and *the tailor took my measures*.
- *memory* has a note for translators about *souvenir*, which means "a memory" in some languages. *menu* has one about a similar word that means "a fixed meal" in some languages.
- *mayor* warns about *major*, and *meal* about *three foods a day*.

Two of these spellings also belong to unrelated words: *match*, the stick for making fire, and *meal*, crushed grain as in *oatmeal*. Each entry says so in its usage note. These other words will get their own entries later.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

I logged 35 decisions: 23 changes applied, 12 rejected, none escalated. Reviewer A found several real problems:

- *matter*, physical sense: "the material that everything is made of" was too broad. It now says material that takes up space and has weight.
- *matter*: *it's only a matter of time* does not mean "soon", so that word is gone.
- *memory*: the computer sense mixed up storage space and the working memory of a computer. The definition now gives the everyday meaning, and the explanation separates the two.
- *medicine*: my learner error *I must drink my medicine* was not an error, because you can drink a liquid medicine. I replaced it with *a medicine for my cough*, since the word is usually uncountable.
- *meal*: the definition said "the time when you eat it". It now says "an occasion when people eat food".
- Reviewer B caught a contradiction in *mayor*: the usage note said "no article after *elect*" while a collocation was *elect a mayor*. The rule now covers only *elect someone mayor*.

I rejected these:

- Removing the American label from the musical sense of *measure*. British English says *bar*, and a region label means "mainly used there".
- Calling *to a great degree* an infinitive. It is a phrase with *to* used as a preposition.

Reviewer B again gave partial answers, on five entries this time. A re-run of both reviewers filled three of them.

The tool that adds reverse cross-references put three of its eight back-links on the wrong sense. I moved them:

- *measure* is now under the "something done" sense of *action*;
- *match* is under the "event" sense of *competition*;
- *matter* is under the "event or situation" sense of *affair*.
