# Run report, 2026-10-25 (fourth run of the day)

## Cycle 1: review

**What this cycle did.** The selector, the tool that picks each cycle's job, chose *review*: rereading entries that already exist rather than writing new ones. Its reason was that review had the largest backlog among the jobs allowed today. No entries were waiting as drafts, so I took the next items from `NEXT.md`, the file that hands work from one run to the next.

The first was a small fix left by an earlier run. The entry for *centimeter* gave a conversion fact ("there are 100 centimeters in a meter") inside its definition, after a semicolon. A definition should be a phrase that could replace the word in a sentence. It now reads "a small unit for measuring length, one of the 100 equal parts of a meter". The usage note now says that the word is written *cm* after a number.

The rest of the cycle continued the coverage pass. This pass rereads the most common words (band 1, the dictionary's top frequency group) to check that no current meaning is missing. It goes through the nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs with the most senses first. This cycle took the six that the previous review named: *strong*, *time*, *stand*, *sharp*, *set* and *keep*.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Two reviewer models read each entry field by field. Reviewer A is a Claude model and reviewer B is a Google model. They raised 102 points on the seven entries, 20 of them "blocking", meaning the entry should not stand until the point is settled. I accepted 89 and rejected 13. Nothing needed to go to the owner.

Missing meanings were added:
- *strong* now covers rude language (*strong language*) and likely chances (*a strong chance of rain*).
- *stand* now covers being strong enough not to be damaged (*these plates can stand high heat*) and the collocation *stand trial*.
- *sharp* now covers short, loud sounds (*a sharp knock*) and an informal sense meaning stylish (*a sharp suit*).
- *set* now covers *set a trap*.
- *keep* now covers keeping a quality or control (*keep your balance*), saving something for someone (*keep a seat for me*), not telling someone something (*kept the news from her parents*), and following a rule or path (*keep to the schedule*).
- *time* gained the phrase *most of the time*.

Several fixes were about quality, not coverage. *Strong* had one sense that mixed "good at", "successful" and "likely to win". It is now narrower, and the "likely" meaning has its own subsense. In *stand*, the very common *can't stand* sense now comes before the rarer "put something upright" sense. Its definition now says "dislike" as well as "accept". *Time* claimed that *the last time* takes the present perfect, as *the first time* does. That is wrong (*It was the last time I saw him*), so the claim was removed.

Three learner errors broke the house rule against invented mistakes. Two were made-up regular past tenses (*standed*, *setted*). The third was a generic grammar slip (*a sharp increasing*) that has nothing to do with the word itself. All three were removed, along with one more on *time* that the reviewer judged invented. Three translator notes said only "translate each sense on its own", which the rules forbid. Each was replaced with a specific trap. For *sharp*, for example, the note now says that in some languages the word for "sharp" also means spicy, but an English *sharp* taste is sour, not hot. Many facts were repeated in two or three fields, and the extra copies were cut.

Most of the rejections followed the keyword rule: an idiom is filed under its first noun, or under its first verb if it has no noun. *Stand a chance*, *stand your ground*, *keep in touch*, *keep someone company* and *strong point* are already under *chance*, *ground*, *touch*, *company* and *point*. *Going strong* and *look sharp* belong under *go* and *look*, but those entries do not have them yet. `NEXT.md` now asks the next reading of those verbs to add them.
