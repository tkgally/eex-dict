## What this cycle did

The scheduler chose a build cycle (new entries from the queue), at most twelve; I wrote twelve.

The claim tool (which reserves words so that two runs never write the same one) first picked the three auxiliary verbs *be*, *do*, and *have*. The owner's ruling of 2026-10-05 says these are to be done one at a time, in review cycles, by moving senses out of the existing verb entries. So I released them back to the queue and took three other words instead. Two of them, the adverbs *home* (*go home*) and *a lot* (*I like it a lot*), had been noted for several days as core words that the queue had ranked too low. The third was the next noun in order, *material*.

The twelve entries:

- **Nouns:** *machine*, *man*, *manager*, *manner*, *map*, *mark*, *market*, *marriage*, *mass*, *material*.
- **Adverbs:** *home* and *a lot*.

Each entry has its common senses, grammar, collocations (typical word combinations), idioms, learner errors, and translator notes. Some examples:

- *man* has six idioms (*a man of his word*, *every man for himself*, *to a man*, and others). It also has two errors that learners really make: *two mans*, and *the mens' room*.
- *mark* has six idioms (*hit the mark*, *make your mark*, *on your mark, get set, go*).
- *market* separates five senses: the place, the American food store, a trade (*the job market*), demand (*a market for healthy snacks*), and the economic system.
- *home* (adverb) explains why there is no *to* in *go home* but there is one in *go to their home*.
- *a lot* (adverb) covers *very much*, *a lot better*, and *often*, consistent with the noun *lot*.

All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each entry) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, and by the open pronouncing data for American English.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models review every field. I logged 41 decisions: 30 changes applied, 11 rejected, none sent to the owner.

Reviewer A was useful this time:

- *marriage*: the definition said "partners for life". It was right that marriage does not have to last for life, so that is gone.
- *marriage*: it also said that *marriage* does not mean the ceremony. I disagreed, because formal writing does use it that way (*the marriage took place at the town hall*). I kept that sense but labeled it formal, and said that *wedding* is the everyday word.
- *market*: my "economic system" sense described only a free market. It now covers any market system, and the explanation says what *the free market* adds.
- *home*: I had marked *be home* as American. Only *stay home* is mainly American; *Is Sam home?* is general. Fixed.

I rejected some points. One was a claim that American soccer teams have managers; most American teams have a head coach, and baseball is the exception, as the entry says. Another said that *sign of question* is not a real learner error; it is a common literal translation from several European languages.

Reviewer B, which moved to a stronger model this morning, again answered three entries (*a lot*, *man*, *manner*) with only its complaints instead of a verdict for every field. This happened on three of eleven entries in the first review cycle that used the new model. I re-ran both reviewers on those three entries, as the note on this problem says. *man* and *manner* then got full answers, both clean. *a lot* again came back partial (5 of 43 fields). I decided its four points: one applied (from reviewer A, on *lots*), and three rejected because they went against house style. The note `wiki/notes/reviewer-b-partial-verdicts.md` now records the recurrence. The fix to the review tool should come in the next lint cycle.

Automatic back-links on *crowd*, *lot*, and *equipment* were checked and are right.
