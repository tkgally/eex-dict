# 2026-10-20, cycle 4 of the 12:44 UTC run: seven phrasal verbs from *look forward to* to *move on*

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: new entries from the queue (the ordered list of words still to write). The queue is in the most common phrasal verbs (verb-plus-particle units such as *give up*). I claimed ten rather than the twelve allowed, since this was the run's last cycle and entries must be as full as the first cycle's:

*listen to, look at, look for, look forward to, look like, look out, look up, make up, move in, move on*.

Three were not drafted. *Listen to*, *look at*, and *look for* are a verb followed by its usual preposition, and their meanings are already senses of `listen-v` and `look-v` (*listen to music*, *look at this picture*, *I'm looking for my keys*). Under the house rule they are marked as duplicates in the queue. *Look like* was drafted, because its main meaning (*resemble*: *you look like your mother*) is not a sense of `look-v`.

The other seven were drafted in full. Some points a learner needs: *look forward to* is followed by an -ing form (*look forward to seeing you*), the most common error with it; *make up* has six unrelated senses (invent, form, become friends again, complete an amount, prepare a bed, put on makeup), and its translator's note says to choose by the object; *move in* takes **into** before the place and **with** before a person.

## Pronunciation and forms

All 14 transcriptions were verified by the pronunciation panel, including *look like* with the stress on *look* (all three panel models agreed). The verb forms come from the inflection rules and the irregular-verb table.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 45 issues; I applied 31 and rejected 14. reviewer-a was right far more often than usual here, especially on *make up* (fifteen issues):

- Several of my grammar notes were too absolute. I had said *make up* (form) has no continuous form and its word order never changes, and that *look like* is never continuous; all three are possible (*women are making up a larger share*; *he's looking more like his father*). The notes and grammar codes now say less.
- *Make up* sense 1 no longer says the inventing is done *to trick someone* (people make up songs and games too); sense 2 now states both constructions (*the parts make up the whole*; *the whole is made up of the parts*); the *prepare* sense lost *make up a list*, which belongs elsewhere; the makeup sense is now transitive only.
- *Move in* sense 2 had mixed three uses (the police moving in, a storm moving in, companies *moving in on* a market). It now covers only coming closer to take control or attack.
- *Look out for someone* (protect) is used in British English too, so its *American* label came off (both reviewers said so).
- Opening summaries of *look up* and *make up* were rewritten; two doubtful learner errors were removed (*make out a story*, *He is looking like his father*).

Rejected: reviewer-b's standing objections to the pattern *verb + adverb or preposition* (house practice); *watch out* as a synonym of *look out* (it is a real phrasal verb, queued); *Look out for our new store* is natural.

Two automatic back-links (reverse cross-references) landed on the wrong sense and were moved by hand: *visit* now lists *look up* under *see a person*, and *continue* lists *move on* under *go further*.
