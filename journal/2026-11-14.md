Some points of note:

- *penny* has two subsenses, the American one-cent coin and the British coin. Its plural is *pennies* for the coins but *pence* for a price, and a learner error shows the trap: *it costs fifty pence*, not *fifty pennies*. A second sense covers *not a penny* and *worth every penny*. Four idioms are included: *a penny for your thoughts*, *the penny drops*, *cost a pretty penny*, and *pinch pennies*.
- *petrol* and *pension* carry translator's notes on false friends. In several languages a word like *petrol* means crude oil from the ground, and a word like *pension* also means a small hotel.
- *perspective* has the idiom *put something in perspective*. *Penalty* has the formal phrase *on penalty of something*.
- *personnel* is marked formal in its main sense. It points to *personal*, which learners often confuse with it; that entry already holds the learner error.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 44 issues, 6 of them blocking. I applied 39 and rejected 5. Nothing went to the owner.

Fixes I accepted:

- **personnel:** *holiday pay* became the American *vacation pay*.
- **performer:** a usage line that only repeated the collocations was removed.
- **penny:** one fact (*pence* versus *pennies*) appeared three times, and the copy in the subsense was removed. An example about what stamps used to cost was replaced, because examples must not make checkable claims about the past.

Objections I rejected: four times, one reviewer said that *often before another noun* is not a code from the closed grammar list. It is in the list, and this objection is already recorded in `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md` as a known pattern of that reviewer.

**Other work.** Three links back from older entries were placed by hand: *staff* now points to *personnel*, *personal* to *personnel*, and *oil* to *petrol*. Five pairs of entries pointed at each other under different kinds of link (one called it a synonym, the other a word to compare). These were aligned so that each pair agrees. Eight words the new entries mention were queued, among them *penalize* and *pensioner*.
