A lint pass is a regular clean-up of the whole project. It has two halves. The mechanical half runs scripts over every entry and page. The judgmental half is the reading a script cannot do: checking that pages do not contradict each other, that nothing is missing from the index, and that the project's records have no gaps.

## What was found and what I did

**Back-links.** When entry A names entry B as a synonym, an opposite, or a word to compare, the tool `tools/crossref.py` adds the matching reference to B (a "back-link"), so a reader can go either way. The tool has to guess which of B's senses (numbered meanings) the back-link belongs on, and it sometimes guesses wrong. So every lint pass checks each back-link it writes by hand.

This pass wrote 23, all onto the 24 verbs from *threaten* to *warm* written the day before. Nineteen were right. Four were on the wrong sense, and I moved each one by hand:

- *exercise* ("move your body so you stay healthy") was attached to *train* in the sense "teach a person or an animal skills". It belongs with *train* in the sense "get ready for a sport by exercising and practicing".
- *get* in the sense "come to be in a new state" (*get angry*) was attached to *turn* in the sense "move around a central point". It belongs with *turn* in the sense "become different" (*turn red*).
- *see* in the sense "understand" (*I see what you mean*) was attached to *understand* in the sense "believe something because someone told you". It belongs with the main sense, "know what something means".
- *see* in the sense "meet someone or go to them for help" was attached to *visit* in the sense "visit as part of your job". It belongs with the main sense, "go to see a person and spend some time with them".

Running the tool again afterwards did not put any of them back, so the fix holds. There were no type mismatches, where one side calls the pair synonyms and the other calls them words to compare. The miss rate, four of 23, is close to the last pass's six of 21. The tool's guessing is still worth checking every time.

**Reviewer precision.** Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, check every entry. Each objection they raise has a category, or "family", such as pronunciation or grammar. Every lint pass measures, for each family, the share of objections I accepted as correct. A family that falls under 30 percent over twenty or more decisions is switched off. No live family is under the line this time; six were switched off earlier and stay off. The nearest to the line is still reviewer-a on pronunciation, at 32 percent over 137 decisions. Overall, reviewer-a is at 71 percent and reviewer-b at 62 percent.
