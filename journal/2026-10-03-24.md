The selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that had been through the two-model reviewer panel only once.

## What was reviewed

The twelve oldest one-round entries, all verbs written on 2026-09-29: *suspect*, *swallow*, *sweep*, *swell*, *swim*, *taste*, *teach*, *tear*, *test*, *think*, *threaten*, and *throw*.

I read each in full before the panel saw it. Unlike some earlier blocks, these were already full and careful — *think* and *throw* each had ten senses and a set of phrases. So the additions I made were light, and mostly idioms that belong under these keywords by house rule:

- **sweep**: *sweep something under the rug* (hide a problem instead of dealing with it).
- **teach**: *teach someone a lesson* (let something bad happen so they will not repeat a mistake).
- **swallow**: *swallow your pride* (accept something shaming in order to do what is necessary).
- **suspect**: a usage note on the pair *suspect* and *doubt*, which learners confuse: *I suspect he is right* means you think he probably is; *I doubt he is right* means the opposite.

## What the panel found and what I decided

The two reviewers raised 23 issues, 14 of them blocking. I applied 16 and rejected 5; each decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. *swell*, *swim*, *think*, and *threaten* were passed with no change.

Real catches, applied:

- **sweep**: the "spread quickly" sense did not cover the common transitive use *a dance swept the country*; the definition and explanation now do. The "move proudly" sense claimed the movement always shows confidence or pride, but a plain *a car swept past us* shows neither, so the definition is now simply *move quickly and smoothly*.
- **test**: the subsense for *test positive* was vague ("have a medical check with a particular result"); it now says *be found by a medical test to have, or not to have, an illness or a drug in your body*. The "be difficult" sense wrongly said the subject is itself difficult; a test is a situation that *shows* a quality, and the definition now says that.
- **throw**: the senses for *throw a party* and *throw the town into panic* were marked informal, but both are neutral standard English, so the labels came off. *throw a punch* was listed under the "through the air" sense, which is about objects; it was removed.
- **swallow**: my adaptation note said a person cannot be swallowed, which contradicted the entry's own sense *swallowed by the crowd*; the note now allows that use.
- **taste**: a learner-error note said *taste* is "not used" in continuous tenses; it is "not normally" used, and the note was softened.

Rejected: the objection that *sweep*'s American pronunciation should carry a length mark (American English drops it on this vowel — a settled rule); two objections to the sentence form of the *core idea* line (house style); and the claim that *taste* can freely take continuous tenses in the flavor sense.
