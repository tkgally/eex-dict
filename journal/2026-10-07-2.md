# 2026-10-07, 00:43 UTC run, cycle 2: twelve nouns, *priest* to *progress*

## What this cycle did

The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue of words still to be written. The day's budget was fresh, so paid review calls were possible. I claimed the next twelve band 1 nouns in queue order. Band 1 is the dictionary's own estimate of the most common, most useful words. I wrote all twelve from my own knowledge and the house style guide: *priest, print, prison, prisoner, prize, problem, process, product, profession, profit, program, progress*. All twelve went through the full checking pipeline and are now marked `reviewed`, which means the public site will show them.

Some points worth knowing about the new entries:

- **print** has five senses: the letters on a page, a picture copy, a photograph, a mark left by a foot or finger, and a pattern on cloth. It also has the phrases *in print*, *out of print* and *the fine print*.
- **prison** explains a trap many learners fall into. *In prison* and *go to prison*, with no article, mean the punishment; *the prison* means the building.
- **problem** has a sense for *have a problem with someone*, a phrase entry for *no problem*, and a comparison of *problem*, *trouble*, *issue* and *difficulty*.
- **program** records the British spelling *programme*, which British English uses except in computing. It has an American-labeled sense for a course of study.
- **progress** is uncountable. Its learner errors cover *a big progress* and *do progress*.
- **prize** warns against confusing it with *price*, which differs only in its last sound.

## What the checks found

Each word's American and British pronunciations were checked by a panel of models. They were also compared with the open CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, a free list of American pronunciations that is consulted but never stored here. All 24 transcriptions were confirmed. The plural forms came from the rules.

Two outside reviewer models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, then read every field. Together they raised 37 issues: 33 from reviewer-a and 4 from reviewer-b. I applied 27 and rejected 10, each with a logged reason. The applied fixes included the following:

- A clearer **core idea** (the one-sentence summary at the top of a long entry) for *print* and *profession*. The first versions left out some senses or claimed too much.
- The *prison* definition now covers the punishment as well as the building.
- A better definition for the *high priest* phrase.
- Some unnatural examples and collocations were removed: *a rich prize*, *set a problem* (mainly British), and an example about a coin collection.
- A learner error on *profit* was dropped, because *many profits* can be correct.
- An adaptation note on *product* claimed that products are made only in factories. That was wrong, since farms grow products too, and it is fixed. (Adaptation notes are short warnings for translators.)
- reviewer-b pointed out that *a prize cow* is really an adjective use. The noun entry now points to it in one clause, and *prize* as an adjective is queued as its own entry.

I rejected these:

- A demand to make *high priest* its own entry. The house rule keeps a phrase under its first noun.
- The claim that a printed concert *program* is chiefly British. Americans use it too, for concerts, plays and graduations.
- An objection to the note that *profit* sounds exactly like *prophet*. It is a real trap, and the note mentions no particular language.
- A formatting objection that goes against a settled house rule on adaptation notes.

## Linking and vocabulary

The definition-vocabulary check found six words in definitions that are outside the core list and have no entry yet, such as *ceremonies*, *broadcast* and *calculation*. I reworded each one. The cross-reference tool added eleven back-links on other entries, and I checked each by hand. Two were on the wrong sense and were moved: *print* on *mark*, and *problem* on *exercise*. Three pairs had a type mismatch, where one side called the link a synonym and the other called it a comparison. I settled each on the type the older entry already used. The tool also queued 31 missing target words.
