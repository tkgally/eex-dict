Some highlights. *High* has seven senses (tall, far above, amount or level, rank, *a high opinion*, sound, and the drug sense), the phrase *high and mighty*, and two learner errors that learners of many languages make: *My brother is very high* (use *tall*) and *The prices are expensive* (a price is *high*; the thing is *expensive*). ## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models (reviewer-a and reviewer-b) read every field. *Hungry* and *ill* came back clean. The others drew 37 issues, 34 marked blocking. I applied 27 and rejected 10, each logged with a reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`; none needed the owner.

Applied, among others:

- *hurt*: sense 1 (injured) used passive-verb examples (*Nobody was seriously hurt*, *Two children got hurt*). Those belong to the verb under the house rule of one part of speech per entry. The examples now show the adjective before a noun (*a hurt ankle*, *its hurt paw*), and the usage note says that *was hurt* and *get hurt* are forms of the verb.
- *heavy*: four definitions were too narrow for their own examples. Sense 2 now covers *a heavy smoker* (*doing something a lot*); sense 3 covers *a heavy blow* (*serious*); *heavy wood* and *a heavy door*, which are about weight, left the *thick* sense; sense 6 no longer calls deep sleep *slow*. A learner-error note had called *strong rain* wrong; it is only less usual, so the note now names only *big*.
- *honest*: sense 3 now covers *an honest mistake* (one made without any wish to cheat or cause harm).
- *illegal*: the usage note on *illegal workers* was rewritten to say what the word means of people (their stay or work is not allowed by law) as well as that many people find the use offensive.
- *high*, *hot*, *human*, *holy*: a drug sense redefined as *under the effect of drugs*; *a hot topic* is *discussed a lot*, not necessarily controversial; *human* now contrasts people with *other animals*; two synonyms that did not fit were removed.

Rejected: reviewer-b again called *comparative with more* an invalid grammar code, four times (it is in the closed list; this is now its main source of noise, and its precision this cycle was 0.2); reviewer-a's claim that *light* is no opposite of *heavy* in *heavy rain* or *a heavy smoker* (*light rain* and *a light smoker* are normal English); a request to split *important* into two senses that translate the same way.

The back-link tool added mirror cross-references to five older entries. One landed on the wrong sense: *ill* on `bad-adj` went from sense 1 (*poor quality*) to sense 2 (*unpleasant or harmful*).
