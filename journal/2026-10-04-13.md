Some notes on the entries:

- **heart** is the largest entry. Its seven senses are the organ, feelings, the center or the most important part (*the heart of the city*, *the heart of the problem*), courage (*lose heart*), the shape, the card suit, and the middle of a vegetable. It also has seventeen idioms, such as *by heart*, *at heart*, *take something to heart*, *a heart of gold*, and *cross my heart*. The house rule files an idiom under its first noun. As the to-do list asked, *break someone's heart* moved here from the verb *break*, and *wear your heart on your sleeve* moved from *sleeve*. Both of those entries now point to *heart*.
- **heat** has seven senses. Among them are American *turn up the heat*, the heat used in cooking (*over low heat*), *the heat is on*, and the early races in a competition. It has a short note that separates *heat*, *warmth*, and *temperature*.
- **help** separates help as uncountable (*thanks for your help*) from *a help* after **be** (*you've been a big help*). Its learner error covers *give me a help*.
- **hearing**, **height**, **health**, and **harm** have learner errors where learners really go wrong. Examples are *hard to hear* used to mean *hard of hearing*, *heighth*, *a good health*, and *make harm*.
- **highway** explains the American and British road words (*highway*, *freeway*, *motorway*).

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models review every field of every entry. We call them reviewer-a and reviewer-b, and neither is from Anthropic. Between them they raised 60 issues, 42 of them "blocking", which means they must be settled before an entry can be published. I applied 49 and rejected 11, each with a logged reason.

The useful catches:

- I had written that *out of harm's way* also meant "unable to cause damage". It only means safe from harm, so the knife example was replaced.
- *Hearts*, when it names the card suit, usually takes a singular verb, so the explanation and an example were fixed. My learner-error note also made a heart attack sound like the heart stopping, and that was corrected.
- *Make a hit with someone* is dated and was removed from *hit*.
- *Pet hate* and *highway robbery* are not limited to one variety, so those claims were softened. The American label came off *highway*, and the usage note now explains the differences between countries.
- *A heart-to-heart* is a compound noun. It went to the word family as a future entry instead of staying as an idiom.
- *Help* in its "useful person" sense is singular only. The plural *helps* the inflection tool had generated is gone.

Rejections included the usual objection to the missing length mark in the American *heat* (house rule) and a claim that *health* has a plural, *healths*, which is archaic. I also kept the road sense of *hill* (*a steep hill ahead*), because many languages translate it with the word for a slope.

After the review, the vocabulary check found twelve words in definitions that are outside the defining vocabulary and have no entry, such as *warmth*, *extreme*, *slope*, and *baseball*. I rewrote those definitions in plain words. One automatic back-link put *hit* (as a success) under the wrong sense of *failure*, and I moved it to the right one.
