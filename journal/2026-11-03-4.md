# Journal, 2026-11-03 (fourth run of the day)

## Cycle 1: closure

**What this cycle did.** The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose *closure*: building entries for words the dictionary already uses or points to but does not yet define. The "closure" source of the queue (words found in definitions) is empty, so the twelve words came from the "family" source: words named as relatives of existing entries. All twelve are band-2 nouns (band 2 is the second most important frequency group): *basics*, *basketball*, *bathtub*, *bedtime*, *beginner*, *being*, *bet*, *billionaire*, *biology*, *bite*, *black* (the color as a noun), and *blessing*. The dictionary now has 3,643 entries, and every one is marked reviewed.

Each entry was drafted from scratch and then went through the full pipeline. A checker confirmed the plural forms from the spelling rules (*basics* and *biology* correctly have no plural). A panel of pronunciation models agreed on all 24 transcriptions, one American and one British for each word, and where a check against an open pronouncing dictionary was possible, it agreed too. Both reviewers then read every field of every entry.

The longer entries were *bite* (five senses: a bite of food, an insect or dog bite, a quick meal, a sharp taste or cold, and a fish taking the food on a line), *bet* (money risked, a guess as in *my bet is that...*, and a choice as in *your best bet*, plus the idioms *hedge your bets* and *all bets are off*), *being* (a living creature, the formal "existence" sense in *come into being*, and the literary *with her whole being*), and *blessing* (a lucky good thing, approval, and a prayer, plus *a blessing in disguise*, *a mixed blessing*, and *count your blessings*). *Beginner* has the idiom *beginner's luck*, *basics* has *back to basics*, and *black* (noun) has *in the black*.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 43 issues. Five were "blocking", meaning the entry could not be marked reviewed until I settled them. I applied 35 issues and rejected 8, each with a logged reason.

- In *bite*, the "sharp quality" sense was marked uncountable even though its examples say *a bite*. Both reviewers caught this, and I changed it to "countable or uncountable".
- In *black*, the collocation *paint something black* was listed, but there *black* is an adjective, not a noun. I removed it.
- One reviewer said *in black and white* was missing from *black*. I rejected this: the idiom is already in the adjective entry for *black*, and each phrase has only one home.

The minor fixes I applied included a garbled definition in *blessing* (approval "from someone who has the power to stop" a plan), a signpost (the short label that helps a reader pick a sense) that was over the three-word limit in *biology*, an invented learner error in *black* that I removed, and a new example for the common *it's a safe bet that...* in *bet*. I rejected a request to hand-mark the headword in a *bite* idiom (that marking is only for split phrasal verbs) and a claim that "often before another noun" is not a noun code (it is one in our closed list).

**What did not go smoothly.** One reviewer wanted the word *bait* in the fishing sense of *bite*. I logged that change as applied, but *bait* is outside the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words definitions must use) and has no entry yet, so the final wording is "the food that has been put on a fishing line to catch it". The decision ledger still says "bait added", and the log entry records the correction. The vocabulary check also caught *chess*, *euros*, *property*, *occasion*, and *complicated* in my first drafts, and I replaced them with plainer words. A cross-reference from *blessing* to *approval* could not be matched to a sense automatically, so I placed it by hand on the "permission" sense of *approval*.

**Spend.** US$0.38 for this cycle (reviewer B and the pronunciation panel; reviewer A runs on the subscription at no charge). The day's total is US$2.53 of the US$15 cap.

**What is next.** Further closure cycles continue through the family rows in queue order, then the cross-reference rows. The hand-claim of `rocket-n-2` noted in `NEXT.md` is still waiting.

**For the owner.** Nothing new this cycle. The open items in `NEXT.md` stand: the names-after-titles question, the out-of-date reviewer line in `PROJECT.md`, and the pronunciation switch-off for reviewer A.

## Cycle 2: build

**What this cycle did.** The scheduler chose *build*: new entries taken from the main queue in its set order, which is now working through band-2 nouns. Twelve were built: *bay*, *bear*, *beef*, *belly*, *belongings*, *bin*, *blouse*, *blow*, *blue* (the color, the music, and the sad feeling), *blueberry*, *board game*, and *bombing*. The dictionary now has 3,655 entries, all reviewed. The pronunciation panel verified all 24 transcriptions.

**A tool problem, fixed in data.** The inflection checker (`tools/inflect.py`, which writes plural and verb forms from spelling rules) gave *blouse* the plural *blice*. It has a rule that turns words ending in *louse* into *lice*, as in *woodlouse*, and *blouse* matched it by accident. The tool already has a list of words that end in one of these patterns but are regular (*human*, *German*, *talisman*), so I added *blouse* to that list in the exceptions table, the place where such words belong. The plural is now *blouses*, and the tool's own tests still pass. No code was changed.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The reviewers raised 63 issues. Ten were blocking (they had to be settled before the entry could be marked reviewed). I applied 59 issues and rejected 4.

- **bear.** My examples used a toy bear under the "wild animal" sense. I added a sub-sense for the soft toy (*a teddy bear*). A reviewer also pointed out that the verb *bear* (to carry or accept something) is an unrelated word, not a member of the animal's word family, so I removed that link.
- **blue.** My usage note said the music is always *the blues*, but people also say *I like blues* and *a blues singer*. I rewrote the note. An example claiming that the blues influenced rock music was a real-world historical claim, which examples may not make, so I replaced it.
- **Out of the blue.** Both reviewers said *out of the blue* belongs under the noun *blue*, not the adjective, because the house rule files an idiom under its first noun. They are right, so I moved it from `blue-adj` to `blue-n` and gave it new examples. In cycle 1 I had rejected the same argument for *in black and white* (it stays under the adjective *black*). That idiom now looks like it should move too, so I left a note in `NEXT.md` for the next review of `black-adj`.

The minor fixes I applied included marking the *parking bay* and *loading bay* sense as British, making the *beef* "complaint" sense also cover a long-running argument (*they've had beef for years*), adding the idiom *a low blow*, and removing a "military" label from *bombing*, since many bombings are not military. I rejected two requests to label the waste sense of *bin* as British: *recycling bin* and *trash bin* are everyday American English, and the usage note already explains how British and American use differ.

**What did not go smoothly.** Two of my decision commands matched more than one reviewer issue on the same field, so the tool logged nothing for them. I caught this by counting the ledger, and I logged both again with a quoted phrase to pick the right issue. The defining-vocabulary check caught *complaint*, *underside*, *southern*, and *quarrel* in my definitions, and I replaced them with plainer words.

**Spend.** US$0.34 this cycle; US$2.88 so far today, against the US$15 cap.

**What is next.** More band-2 nouns from the main queue, then closure cycles.

**For the owner.** Nothing new.
