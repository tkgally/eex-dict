# Journal, 2026-11-03 (fourth run of the day)

## Cycle 1: closure

**What this cycle did.** The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) chose *closure*: building entries for words the dictionary already uses or points to but does not yet define. The "closure" source of the queue (words found in definitions) is empty, so the twelve words came from the "family" source: words named as relatives of existing entries. All twelve are band-2 nouns (band 2 is the second most important frequency group): *basics*, *basketball*, *bathtub*, *bedtime*, *beginner*, *being*, *bet*, *billionaire*, *biology*, *bite*, *black* (the color as a noun), and *blessing*. The dictionary now has 3,643 entries, and every one is marked reviewed.

Each entry was drafted from scratch and then went through the full pipeline. A checker confirmed the plural forms from the spelling rules (*basics* and *biology* correctly have no plural). A panel of pronunciation models agreed on all 24 transcriptions, one American and one British for each word, and where a check against an open pronouncing dictionary was possible, it agreed too. Both reviewers then read every field of every entry.

The longer entries were *bite* (five senses: a bite of food, an insect or dog bite, a quick meal, a sharp taste or cold, and a fish taking the food on a line), *bet* (money risked, a guess as in *my bet is that...*, and a choice as in *your best bet*, plus the idioms *hedge your bets* and *all bets are off*), *being* (a living creature, the formal "existence" sense in *come into being*, and the literary *with her whole being*), and *blessing* (a lucky good thing, approval, and a prayer, plus *a blessing in disguise*, *a mixed blessing*, and *count your blessings*). *Beginner* has the idiom *beginner's luck*, *basics* has *back to basics*, and *black* (noun) has *in the black*.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 43 issues. Five were "blocking", meaning the entry could not be marked reviewed until I settled them. I applied 35 issues and rejected 8, each with a logged reason.

- In *bite*, the "sharp quality" sense was marked uncountable even though its examples say *a bite*. Both reviewers caught this, and I changed it to "countable or uncountable".
- In *black*, the collocation *paint something black* was listed, but there *black* is an adjective, not a noun. I removed it.
- One reviewer said *in black and white* was missing from *black*. I rejected this: the idiom is already in the adjective entry for *black*, and each phrase has only one home.

The minor fixes I applied included a garbled definition in *blessing* (approval "from someone who has the power to stop" a plan), a signpost (the short label that helps a reader pick a sense) that was over the three-word limit in *biology*, an invented learner error in *black* that I removed, and a new example for the common *it's a safe bet that...* in *bet*. I rejected a request to hand-mark the headword in a *bite* idiom (that marking is only for split phrasal verbs) and a claim that "often before another noun" is not a noun code (it is one in our closed list).

**What did not go smoothly.** One reviewer wanted the word *bait* in the fishing sense of *bite*. I logged that change as applied, but *bait* is outside the defining vocabulary (the list of plain words definitions must use) and has no entry yet, so the final wording is "the food that has been put on a fishing line to catch it". The decision ledger still says "bait added", and the log entry records the correction. The vocabulary check also caught *chess*, *euros*, *property*, *occasion*, and *complicated* in my first drafts, and I replaced them with plainer words. A cross-reference from *blessing* to *approval* could not be matched to a sense automatically, so I placed it by hand on the "permission" sense of *approval*.
