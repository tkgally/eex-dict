# Run report, 2026-11-01 (second run)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from what is most overdue) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue of words still to be added. It allowed up to twelve new entries and US$3 of paid model calls. Nothing was stranded from earlier runs: there were no open pull requests and no leftover `claude/*` branches, the inbox (the folder where the owner leaves instructions) was empty, and there were no reader reports of problems with entries.

The cycle wrote twelve band-2 verbs, the next ones in queue order: *scatter*, *schedule*, *scrape*, *scream*, *seat*, *seek*, *seize*, *select*, *separate*, *settle*, *shift*, and *shiver*. ("Band 2" is the second of five frequency levels the project assigns by model vote; band 1 is the most common words.) Each entry has every sense a learner needs, signposts, grammar codes, two to four examples per sense, collocations, cross-references, and notes only where a learner would otherwise go wrong. *Settle* is the largest, with seven senses (end an argument, decide, go to live, get comfortable, become calm, come to rest, pay). *Schedule* records both the American *sk* and the British *sh* pronunciations, in the same way as the existing noun entry. *Seat* warns against the common mistake *seat down*, and *seek* warns against *seek for*.

**Checks.** Every inflected form came from the rules or the exceptions table (*sought* for *seek*). All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each verb) passed the agreement check: the pronunciation panel of models agreed with our transcription, and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary agreed for American English.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewer models (reviewer A, a Claude model, and reviewer B, a Google model) raised 85 issues, 14 of them blocking, meaning the entry could not be marked reviewed until each was decided. I applied 71 and rejected 14, with a logged reason for each; none needed the owner. The main fixes:

- *scatter*: the third sense (*farms are scattered across the hills*) was coded as a passive verb use but defined as a state (*be in many places*). Both reviewers caught the mismatch; it is now defined as an action, *spread things over a wide area*, used mostly in the passive.
- *settle*: three examples did not fit their senses. One was transitive (*settled the valley*) in a sense marked intransitive; one used the phrasal verb *settle up* inside an ordinary sense; and one (*ginger can settle your stomach*) made a health claim, which the example rules forbid. All three were replaced. *Settle up* is now linked as a phrasal verb to be written later.
- *shift*: a catch-all sense joined two meanings (*shift a stain* and *shift old stock*), so it was split into two British informal senses. The figurative American *shift gears* (*let's shift gears and talk about next week*) was added as a subsense.
- *seat*: the formal *please be seated* was a separate sense defined as *sit down*, which clashed with its transitive grammar and with *remain seated*. It is now a subsense of the first sense, defined *sit down, or be sitting*.
- *scream* gained an informal sense, *show clearly* (*that house screams money*). *Scrape* in *scrape a pass* is now labeled British.

Rejected objections: reviewer B asked for hand marks on *sought* (the site marks listed forms itself); both reviewers asked for idioms whose keyword is another word (*settle a score*, *when the dust settles*, *scrape the bottom of the barrel*), which by house rule live under their first noun; and reviewer B objected to a closed grammar label. Eighteen links back from older entries (for example from *pick* to *select*, and from *pay* to *settle*) could not be placed by the cross-reference tool, so I placed them by hand in 17 existing entries.

**Spend.** US$0.42 for this cycle; US$1.39 of the US$15 daily cap so far today.
