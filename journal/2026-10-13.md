This was a **build** cycle: new entries written from the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written). I claimed the next twelve band-1 words (band 1 is the most common words, the ones learners meet first) and wrote all twelve: *silent*, *silly*, *silver*, *similar*, *simple*, *single*, *skilled*, *slim*, *slow*, *small*, *smart*, and *smooth*. All are adjectives. Each went through the full pipeline (format check, generated word forms, a pronunciation check, two outside reviewers, my decisions on what they found, and the final checks), and all twelve are now marked `reviewed`, which means they will appear on the public site.

Some points from the entries:

- *silent* has six senses, from a silent person to a silent film and a silent letter (*the k in knife*), and the phrases *the silent majority* and *the strong, silent type*. It explains when to use *quiet* and when *silent*.
- *simple* has seven senses, including the grammar use (*the simple present*) and an old-fashioned, hurtful use (*a bit simple*), which carries a warning.
- *single* warns learners that a child with no brothers or sisters is *an only child*, not *a single child*, and notes that the British *single ticket* is the American *one-way ticket*.
- *small* has a learner error for a common mix-up: using *smaller* where English needs *younger*.
- *smart* explains that in American English it usually means intelligent, while in British English it often means well dressed.

All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were confirmed on the first pass. Six one-syllable words briefly carried a stress mark, which the house style forbids; I removed them before review. Two words, *silent* and *skilled*, needed an extra grammar code on one sense before the word-form tool would decide that they make their comparatives with *more*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers (outside language models from two different companies, who check every field) raised 60 issues, 54 of them marked blocking. I accepted 49 and rejected 11. None needed to go to the owner.

Most of the work was on two entries. On *simple* (15 issues) the reviewers were right that the *simple life* sense means modest, not just ordinary, and that *simple people* would be read as the hurtful sense; I redefined the sense and replaced the example. They were also right that *the simple truth* stresses that a point is plain, not that it is the only thing that matters, and that in grammar *simple* contrasts with continuous forms only (British teaching speaks of the *present perfect simple*). On *small* (12 issues) I redefined the business sense by size rather than activity, rewrote the *feel small* sense, and removed a weak learner error.

I rejected: the claim that `offensive` is an attitude label (it is in the closed register list); two complaints about British region labels on *smart* (our rule is that a region label means *mainly used there*); the claim that *smoothie* is not related to *smooth*; and a request to merge the *simple machine* sense into *easy*, since it has its own collocations and opposite (*complex*).
