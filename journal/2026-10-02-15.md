# 2026-10-02 (16:43 UTC run, cycle 3): twelve entries, *switch* to *deal*

## What this run did

This was the third cycle of the run that started at 16:43 UTC. The first two cycles were merged as pull requests #186 and #187. The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **build**, which means drafting new entries from the queue of words still waiting.

The queue first gave the last two core verbs that the lint pass of 2026-10-02 had found missing, *switch* and *wave*. After them came ten of the most common nouns, in queue order: *cut, damage, dance, danger, dark, data, date, daughter, day,* and *deal*. Some points about what went in:

- *day* has five senses: twenty-four hours, the hours of light, the hours of work, a special day, and a time in the past. It also has thirteen idioms, among them *these days*, *the other day*, *day after day*, *make someone's day*, and *call it a day*. The learner errors cover *everyday* versus *every day* and *on Monday*.
- *deal* separates a business agreement, an offer judged by its price, a way of being treated (*a raw deal*), the amount sense in *a great deal*, and dealing cards.
- *data* explains that it takes a singular verb in everyday English and a plural one in some scientific writing.
- *date* includes the difference between American and British date order, and a learner error that separates a *date* from an *appointment*.

**The keyword rule.** An idiom is listed under the first noun it contains. So *call it a day*, which had been waiting in *call* (verb), now lives in *day*. *Big deal*, which was in *big* (adjective), now lives in *deal*. I wrote both again in my own words and removed the old copies. The fruit called a *date* is a different word with a different origin, so it was left out. It is noted in `NEXT.md` as a future second entry, `date-n-2`.

## Checks and review

All inflections were produced by the rules and verified. All 24 pronunciations were confirmed by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU pronouncing dictionary.

The two reviewer models raised 72 issues, and 38 of them were *blocking*, meaning I had to decide each one before the entries could be marked reviewed. I applied 49 decisions, rejected 23, and escalated none. The main corrections:

- *after dark* means after nightfall, not "during the night". I rewrote the definition and removed *night* as a synonym.
- My notes on *data* said flatly that it is never used with *a*. That is too strong, because technical writing sometimes does it. The notes now speak of ordinary use.
- The price sense of *deal* was defined as any purchase, but *a bad deal* usually means unfair treatment. That sense now reads "an offer or something you buy, judged by how good its price is", and *a bad deal* moved to the treatment sense.
- The learner error for *dance* was about the verb (*go dance*), so I replaced it with a noun error, *Let's have a dancing*.
- Two labels on *date* ("informal", "American") were wrong for the sense meaning a person you go out with, and I removed them.

As usual, the rejected issues were mainly points the house rules already settle. One example is the American transcription of *deal* without a length mark, which follows a fixed house rule.

The basic-vocabulary check made me reword thirteen definitions that used words outside the defining list (*reduction*, *romantic*, *purchase*, *gradually*, and others). Two back-links needed fixing by hand. *switch* was linked from the "become different" sense of *change* and now sits under "replace". The link between *data* and *information* was a synonym on one side and a compare on the other, and I made both sides compare.
