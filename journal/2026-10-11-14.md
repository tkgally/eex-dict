## What the reviewers found and what I decided

They raised 30 issues, 27 of them blocking. *Funeral* and *furniture* came back clean. I logged 30 decisions in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`: 21 applied and 9 rejected. None went to the owner.

Applied:

- *gain*: sense 3 was defined as money from selling something at a higher price, but its own example was *for personal gain*, which usually means any benefit for yourself. Sense 3 now reads *money or another benefit that you get for yourself*.
- *fur*: the sense for animal skin used for clothes also covered *a fur* (a fur coat), which is countable and means something different. The coat is now subsense 2a, with its own examples.
- *fuel*: the figurative sense (*fuel for an argument*) was too broad; it now says *makes a feeling or an argument stronger, or gives people more reason for it*. The adaptation note no longer says fuel is always burned.
- *front*: a front for crime may hide something secret rather than illegal, and a weather front is where two air masses of different temperatures meet, not always cold meeting warm. Both definitions are fixed.
- *future*: the finance sense (*oil futures*) no longer suggests that money itself is the usual thing traded. The repeated link to *tomorrow* under "see also" was removed.
- Smaller fixes: *fail* (a silly mistake) no longer says the person must look stupid; *hollow* lost an awkward example (*the hollow of her hand*); *fruit* has a clearer core idea; the note on *friendship*'s learner error no longer implies one tense is the only choice; *the fun part* was removed from the noun *fun*, because there it is the adjective.

Rejected:

- Reviewer-a wanted *in front*, *out front*, *up front*, and *front and center* moved out of the noun *front*. The house rule files an idiom under its first noun, which here is *front*.
- *Tomorrow* stays as a synonym of *the future*: the *tomorrow* entry has a sense meaning exactly that. *Tail* stays as an opposite of *front* for the back of a line or a vehicle.
- *Hollow* (a hole, or a place where a surface goes in) stays one sense; the uses share one core meaning.
- *In fun* is not formal. *Fun and games*, used straight or as a joke, stays one phrase.
