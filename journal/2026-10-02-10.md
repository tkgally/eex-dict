# 2 October 2026, 12:43 UTC run, cycle 2: a second review of twelve older entries

## What this run did

This was the second cycle of the run that started at 12:43 UTC. (A "cycle" is one complete unit of work with its own pull request, a proposed change that is checked automatically and then merged; the first cycle added twelve nouns, *cost* to *crack*.) The mode selector, the script that picks the kind of work by how long each kind has waited, chose **review**: a second reading of entries that so far have had only one round from the review panel. The panel is two reviewer models from two different companies; each reads every field of an entry and gives a verdict.

I took the twelve oldest entries with only one round, all written on 26 September 2026: the adjectives *relevant* and *urgent* and the verbs *imagine, improve, include, increase, inform, injure, install, intend, interest*, and *introduce*.

Before sending them to the panel I read each one myself, looking for what the notes in `NEXT.md` say early entries tend to lack: learner errors, usage notes, and missing senses. The senses were complete. I added by hand:

- three learner errors: *I need to see you urgent* (the adverb **urgently** is needed), *Please inform me the result* (**inform** needs **of** or **about** before the thing told), and *I installed the app in my phone* (software is installed **on** a phone);
- a short paragraph on *injure*, telling apart **hurt** (everyday, also pain and feelings), **injure** (harm to the body, often in accidents), **wound** (harm from a weapon), and **damage** (for things, not people);
- a fix to a note in *interest* that showed a wrong form in italics, which house style forbids: wrong forms appear only in the learner-error box.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 21 issues, 6 of them marked blocking. I applied 5 and rejected 16, each with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`; none needed the owner.

Applied:

- *include*: the learner-error note said the verb is "not used" in continuous tenses. A reviewer pointed out that forms such as *is including* do occur. The note now says "not normally", which matches the wording the project already uses for *need* and *own*.
- *intend*: the example *He never intended that anyone should get hurt* is grammatical but stiff. It is now *He never intended anyone to get hurt*, and the list of patterns was updated to match.
- Small collocation fixes: *fully inform* is now *keep someone fully informed*, and *slightly injure* is now *be slightly injured*. One pattern label on a passive example of *introduce* was cleared.

Rejected, with reasons:

- A request to merge the second sense of *relevant* (*the relevant department*, meaning the right one for the purpose) into the first (*relevant to the topic*). The two differ in grammar (the second comes only before a noun and has no comparative) and are often translated differently, which is the house test for splitting a sense.
- Flags against notes that open with the headword in quotation marks, and against core-idea lines written as full sentences. Both are house style, already settled and recorded in `NEXT.md` as "fences" (points not to re-argue).
- Removing *dream* and *see* as cross-references from *imagine*. Each mirrors a link from the other entry's matching sense, and removing only one side would be re-created by the next lint pass.
- Adding the British spelling *instal*: *install* is the usual spelling in Britain too.

Reviewer precision is low on this block (about a quarter of reviewer-a's issues were accepted), which is normal for second rounds: most real problems were fixed in the first round, and most of what remains is style the house has already settled.
