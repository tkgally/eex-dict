# Run of 2026-11-02 (second run of the day)

## Cycle 1: closure

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work by weighing what is most overdue) chose *closure*: writing entries for words the dictionary already uses but does not yet define. Ten new entries were written and taken through the full review pipeline, and all ten are now published:

- Three words used in existing definitions: **missile** (the weapon, and the formal sense of an object thrown at someone), **rocket** (into space, as a weapon, and as a firework, with the idiom *it's not rocket science*), and **offensive** as an adjective (rude, unpleasant, attacking, and the American sports sense, which is often said with the stress on the first part).
- Three second entries for words with the same spelling but a different origin, which earlier runs had asked to be written by hand: **bat** the flying animal (with *as blind as a bat* and *like a bat out of hell*), **pound** the place where lost dogs or towed cars are kept, and **bit** the computing unit.
- Four words named in other entries' word-family boxes: **average** as a noun (with *on average* and a learner error, *in average*), **background** (five senses, from a person's life to a phone screen), **backyard** (the American and British senses differ), and **baker** (with the British *the baker's* for the shop, and *a baker's dozen*).

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Each entry goes to two independent reviewer models, which mark every field as fine or as an issue. They raised 32 issues, none of them *blocking* (serious enough to stop publication). I applied 27 and rejected 5, each with a logged reason. The useful catches: an example for the thrown-object sense of *missile* that did not actually show something thrown at people; *keen baker*, which is mainly British; *police pound*, which is not a typical phrase; a plural form of the fixed idiom *like a bat out of hell*; and a missing phrase, *against a background of*. Both reviewers objected to the translator notes on the two homograph entries (*bat*, *pound*), which only repeated that the two words are different; I removed them. The rejections were house-style points: a phrase named as a word takes bold marks, and a restriction on a sense's form belongs in its explanation.

Both reviewers also noted that the British salad leaf called *rocket* (American *arugula*) is missing. It has a different origin, so it needs its own second entry, `rocket-n-2`. The work queue has no way to list a second entry with the same spelling, so it is recorded in `NEXT.md` for a later run to claim by hand.

After review, the vocabulary check found five words in definitions that are outside the defining vocabulary (the limited word list definitions are written in): *furry*, *upside*, *insulting*, and *firework* twice. I rewrote those definitions in plainer words. Three cross-references needed a matching link placed by hand: *offensive* is now listed as a synonym in **rude**, and **garden** now points to **backyard** as the usual American word. The third, a synonym link from *pound* to **shelter**, was removed, because the **shelter** entry has no sense for an animal shelter.
