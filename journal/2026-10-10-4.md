## What this cycle did

An **originality check** tests whether any of our definitions copy a published dictionary. The project's rule is that entries are written from the drafting model's own knowledge, never from a commercial dictionary; this check is how we find out whether that held. The scheduler runs it on every tenth run, and this was one.

The tool `tools/originality_check.py` picked ten fields at random from the whole dictionary (definitions, phrase definitions, and one usage note). For each one I ran an exact-phrase web search and looked only at whether a published dictionary showed the same wording. I also put one question to a reviewer model: does this read as copied, and if so, from where, quoted exactly?

## What the search found

| Entry | What was checked | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| *fog* | "a state in which you cannot think clearly or remember things well" | original |
| *angle* (verb) | a usage note on the fishing sense | original |
| *promise* (verb) | "tell someone that you will certainly do something or that something will certainly happen" | **rewrite** |
| *history* | the phrase *the rest is history* | original |
| *contest* (verb) | "take part in an election or a game and try to win it" | original |
| *minute* | the phrase *up to the minute* | generic overlap |
| *recipe* | "a way of doing something that is likely to lead to a particular result" | generic overlap |
| *as* (conjunction) | "during the time that, or at the moment when, something happens" | original |
| *neck* | the phrase *up to your neck* | original |
| *light* | the phrase *come to light* | generic overlap |

"Generic overlap" means a short, plain definition that any dictionary would write in much the same way; that is not copying. "Rewrite" means our wording followed a published dictionary's particular way of putting it.

The one rewrite: a well-known learner's dictionary defines *promise* as telling someone that you will *definitely* do something, or that something will *definitely* happen. Ours had the same structure, with *certainly* in place of *definitely*. That is too close. I reworded the first sense as "say to someone that they can be sure you will do something, or sure that something will happen". The second sense (*promise someone a bike*) used the same frame, so I reworded it to match: "say to someone that they can be sure you will give them something".

Because the entry changed, it went back through review: both reviewer models read it again. They raised one small point, that the see-also note should say *promise someone the moon*; I rejected it, because the note names the phrase exactly as it is filed in our *moon* entry. The entry is `reviewed` again.

## What the reviewer said

On *promise* it said the wording "closely resembles learner-dictionary wording" but could not quote a match; the search did. As in earlier checks, the verdicts rest on the searches and my own reading, not on the reviewer.
