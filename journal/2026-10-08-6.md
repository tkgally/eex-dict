# 2026-10-08 (6): originality — the twenty-fourth check, one definition rewritten

## What this cycle did

The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) runs an **originality check** every tenth run, and this was run 310. An originality check makes sure the dictionary's wording is our own and not copied from a published dictionary. The tool picked ten fields at random: two usage notes (on *potato* and *set*) and eight definitions (from *box, write, call, buy, place, consider, joint,* and *day*). I searched the web for the exact wording of each and asked one of the outside reviewer models (reviewer-a) whether each reads as copied. Search results decide the verdict; the model's opinion is only a second signal, because past checks showed it is unreliable in both directions.

## What the searches found

- Seven fields had no published match at all: the two usage notes, and the definitions of *nothing to write home about* (under *write*), *call* meaning "give a name to", *in the first place* (under *place*), *consider* meaning "think about a fact when judging", the drug sense of *joint*, and *day in, day out* (under *day*). Each is marked **original**.
- *buy*, sense 1, reads *get something by paying money for it*. A learner's dictionary has *to obtain something by paying money for it*. This is a short, plain definition that almost any dictionary would write the same way, so it is marked **generic overlap** and kept.
- *box*, sense 1, read *a container with flat, stiff sides and usually a lid, used for keeping or carrying things*. Two learner's dictionaries describe a box with the same frame (stiff sides, and a lid that is often or sometimes there). The wording was not identical, but the frame was close enough that, as with *bag* and *package* in the previous check, I rewrote it: *a container with corners and a flat bottom, often closed at the top, for keeping, carrying, or sending things*.

## What the reviewer model said

Reviewer-a, asked with its reasoning switched off (which earlier checks found gives cleaner answers), said "copied" for all eight definitions. Each time it named one well-known dictionary and gave as the "quote" our own wording, sometimes with *to* added. Search found none of those quotes. This is the same pattern seen in earlier checks, and it is recorded in `wiki/notes/reviewer-noise.md`. It said "cannot tell" for the two usage notes.

## The re-review of *box*

A rewritten definition is a change to the entry, so the whole *box* entry went back through the reviewer panel. The reviewers did not object to the new definition. They raised six other issues; I agreed with four:

- The post-office sense said the address form is *PO Box*. American usage commonly writes *P.O. Box*, so the explanation now gives both, says this is the form used in an address, and the example uses *P.O. box* in a sentence. The word combinations *a post office box* and *a P.O. box* were added.

I rejected two: reviewer-a said a sports ground does not have a "box" in the theater sense, but stadiums do have press boxes and private boxes; and it wanted a collocation for *the box* (television), which the explanation already covers ("always **the box**").
