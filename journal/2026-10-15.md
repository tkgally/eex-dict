## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewers ("reviewer-a" and "reviewer-b", two different language models) raised 38 issues, 30 of them marked blocking (meaning the entry should not stand until someone decides). I accepted 14 and rejected 24; none needed to go to the owner.

Changes made:

- **home** (adverb): the sense *into place* had the definition "into the right place, or as far as possible", which was vague. It now reads "fully into the right place, as far as it will go". The phrase *drive something home* had been listed under both this sense and the sense *make someone understand*; it now sits only under the second, where it belongs.
- **a lot**: the usage note told learners to use *a lot of* before a noun, but left out the common informal *lots of*. Both are now named.
- **mass**: the learner-error note said "use **weigh** or **weight** for people", which suggested the noun and the verb could swap. It now gives each its own pattern (*I weigh sixty kilos*; *my weight is sixty kilos*). The note comparing *mass* with *weight* now gives the science meaning of *weight* as well.
- **meal**: *a square meal* was defined as "a big, healthy meal"; it means a big, filling meal, not necessarily a healthy one. Corrected.
- **meaning**: the sense about purpose (*the meaning of life*) had an example and a collocation, *lose its meaning*, that a reader could take as the other sense (a word losing what it signifies). Both now use *lose all meaning* about a life. The learner-error note said questions with *mean* need *does*; it now says *do*, *does*, or *did* (*What did she mean?*).
- **meat**: an example with the plural *cold meats* now carries a short note ("plural: kinds of meat"), so it no longer seems to contradict the note that meat as food is usually uncountable.
- **member** and **menu**: two British expressions in examples were changed to the American forms the dictionary uses: *staff members* (for *members of staff*) and *pay raise* (for *pay rise*).

Main rejections, with reasons logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`:

- On **memory**, reviewer-a objected three times that the sense "the space in a computer or a phone where information is kept" confuses memory with storage. In everyday speech (*my phone is running out of memory*) that is exactly what the word means, and the entry's explanation already separates the technical meaning. Kept.
- Reviewer-a said three learner errors were not errors: *We played a match of tennis*, *The tailor took my measures*, and *Can I have the card, please?* (for the menu). All three are unidiomatic, and the last is a common mistake by speakers of languages that use a word like *card* for a menu. Kept.
- Reviewer-b wanted *meal ticket* moved out to its own entry and objected to a definition that was already in the required form. Both rejected under the house rules.

Reviewer-a's objections were accepted about one time in three this cycle, in line with its long-run record; reviewer-b raised only four issues.
