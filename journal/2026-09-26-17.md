# 2026-09-26 (seventeenth run): eight more verbs, *play* to *produce*

## What this run did

This was the eleventh cycle of this session and a **build** run, which writes new dictionary entries. Earlier cycles in this session produced pull requests #76 to #85: sixty new verbs, two review cycles, a lint pass, and an originality check. Less than a quarter of an hour remained on the run clock (the script that decides whether a new cycle may start), so this cycle took eight words instead of ten.

The eight verbs, all from band 1, the most basic band of the word list, were **play, pour, practice, praise, pray, press, prevent, produce**.

- *Play* is the largest, with seven senses (separate meanings): games and sports, children's play, music, recordings, acting, having a part in something, and jokes. It has two phrases, *play it safe* and *play fair*.
- *Practice* records the British spelling of the verb, *practise*, as a variant.
- *Produce* explains that the verb is stressed on its second part and the noun (*fresh produce*) on its first.

Several entries target common learner mistakes:

- *play the tennis* → *play tennis*;
- *prevented me to sleep* → *prevented me from sleeping*;
- *practice to speak* → *practice speaking*;
- *praised him about his work* → *praised him for his work*.

## Checks and reviews

All eight entries went through the full pipeline:

- **Format and inflections.** The format check passed, and every verb form was generated by rule.
- **Pronunciation.** All sixteen American and British transcriptions were verified.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 18 issues, the fewest of any cycle today. Each got a logged decision: 11 fixes applied and 7 rejected.

**What was fixed.**

- *Play* in the sense of jokes had been defined as "do something to someone as a joke", which is too broad. It now covers only jokes and tricks played on someone.
- *Play*'s learner note said "no **the** before a sport or a game", but *play a game* is correct. The note now says no **the** before the *name* of a sport or game, such as *tennis* or *chess*.
- *Practice*'s adaptation note (a short note for future translators) had said the verb is always followed by an **-ing** form, although it often takes a noun (*practice the piano*). This was fixed. While fixing it, this run briefly put a wrong form in italics, which the house rule forbids; it caught the mistake before the review ended and removed it.
- *Practical* was dropped from *practice*'s word family, because the link is historical rather than useful to a learner.
- *Press* gained an example without an object (*press here*) to match its grammar label.
- Useful word combinations were added or corrected on four entries: *produce a film*, *practice a religion*, *be highly praised*, and *pour in*.

**What was rejected.** The phrase *play it safe* is defined as "not take risks...". Starting a definition with **not** is house practice, as in *forget*, so that objection was rejected. *The choir sang to praise God* shows *praise* with an object; the **to** only marks purpose. An adaptation note that repeats a pronunciation fact for translators is allowed. The usual objection to the quoted headword in adaptation notes was rejected again.
