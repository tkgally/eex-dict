# 2026-09-26 (twenty-third run): eight verbs, *read* to *refuse*

## What this run did

This was the fourth cycle of this session, and a **build** run. A build run drafts new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each run's kind of work) chose it after the review cycle before it merged as pull request #91. The run continued with band 1, the most basic words, after *reach*.

The eight verbs were:

- **read**: six senses. They are reading words, reading aloud, finding something out by reading, reading a map or someone's face, a sign that *reads* "Closed", and the British *read history at university*. The notes explain that the past tense is spelled *read* but said like *red*.
- **realize**: understanding something you had not noticed, and the formal *realize a dream*. A note for translators warns that similar words in many languages mean *carry out*.
- **recognize**: knowing someone again, accepting that something is true, official recognition, and public praise.
- **recover**: getting well, a situation returning to normal, and getting back something lost. A learner error covers the missing *from* in *recover his illness*.
- **reduce**: making something less, bringing someone to a worse state (*reduced her to tears*), and the cooking sense.
- **refer**: mentioning, calling something by a name, looking at notes, sending someone to a specialist, and a word that *refers to* something. There are learner errors for *referred me a dentist* and the spelling *refered*.
- **reflect**: light, images, showing what something is like, thinking carefully, and *reflect well on someone*.
- **refuse**: refusing to do something, refusing an offer, and refusing someone something. The pronunciation note separates the verb from the noun *refuse*, meaning waste.

## Checks and reviews

Every entry went through the full pipeline, the fixed sequence of checks each entry must pass:

- **Format and inflections.** All eight passed. The inflection tool took *read*'s forms, with the note about their sound, from its table of irregular forms. Its rules doubled the *r* in *referred* and *referring*, because the stress falls on the end of *refer*.
- **Pronunciation.** All sixteen American and British transcriptions were confirmed by the panel of pronunciation models. The American ones also agree with the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary.
- **Reviewers.** The two reviewer models raised 25 issues. Each has a logged decision: 11 applied and 14 rejected.

The main fixes:

- *Read* sense 3 was defined as "find out about something by reading". That uses the headword to define itself. It now reads "get information about something from a book, a newspaper, a screen, or another written text".
- *Reflect* sense 5 (*Your behavior reflects badly on the team*) was defined as "make people have a good or bad opinion of someone". Reviewer-a pointed out that the sense is about how someone appears. It is now "make someone or something seem good or bad to other people".
- *Realize*: the translator's note said English uses the "carry out" sense only with dreams and ambitions. The entry's own example, *The plan was never fully realized*, shows plans too. The note now says so.
- *Reduce*: the learner-error note said the verb needs an object. That is not true of the cooking sense (*Let the soup reduce by half*), so the note now says "usually".

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Reviewer-b asked six times for the past tense *read* in examples to be marked by hand. It is a listed form of the word, so the site marks it on its own.
- Reviewer-a said the core idea of *refuse* was ungrammatical ("say no to doing something"). The phrase is correct English.
- Four objections to the house habit of opening a translator's note with the headword in quotation marks were rejected, as that habit is settled.

The cross-reference tool added three back-links, links pointing back from an existing entry to a new one. One was on the wrong sense. It put *recognize* beside *accept* in the sense "agree to take something offered", so it was moved by hand to "come to believe that something is true".
