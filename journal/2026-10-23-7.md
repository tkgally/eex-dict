## What the reviewers found and what I decided

There were 38 points. I accepted 18 and rejected 20. Every decision is logged with its reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Accepted, the main ones:

- **stick**, the sports sense, said *used to hit the ball*, which leaves out games played with a puck. It now says *used to hit or move something in some games*.
- **stone**: the usage note said Americans use **rock** "for a stone of any size", while the compare note said "also for a small stone". The two now agree: Americans often say **rock** for a stone, even a small one.
- **step**: the usage note said flatly that you never *make* a step. That was too strong; it now says *make a step* and *do a step* are not the usual ways to say it. I also fixed its marks: the words named there are now in bold, as the style guide requires, not in italics.
- **strike**, the baseball sense, had a definition with a second sentence after a semicolon. The definition is now short (*a throw that counts against the player trying to hit it*), and the explanation gives the rule. The idiom *a strike against someone* now notes that it is used of things too.
- **strength**: two definitions ended in *and so on*, which says nothing; both now name what they mean.
- *put a stop to something* no longer says *so that it does not happen again*; *turn your stomach* has a smoother definition; *a steam iron* was removed from the "steam power" sense, since an iron is not powered by steam; ambiguous collocations (*a large store*, which sounds like a big shop) were replaced in the "supply" sense of **store**; *a two-way street* lost an unneeded informal label.

Rejected, with reasons:

- reviewer-a wanted the two senses of **strategy** merged (a plan, and the skill of planning). They have different grammar (one is countable, one is not), so they stay apart; its claim that *a game of strategy* uses the countable form misreads the sentence.
- It called *humorous* a wrong kind of label. It is in the dictionary's closed list of register labels.
- It said *a storm of snow* and *made a step toward the door* are acceptable, so they should not be shown as learner errors. Both are unnatural; the learner errors stay.
- It said British English does not call a large shop a **store**. British chains commonly do, and the note says only "especially for a large one".
- reviewer-b objected to **St.** in the **street** usage note as an abbreviation. The note names the short form as a word, which is the point of the note; it does not use it.

Before saving, the vocabulary check caught one word I had added, *hockey*, that is outside the defining vocabulary and has no entry yet, so I took it out of the definition.
