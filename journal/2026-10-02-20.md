Points for learners that the entries make:

- *desert* (dry land, stressed on the first part) is not *dessert* (sweet food, stressed on the second).
- The noun *device* is spelled with *c*; the verb *devise* is spelled with *s*.
- *in detail* has no *s*.
- *the deep of the river* should be *the depth*.
- You sit *at* a desk, not *on* it.
- *destruction* almost never has a plural.
- You *look up* a word in a dictionary.

## A pronunciation question settled by the check

The pronunciation check compares each transcription (the written form of how a word sounds) with three other models, and with an open pronouncing dictionary for American English. My British transcription of *dictionary* had four syllables, and only one of the three checkers agreed with it. The other two agreed with each other on three syllables (*DICK-shun-ree*), which is in fact the usual British form. I changed the main British transcription to that form, and it was then verified. The four-syllable form is kept as a variant.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 44 issues. Nineteen were *blocking*, meaning each had to be decided before the entries could be marked as reviewed. I applied 24 and rejected 20, and none went to the owner. Every decision is in `reviews/decisions.jsonl` with its reason. Once, the second reviewer sent back a reply with no verdicts in it, for *depth*. I ran that reviewer again on that entry, and the second reply was complete.

What I changed:

- *detail*: American speakers stress either syllable, and my note said so. The transcription now records the second form too. One definition had mixed the idea of small facts with the care people give them; it now covers only the small facts.
- *device*: the sense meaning a phone or a computer no longer says it must be something you carry or use at home. The sense meaning a bomb now also covers things that are not exactly bombs.
- *destruction*: I had called it uncountable with no exceptions. It is now "almost always" uncountable, and the note no longer claims that destruction leaves nothing at all to repair. I kept the learner error *many destructions*, which is a real and common mistake.
- I took out two learner errors that were not clearly wrong: *the developing of the project* and *the department of sales*. In place of the second I added *the sale department*, which should be *sales*.
- *design*: the usage note now agrees with the sense it explains. *desire*, *desk*, and *desert island* were also made more exact.

Most rejections were the second reviewer's repeated objection to a settled house rule: a note for translators may open with the headword in quotation marks.
