## What this cycle did

The scheduler chose **closure**: writing entries for words the dictionary already uses. A "closure" row is a word that appears in one of our own definitions but has no entry yet, so a reader who clicks it finds nothing. A "family" row is a related form of a word we have (for example, the verb *bug* beside the noun *bug*).

The closure source gave five rows, each with a part of speech guessed by the queueing tool, so I checked each one first:

- ***history*** (used in the definition of *museum*): a real noun, drafted.
- ***january*** and ***june*** (used in the definition of *month*): month names. The queueing tool drops capital letters, so I drafted them as *January* and *June*, the way *Christian* was drafted earlier. Their definitions say *the first month of the year* and *the sixth month of the year, after May*, because the other month names are not in the defining word list.
- ***rocky*** and ***shiny*** were queued as nouns, but they are adjectives. I marked the noun rows as duplicates and drafted the adjectives, whose rows were already waiting in the queue.

The family source then gave five verbs. I drafted four: *bug* (annoy; hide a listening device), *bus* (carry people by bus; clear tables, American), *butter*, and *capitalize* (write a capital letter; *capitalize on* a chance; give a business money). I released the fifth, *cable*, because the verb is rare and dated and would make a thin entry; it is back in the queue.

So the cycle made nine entries, not the twelve allowed. Highlights:

- ***history*** has four senses (the past, the school subject, the past of one person or thing, a written account), five idioms (*make history*, *go down in history*, *the rest is history*...), and a translator's note: in several languages one word means both *history* and *story*.
- ***January*** and ***June*** carry a usage note on capitals, *in January* but *on January 5*, and American and British ways of saying dates, with two learner errors, and a note that these months are winter in one half of the world and summer in the other.

## What the checks found

The reviewers raised 17 issues; I made 15 decisions (two issues shared one fix), applying 13 and rejecting 2:

- ***capitalize***: its explanation and its learner error said the verb is "always" followed by *on*. That is true only of the sense *take advantage* (*capitalize on a mistake*); the finance sense takes a direct object (*capitalize a business*). Both notes now name their sense. The core idea was also rewritten.
- ***be history*** was defined partly as "about to lose your job"; it now means *be finished or no longer important*, or, of a person, *be about to be punished or sent away*.

Rejected: a claim that *He buses tables* is an intransitive use (*tables* is its object), and a claim that the plural *Junes* conflicts with the grammar (it is rare but real).
