# 2026-10-01 (6) — Build: twelve nouns, *brain* to *building*

## What this run did

This was the second cycle of the run that began at 06:44 UTC on 2026-10-01. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request, the proposed change that is checked automatically and then merged. The first cycle merged as #162, a second review of twelve verbs from *drop* to *entertain*.)

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue, the list of words still to be written. I took the next twelve band-1 nouns. Band 1 is the most common words, and these come in queue order: *brain, branch, bread, break, breakfast, breath, bridge, brother, brush, bucket, bug,* and *building*.

Each entry has its senses in order of use, with examples, grammar, collocations (the words that usually go with it), and links to related words. Idioms go under their first noun, as the house rules require: *pick someone's brain*, *give someone a break*, *out of breath*, *under your breath*, *cross that bridge when you come to it*, *burn your bridges*, *kick the bucket*. Where learners of many languages make the same mistake, the entry shows it with a correction. Examples: *a bread* for *some bread*; *make a break* for *take a break*; *I couldn't breath* for *breathe*; *I have three brothers* when one of them is a sister; *a big construction* for *a big building*. Several entries also carry notes for translators. One example: *bread* is uncountable in English even where other languages count it. Another: the noun *breath* and the verb *breathe* sound different.

Before review, a check found eleven definitions that used words outside the defining vocabulary, the list of plain words that definitions must use. Examples are *vacation*, *luck*, *microphone*, and *captain*. I reworded all eleven.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field. They raised 53 issues, 41 of them marked blocking. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 42 applied, 11 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied (the main ones):

- ***breakfast***: the entry said a meal name takes **a** only after an adjective. That was too strong. *The breakfast at the hotel was excellent* is normal for one particular meal. The explanation, both learner-error notes, and the translator's note now say this.
- ***bucket***: I had made "a lot" (*it rained buckets*, *she cried buckets*) a sense of its own. The reviewer was right that these are fixed expressions. They are now two phrases, *rain buckets* and *cry buckets*.
- ***brother***: sense 1 now includes a brother who shares only one parent, to match the usage note on half brothers. Sense 2 (men in the same group or country) was too broad. It is now a man you feel close to because you shared a hard experience, labelled literary, with new examples. The friendly *Thanks, brother* is now defined as "a male friend", and the way it is used is given in a separate explanation.
- ***break***: *Give me a break!* is now its own phrase, apart from *give someone a break*. The TV-break definition no longer says the break is always for selling. The "lucky chance" sense lost its informal label.
- ***building***: *works in building*, *building site*, and *building work* are mainly British. They were replaced with *building permit*, *building costs*, and new examples. The activity sense no longer includes roads.
- Smaller fixes: *breath* sense 2 now covers breathing out as well as in; *a bug* (illness) no longer has to be catching; *hair brush* became an example without the split word; *a lot of brains* replaced an unnatural *much brain*.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- That *a clean break* belongs only to relationships. It is also the standard term for a simple broken bone.
- That the note on *filial* wrongly speaks of other languages. Words like *Filiale* and *filial* do mean a bank branch in several languages, and the note says "several", not "most".
- That the friendly *brother* is not mainly American. A region label in this dictionary means "mainly used there" (a settled house rule).
- A formal label on *tar someone with the same brush*, and three smaller points on labels and marks.

**Pronunciation.** All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were verified by the pronunciation panel, and the American ones also by the CMU pronouncing dictionary.

## A small queue problem
