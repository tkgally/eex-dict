# 2026-10-23, cycle 4 of the 12:43 UTC run: twelve verbs, bear to deal

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle, the fourth and last of this run: new entries taken in order from the queue (the list of words still to be written). The scheduler allowed twelve. Before claiming, I marked one queued row, *care for* as a separate verb, as a duplicate: it is a verb plus a preposition that the existing **care** entry already covers, and the house rule is not to give such patterns entries of their own.

The twelve new entries, all band 2 (common words just below the core vocabulary), are the verbs **bear**, **blend**, **browse**, **color** (British spelling **colour** as a variant), **concern**, **conduct**, **construct**, **contribute**, **convince**, **crawl**, **cycle**, and **deal**.

Some points worth knowing:

- **bear** has seven senses, from *can't bear the noise* to *bear left at the fork*, and a usage note on the past participle: **borne** for costs and weights, **born** only in *be born*.
- **concern** carries the phrase *to whom it may concern* and a usage note separating *be concerned about* (worried) from *be concerned with* (about a subject).
- **conduct** notes that the verb is stressed on the second part and the noun on the first.
- **contribute** warns that *to* in *contribute to* is a preposition, so it takes an *-ing* form: *contributed to making things worse*.
- **deal** points to the phrasal verb **deal with**, which is how most learners meet the word, and its learner-error note catches *deal this problem*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 35 issues. One reply, reviewer-b's on **bear**, came back cut off; I re-ran that reviewer alone on that entry and the second reply was complete. I applied 22 and rejected 13, each decision logged with its reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Applied, the main ones:

- **browse**: I had listed *browsing on the internet* as a learner error. The reviewer was right that it is normal English, and the entry itself had a similar example, so the error note is gone.
- **convince**: the usage note suggested that *convince someone to do something* is less careful than *persuade*. Both are standard; the note now says so and only describes the preference some writers have.
- **color**: the "affect an opinion" sense no longer says the result is unfair, and the blushing sense no longer includes anger.
- **conduct**: the music sense now covers singers and does not require standing in front; the electricity sense lost an unclear *it*.
- **cycle**: a "technical" label and a weak example were removed, and *ride* moved from synonyms to "see also"; **bear**, **blend**, **construct**, **contribute**, and **deal** got label and collocation fixes.

Rejected, with reasons:

- reviewer-b said that *be convinced that* and *where money is concerned* are adjective uses that belong in other entries. They are passives of the verb senses, and learners meet them with the verb.
- Three flags said the core idea (the one-sentence summary at the top of a long entry) should not be a sentence; house style says it is one.
- Two said an explanation naming the kind of subject (*used of animals such as deer*) is unneeded; house rule puts such restrictions there.

The cross-reference tool adds matching links in related entries but always on their first sense. Three were on the wrong sense and I moved them by hand: **concern** to the "be about" sense of **deal with**, **contribute** to the "provide" sense of **give**, and **conduct** to the "act" sense of **behave**.
