# 2026-10-04 (2) — Closure: twelve words the dictionary already uses

*Second cycle of the run that started 00:43 UTC. A "cycle" is one unit of work that ends in its own merged pull request.*

## What this cycle did

The mode selector chose **closure**. Closure work drafts words the dictionary already uses but does not yet define: words that appear inside existing definitions, words in an entry's family (related forms such as a verb for an existing noun), and cross-reference targets.

**The closure rows.** The queue's "closure" source held only five rows, all of them problem rows whose part of speech had been guessed by a script:

- *Christian* (noun) was released again. It cannot pass the vocabulary check until you decide whether proper names may appear in definitions (open question 7).
- *deliberately* had been queued as a noun. It is an adverb (it is used in the definition of *ignore*), so the noun row was marked as a duplicate and **deliberately** (adverb) was claimed and drafted instead, with two senses (*on purpose*; *slowly and carefully*), a note comparing it with *on purpose* and *intentionally*, and learner errors for *by purpose* and *She deliberate ignored me*.
- *cheat*, *fail*, and *hollow* had been queued as nouns because the words appear in definitions, where they are actually used as a verb or an adjective that already has an entry. Earlier runs released them each time, so they kept coming back. All three are real nouns, so this time I drafted them: **cheat** (a person who cheats, with a subsense for an unfaithful partner, and a code that gives an advantage in a computer game); **fail** (a British exam result, the informal *a total fail*, and the phrase *without fail*); **hollow** (a small area of low ground, and a hole or space inside something).

**The family rows.** The rest came from the "family" source: eight verbs that pair with nouns already in the dictionary. They are **battle** (try hard, fight in a war, compete, and the phrase *battle it out*), **beach** (bring a boat onto a beach; of whales, become stuck), **beautify**, **bed** (fix firmly in something; an old-fashioned informal sense), **befriend**, **belt** (hit hard, fasten, move fast), **bench** (keep a player out of a game; lift weights), and **benefit** (*benefit from something*, *something benefits someone*).

All twelve passed the full pipeline and are published as `reviewed`.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewer models raised 35 issues, 19 of them blocking (must be settled before publishing). I applied 32 and rejected 3.

Applied, among others:
- *bed* (verb) had a sense "put young plants into the ground". The reviewer was right that the bare verb is rare for this; gardeners say *bed out*. The sense was removed and *bed out* is mentioned in the usage note instead. The note on *bed down* was also corrected: it means settle down to sleep somewhere unusual, not just sleep.
- *cheat*: I had called *cheater* "mainly American". It is more common there, but it is used in Britain too; the wording is softer now. The unfaithful-partner subsense was reworded, and the game sense no longer says the trick must be secret.
- *bench*: the weightlifting sense can be used without an object (*How much do you bench?*), so its grammar now allows that.
- *beach*: the whale sense now covers being *left* stuck, not only becoming stuck, and names seals as well.
- Smaller fixes to usage notes on *fail*, *hollow*, *deliberately*, *battle*, and *beautify*.

Rejected: three objections, all the same one, that the American transcription of *beach* should carry a length mark. The house style for American English leaves it out; this is settled.

The vocabulary check found five definition words outside the defining vocabulary (the list of about 2,400 plain words that definitions should use): *rival*, *romantic*, *sexual*, *embarrassing*, and *curves*. Those definitions were rewritten in plainer words.

The cross-reference tool added mirror links on seven existing entries; I checked them (*battle* landed on the "in a war" sense of *fight*, correctly).
