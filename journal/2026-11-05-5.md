I wrote each one from my own knowledge and the house style guide, with no published dictionary open.

**The checking pipeline.** Each entry then went through the fixed sequence of checks:

- **Schema check.** The validator found no errors.
- **Inflections.** The plural forms were generated by rule. *Coaching* and *darkness* correctly got no plural.
- **Pronunciation.** All 24 American and British transcriptions were confirmed by the pronunciation panel (three outside models voting) and by the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, which is consulted at run time and never stored.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The reviewers raised 30 issues. I applied 28 and rejected 2, each with a logged reason. Only one issue was *blocking* (one that must be settled before an entry can be marked reviewed). In *darkness*, the sadness-and-cruelty sense was coded as uncountable, but its example says *a darkness in his later paintings*. That use is natural, so I changed the code to "countable or uncountable, usually singular" to match.

The changes worth knowing:

- **pillow.** The American sofa-cushion sense no longer mentions beds, so it does not overlap with the first sense.
- **deadline.** It is now "a time or a day by which something must be done", not "...must finish something", because many deadlines are for sending or paying something.
- **deck.** Reviewer A noticed three gaps, and I filled them:
  - a sense for a set of presentation slides (*a slide deck*)
  - the American phrase *on deck* (next in line)
  - *stack the deck* (arrange things unfairly)

  *All hands on deck* stays out: by the house keyword rule it belongs under *hand*.
- **darling.** The core idea now covers the "favorite of a group" sense (*the darling of the fashion world*).
- **dentist.** The usage note about *the dentist's* was dropped. It overstated a British–American difference and added little.

My two rejections:

- Reviewer A thought the color sense of *darkness* (*the darkness of the wood*) too thin to stand alone. I kept it, because many languages translate that sense with a different word.
- Reviewer B wanted the translator's note on *demonstration* removed as generic. The note names a real trap: the protest sense and the "showing how something works" sense are separate words in many languages. That is the same kind of note the style guide gives as its model for *bank*. I did fix reviewer A's point about its quotation marks.

**Afterward.**

- **Cross-references.** The tool added mirror links on *book*, *coach*, and *increase*. I removed one weak link, *coaching* as a synonym of *training*: *training* means teaching skills or physical exercise, not quite the same thing.
- **Queue.** Five words that the new entries mention went into the queue: *bookshelf*, *demo*, *dental*, *dentistry*, and *sweetheart*. They wait without a band until the next band vote.

**Spend.** About US$0.32 this cycle, for the review panel and the pronunciation check. The day stands at US$4.18 of US$15.

## Cycle 3: review

**What this cycle was.** The selector chose *review*: a second reading of entries already in the dictionary. There were no unreviewed drafts and no reader reports, so the cycle went to the coverage pass. That is the owner's 2026-10-24 ruling to reread the most important (band 1) entries for missing senses, starting with the entries that have the most senses.

I read six entries:

- *baby* and *around*, the last two six-sense entries in that pass.
- *black* (adjective), *business*, *face*, and *right* (noun), four five-sense entries that already had idioms waiting for them in the hand-over note.
- *black* (noun), a seventh, because it received a phrase moved from the adjective. In *in black and white*, *black* is a noun, so the house keyword rule files the phrase there.

**What I added.**

- **around**:
  - a sense for passing something to each person in a group (*pass these photos around*, *enough cake to go around*)
  - a measurement use (*three meters around*)
  - a sub-sense for being somewhere with nothing useful to do (*sat around talking*)
- **black**: a sense for being dirty (*black with oil*). The phrase *black and white* now also covers its figurative use (*not as black and white as it seems*).
- **business**: a sense for a kind of industry (*the music business*), now sense 3 because it is common, plus the phrase *big business*.
- **face**:
  - a sub-sense for the person who represents a group (*the face of the campaign*)
  - the idioms *stare someone in the face* and *show your face*
- **right**: the idioms *reserve the right to do something* and *the rights and wrongs of something*.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two model reviewers raised 93 issues. I applied 83, rejected 10, and escalated none. Six were blocking:

- Two examples did not fit their sense. *I hope she stays around* is about staying nearby, not about still existing. *The small town is worth visiting in its own right* did not fit the definition of *in your own right*. I replaced the first example and widened the phrase's definition so the second fits.
- The "person" sense of *face* said "especially one you know", but its examples were about new faces. I reworded the definition.
- My own new sense of *baby* for *baby carrots* and *baby spinach* mixed noun and adjective grammar. Reviewer A argued that this use is really an adjective. I agreed, took the sense out, and put *baby* (adjective) in the queue to be written later.

Many minor fixes removed facts stated twice ("one fact, one field" is a house rule). Examples include a pronunciation note repeated in a translator's note in *business*, and a grammar fact repeated in *baby*. One learner error in *around* was dropped: *around about twenty dollars* is real informal English, not a mistake.

What I rejected, and why:

- **Phrasal verbs.** *Come around* (visit) is a phrasal verb, and phrasal verbs get their own entries.
- **Keyword rule.** *Have been around* (be experienced) has no noun, so the keyword rule files it under the verb *be*. I added it to that entry's waiting list.
- **Adverb uses.** *Face down* is an adverb, so I queued it as its own entry.
- **Translator notes.** Both reviewers called the note on *right* "generic". It follows the style guide's own model for *bank*: two senses that are unrelated words in many languages, so I kept it. On *around*, reviewer B's version of the same complaint was fair, and I rewrote that note to name the specific trap.
- **Form of address.** For *baby* used to address a partner, I kept the explanation, because the closed list of grammar codes has no code for that use.

**Spend.** US$0.39 for the review panel. The day stands at US$4.57 of US$15.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. The *baby carrots* sense was a misjudgment on my part, caught by the reviewer.

**What is next.** The coverage pass continues with 124 unread five-sense band-1 entries, starting with *act*. Build and closure work continue as listed in `NEXT.md`.
