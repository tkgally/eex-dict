- *Back-links.* A back-link is the reverse of an existing cross-reference: if *cycle* lists *bicycle* as a word to compare, *bicycle*'s side gets the matching link. The cross-reference tool adds these on its own, because they only mirror something a reviewed entry already says. It wrote them in six entries: *charge* (verb), *currency*, *cycle*, *cyclist*, *daylight*, and *daytime*. No pair was left for a person to place by hand.
- *Claim files.* These record which headwords a run has reserved so two runs do not write the same word. Five had served their purpose and were pruned.
- *Band vote.* Every word waiting in the queue gets a frequency band from 1 (most important) to 5 (least). The band comes from a vote by three cheap models, and the lower median wins. Twenty-four rows that tools had queued without a band went through the vote. It cost US$0.0043, and no vote split by more than one band, so nothing needed settling by hand. All 24 rows are now ordinary pending words.
- *Hyphen check.* Some earlier tools turned hyphenated words into spaced ones (*award winning* for *award-winning*). The repair tool found no such rows this time.

**Reviewer precision.** Two model reviewers read every entry. Precision means the share of a reviewer's objections that I applied rather than rejected. The rule is that a reviewer whose precision in a kind of issue stays under 30 percent over at least twenty decisions is switched off for that kind. No kind fell under the line this time. Reviewer A (a Claude model) stands at 0.84 overall. Reviewer B (a Google model) stands at 0.82; its spelling-and-format objections are at 0.32 and stay on by the owner's earlier ruling, and its inflection objections, at 0.33, have too few decisions to count.
