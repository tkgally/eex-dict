# 2026-10-05 (12): infinitive *to* and seven religion words

*Fourth and last cycle of the run that started at 08:43 UTC. Mode: build. Run id `20261005T091046Z-cp7jp7`.*

## What this cycle did

The scheduler chose a build cycle. I used it for two of the owner's rulings of 2026-10-05 that create new entries. Each went through the full pipeline.

**Infinitive *to* (ruling 4).** There is a new part-of-speech value, `inf` ("infinitive marker"), recorded in `wiki/decisions/infinitive-marker.md`, and the first entry that uses it, `to-inf`. It has six senses:

1. after verbs (*want to go*, *ask me to help*);
2. purpose (*went to the store to buy milk*, *in order to*);
3. after adjectives (*easy to read*, *too tired to walk*);
4. after nouns (*work to do*, *something to write with*);
5. after question words (*what to say*, *how to use this*);
6. standing in for a verb at the end of a clause (*if you don't want to*).

It also has three learner errors (*I want go*, *You must to wear*, *I came here for learn*), a usage note separating it from the preposition *to* (*look forward to seeing you*), and translator notes.

Two tools needed a one-line change to know the new value. The forms generator (`tools/inflect.py`) now treats it as a word with no forms, and the queue orders it with the other function words. The panel of pronunciation models disagreed with *tu* as the main form for this word, so the main form is now the weak *tuh* (tə). The full *too* form is listed as a variant for the end of a clause. All the panel members then agreed.

The ruling also asked that the preposition entry *to* point to the new one. Its usage note now says that *to* before a verb is the infinitive marker, a separate entry, and each entry links to the other. Because I edited it, the preposition entry went back through the reviewers. Reviewer A raised five issues on its older senses. I applied two: a confusing example (*a door to the garden*) was removed, and *dance to the music* now means "in time with it", not "following it". I rejected two, including the American *a quarter till eight*, which is real regional usage.

**Proper names (ruling 3).** Seven words that were blocked until proper names were allowed in definitions are now written: *Christian*, *Christianity*, *Islam*, *Muslim*, *Judaism*, *Buddhism*, and *Buddhist*. Each is short, neutral, and labeled with the domain "religion". Definitions say what followers believe or follow, never whether it is true. Each entry has a usage note on the capital letter, and the follower nouns have the learner error of writing them in lower case. The *Muslim* entry also has *She is an Islam* (the religion versus the person).

For *Judaism*, the natural definition ("the religion of the Jewish people") needs the word *Jewish*, which has no entry yet. So I added **Moses** to the closed list of proper names, as the ruling allows when an entry needs one. The definition is "the religion of people who believe in one God and follow the laws that they believe God gave to Moses". The decision page records the addition.

The pronunciation panel moved two main forms: *Islam* is now *iss-LAHM* (American) and *iz-LAHM* (British), and American *Judaism* is now *JOO-day-iz-um*. The other common ways of saying them are listed as variants, and all transcriptions are now verified.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

There were eleven decisions: eight applied and three rejected.

- On *Buddhism*, reviewer A said my first definition (escaping pain by letting go of desires) was too narrow. It is now "the religion and way of life based on the teaching of Buddha".
- Reviewer A also objected that "used without **a** or **the**" was too absolute. That note now says "usually", on all four religion nouns.
- The *Judaism* definition lost two commas that broke up its clause.
- The *to-inf* sense "after nouns" now also covers what something is for (*something to write with*).

Reviewer B raised no issues on the eight new entries and gave a full verdict list on all of them. On the preposition *to* it again listed only part of its verdicts (see `wiki/notes/reviewer-b-partial-verdicts.md`).
