## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the script `tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each cycle's kind of work from how long each kind has waited) chose a build: new entries taken from the head of the work queue. Its reason was "highest scheduler debt among eligible modes"; no draft entries were waiting, the day's budget was untouched (US$15 available), and no weekly digest was due. Twelve entries were written, all from the queue's band 2 (the second most important of five frequency bands): three verbs, **prioritize**, **proceed**, and **puzzle**, and nine nouns, **certificate**, **compliment**, **fantasy**, **front desk**, **inspiration**, **mining**, **mystery**, **receptionist**, and **recycling**. Several fill gaps that existing entries already pointed to: *reception* named *front desk* and *receptionist*; *praise* named *compliment*; *priority* named *prioritize*; *puzzle* (the noun) named *mystery* and the verb.

**Pronunciation.** All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each entry) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, three outside models voting with an open pronouncing dictionary as a fourth voice. No disputes.

**What the reviewers found, and what I decided.** Each entry was read field by field by two reviewer models from different labs. Together they raised 39 issues, 4 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry can be published). I applied 37 and rejected 2.

- The main blocking problem was in **mystery**. I had given it a subsense for uses like *a mystery guest* and *a mystery prize*, defined as "someone or something whose name is not known". Both reviewers pointed out that in those phrases the word works only as a modifier before another noun, so a definition of a person or thing misleads. I removed the subsense and moved the use into the first sense's collocations, with one example and a short note. While there, I widened the first sense to cover people (*he's a bit of a mystery to me*), which one reviewer said was missing.
- In **compliment**, the sense "an action that shows respect" had the example *I'll take that as a compliment*, which is about something said. I moved it to the first sense and wrote a new example about an action. The formal plural sense (*my compliments to the chef*) was redefined as praise or good wishes rather than thanks, and *a backhanded compliment* was added.
- In **proceed**, the reviewers noted that the first sense covered only a person continuing an action, while the examples also had the activity as subject (*the work is proceeding slowly*). The definition was broadened and a one-line note on the two kinds of subject added. *Proceed with caution* moved to that sense.
- Smaller fixes: **fantasy** now allows *a complete fantasy* (countable) and no longer says fantasy is "the activity of imagining", which contradicted its own note; **front desk** gained a subsense for the people who work there (*the front desk said our room was ready*); **certificate** lost an example about cars that depended on local law, and *diploma* became a "compare" rather than a synonym.
- The two rejections: one reviewer called *often before another noun* an invalid grammar code, but it is in the closed list; the other said *puzzle about* is too rare to list beside *puzzle over*, but it is real and *over* already comes first.

The cross-reference checker asked for two links to be placed by hand. *Fantasy* had named *dream* as a synonym, but no sense of *dream* really matches (a dream is usually something you hope to achieve), so I removed the link. *Confuse*, first sense, now lists *puzzle* as a synonym. One definition word, *dig*, was outside the defining vocabulary, so *mining* now says "taking ... out of the ground".
