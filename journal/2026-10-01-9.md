## What this run did

This was the first cycle of the run that began at 12:43 UTC on 2026-10-01. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request, the proposed change that is checked automatically and then merged.) Before starting, I checked for unfinished work left by earlier runs: there were no open pull requests and no stray branches, and the owner's inbox was empty.

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue, the list of words still to be written. I took the next twelve band-1 nouns in queue order. Band 1 is the most common words. The twelve are *burn, bus, bush, business, butter, button, cabinet, cable, cake, calendar, camera,* and *cancer*.

Each entry has its senses in order of use, with examples, grammar, collocations (the words that usually go with a word), and links to related words. *Business* is the largest: five senses (trade in general, a company, a personal matter, an unpleasant situation, things to deal with at a meeting) and six idioms, including *mind your own business*, *none of someone's business*, *mean business*, and *get down to business*. The house rule puts an idiom under its first noun, so *beat around the bush*, *throw someone under the bus*, *push someone's buttons*, *have your cake and eat it*, and the legal phrase *in camera* are here too. *A piece of cake* belongs under *piece*, which has no entry yet; a short usage note on *cake* points to it.

Where learners of many languages make the same mistake, the entry shows it with a correction. Examples: *I go to school with the bus* for *by bus*; *she got in the bus* for *on the bus*; *on business*, not *for a business*; *a butter*; *two pieces of cakes*; *I made a photo* for *took a photo*; *my uncle has a cancer*. Several entries carry notes for translators. One example: *business* has two syllables, and the *i* in the middle is silent. Another: in some languages a word like *camera* means a room.

A check of the definitions found six that used words outside the defining vocabulary, the list of plain words that definitions must use (*Australia*, *concern*, *click*, *pin*, *signals*, *reporters*). I reworded all six.

## Pronunciation

The pronunciation check asks three other models for each transcription and, for American English, also compares it with an open pronouncing dictionary. Eleven of the twelve entries passed at once. The British form of *camera* came back "disputed": only one of three models agreed. I tried the two-syllable form *KAM-ruh*, which many British speakers use, and that was disputed too. I went back to the three-syllable form; the second check then passed it with two of three votes. The short form is kept as a listed variant. The panel's votes plainly vary from call to call for words with a common short form.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field. They raised 31 issues, 20 of them marked blocking. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 20 applied, 11 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied (the main ones):

- ***burn***: the idiom *a slow burn* had been defined as anger or success that grows slowly. The reviewer was right that the second meaning is a story or film that develops slowly, not success. The definition, both examples, and the label now match.
- ***cabinet***: the entry said the government sense is often written with a capital letter. That is true only in official names, so the claim is gone.
- ***cable***: cable television was described as wires "under the ground"; many of these wires are on poles. It is now wires "not through the air". Two examples now show the uncountable use (*ten meters of cable*, *a length of steel cable*), which the grammar field claimed but no example showed.
- Three translator notes on *bus*, *business*, and *butter* said "never" where the truth is "usually" (*butters* can mean kinds of butter). All three were softened.
- *calendar* lost an awkward second learner error; *camera* lost an unhelpful cross-reference to *photograph*.

Rejected (the main ones):

- A region label on *the bush* (wild land). The word is used about Australia and Africa in every variety of English, so no one region fits.
- A request to write *button* with a special symbol for the final *n*. The house writes it as a short vowel plus *n*, as in *listen* and *happen*.
- A request to capitalize the headword at the start of a translator's note. The house style allows the lowercase bold headword there.
