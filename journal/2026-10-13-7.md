# 2026-10-13, 04:44 UTC run, cycle 3: twelve adjectives, *thirsty* to *upset*

## What this cycle did

This cycle was a **build**: it wrote new dictionary entries from the queue (the ordered list of words still to be written). The selector, the script that picks each cycle's job, chose build again because it was the most overdue job. Twelve words were claimed (reserved so that no other run takes them), and all twelve were written and finished:

*thirsty*, *tight*, *tired*, *total*, *tough*, *true*, *typical*, *ugly*, *unable*, *unfair*, *unlikely*, *upset*.

All are band 1 adjectives (band 1 is the most common words). The entries were written with the same care as the first cycle's. *Tight* has eight senses, from clothes (*tight jeans*) and money (*Money is tight this month*) to an informal *mean with money*. *Tough* has seven senses and a short guide to choosing between *tough*, *hard*, and *difficult*. *True* now has six senses. Almost every entry has learner errors, the mistakes learners often make. Examples: *I have thirst* for *I'm thirsty*, *This dress is narrow for me* for *tight on me*, *typical from this region* for *typical of*, and *The news was very upset* for *upsetting*.

## Checks

- **Pronunciation.** All 24 transcriptions were confirmed by the pronunciation panel on the first pass. *Tired* follows the house transcription of *tire*, with two parts (*TIRE-d*), and lists the one-part forms as variants.
- **Inflections** (comparison forms such as *tighter*, *tougher*) all came from the rules. Seven words take *more* and *most*, recorded as a grammar code.
- **Review panel.** The two outside reviewer models raised 36 issues; 30 were blocking, meaning each had to be decided before the entry could be published. I applied 24 and rejected 6, with a logged reason for each. None needed the owner. *Thirsty* and *upset* came back clean.

## What the reviewers found, and what I decided

Applied:
- *true*: sense 2 mixed two meanings. *A true friend* means sincere; *the true cost* means the actual one, as opposed to the one people are told. They are now senses 2 and 3. A note on *True, it's expensive, but...* was removed: both reviewers said that use is a discourse marker, a word that organizes speech, and belongs elsewhere.
- *tough*: *a tough neighborhood* and *a tough guy* are now a sense and a subsense, instead of one definition. *That's tough* is no longer defined as "unlucky". The guide no longer calls *tough* informal.
- *total*: the *complete* sense (*a total failure*) can also follow a verb in formal writing (*The silence was total*). The entry had said it never does.
- *ugly*: the *ugly mood* sense no longer says that violence always follows. The usage note no longer offers *plain* as the polite word.
- *tired*: one learner error was removed, because *tired from waiting* can be correct.

Rejected:
- Four times, the second reviewer said *comparative with more* is not a grammar code. It is one of the closed codes; this has been rejected in earlier runs too.
- Objections to cross-references that the target entries already contain: *sick of* beside *tired of*, and *dirty* beside *unfair*.
- A *formal* label for *unable*. The entry says it is more formal than *can't*, but it is ordinary in neutral writing.

After the review, the cross-reference tool added mirror links in thirteen existing entries. Three landed on the wrong sense and were moved by hand: *tight* on `strict-adj` (to the *rules* sense) and on `close-adj` (to *nearly equal*), and *tired* on `new-adj` (to *not known before*).
