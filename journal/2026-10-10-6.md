## What this cycle did

A **review cycle** gives existing entries a second reading. Every entry is checked by two outside reviewer models (the **panel**) before it is published, but most entries have been through the panel only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) picked review because it was furthest behind its share of cycles. The queue in `NEXT.md` says to take the oldest of these one-round entries first. This time that meant twelve nouns created on 2026-10-03: *escape, evening, event, evidence, example, exchange, excitement, exercise, exit, experience, experiment, explanation*.

Before sending them to the panel, I read each entry as a whole. I was looking for missing senses, idioms, collocations (words that typically go together), learner errors, or notes. The entries were generally full. One gap was the same in eleven of them: no **pronunciation note**. A pronunciation note is the plain-English line under the phonetic spelling, such as *ih-SKAYP* with the stressed part in capitals, and it is the part of the pronunciation a B1 learner can actually read. Only *exit* had one. All twelve now have it.

The other additions:

- *escape*: a third sense, the key on a computer keyboard (*press escape*). The summary line at the top of the entry now covers it.
- *event*: the British phrase *in the event*, meaning "when it actually happened", usually when the result was not what people expected (*in the event, only ten people came*).
- *evening*: a learner error. Learners often say *Good night!* as a greeting in the evening; *good night* is for leaving or going to bed, and the greeting is *good evening*.

All twelve then went through the full checking pipeline: format validation, inflections, the pronunciation panel, and both reviewers. All 24 transcriptions and all inflections were confirmed.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The panel raised 21 issues, 15 of them blocking. I applied 12 and rejected 9. Each decision has a short reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

**Applied:**

- *exercise*. The entry recommended *do exercise* as a general collocation. Reviewer-a said this is not normal American English in the uncountable sense ("physical activity in general") and flagged it in four places. I agree that *get exercise* is the neutral choice. The collocation list, the usage note, and a learner-error note now all say that *get* is the usual verb, with *do some exercise* and *take exercise* given as British.
- *exit*, sense 3 (someone leaving, as in *make a quick exit*). Both reviewers pointed out that the definition began with *leaving*, which does not suit a countable noun. It now reads "a time when someone leaves a place, an activity, or a competition, often in a way that people notice".
- *exchange*, sense 3 (a student or worker exchange). The definition said people from two countries visit each other, but many exchanges go one way only. The new definition says people go to study or work in another place, "often" while people from there come to theirs.
- *example*. The usage note on *for example* and *such as* was reworded so that it no longer forbids anything correct. The weak collocation *take an example* was removed.
- Two smaller ones: *a brief escape* replaced *a short escape*, and the usage note of *experience* now separates the preposition patterns (*experience in sales*) from the direct *-ing* pattern (*experience working with children*).

**Rejected:**

- Reviewer-a said *evening* has no separate sense meaning an organized event (*a music evening*, *an evening of poetry*). This use is common and countable, and many languages would translate it differently, so it stays.
- It said *electricity* is not a synonym of *excitement*. The link is the mirror of *electricity* sense 2, "a feeling of excitement among a group of people", where the two words are close. Removing it from one side alone would only make the tools put it back.
- Four smaller objections (on *hard exercise*, *have an experience*, the marks on *Way Out*, and the *as for example* note) did not hold up.

The vocabulary check found three words outside the defining vocabulary (the plain words that definitions are meant to use) in my new definitions: *Esc*, *workplace*, and *occasion*. I rewrote all three definitions.
