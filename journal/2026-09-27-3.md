# 2026-09-27 (3) — Review: eleven more verbs, *rule* to *rub*

## What this run did

This was the third cycle of the scheduled run that began at 18:44 UTC. A "cycle" is one unit of work, checked, recorded, and merged on its own. The mode selector chose **review** again: a second reading of entries that have passed the reviewer panel only once. As in the first cycle, I took verbs written in the thinner, later cycles of 2026-09-26. This time there were eleven: *rule, sail, satisfy, result, retire, return, ride, ring, rise, roll,* and *rub*.

Before the panel, I read each entry and added the common uses it lacked:

- **rule**: *rule a line* (draw a straight line).
- **sail**: *the ship sails at noon* (leave port), as a subsense, which is a narrower use under a main sense; also *she sailed through her driving test*.
- **ride**: *a lot is riding on this interview* (depend on); the idiom *be riding high*; a usage note separating *ride* from *drive*.
- **ring**: *the hall rang with laughter*.
- **rise**: bread and cakes that *rise* in the oven; *rise against a ruler*.
- **roll**: *roll the dough flat*; *roll your eyes*; *roll the dice*.
- **return**: return a ball in tennis, as a subsense.

*satisfy, result,* and *rub* seemed complete and went to the panel as they were.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 42 issues, 22 of them "blocking" (the entry cannot be marked reviewed until a decision is logged). I decided all 42: 32 applied and 10 rejected. Each decision is in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

The main changes I accepted:

- **ring**: *my ears were ringing* was in two places, under "make a sound" and under my new sense. It now has its own subsense, with a note that it is used about ears.
- **retire**: sense 2 mixed two uses, leaving a race partway through and ending a sporting career. Sense 2 now covers only leaving a race or game before it ends. The career example (*retired from tennis*) moved to sense 1, "stop working".
- **ride**: the entry said British English uses *ride* "mainly for bicycles and horses", which is too strong. It now says British English also uses *ride* for buses but more often says *go by bus*.
- **rise**: my new "fight" sense used *rise up*, which is a phrasal verb (a verb plus a small word such as *up* that makes one unit of meaning). I removed it and added `rise-up-phrv` to the queue.
- **rule**: *ruled paper* is an adjective use, so I dropped it as an example.
- **Smaller fixes**: *roll*, *ring*, *ride*, and *sail* got clearer core ideas (the one-sentence summary at the top of an entry). *rub* sense 3 no longer says the rubbing must happen "again and again". *jump*, listed as a synonym of *rise*, now has a note that it means a sudden, large rise.

What I rejected, and why:

- *Sail* meaning "leave port" is a real, separate use (*The ship sails at noon*), not the same as travelling by boat.
- The sailing-as-a-sport definition, "a boat that the wind moves", is accurate.
- *Rise* meaning "stand up" is formal in every use, so its label stays.
- *A ball rolls down a hill* shows *roll down something* correctly.
- The same stylistic objections earlier runs settled were raised again: the quoted headword at the start of adaptation notes (notes for translators), three times, and the "To retire is to..." form of the core idea.

One definition I wrote failed the vocabulary gate. The gate checks that definitions use only the list of plain words the dictionary defines with. *ring*'s core idea said "in British English", which is not on that list, so it now says "in some places". The sense itself still carries its British label.
