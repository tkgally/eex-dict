# 2026-10-03 (04:43 UTC run, cycle 4): second review of twelve entries, *shock* to *bubble*

## What this run did

This was the fourth and last cycle of the run that started at 04:43 UTC. The first three cycles merged as pull requests #198 (a second review of *multiply* to *shine*), #199 (twelve new nouns, *disease* to *dream*), and #200 (twelve new nouns, *dress* to *economy*). The mode selector (the script that chooses each cycle's kind of work) picked **review**: a second reading of entries that have passed only one round of the two outside reviewer models. I add what is missing by hand, and then the reviewers read the entries again.

The rule is to take the oldest such entries. These were eleven verbs drafted on 2026-09-29, *shock, shop, shout, show, shrink, shut, sing, sit, sleep, slide,* and *border*, and the noun *bubble*.

These were already full: most had a core idea (a one-sentence summary of what the senses share), learner errors (common mistakes shown with a correction), and usage notes. So my hand additions were small:

- **One idiom**: *shop till you drop* (informal and humorous).
- **Two usage notes**: *slide*, whose past form is *slid*, compared with *slip* (sudden, not meant); and, in *shock*, that *get a shock* is the everyday way to talk about electricity.
- **Collocations** (typical word combinations) for *sing*: *sing in a choir*, *sing in the shower*, *sing someone to sleep*.
- **Two cross-reference fixes** (links between entries): *represent* was listed as a synonym of *show* in its sense about films; it belongs with the sense about pictures and maps, and moved there. In *shout*, the synonym *cry* now carries a note saying that this use is more literary.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 27 issues. Twenty-one were *blocking*, which means I had to decide each one before the entries could stay marked as reviewed. I applied 16 decisions and rejected 11. None needed to go to the owner. Every decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

The reviewers were right about these:

- *Sleep*: sense 1 said you sleep "with your eyes closed and your mind and body not active". Eyes are usually closed but not always, and the mind is not simply inactive. It now says "the natural state that people are in each night, usually with your eyes closed". The core idea also described the sexual sense as sharing a bed, which can happen without sex; it now says "have sex with someone".
- *Bubble*: the core idea said a bubble is "round" and holds "air"; it can hold any gas. Sense 3, a protected world, had made ignorance part of the meaning. The translators' note (adaptation note) said that a protected world could "burst at any moment", which fits only the price sense. All of these were reworded. My new learner error (*the water is making bubbles because it is boiled*) could be correct in some situations and was removed.
- *Shut*: sense 3 said a person is kept in a place "by closing the door"; it can be any door or a gate. The usage note said *shut* and *close* are the same for books; for books *close* is usual.
- *Slide*: both reviewers said *run* is not a synonym of *slide* meaning "move something across a surface". I removed it, and also removed the matching reference in the entry *run*, because the back-link tool would otherwise put it back at the next lint pass (the periodic check-up of the whole dictionary).

I rejected these:

- The American transcription of *sleep* without a length mark is house practice.
- *Shut* meaning to stop being open for business stays labelled British: in this dictionary a region label means *mainly* used there.
- *Cry* stays a synonym of *shout*: the entry *cry* has a sense "shout or call out loudly" that lists *shout*.
- *Say* and *tell* stay as synonyms of *show* in the sense "make it clear that something is true": *the figures tell us* and *the results say* are common.
- *Sing* sense 2 (*birds sing*) keeps its short definition; the restriction to birds lives in the explanation, as the style guide requires.
