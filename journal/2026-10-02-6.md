## Reviewer precision

The two outside reviewer models raise issues in categories called families. Precision is the share of a reviewer's issues that I apply rather than reject. A standing rule switches a family off for a reviewer when its precision falls below 30 percent over twenty or more decisions. Six such pairs are already switched off.

This pass, the second reviewer's **phrase** family crossed the line: 29 percent, 7 applied out of 24. Nearly all its rejected objections asked to move an idiom away from the entry where the house keyword rule puts it. That rule files an idiom under its first noun, so *at least* stays under *least*.

On 2026-10-01 the session's safety check refused the same kind of edit for the first reviewer's pronunciation family, treating it as a reduction of review. So I did not attempt a switch-off. The phrase family is held for your ruling, together with the pronunciation family, which is now at 28 percent. Both stay switched on, and their objections that break settled rules are rejected one by one. The line is in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`, and the figures are in `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md`.

Overall precision: the first reviewer 71 percent (3,040 of 4,308), the second 59 percent (836 of 1,429).
