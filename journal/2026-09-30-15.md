Some points worth knowing:

- Idioms are filed under their keyword, the first noun: *beauty is in the eye of the beholder*, *have a bee in your bonnet*, *the beginning of the end*, *be on your best behavior*, *tighten your belt*, *below the belt*, *give someone the benefit of the doubt*.

- *beer* separates the drink (usually uncountable) from a glass or bottle of it (*two beers, please*).
- *behavior* has the British spelling *behaviour* as a variant.
- *bench* has four senses: the seat, a work table, the players' bench in sport, and the judges in a court.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field of every entry. They raised 31 issues, 17 of them marked blocking. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 25 applied, 6 rejected, none sent on to the owner. reviewer-a was right unusually often this time.

Applied:

- *belief*: all three definitions called a belief "a feeling". reviewer-a pointed out that a belief is an idea you accept as true, and that belief in a god is accepting that it exists. The senses now read "an idea that you accept as true, often without proof", "the state of accepting that something exists or is real", and "trust that someone or something is good, able to do well, or will succeed". The core idea (the one-sentence summary of the senses) and the usage note were changed to match.
- *behavior*: I had written that the plural *behaviors* belongs only to technical writing. That overstates it; the plural is common in ordinary writing for different kinds of behavior. Three fields now say so consistently.
- *bee*: the definition said bees make honey; only some kinds do. The meeting sense (*spelling bee*) no longer says it is for children.
- *beginning*: the second sense (*small beginnings*, *the beginnings of a cold*) is now "the first stage of something as it starts to grow or develop, or the first signs of it", which covers all its examples.
- *bench*: the sports sense now includes the coaches who sit there.
- Smaller fixes: *beer*'s usage note now agrees with its grammar; the *beauty* proverb lost a wrong *formal* label; the seat-belt sense of *belt* is defined directly; *berry*'s note no longer claims that strawberries are true berries.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- That *spelling bee* is not mainly American. House rules use a region label to mean *mainly* used there.
- That *fifty different beers* belongs with the glass-or-bottle sense. It means kinds of beer, a use of the drink sense, and the explanation says so.
- That *bed* should be marked uncountable because of *go to bed*. The no-article use is explained in the sense.

**Cross-references.** The back-link script added two: *origin* now lists *beginning* as a synonym, and *advantage* lists *benefit*. Both are on the right sense.
