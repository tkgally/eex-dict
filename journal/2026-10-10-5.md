## What the checks found

**Pronunciation.** All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the pronunciation panel, and the American ones also by the open CMU pronouncing dictionary. Every plural came out right from the spelling rules, *wives* included.

**The two reviewers** (independent models from other companies, which check every field) raised 22 issues, 15 of them blocking. I accepted 11 and rejected 11:

- *Accepted.* *weekend* was defined as Saturday and Sunday only. It now also covers Friday evening to Sunday night, which is how many speakers use it. In *week*, *in the week* was shown as if it were neutral English; it now appears only in the explanation, as British. In *weight*, the usage note had said *Her weight is 50 kilos* was just as correct as *She weighs 50 kilos*; it now says the verb is much more natural. A literal example of the "heavy thing" sense read like the "worry" sense, so I replaced it. In *wedding*, the note on *marriage* versus *wedding* now says "usually", because *marriage* can name the ceremony in formal English. One reviewer pointed out that the adaptation notes used quotation marks for words. I changed those to the house marks (bold for a word named as a word, italics for an example) in all twelve entries.
- *Rejected.* One reviewer said that *the whole of* is a determiner, not a noun, and flagged five fields on that ground. I rejected all five: in *the whole of the morning*, *whole* comes after *the* and takes *of*, so it is a noun. The other reviewer said two phrase definitions under *wheel* did not start with a base-form verb, but they do (*waste time…*, *do the things…*). Three minor link objections on *wheelchair* were rejected as house practice.

**Vocabulary check.** The check found three words outside the defining vocabulary (the plain words that definitions are meant to use). *Monday* and *Friday* appeared in a definition of *week*, which now reads "the days of the week that are not the weekend". *steers* appeared in the summary line of *wheel*, which was reworded.

**Back-links** (the matching link the tools add in the other direction): four were added. They are in *web*, in *importance* sense 2 (power or influence), in *depth*, and in *chair*. I checked all four and all are on the right sense.
