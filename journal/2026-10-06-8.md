## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 56 issues, 53 from the first reviewer ("reviewer-a") and 3 from the second ("reviewer-b"). I applied 42 and rejected 14, and logged a reason for each.

Applied, among others:

- *paper*: the idiom *on paper* had two meanings joined by "or" (in writing; in theory rather than in practice). Both reviewers flagged it. It is now two idioms: *put something down on paper* and *on paper*.
- *parliament*: the examples now write *Parliament* with a capital letter where they mean one country's parliament (*She was elected to Parliament*), and the definition says "most of them elected", since some members of some parliaments are not.
- *park*: the senses for nature parks, theme or business parks, and baseball parks were made more exact.
- *part*: the usage note claimed that *Part of the walls were painted* was correct. Both reviewers objected, and the sentence is doubtful at best, so the note now gives a safe example: *Part of the cake was eaten*.
- *pair*: an example with a plural verb after *the pair*, more British than American, was replaced, and the usage note now says the plural verb is especially British.

Rejected, among others: objections to the way word combinations are grouped (*a pair of shoes* is filed under "noun + preposition" in every entry of this kind), and several objections to region labels (*pants* for underwear, *part* for the line in your hair, an exam *paper*), since a region label in this dictionary means "mainly used there", not "only used there".
