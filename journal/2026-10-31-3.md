# Run report, 2026-10-31 (third run)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the tool that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to write. Its reason was that build had the largest "debt" of the eligible kinds of work; the day's budget had US$13.58 left and no entries were waiting in draft. It claimed twelve band-2 verbs (band 2 is the second most important frequency group) in queue order: *postpone*, *predict*, *present*, *pretend*, *promote*, *propose*, *protest*, *publish*, *purchase*, *pursue*, *qualify*, and *recall*. All twelve were drafted from scratch, checked, reviewed by both reviewers, corrected, and marked `reviewed`, which means they will appear on the public site.

Pre-flight found no open pull requests, no entry reports from the public, and nothing in the owner's inbox. Five old branches with no unmerged work remain; each already has a line in the curator file asking for it to be deleted, since this tool set cannot delete branches.

**Checks before review.** The inflection tool generated every verb form from its spelling rules (*qualifies*, *qualified*; *pursuing*). The pronunciation check, a panel of models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary consulted at run time, accepted all 24 transcriptions (American and British for each verb). For *present* and *pretend* the CMU dictionary preferred a different first vowel, but three of four models agreed with ours, which meets the agreement rule.

**What the reviewers found.** The two reviewers (reviewer A, a Claude model; reviewer B, a Google model) raised 75 issues, 10 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry can be marked reviewed). Every one was decided by hand: 69 applied, 6 rejected, none escalated to you. The main changes:

- Examples that stated general truths about the world broke the rule against checkable facts: *early reading skills predict success at school* and *does a tomato qualify as a fruit?* were replaced with everyday sentences.
- *present* gained a sense the draft missed: putting on a play, a film, a concert, or a show of art (*The theater is presenting a new play*). Its "problem or chance" sense was reworded from *cause* to *be or create*, which fits *the hills presented no problem*.
- *pretend*: the "claim" sense (*I don't pretend to understand it*) was redefined so it no longer says the claim is usually false, and an example that really belonged to the first sense was replaced.
- Grammar: *propose* in the marriage sense can take an object (*propose marriage*), so it is now transitive or intransitive; the chase sense of *pursue* is not usually passive, and *recall* does occur in continuous tenses (*she was recalling her childhood*). Those wrong restrictions were removed.
- Coverage: *pursue* gained a subsense for trying to start a romantic relationship, and *recall* gained the American political sense (*recall the governor*). Several examples were changed from British to American wording (*vacations*, *the store*, *go to college*).

**What was rejected, and why.** Both reviewers asked for *propose a toast*. Under the house keyword rule an idiom goes under its first noun, *toast*, which has no entry yet, so it was rejected here and noted for later. Two objections to short explanations naming a subject or form restriction were rejected because house rule 15 puts such restrictions in the explanation. One request to change the pattern *verb + object + as + noun or adjective* was rejected: it is the closest label in the closed list.

**Cross-references.** The cross-reference tool adds a back-link in the other entry when it can tell which sense to attach it to. In nine cases it could not, and all nine were placed by hand (for example, *remember* now lists *recall*, *sell* lists *purchase* as an opposite, and *guess* and *remind* carry short notes telling them apart from *predict* and *recall*). Twenty-seven missing words named in the new entries were added to the queue, among them *proposal*, *publisher*, *pursuit*, and *qualification*.

**Spend.** US$0.45 of the day's US$15 budget (reviewer B and the pronunciation panel; reviewer A runs at no charge). The day's total is US$1.87.

**What did not work.** A background review batch finished one entry (*recall*) while its log line was being read, and the entry was reviewed a second time by mistake, costing about US$0.03. Five definitions first used words outside the defining vocabulary (*exhibition*, *rank*, *buyers*, *membership*, *further*); all were reworded.

**What is next.** More band-2 verbs in queue order, or whatever the selector picks; the next whole-dictionary lint pass is due in four runs.

**What needs you.** Nothing new from this cycle. The standing items are in `NEXT.md` under "For the owner".
