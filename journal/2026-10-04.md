## What this cycle did

The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks the kind of work each cycle does so that building, reviewing, and checking stay in balance) chose **build**: drafting new entries from the queue of words still to write. It allowed up to twelve. The claim tool took the next twelve words from band 1, the most frequent words, in queue order: the nouns *fog, folder, food, fool, foot, force, forest, fork, form, freedom, fridge,* and *friend*. All twelve were drafted, put through the full checking pipeline, and are now published as `reviewed`.

Each entry has senses, examples, collocations (words that typically go with the headword), learner errors (common mistakes, with the correction), and adaptation notes (warnings for a future translator). Highlights:

- **foot** has three senses (the body part, the unit of length, and the bottom of something, with a subsense for *the foot of the bed*) and eleven idioms, among them *on foot*, *get cold feet*, *put your foot in it*, *find your feet*, and *stand on your own two feet*. Its learner errors cover *foots*, *a ten-feet wall*, and *by walk*.
- **force** has six senses, from physical strength to *the police force* and *the armed forces* to the force of a law (*come into force*), with idioms such as *join forces* and *force of habit*, and a note separating **force**, **strength**, and **power**. One learner error targets a common confusion: *with all my forces* for *with all my strength*.
- **form** has seven senses; **friend** has four, including the social-media sense.
- Five entries have a short note on choosing between near-synonyms: *fog/mist/haze*, *food/meal/dish*, *force/strength/power*, *forest/wood/jungle*, *freedom/liberty*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside reviewer models read every field of every entry. They raised 42 issues, 30 of them "blocking" (an issue that must be settled before an entry can be published). I read each one and decided it myself: 33 applied, 9 rejected, none escalated to you.

Applied (the reviewer was right):
- *fog*: a subsense for *a fog of smoke* was dropped; *a cloud of smoke* is the normal phrase.
- *form*: the "shape" sense was also covering *in the form of a chart*, which is not about shape. That use now has its own sense ("the way in which something exists, appears, or is presented"). *True to form* no longer says "especially in a bad way", and the British and old-fashioned labels came off *bad form*.
- *fork*: the road sense no longer claims a tree "fork" can be either branch; the garden-fork definition now names the long handle and long metal points.
- *freedom*: both reviewers objected that *prison* is not an antonym of freedom (a place, not a state). It is now *captivity*.
- *food*: the "kinds of food" sense defined *food* with the word *food*; it was reworded.
- Smaller fixes on *forest*, *fridge*, *friend* (the *my friend* note wrongly said it needs prior mention), *force*, and *fool*.

Rejected (with a logged reason):
- The objection to the American transcription of *food* without a length mark. That is house style for American English, settled long ago.
- The objection to the *folder* false-friend note. In several European languages a word like *folder* does mean an advertising leaflet; the note already hedges with "several".
- Two objections on *foot* and three label requests on *friend* and *forest* phrases (a region label means "mainly used there").

The linter that checks definitions against the defining vocabulary (the roughly 2,400 plain words definitions should be written with) found seven words outside it, such as *navy*, *slave*, *cage*, and *dig*. I rewrote those definitions in plainer words.
