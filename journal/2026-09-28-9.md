The oldest such group was the function words written on 2026-09-22. I took its eight modal verbs as one block, because they describe each other and must agree: *could, may, might, ought, shall, should, will,* and *would*.

These entries were correct as far as they went, but several common uses were missing. Before sending them to the panel, I added by hand:

- **could**: past permission (*we could stay up late on Fridays*) and imagined situations (*If I had a car, I could drive you*).
- **may** and **ought**: *may have* (*She may have missed the bus*) and *ought to have* (*You ought to have told me*).
- **might**: imagined situations, the "true, but" use (*He might be rich, but...*), and the old mixed third sense split into a gentle suggestion and a very formal request (*Might I ask...?*).
- **shall**: a strong promise or order (*You shall have your money back*).
- **should**: *should* after *that* (*It's strange that you should mention her*).
- **will** and **would**: refusing (*The car won't start*; *The door wouldn't open*), a guess about now (*That'll be Sam*), and softened opinions (*I'd say he's about forty*).
- Phrases: *could be, may well, might well, I might have known, how should I know?, I should think, if you will*.

I also fixed two kinds of house-style problems. Eight notes showed a wrong form in italics (such as *could of* or *I would know her*); the house rule is that wrong forms appear only in the "incorrect" line of a learner-error box, so I rewrote those notes and added *could of* and *might of* as learner errors instead. And nine cross-references listed the same word twice in one sense, once as a synonym and once as a word to compare (for example *may* and *might*); each pair now has one type.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 19 issues, 10 of them "blocking" (the entry cannot stay marked reviewed until I log a decision). I applied 14 and rejected 5. *will* and *would* had no issues at all.

What I accepted:

- **might**: my new formal-permission sense said "ask if you may do something", but both examples (*Might I suggest...?*, *Might I ask why...?*) are really polite ways to make a suggestion or ask a question. The definition now covers both.
- **ought**: my *ought to have* sense said the action was not done; that is usual but not certain, and the definition now says "usually". The usage note had called *oughtn't to* "mainly British"; it now says it is rare and formal, especially in American English.
- **should**: I had labeled the whole new "after *that*" sense formal, but *It's strange that you should say that* is ordinary speech. The label is gone; the explanation says the *suggest that ... should* use is formal.
- **shall**: the tag *Let's start, shall we?* was marked with a verb pattern it does not show; the pattern is removed.
- Three missing bold marks, and a too-narrow note on *may* for permission.

What I rejected, and why:

- The first reviewer said *could* does not rhyme with *good* and *should*. It does; all three have the same vowel. It is now a settled point.
- The second reviewer objected to the "British" label on the future sense of *shall*, because the note mentions formal American writing. A region label here means "mainly used there", which is the house rule.
- Two small format objections (a missing grammar pattern where none exists for *have* plus a past participle, and an ellipsis in a quoted question) were house style.
