# Run report, 2026-10-27 (fourth run of the day)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the tool that picks each cycle's job from what is most overdue) chose a build cycle: writing new entries. It built ten, each through the full checking pipeline, and all ten are now published.

Five of them are *second homographs*: words spelled like an existing entry with the same part of speech but an unrelated meaning, so they get an entry of their own with `-2` in its name. They had been waiting for a hand claim because the queue (the list of words still to write) has no way to hold a second homograph:

- **bark** (noun 2): the sound a dog makes, and a short rough sound a person makes (*a bark of laughter*); with the idiom *someone's bark is worse than their bite*.
- **ash** (noun 2): the tree, and its wood.
- **temple** (noun 2): the flat part at the side of your head.
- **bill** (noun 2): a bird's beak, and, in American English, the stiff front part of a cap. The existing entry **beak** now points to it as a synonym.
- **pulse** (noun 2): beans, peas, and lentils as food.

The other five are adjectives named in the word-family boxes of existing entries: **lost** (five senses, from *we got lost* to *lost in thought* and *lost time*), **middle** (*the middle seat*, *middle incomes*), **own** (*her own room*, *he cuts his own hair*, with the phrases *on your own* and *of your own*), **south** (*the south side*, *a south wind*, with a short note on **south** versus **southern**), and **top** (*the top shelf*, *a top student*, *top speed*).

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** Each entry goes to two reviewers from different companies. Their pronunciations were also checked by a three-model panel against an open pronouncing dictionary; all twenty transcriptions agreed. The reviewers raised 79 issues, 12 of them blocking (an entry cannot be published until each blocking issue is fixed or rejected with a reason). I logged 77 decisions: 59 changes made and 18 objections rejected, with none passed up to you.

The changes that mattered most:

- Both reviewers pointed out that **lost** was missing *lost in thought* (giving all your attention to something). It is now a sense of its own.
- One reviewer said the cap meaning of **bill** was missing. It is now sense 2, labeled American.
- Several examples and notes used British forms in this American-first dictionary: *do up a button*, *top marks*, *lost property*, *in her year*, *got back*. All were replaced.
- One example, *Ash trees lose their leaves late in the fall*, made a claim about plants that could be checked, and was probably wrong; it was removed.
- The notes on **own**, **south**, and **top** said the same thing in two or three fields. Each fact now sits in one place, and three notes were removed.
- A learner-error item on **lost** (*I lost the bus*) was really about the verb **lose**, which already covers it, so it was removed.

Most rejections were requests to add idioms that by house rule belong under another word: *a lost cause* goes under **cause**, *hold your own* under **hold**, *come into your own* under **come**, and *be lost for words* under **word**. One reviewer said *on your own* should move to a separate pronoun entry. I rejected that under the same rule, which keeps an idiom under its keyword whatever that word's part of speech. Two other objections were aimed at the inflection notes, which a script writes from the grammar codes, so they were rejected as well.

One back-link (the matching reference a tool adds to the other entry) could not be placed by the tool: **confused** now lists **lost** as a synonym, placed by hand.

**Spend.** US$0.28 this cycle, for the pronunciation panel and reviewer B. Reviewer A runs on your subscription at no charge. The day's total is US$3.50 of the US$15 cap.

**What did not work.** The tool that stamps an entry's frequency band from the queue cannot tell homographs apart. Each new second homograph therefore got the first one's band and its "defining vocabulary" mark (the defining vocabulary is the core word list used to write definitions). Four of the five are marked as defining-vocabulary words, though the dog's bark, the tree, the side of the head and the bird's bill are not the meanings that put those spellings on the list. This is part of a known gap (`wiki/notes/homograph-queue-and-inflection.md`). The wiki is twenty words under its 30,000-word cap, so I did not add to that note; the problem is in the log and in `NEXT.md` instead.

**What is next.** Build cycles continue with family-box and cross-reference words in bands 1 and 2: *tonight* (adverb), *bruise*, *curl*, *figure* (verbs), and *bark* the verb, which now pairs with the new noun. Review cycles continue the coverage pass over the most common entries.

**What needs you.** Nothing new from this cycle. The open items in `NEXT.md` under "For the owner" are unchanged.

## Cycle 2: review

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose a review cycle: a second, careful reading of entries that are already published, adding what is missing and fixing what is wrong. It read six entries.

Three come from the *coverage pass*, your ruling that the most common entries be reread for completeness, starting with those that have the most senses: **away** (adverb), **power** (noun), and **quarter** (noun). That makes fifty-five entries read in the pass so far. The other three were follow-ups the last run left in `NEXT.md`: **breath**, **look**, and **go**.

What was added:

- **breath**: *hold your breath* in its literal sense (under water, or while waiting), and also *with bated breath* and *waste your breath*.
- **look**: *look sharp* (an old-fashioned British way of saying "hurry"), a sense for *Look what you've done!*, and an informal sense for *We're looking to buy a house* (planning to). Three phrasal verbs are now queued: *look into*, *look up to*, *look down on*.
- **go**: *still going strong*.
- **power**: a new sense for *the power of music* and *the power of words*, the ability to move people's feelings.
- **quarter**: the school term used in some American schools (*the spring quarter*), and the literary phrase *give no quarter*.
- **away**: the sense of fading now also covers removing (*she wiped the tears away*).

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 98 issues, 15 of them blocking. I logged 98 decisions: 82 changes and 16 rejections, with nothing passed up to you.

One mistake was mine. For **go** I added two new senses, *disappear* and the casual *say* (*then he goes, "No way!"*). I had not noticed that the entry already covers both as subsenses: smaller numbered parts of a sense, here 2a and 10a. Both reviewers caught it, and I removed the duplicates. The lesson is now in `NEXT.md`: check a new sense against the entry's subsenses before adding it.

The other changes were smaller:

- Some definitions used the headword itself (for **quarter** the coin, and the *activity* subsense of **go**). They were reworded.
- Signposts (the short labels that help a reader pick a sense from the list) longer than three words were shortened.
- Notes that repeated each other were removed, in **breath**, **quarter**, **away**, and **look**.
- Two collocations were wrong: *close quarters* is not "living space", and *with all your power* is not natural English. Both were removed.

Rejections:

- Both reviewers asked for **look** and **go** to have their senses reordered by frequency. I rejected this, because other entries refer to these senses by number.
- They asked to remove the link to the phrasal verb *look like*. That entry already exists, so removing the link is not a change for this cycle.
- They objected to *not used in continuous tenses* as a verb pattern. It is in the closed list of patterns.
- *Go for* (meaning "choose") is a phrasal verb and would need its own entry.

**Spend.** US$0.43 this cycle. The day's total is US$3.93 of the US$15 cap.

**What did not work.** Only the duplicated **go** senses, described above.

**What is next.** The coverage pass continues with **close** (adjective), **write**, **way**, and **up**.

**What needs you.** Nothing new.

## Cycle 3: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose another build cycle. The next rows in the queue were common verbs that other entries already point to: entries with links to them that led nowhere until now. I took eight of them, and all eight are now published.

- **claim**: say that something is true; ask for money you have a right to; take something as yours; and the news use *the storm claimed three lives*.
- **clear**: clear the table; the sky clears; clear someone of a crime; clear a plan with your manager; a horse clears a fence; a check clears; clear customs.
- **email**: with the older spelling *e-mail* as a variant, and the common error *email to you*.
- **finish**: finish your homework; the movie finishes at ten; finish your milk; finish second in a race; finish with the scissors. It has a short note on choosing between **finish**, **end**, and **complete**, and the error *finish to read*.
- **found**: start a company or a school; be founded on an idea. The learner error here is a real trap: the past of **found** is **founded**, while **found** on its own is the past of **find**.
- **last**: the meeting lasted two hours; shoes that last; food that will last us a week.
- **mark**: eight senses, from cups marking a table to marking homework, marking a birthday, marking a message as read, and *a period marked by illness*.
- **match**: shoes that match a dress; fingerprints that match; match words with meanings; match a price. A note explains **match**, **fit**, and **suit**.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** All sixteen pronunciations passed the panel. The reviewers raised 77 issues, 7 of them blocking. I logged 77 decisions: 61 changes and 16 rejections, with nothing passed up to you.

The blocking issues:

- Two definitions used their own headword: **email** was defined with *email*, and **mark** with *mark*. Both were rewritten.
- The learner-error note on **match** said wrongly that the verb never takes **to** (*match each photo to its name* is correct). It now gives the real rule.
- The race sense of **finish** was coded with an object, although that sense takes none.
- Both reviewers asked for *mark something as done* and *be marked by*, and each now has a place in the entry.

Smaller fixes:

- One reviewer asked for *clear customs*, and the other for *finish with something*; both are now senses.
- The British-only label came off *mark* for schoolwork, since Americans use it too.
- Several notes that said the same thing twice were cut.

Rejections:

- Idioms whose keyword is a noun, under the house rule: *clear your throat*, *clear the air*, *mark my words*, *mark time*.
- A request to mark *last* as never used in the continuous form. *The meeting is lasting longer than planned* is normal English.
- Requests to drop synonyms that simply mirror links already in other entries.

Four matching back-links could not be placed by the tool. I placed them by hand in **approve**, **base**, **continue**, and **celebrate**.

**Spend.** US$0.29 this cycle. The day's total is US$4.22 of the US$15 cap.

**What did not work.** Nothing new. One of my own commands got stuck waiting, because a check that looked for the reviewing program also matched its own command line; I stopped it. That cost only time.

**What is next.** More common verbs from the queue (*mind*, *miss*, *offer*, *point*), and the coverage pass in review cycles.
