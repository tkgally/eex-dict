# 2026-10-20, cycle 2 of the 12:44 UTC run: eleven phrasal verbs from *go out* to *lie down*

## What this cycle did

This was a **build** cycle: new entries written from the queue (the ordered list of words still to write), chosen by the scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) because building was the most overdue kind of work. The queue is working through the most common phrasal verbs (verb-plus-particle units such as *give up*). Twelve were claimed (reserved for this run so that no other run drafts them):

*go out, go over, go through, grow up, hand over, hang up, hold on, keep on, keep up, lead to, leave out, lie down*.

*Lead to* was not drafted. It means *cause* or *be the way to a place*, and the verb entry `lead-v` already covers both uses in its senses 4 and 5. The house rule for a verb followed by a preposition whose meaning is already in the verb's own senses is to mark the queue row as a duplicate, and that is what I did.

The other eleven were drafted in full: every sense with a definition, grammar, two to four examples, collocations (typical word partners), and cross-references; a short opening summary (the *core idea*) where an entry has three or more senses; learner errors, usage notes, and translator's notes (*adaptation notes*) where they help. For example, *lie down* warns about its past tense *lay down* (not *lied down*) and about *lay*, which needs an object; *grow up* warns against *When I will grow up*; *hang up* says English uses *hang up*, not *close the phone*.

Before drafting, I also did a small pronunciation task left by the last lint pass. *Break up* lacked the secondary stress mark the other phrasal verbs have; I added it and re-checked the ten phrasal verbs whose stress the pronunciation panel disputed on 2026-10-19. They are all still disputed, so their flags and the owner's curator lines stay, and the note now says not to re-run them without a new reason.

## Pronunciation and forms

All 22 transcriptions (American and British for each entry) were verified by the pronunciation panel. The verb forms come from the inflection tool and its table of irregular verbs (*went out*, *hung up*, *lay down*, *lain down*). One oddity: the table's note for *hang*, "hanged for execution", is copied onto *hang up*, where it does not apply. That note is written by a script, so I left it; it is harmless but worth a look when the inflection notes are next reviewed.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 42 issues on the eleven entries; *lie down* drew none. I applied 22 and rejected 20.

Applied, the main ones:

- Four opening summaries (*go out*, *go over*, *go through*, *hold on*) did not cover every sense; all four were rewritten. *Leave out*'s summary said *leave ... outside* (using the headword's own verb) and now says *keep ... outside*.
- *Go out* sense 6 mixed letters being sent with programs being broadcast, but the examples showed only sending; the broadcast part is gone. Its usage note now separates *go out with friends* from *go out with* one person.
- *Hang up* can take an object (*hang up the phone*), so the sense is now *transitive or intransitive*, with a new example.
- Notes in *keep on* and *go through* stated a rule for the whole entry that holds only for some senses (the *-ing* form after *keep on*; the object after *through*); they now say which senses.
- *Hold on* "keep going" now also covers keeping a lead until the end of a game, which its collocation *hold on to win* needed.

Rejected, with the main reasons:

- reviewer-b again called the pattern *verb + adverb or preposition* non-standard and wanted definitions to start with *to*. Both are settled house practice.
- reviewer-a said *hold onto* is not a normal way to write *hold on to*. It is, for keeping things as well as gripping them.
- reviewer-a said *go over well* (be received) is standard British too. The region label means *mainly used there*, and British usually says *go down well*.
- *The police went over the house* (searched it carefully) is natural English.

reviewer-a's reply on *hold on* had no verdict for one field (the cross-references of sense 3). A known problem; the partial record was adjudicated as it stands.

One automatic back-link (the reverse cross-reference a script adds) landed on the wrong sense: *wait* sense 3 (*be left until later*) got *hold on* as a synonym. I moved it by hand to sense 1.
