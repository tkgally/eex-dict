## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 22 issues, 21 from reviewer-a and 1 from reviewer-b. I agreed with 10 and fixed the entries in my own words:

- *council*: the note on British uses such as *council house* and *council tax* said **council** before a noun means *provided by the local government*. Council tax is paid to the local government, not provided by it, so the note now says *of or run by the local government*.
- *country*: the music sense said country music has "simple songs about ordinary life", which is a judgment, not a definition. It now says the music grew from the old songs of farming areas of the United States. The note that compares *country*, *nation*, and *state* used *the state* in its meaning "the government", which is a different sense; it now treats a state as a country seen as a political unit.
- *courage*: a learner-error note said that before a to-infinitive you must say *the courage*. That is too absolute (*few have courage to resist* is possible in formal writing), so it now says *the courage* is the usual form.
- *course*: *coursework* was listed as a word combination with *course*, but it is a single compound word, so it was removed.
- *cow*: the example for the sense "the female of some other large animals" said *the cows and their calves*, which a reader would take to mean farm cows. It now says *two cow whales and their calves*.
- *crack*: the drug sense now says, in its explanation, that crack is a form of cocaine. The word *cocaine* stays out of the definition itself because it is outside the defining vocabulary (the 2,394 plain words definitions must use).
- *crash*: the note comparing *crash*, *accident*, and *collision* now says a crash is a vehicle hitting something, *such as another vehicle*, with great force; a short comparison note was made to apply only to the road-accident sense.

I rejected 12. Reviewer-a called three sets of word combinations such as *a country road* and *country music* "adjective + noun"; the house style calls a noun used before another noun "noun + noun". It said *councilor* is a wrong spelling of *counselor*, but they are different words (a council member and an adviser). It said the explanation of *second cousin* was wrong; a child of your parent's cousin is exactly a second cousin. It wanted a British label on *pluck up the courage*, which is used widely in American English too, and said a plural verb with *couple* (*the couple are*) is British only, though it is common in American writing for two people. Reviewer-b's one issue said the grammar code "singular or plural verb" on *council* contradicts the translator's note saying American English uses a singular verb; the code records that both are possible and the note says where each is used, so I rejected it, but softened the note to *usually*, as the entry for *crowd* already says.
