## Cross-references

When an entry names another as a synonym or a word to compare, a script adds a matching link on the other side (a "back-link"). It has to guess which sense of the other word to attach it to, and it often guesses the first sense. This pass it wrote ten back-links, all prompted by the new entries from today. I checked each one by hand. Five were on the wrong sense, and I moved them:

- *alarm* ("make someone afraid or worried") now sits beside *worry* in the sense "make anxious", not "feel anxious".
- *dream* ("think about something you would very much like") now sits beside *wish* in the sense "wish for".
- *go* and *run*, in their "machine works" senses, now sit beside *work* in its "machine" sense, not "have a job".
- *take* ("win something in a competition") now sits beside *win* in the sense "get a prize".

The other five were right: *breathe/whisper*, *hand/arm*, *hope/wish*, *house/apartment*, and *threaten/warn*. There were no type mismatches, where one side calls a pair synonyms and the other calls them words to compare. After the moves, a second run of the script changed nothing.
