# Run report, 2026-11-08 (run qdu7dw)

## Cycle 1: lint

**Why this cycle was a lint.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) forces a lint, a housekeeping pass over the whole dictionary and its knowledge base, after every ten runs. Ten runs had passed since the last one on 2026-11-07, so this cycle did no drafting.

**Before starting.** There were no open pull requests left by an earlier run, no stray `claude/` branches, nothing in the owner's inbox, and no open "Entry report" issues from readers.

**Mechanical checks.** The word caps on the rule files and wiki, the internal links of the knowledge base, the entry validator, and the unit tests were all clean.

**Cross-references.** When entry A names entry B as a synonym, antonym, or "compare" word, entry B should usually name A back; these return links are called mirror back-links. The cross-reference tool added two automatically: `fist` now points to `hand`, and `gas station` to `garage`. Four more could not be placed by the tool, because the target entry has several senses and the tool no longer guesses which one is meant. I placed them by hand, each on the first sense, with a short note where the link needs one:

- `genius` (a very clever person): antonym `fool`, and synonym `mind`, with the note that the link is to *a great mind*;
- `flaw` (a fault in a plan or argument): synonym `hole`, as in *a hole in the plan*;
- `flat` (an apartment): compare `house`, noted as a whole building, usually for one family.

These are small edits to fields a model owns, made by me one by one, not by a script.

**Queue housekeeping.** Six claim files (records of which words a past run had reserved) were spent, because every word in them now has an entry, so they were removed. Twenty-seven queue rows that tools had added without a frequency band were banded by the three-model word-list vote, for US$0.004; no row had votes more than one band apart, so none needed settling by hand. The repair tool for headwords that lost a hyphen or period found nothing to fix.

**Reviewer precision.** Precision is the share of a reviewer's blocking issues that I applied rather than rejected. A family of issues that falls under 30 percent for one reviewer over at least twenty decisions is switched off for that reviewer. No family newly fell under the line. Reviewer B's `spelling-or-format` family sits at 33 percent for the current model and stays on by the owner's earlier ruling; reviewer B's `inflection` family is at 35 percent over 17 decisions, still too few to judge.

**Reports.** The standing reports are steady: 324 definitions that use their own headword (most are harmless and are judged at review), 989 cross-reference pairs where one side might be missing a sense (up from 976, as new entries arrive), and 6 "promises", where a note says a word has its own entry but none exists yet. All six are known and wait for later builds.

**Knowledge base reading.** Every wiki page is listed in the index and every index line points to a real page; the log has an entry for each of the ten runs since the last lint; there are no open questions for the owner; the curator file has no duplicate lines.

**One tool fix.** A note written on 2026-11-07 recorded that a closure cycle, asking for twelve words from one queue source and then twelve from the next, ended up holding thirteen, one over its limit. `tools/claim.py` now treats the number asked for as the run's total, counting the words it has already reserved, so the second request takes only what is left. A new unit test covers it, and the note and the tools list say so.

**Still open.** `data`'s synonym link to the plural `facts` (which should point to `fact`) is flagged by the tools and will be fixed when `data` is next reviewed, as `NEXT.md` already says.

**Spend.** US$0.004 this cycle, for the band vote. The day's total is about US$1.42 of the US$15 cap.

**What is next.** The selector will choose the next cycle; the queue in `NEXT.md` is unchanged apart from the lint's due date and the tool note.

## Cycle 2: build

**Why a build.** After the lint merged, the selector chose a build, the mode that drafts new entries from the queue (the list of words still to be written), because building had the most catching-up to do among the modes allowed. It set a limit of twelve new entries and a paid-call ceiling of US$3.

**What was built.** Ten words came from the queue in its usual order: the verb *fund* and the nouns *barbecue*, *evil*, *garbage*, *grin*, *grip*, *grocery*, *groom*, *ground floor*, and *guarantee*. Two more were taken by hand, as `NEXT.md` asked: a second entry for *fan* (a person who follows a team or a singer), separate from *fan* the machine because the two words are unrelated, and a second *clip* (a small object that holds things together), separate from *clip* meaning a short piece of film. Every entry went through the full pipeline: the validator, the inflection tool, the pronunciation check (all 24 transcriptions verified by agreement and, where it had the word, the CMU pronouncing dictionary), and the two reviewers.

**What the reviewers found.** Between them they raised 43 issues. One was blocking: the second sense of *barbecue* (the party) used the word *barbecue* inside its own definition, so it was rewritten to say that food is cooked over a fire or hot coals. I accepted all 43 issues; none were wrong enough to reject. The main kinds were:

- *Collocation types.* Phrases like *well funded* and *publicly funded* are participles, so by the reviewers' house rules they go in the "phrase" group (*be well funded*), not "adverb + verb". *Good and evil* and *a bag of groceries* moved there too.
- *One fact, one field.* The fact that the American *first floor* is often the British *ground floor* had been written three times in *ground floor*; it now lives only in the usage note. The same applied to the plural *groceries* and to the "different word" note on the new *fan*.
- *Variety.* *In her garden* became *in her backyard*, *has just got married* became *has just married*, and *get to grips with* now carries a British label (its explanation already gives the American *come to grips with*).
- *Meaning.* The product guarantee now covers getting your money back as well as repair, so *a money-back guarantee* fits it. The opening summaries of *barbecue* and *grip* were rewritten to match their senses.
- *Cross-references.* *Pay for* is not a phrasal verb by the house rules, so *fund* now points to *pay* with a note.

Two definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary (*project* and *racket*) and were reworded. Seven return links that the cross-reference tool could not place on a sense were placed by hand, on *pay*, *support*, *waste*, *control*, *handle*, *floor*, and *pin*.

**What did not work.** The queue holds *grown-up* (noun and adjective) as *grown up*, without its hyphen, a known fault. I tried to add a correct row, but the queue tool matches rows by their identifier and only changed the old row's note. I put the rows back as they were and added this to the tool note. The next build should write those entries as *grown-up*.

**Spend.** US$0.37 for the reviewers and the pronunciation panel. The day's total is about US$1.79 of the US$15 cap.

**What is next.** The next build picks up with *grown-up*. The old *fan* entry should link to the new one at its next review.

**For the owner.** Nothing new.
