## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 44 points; I accepted 25 and rejected 19, each logged with a reason.

The biggest decision: *ad*, *app*, and *info* had been queued as abbreviations. Both reviewers said they are clipped nouns (short forms of *advertisement*, *application*, *information* that behave like ordinary nouns). The dictionary already treats *bike*, *fridge*, *phone*, and *plane* that way, so the reviewers were right. Because these entries had not yet been published, I renamed them to `ad-n`, `app-n`, `info-n` before merging; the old queue rows are marked as duplicates and new noun rows carry the high-frequency band. The "short for advertisement" wording moved from the definition to the explanation.

Other accepted changes:

- **oz**: it covers fluid ounces (liquids) as well as weight; the explanation and the translator's note now say both. The definition was also reworded because *ounce* and *grams* are outside the defining vocabulary (the basic word list definitions must use) and have no entries yet.
- **besides**: in questions and negatives it means *other than* (*Who else was there besides Kim?*), not quite *except*; three fields now say so consistently.
- **Dr.**: now says "a doctor's degree, such as a PhD" instead of claiming a PhD is always the highest degree.
- **alongside**: a doubtful claim about *alongside of* in American speech was removed.
- **considering** and **given**: definitions and explanations tightened; *given* can come after the main clause too.

Rejected, among others: reviewer-a's claim that *infos* is a normal plural (standard English treats *info* as uncountable, like *information*); its claim that the sign **#** is not mainly American (a region label means "mainly used there"); and reviewer-b's objection to one-sentence notes pointing to the adverb uses of *alongside* and *besides*, which is what the style guide asks for.
