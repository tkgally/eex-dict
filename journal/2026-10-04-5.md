## What I added before the panel

I read each entry in full first. They were mostly sound; the gaps were a missing sense, a few idioms that belong under these words by the house keyword rule, and one common learner mistake:

- **touch**: a new sense, *reach a level* (*The temperature touched forty degrees*).
- **tie**: the idiom *fit to be tied* (extremely angry; informal, mainly American).
- **travel**: *travel light* was sitting in a list of collocations; it is an idiom, so it is now a phrase with its own definition and examples.
- **trust**: the idiom *trust someone to do something*, said when someone does something annoying that is typical of them (*Trust Sam to forget the tickets!*).
- **trade**: the collocations *trade insults* and *trade blows*.
- **turn**: the learner error *open the light* (lights and machines are turned **on** and **off**), which speakers of many languages make; and a missing grammar pattern on the first sense.

## What the panel found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 40 issues, 30 of them marked blocking. I applied 24 and rejected 16; none needed the owner. Every decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

Real catches, applied:

- **try**: a learner-error box called *try my best to not be late* wrong. It is not; putting **not** after **to** is normal English. The box was removed. The *patience* sense was defined as "make someone tired of waiting", which is not quite it; it now reads *make it hard for someone to stay calm and patient*. An example about trying three shops did not fit the *test* sense and was replaced.
- **train**: the note telling learners to *practice* a skill they already have, not *train* it, was too absolute (athletes train skills they already have). It now says that for a language or a school subject English uses **practice**.
- **touch**: the feelings sense said *make someone feel kind*, which is not what being touched means; it now says *cause warm or sad feelings in someone, such as love, sadness, or thanks*. The new *reach a level* sense claimed in its definition that the level is reached only briefly; that is a tendency, so it moved to the explanation.
- **tie**: the *same score* sense listed *tie the game* but defined only the intransitive use; the definition now covers making the score equal, with an example.
- **translate**, **transport**, **trust**, **tire**: notes that overstated a rule were softened (*translate* is used for spoken words too; *trust in* is not limited to God; *tire* is heard in speech in *tire easily*).

Rejected: two flags against *usually passive* as a grammar pattern (it is a value in the dictionary's closed list); two against transcribing *tire* with two syllables (a settled rule, verified by the pronunciation panel and the open CMU dictionary); one asking to move *touch and go* out of the verb entry (idioms stay under their keyword word); one saying *doubt* is not an opposite of *trust* (the *doubt* entry's second sense is exactly "not believe that someone is honest"); and a few label flags where the informal label is right.

One tool slip: when I gave the new *touch* sense the synonym *reach*, the cross-reference tool added the mirror link to the wrong sense of *reach* ("stretch out your arm"). I moved it by hand to "get to a particular level". This is a known weakness of the tool and is already recorded in the wiki.
