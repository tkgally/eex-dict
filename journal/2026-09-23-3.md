# 2026-09-23 (third run): eleven new function-word entries, and relative "that" moved to the pronoun entry

## What this run did

This was a **build** run. A build run writes new dictionary entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to be written. The scheduler picks the kind of run from which kind is most overdue, and today that was build.

Pre-flight checks were clean: no pull request left open, no leftover branches, and nothing in `inbox/`, where the owner leaves notes.

I wrote eleven entries. All are "function words": short, very common words whose job is grammatical rather than descriptive.

- **Three conjunctions** (words that join clauses): *so* (a result, *it rained, so we stayed in*; a purpose, *so I wouldn't miss the train*; and the spoken *so* that opens a question), *than* (comparisons, *rather than*, and the formal *no sooner ... than*), and *that* (the clause after verbs like *say* and *think*, and the result after *so* or *such*).
- **Eight prepositions**: *through, throughout, toward, under, upon, via, within, without*.

The queue also had *towards* as a separate word. *Towards* is the usual British form of *toward*, so I recorded it inside the *toward* entry as a variant and marked its queue row as a duplicate instead of writing a second entry. I left the conjunction *as* for a later run because it is the largest of the group.

Each entry then went through the full pipeline, the fixed sequence of checks every new entry must pass: a format check (validate), generated word forms (these words have none), a pronunciation check, a review by two outside AI reviewers from different companies, my decisions on their findings, a check that definitions use the dictionary's basic defining vocabulary, and cross-references.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

**Pronunciation.** All 22 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the panel of models and, for American English, by the open CMU pronouncing dictionary, which is looked up at run time and not stored.

**Content review.** The two reviewers raised 50 issues, 42 of them "blocking" (must be decided before the entry can be published). I accepted 40 and fixed the entries in my own words. I rejected 10, and each rejection has a logged reason. *Throughout* and *toward* came back with no issues.

The most important finding came from both reviewers. My draft of *that* (conjunction) included the relative use, as in *the book that I read*. Most grammars treat that use as a pronoun, and the dictionary's rule is one part of speech per entry. The pronoun entry for *that*, written on the eleventh run, had sent this use to the conjunction entry. Both reviewers were right, so I moved the relative use into the pronoun entry as a new second sense, with its two learner errors (*the man which I met*; *my sister, that lives in town*). I updated the pointers in both entries and put the pronoun entry through the reviewers again. That second review found no issues.

Other accepted fixes: I removed an "informal" label from the conversational *so*, which is neutral in speech. I rewrote several over-absolute rules, for example that a *that* clause never follows a preposition (*in that* and *except that* are exceptions). I dropped a learner error claiming that *under zero* is wrong, because it is in fact used.

Rejected claims, in brief:
- That the pointing word *that* (*that book*) is often said with a weak vowel. In fact it keeps its full vowel, and only the linking *that* weakens.
- That definitions starting *used after ...* break the style guide. The guide allows this formula for function words.
- That *upon Monday* is current English.
- That the definition *up to and including* (American *Monday through Friday*) is circular. It does not contain the headword.
