## What this cycle did

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **build** again: new entries from the queue of words still to be written. The next twelve band 1 words (the most common group) were all nouns: *lane, language, laugh, law, lawyer, layer, leader, leaf, leather, leg, leisure,* and *length*.

Every entry was drafted in full: senses with signposts (short labels for picking a meaning), grammar, examples, collocations (typical word combinations), idioms, learner errors, usage notes, and adaptation notes (short warnings for translators). Some highlights:

- *law* has five senses, from *obey the law* to *the laws of physics*, four idioms (*lay down the law*, *take the law into your own hands*, *law and order*, *a law unto yourself*), and a short guide to choosing between *law*, *rule*, and *regulation*.
- *leg* has five senses (the body, furniture, clothes, food, and one part of a journey) and six idioms. Two of them came from older entries under the project's keyword rule, which files an idiom under its first noun: *break a leg* moved from the verb *break* and *shake a leg* from the verb *shake*. A note warns that in English the leg stops at the ankle; some languages use one word for leg and foot.
- *language* explains why learners should say *I speak French* rather than *I speak the French language*.
- *length* teaches *two meters long* in place of the common error *two meters of length*.

## What the checks found

Inflections came from the rules (*leaves*; *leather* and *leisure* have no plural). Twenty-three of the twenty-four pronunciations were confirmed. The British pronunciation panel disagreed with *lawyer* written as *LOY-uh*; they probably prefer the two-part *LAW-yuh*. Both are standard British, so I kept the draft, added *LAW-yuh* as a British variant, flagged the entry, and wrote a line for the owner in the curator file.

The two reviewer models raised 40 issues, 24 of them blocking (must be settled before publication). I applied 18 and rejected 6. Applied:

- *air lane* is not normal English; the route sense of *lane* now covers only shipping lanes.
- *language* as a human ability now includes signs, so signed languages are covered, and the non-word sense (*body language*) no longer overlaps the first.
- The note on *the French language* had said it is used *only* when the language is the topic; it now says *mainly*, and calls it more formal.
- The informal British *a laugh* now separates a fun time (*it'll be a laugh*) from a fun person (*he's a real laugh*), the second as a subsense.
- In racing, *a length* is now defined as a distance equal to how long a horse or a boat is.
- The note on British *solicitors* and *barristers* now says what each *mainly* does.

Rejected, with reasons logged: reviewer-b said *leather* cannot have no plural while its motorcycle-clothes sense is *leathers*. The plural column is filled by a script, and that script cannot record a plural for a word that is mostly uncountable; this is a known gap already noted in the wiki, and the sense itself names *leathers*. Reviewer-a's wish to split *law* the subject from *law* the profession, and its standing objection to American transcriptions without a length mark (*leaf*), were also rejected.

The cross-reference tool added two back-links on older entries (*boss* and *fur*); I checked both and they are in the right senses. Three definitions used words outside the defining vocabulary (*offensive*, *advanced*, *hurrying*) and were reworded.
