## What this cycle did

A **lint cycle** is a maintenance pass over the whole project rather than new writing. It runs the mechanical checks across every entry and wiki page, and then looks for what a script cannot see: pages that contradict each other, notes that a later change made out of date, and how well the two reviewer models are doing. The scheduler forced this one because five cycles had passed since the last lint (the rule is at least one in five). It costs nothing: no paid model calls.

## The mechanical checks

- **Size caps and links.** Every capped file (the rules, the style guide, the wiki, and so on) is under its word or line limit, and every link between wiki pages works.
- **Back-links.** When entry A names entry B as a synonym, a word to compare, or a family member, B should name A too. The tool `tools/crossref.py` adds these mirror links. This pass added six, all from yesterday's review and build cycles: in *violence*, *wage*, *email*, *view*, *voice*, and *volume*. The tool always attaches a mirror to the first sense of the target, which has been wrong before, so I checked each by hand against the sense that named it. All six were right. There were no type mismatches (one side calling a word a synonym while the other calls it a word to compare).
- **Defining vocabulary.** No definition anywhere uses a word that is outside the defining vocabulary and has no entry.
- **Claims.** Three old claim files (the records of which words a cycle had taken) were cleared.

## How the reviewers are doing

Each reviewer's objections are grouped into families (definition wording, labels, examples, and so on). Each lint measures **precision** for every family: the share of that reviewer's objections that I accepted. A family under 30 percent over twenty or more decisions is switched off for that reviewer, because it mostly wastes time. Nothing new crossed that line. The eight families already switched off stay off. Overall, I accepted 72 percent of reviewer-a's objections and 54 percent of reviewer-b's.

Two cycles yesterday, reviewer-b raised almost nothing (0 issues on twelve entries, then 2 on twelve). The earlier note on this (`wiki/notes/reviewer-b-low-yield.md`) asked the next lint to run a sampling check: take the faults reviewer-a found that turned out to be real, and see what reviewer-b had said about the same fields. I did this for every such fault since yesterday evening. Of 36 fields, reviewer-b had called 34 "ok". I read five by hand, and each was a real fault it let through: an example that belonged to a different sense, a wrong claim about American and British verb agreement, a false synonym, a "sense" that was only a shortened phrase, and a translator note that forgot pocket watches.

What this means: when reviewer-b says a field is fine, that tells us little. In practice the second reading of each entry is reviewer-a plus my own adjudication. The note now proposes a remedy for a later run: one added line in reviewer-b's instructions asking it to test every example against its sense and every note for truth. Under the project's rules a change like that comes in a later run with a logged reason, not in the lint that found the problem. Whether to replace reviewer-b's model is a decision for the owner.
