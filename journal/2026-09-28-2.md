# 2026-09-28 (2) — Review: fifteen verbs, *need* to *pay*

## What this run did

This was the second cycle of the scheduled run that began at 00:44 UTC. A "cycle" is one unit of work, checked, recorded, and merged on its own; the first cycle's pull request (#110) merged before this one started. The mode selector (the script that decides what each cycle does) chose **review** again: a second reading of entries that have passed the reviewer panel only once.

The block was fifteen verbs written late on 2026-09-26: *need, notice, obey, observe, occur, open, operate, order, owe, own, pack, paint, park, pass,* and *pay*.

I read each one before sending it to the panel. Most were in good shape; six lacked a common use, which I added by hand:

- **open**: two subsenses. One is for spreading something apart, as in *open your mouth*, *open an umbrella*, or *open a book*; the entry had only covered doors, windows, and lids. The other is for a play, a film, or an event beginning (*The new play opens next week*).
- **owe**: the phrase *owe it to someone to do something* (*You owe it to yourself to take a real vacation*).
- **pack**: a subsense for crowding into a space (*Six of us packed into the tiny car*).
- **pass**: a new informal sense, *decide not to take something that is offered*, as in *"More cake?" "I'll pass, thanks."*
- **pay**: a subsense for a job giving a certain amount of money (*The job pays well, but the hours are long*).
- **paint**: an example with nails, *She painted her nails bright red*.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two reviewer models raised 33 issues, 19 of them "blocking" (the entry cannot stay marked reviewed until a decision is logged). I decided all 33: 8 applied and 25 rejected. Each decision is in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`. That is a high rejection rate. Most of the rejected issues repeated a few objections, listed below.

Changes I accepted:

- **need**: the one-sentence summary at the top of the entry said "to have to have or do something", which reads badly. It now says "to find it necessary: to have it, to do it, or to have it done".
- **open**: a note for translators said English uses *turn on*, not *open*, for lights, televisions, and taps. *Open the tap* does occur in British English, so taps were taken out of the note.
- **pay**: the new subsense's definition, "give a particular amount of money to the person who does it", was unclear about who "the person" is. It now reads "give the worker a particular amount of money". *Pay in*, which usually means putting money into a bank account, was removed from the first sense's collocations.
- **Smaller fixes**: collocations were completed for *operate* (*operate normally*, *operate on gas*) and reorganized for *pack*. A passive example in *order* lost a label that described the active pattern.

What I rejected, and why:

- The first reviewer objected to the "not used in continuous tenses" grammar marker on *need* and *own*, and to the learner errors *I am needing* and *I am owning a house*. The dictionary's grammar code describes normal use. The learner-error notes already say "not normally", which covers the rare exceptions.
- The second reviewer objected that three definitions of *need* begin with *must* or *should*. Those definitions replace the word cleanly in a sentence (*I need a phone* / *I must have a phone*) and are the plainest wording available.
- The first reviewer said *It's easy to operate* shows an intransitive use. It does not: the object is understood (*easy to operate it*).
- It said *she painted the wall with yellow* is not a real error. Learners do say it, and the natural English is *painted the wall yellow*.
- It objected to *give* as a synonym of *pay* and *run* as a word to compare with *own*. *I gave him ten dollars for it* is ordinary English, and learners do confuse *own a shop* with *run a shop*.
- The second reviewer again objected, six times, to the quoted headword at the start of translators' notes, which is a settled house convention.
