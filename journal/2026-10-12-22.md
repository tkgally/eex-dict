# 2026-10-12, run at 20:44 UTC, cycle 2: the thirty-fourth originality check

## What this cycle did

This was an **originality** cycle. Its job is to make sure no wording in the dictionary was copied from a published dictionary. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) forces one every tenth run, and this was run 410.

The check tool (`tools/originality_check.py`) picked ten fields at random from the 2,262 entries: seven definitions and three usage notes. For each one I searched the web for the exact wording and looked only at whether a published dictionary or word list used the same words. I also put a fixed question to one reviewer model, as the checklist asks. Each field then got one of three verdicts:

- **original**: no match found;
- **generic-overlap**: a short, plain wording that any dictionary would write the same way;
- **rewrite**: close enough to a published dictionary's own wording that it must be rewritten.

## Results

Five fields were original: the *honey* and *sun* usage notes, the *thumbs down* gesture, *in someone's good books*, and the "very exact and organized" sense of *military*. Three were generic overlaps: the *spoon* definition (every dictionary describes a bowl at the end of a handle), *justice* as "the quality of being fair or reasonable", and the phrase meaning "unpleasant to look at". All three are short, plain wordings.

Two needed a **rewrite**:

- *spark*, sense 3, read "a small amount of a feeling or a quality". A well-known learner's dictionary has the same words in a slightly different order. It now reads "a very small sign that a feeling or quality is there". My first try used *slight*, which is not in the defining vocabulary (the list of about 2,400 plain words that definitions must use), so I changed it to *small*.
- *out of town* (a phrase in the entry *town*) read "away from the place where you live, especially for a short time". A learner's dictionary defines it as away from the town or city where you normally live, usually for a short period. Ours was not word for word the same, but it followed that frame closely. It now reads "not at home because you have gone to another city or area for a few days".

Earlier checks rewrote *egg*, *weekend*, *confuse*, and *lung* on the same grounds.

## The reviewers

I sent both rewritten entries back to the two reviewer models. reviewer-b found nothing. reviewer-a raised seven issues, all marked blocking. I accepted four:

- On *spark*, "be the spark for" was listed as a verb + noun collocation. It is really a fixed phrase, so I moved it to a "phrase" collocation group.
- On *town*, the phrase *in town* was defined as "staying in or visiting the place". That missed people who are simply present there. It now reads "present in the town or city where you are or are talking about, often for a visit".
- On *town*, the usage note gave *in town* as an example of *town* meaning the town center. That clashed with the phrase *in town* defined just below it. I replaced the example with *walk to town*. This fixed two of reviewer-a's issues at once: the usage note and the clash between the two fields.

I rejected three:

- The explanation "Usually without **a** or **the**" already allows for the article.
- "Let's go and paint the town red" is natural English.
- *downtown* in the word family is allowed: the style guide accepts related words there, compounds included.

Each decision is logged in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

## The reviewer's answers to the originality question

reviewer-a (with its hidden reasoning turned off) answered *copied* five times, *cannot tell* three times, and *original* twice. For *spark* its quotation matched what the search found. For *good books* and *out of town* it "quoted" our own text back to me, a pattern seen in earlier checks. Its other two *copied* answers were for short, plain phrases. The verdicts rest on the searches.
