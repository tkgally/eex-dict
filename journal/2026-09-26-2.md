# 2026-09-26 (second run): twelve words the dictionary was already using

## What this run did

This was a **closure** run. A closure run writes entries for words that the dictionary already uses in its own definitions but has no entry for yet, so that a reader who meets such a word can click through to it. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each run's kind of work) chose closure because it was the kind of work that had waited longest.

Before starting, the run found no unfinished work from earlier runs and nothing in the owner's inbox.

The run then looked at the top of the closure list. Three of its rows were wrong. The script that adds words to that list guesses each word's part of speech from its spelling, and it had guessed badly: *advanced* was listed as a verb and *eager* as a noun, although both are adjectives here and both were already listed correctly; and *packages*, a plural, was listed as if it were a separate word. The run marked the three rows as duplicates, with the reason on each, and added *package* (noun) in their place. The plural mistake is new, so it was added to the notes page on the script's guesses.

Twelve entries were written:

- the verb *commit*;
- the nouns *achievement, border, bullet, criticism, ladder, pen, pencil,* and *package*;
- the adjectives *eager, relevant,* and *urgent*.

Each has one to four senses with examples, collocations (the words a word typically goes with), and grammar, plus notes on learner mistakes and translator traps where they help. *Bullet* carries three idioms (*bite the bullet*, *dodge a bullet*, *silver bullet*); *pen* carries *put pen to paper*.

## Checks and what the reviewers found

Every entry went through the full pipeline. Word forms (plurals, past tenses) were generated by rule and all twelve were confirmed. All 24 pronunciations (American and British for each word) were confirmed by the panel of models and by the open CMU pronouncing dictionary.

The two outside reviewer models, from two different companies, read every field and raised 26 issues, 15 of them marked blocking. I accepted 11 and rejected 15; none needed the owner.

The accepted issues that mattered:

- ***commit*, the "send away" sense.** The draft said this sense means officially sending someone "to a prison or a hospital", with the example *She was committed to a hospital for treatment*. The reviewer was right that this suggests ordinary hospital care; the sense is about sending someone, usually against their will, to a hospital for mental illness. The definition, example, collocation, translator note, and the one-sentence summary at the top of the entry were all corrected so that they agree.
- ***commit*, the "promise" sense.** The pattern *commit someone to something* (*The contract commits us to three years of payments*) was added to the explanation.
- ***eager*.** A translator note said *eager* is never followed by an **-ing** form. That was too strong; it now says *eager* is not followed *directly* by one (*eager to start*, not *eager starting*).
- ***relevant*.** The explanation of the use *the relevant department* now allows words like *your* as well as *the*.

The rejected issues were mostly repeats of objections this project has already settled: four times, one reviewer asked for a headword quoted at the start of a translator note to be changed to bold, which the house style allows; one reviewer said *package* for a store container is not mainly American, but a region label here means "mainly used there", and British English mainly says *packet*. Two "contradictions" on *relevant* were not contradictions, because the grammar codes they compared belong to different senses.

After review, a check of the definitions against the defining vocabulary (the list of about 2,400 plain words that definitions should use) found nine words outside it, such as *ink*, *mail*, and *dot*. All nine definitions were reworded in plainer words (a pen is now "a tool with colored liquid inside, used for writing or drawing").

The words these entries point to but that have no entry yet, such as *achieve*, *parcel*, *packet*, and *keen*, were added to the list of words to write: 24 in all.
