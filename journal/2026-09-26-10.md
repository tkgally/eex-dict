# 2026-09-26 (tenth run): a second reading of twenty prepositions and pronouns

## What this run did

This was the fourth cycle of this session and a **review** run. A review run gives existing entries a second reading by the two reviewer models. Every entry is reviewed once before it is published. Since the dictionary has grown, the scheduler (`next_mode.py`) now alternates new building with second readings, oldest first. The first three cycles built thirty new verbs (pull requests #76, #77 and #78).

The block was the twenty oldest entries that had had only one reading, all from 2026-09-20:

- **prepositions:** *about, above, after, against, along, among, around, as, at, before*;
- **pronouns:** *neither, nobody, none, some, which, who, whom, whose, you, yours*.

Function words like these are short entries, but they are where learners' grammar questions concentrate.

Before the reviewers read them, one entry was tidied by hand. *As* (preposition) has only one sense but carried a signpost. A signpost is the short label that helps a reader pick a sense from a list, so a one-sense entry should not have one. It was removed. Forty-one other entries still have this problem and are fixed as reviews reach them.

## What the reviewers found and what was decided

The reviewers raised 57 issues. Each was read against the entry and got a logged decision: 43 fixes applied and 14 rejected. Three entries (*before*, *whose*, *yours*) came through with no issues.

**Claims that were too absolute.** The most useful findings were statements stronger than the facts:

- *which*: the entry said American English "normally uses **that**" in a clause with no comma. It now says **that** is more common there and many style guides prefer it. It adds that **which** is still correct, and is the only choice directly after a preposition (*the house in which I grew up*).
- *whom*: the entry said it "cannot be replaced" after a preposition. It now says careful English uses **whom** there.
- *among*: the learner error called *share the cake between the six children* wrong. Both reviewers said it is normal English, so the error was replaced with a real one: *divided among the two brothers* → *between the two brothers*.
- *neither*: the correction implied that only a singular verb is right, although the entry itself says a plural verb is also standard. That is fixed.

**House-rule problems.** Two explanations, in *nobody* and *whom*, showed a wrong form in italics (*nobody doesn't know*, *to who I spoke*). The house rule is that wrong forms appear only in the learner-error box, so both were rewritten. *Neither* pointed readers to its determiner entry for *neither ... nor*; the house rule gives correlative pairs a conjunction entry, so it now points to *neither* (conjunction).

**Smaller fixes.** *None too* is now defined as "not very" and lost its "informal" label. *Some* gained a weak spoken form. Two definitions were narrower than their own examples and were widened: *after* ("coming behind in place or order") and *about* ("in or to different parts of a place", now labeled British). *At* sense 2 was rewritten, because the old wording was not a phrase that could replace the word.

**Rejected.** Reviewer-a called several examples adverbs or adjectives, although each has an object after the preposition (*about the office*, *above ours*, *he's above that*). They are preposition uses, so the objections were rejected. Its requests to move *be about to* out of the adjective treatment, and to treat *around fifty dollars* as a preposition, were rejected to keep the dictionary consistent with decisions already in force. It also asked to move *above all* to another entry. The house keyword rule puts that phrase under *above*, so it stayed.
