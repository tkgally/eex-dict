# 2026-10-20, cycle 1 of the 00:43 UTC run: second review of *pin*, *section*, and *race* to *representative*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that had been through the two outside reviewers (the review panel, two language models from other companies) only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose review because it was the most overdue kind of work, and the block is always the oldest such entries. This time the block was twenty nouns drafted on 2026-10-07: *pin, section, race, radio, rail, rain, range, rate, reader, reason, receipt, recipe, recovery, relationship, relief, religion, rent, repair, report, representative*.

Before starting, the usual checks found nothing stranded: no open pull request from an earlier run, the four leftover branches already fully merged and already listed for the owner to prune, and nothing in the inbox.

Each entry went to both reviewers again. I read every issue against the entry and either changed the entry (*applied*) or left it as it was (*rejected*, with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`). Nothing was escalated to the owner.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Six entries drew no issues at all (*range, receipt, relationship, relief, rent, report*). reviewer-a raised 42 issues and reviewer-b 6; 33 were blocking (meaning the entry should not stand without a decision). In all I applied 25 and rejected 23.

Applied:

- ***radio***: the opening summary sentence (the *core idea*) listed three unlike things after *is* and read badly. It now says radio is the sending of sound or messages without wires, and that the word also names the programs and the machine.
- ***race***: the idiom *a race against time* was listed both as sense 3 and as a separate phrase with almost the same definition. The phrase is gone; sense 3 keeps it as a collocation and an example.
- ***rail***: *back on the rails* (working well again) is mainly British; Americans say *back on track*. The phrase now carries a British label.
- ***rain***: the phrase *take a rain check* said that *give someone a rain check* is the same thing; it is the other side (the person who offers promises to offer again), and the explanation now says so. The figurative *a rain of stones* lost its *literary* label, and a translator's note that said *a rain* needs an adjective now says *mostly*.
- ***reader***: the device sense now covers machines that show books on a screen, and the British university rank (*a reader in history*) is now *one level below the most important teachers*, with an explanation naming *professor* (a word outside the defining vocabulary, the fixed list of plain words definitions are written in).
- ***recovery***: the addiction sense said *after using too much alcohol or drugs for a long time*; it now says *after depending on alcohol or drugs*, and its signpost (the short label that helps a reader pick the sense) is shorter.
- ***repair***: the *in good repair* sense is now about physical condition rather than *how well it has been looked after*; the translator's note now allows *a repair* for one piece of work; and a grammar fact sitting in an explanation moved to the grammar field.
- ***section***: the *law* label came off the *numbered part* sense, since it covers contracts and other documents too.
- ***rate*** and ***reason***: a learner-error note on *cheap rate* was softened; two thin collocations replaced; a pointer to a nonexistent *grounds* entry removed, because `ground-n` sense 6 already covers that use (the queue row for *grounds* is now marked duplicate).
- ***pin***: the British *drawing pin* collocation was replaced with *tie pin*.

Rejected, with the main reasons:

- ***representative***: reviewer-a said three times that calling the House of Representatives *one of the two groups of elected people who make laws* is wrong. It is right: Congress is made of two such groups, and the House is one.
- ***reader***: reviewer-a said the false-friend note was wrong to say *lector* means *reader* in some languages. In Spanish and Portuguese it does.
- ***rain***: both reviewers objected to the forms line *no plural* while sense 2 is *the rains*. That line is written by a script, `tools/inflect.py`, which has a known gap for nouns with one plural-only sense; the case was added to the existing note on that gap.
- Smaller: *do repairs* is natural; *on pins and needles* is mainly American, so its label stays; *right as rain* is used in America too; *a set of instructions that tells you* is grammatical.
