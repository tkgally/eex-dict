# Run report, 2026-10-29 (second run of the day)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's kind of work from how long each kind has waited) chose **build**: writing new entries from the queue of headwords still to be written. Milestone 1, the defining vocabulary, is finished, so the queue now offers band-2 words (band 2 is the second most important of five editorial frequency bands). The twelve rows at the top were all function words that other entries already point to: seven pronouns (*least*, *one another*, *plenty*, *several*, *whatever*, *whoever*, *yourselves*) and five prepositions (*ahead of*, *apart from*, *as for*, *but*, *close to*). Each is the pronoun or preposition use of a word whose other parts of speech already had entries. *Several* the pronoun (*several of them*) now stands beside *several* the determiner (*several books*), for example.

All twelve went through the full pipeline: schema check, inflection check, the pronunciation panel, both reviewers, my adjudication of every issue, a second schema check, the defining-vocabulary lint, and the cross-reference tool. All twelve are now `reviewed` and will appear on the site at the next build. The dictionary has 3,233 entries, none in draft.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers (reviewer A, a Claude model, and reviewer B, a Google model) raised 144 issues, 17 of them blocking. I applied 136 and rejected 8.

- The biggest group was my own fault. In drafting I stated the same rule in several fields: in the explanation, the usage note, a learner error, and an adaptation note (the note for translators). The house rule is one fact, one field. For example, the rule that a verb after *close to* takes the *-ing* form (*close to finishing*) appeared three times. I cut each such rule to one field. I also removed several learner errors that reviewers rightly called invented rather than real.
- **Wrong part of speech.** *Plenty more* (*there's plenty more where that came from*) is an adverb use, not a pronoun, so the second sense of `plenty-pron` was folded into the first and the adverb is now pointed to and queued. One learner error in `least-pron` showed *least* before a noun, which is the determiner, so it was removed.
- **Circular definitions.** Two definitions used their own headword (*whatever the situation is*, *whoever the person is*). Both now say *no matter what* and *no matter who*.
- **Examples that did not fit.** *See the problem for yourselves* means "directly, by your own experience", not the emphatic sense it sat under. It is now its own phrase, *for yourselves*. *But for the rain* means "except for", which the phrase definition now covers.
- **Coverage added on review:** *or whoever* (informal, "or someone like that"); *ahead of* meaning "more important than" (*put safety ahead of speed*); *close to* meaning "similar to" (*close to the truth*).
- **Rejected:** requests to add *at least*, *to say the least*, *all but*, and *ahead of time* to these entries. Under the house keyword rule these idioms already live in `least-det`, `say-v`, `all-adv`, and `time-n`. *Anything but* and *nothing but* belong under *anything* and *nothing*, and I put them on the queue in `NEXT.md` for the next review.

**Pronunciation.** The panel (three models, plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary for American English, consulted at run time and not stored) verified ten of the twelve. For *several*, the panel wanted three syllables (*SEV-er-ul*), so I made that the main form and kept the two-syllable form as a variant; it then verified 4 of 4. For *as for* and *close to*, the votes split over where to put a stress mark in a phrase of two one-syllable words. The panel's answers also changed from one call to the next. Both entries carry the `pronunciation-disputed` flag and a line in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`, and the drafted forms stand.

**Spend.** US$0.40 this cycle (the panel and pronunciation calls); US$1.88 of the US$15 daily cap so far today.

**What did not work.** The cross-reference tool again queued two headwords with a capital letter (*Aside from*, *Whomever*). I lowercased them by hand and added a line to the existing note, `wiki/notes/crossref-queue-capitalized-headword.md`. That note has now seen the same problem twice and is a candidate fix for a lint run. The wiki is 44 words under its 30,000-word cap, so the next lint should trim before adding anything.

**What is next.** The selector decides the next cycle. The queue continues with band-2 function words and the closure `family` rows. The lint pass is due in five runs.

**For the owner.** Nothing new needs a ruling. The two pronunciation disputes are in the curator file, with the working assumption stated there.

## Cycle 2: closure

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose **closure**: writing entries for words the dictionary already uses or points to but has not yet defined. The `closure` source (words that appear in definitions without an entry) had one row left, *lord*, which the definition of *lady* uses. The other eleven came from the `family` source: verbs whose noun already has an entry and lists the verb in its word family, such as *process* for the noun *process* and *rate* for the noun *rate*. The twelve are *lord* (noun) and the verbs *oppose*, *pair*, *part*, *position*, *process*, *program*, *progress*, *range*, *rate*, *rebuild*, and *relieve*. The claim tool took thirteen when I asked the two sources in turn, so I released *replace* back to the queue to stay within the twelve-entry limit the selector set. All twelve are now `reviewed`, and the dictionary has 3,245 entries, none in draft.

**Pronunciation.** All 24 transcriptions (American and British for each word) were verified by the panel, most of them unanimously.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 120 issues, 20 of them blocking. I applied 110 and rejected 10.

- **Explanation fields where none belong.** The style guide keeps the `explanation` field for function words and for restrictions a definition cannot carry. I had added explanations to ordinary verb senses that only repeated the grammar or the collocations. These were removed. Where a sense is limited to one form (*part with something*, *relieve someone of something*), I moved that form out of the start of the definition and into the explanation, as the style guide asks.
- **Definitions that missed their own examples.** *This soup pairs well with bread* has the food as subject, but the definition of *pair* covered only a person serving food. The *pair* definitions now cover both uses. A film "rated for adults" is classified, not scored, so `rate-v` gained a sense, *classify officially*.
- **Senses split or added.** `relieve-v` now separates *take a load from someone* from *remove someone from a job* (*he was relieved of his duties*). `progress-v` now covers an illness getting worse. `lord-n` gained the phrase *my lord* and the emphatic *Lord knows I tried*.
- **Small fixes.** Unnatural examples were replaced: *relieve traffic*, and a checkable claim about how far wolves travel. I removed see-also links that only repeated the word family, and removed *lady* from the word family of *lord*, because the words are not related in form.
- **Rejected.** Reviewer B asked three times for the irregular form *rebuilt* to be marked by hand in examples. It is a listed inflection, so the site marks it automatically. *As opposed to* stays under *oppose* because the keyword rule files an idiom under its first verb. *Part company* is already under *company*. *Lord it over someone* belongs to a verb entry, *lord* (verb), that does not exist yet.

The cross-reference tool left eight back-links where it could not tell which sense of the target entry should hold the mirror link (for example, *judge* for *rate*). I placed each one by hand.

**Spend.** US$0.38 this cycle; US$2.27 of the US$15 daily cap so far today.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. The repeated objections suggest my drafts still fill the explanation field too readily; I will watch for that when drafting.

**What is next.** The selector decides. *Replace* (verb) is back at the head of the `family` rows.

**For the owner.** Nothing new.
