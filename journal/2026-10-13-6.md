## What this cycle did

This cycle was a **review**: a second reading of entries that had been through the review panel only once. The panel is two reviewer models from other companies that read every field of an entry and report problems. The selector, the script that picks each cycle's job, chose review because it was the most overdue job. About 960 entries still have only one panel round. This cycle took the eighteen oldest of them, two batches written on 2026-10-05:

*knee*, *knife*, *knowledge*, *lake*, *lamp*, *land*, *lane*, *language*, *laugh*, *law*, *lawyer*, *layer*, *leader*, *leaf*, *leather*, *leg*, *leisure*, *length*.

I also read each entry myself for gaps. Eight entries had no pronunciation note, the short plain-English line on how to say the word: *lake*, *lamp*, *land*, *lane*, *law*, *leader*, *leaf*, *leg*. I added one to each, for example "It rhymes with **saw**. There is no **w** sound at the end" for *law*. The note on *leaf* says that the **f** becomes a **v** sound in **leaves**.

## What the reviewers found, and what I decided

The panel raised 32 issues; 22 were blocking, meaning the entry could not stay published until each was decided. I applied 20 of the blocking issues and rejected 2, and I settled 10 minor issues (5 applied, 5 rejected). Each decision is logged with a reason. Nothing needed the owner. Seven entries came back with no issues at all, including *knowledge*, *laugh*, and *lawyer*.

Most of the work was on *land*, which drew ten issues. The old entry called the sense *a land*, meaning a country, literary. It also said in two notes that *a land* is "never" possible, which contradicted that same sense. The literary label is gone. The usage note now says the countable use is "a little formal or literary" (*distant lands*), and the *never* is limited to ground: *a land near the river* is wrong, *some land* is right. The *farming* sense (*leaving the land*) was redefined as "the work and life of farming, in the areas outside towns". One learner error was removed, because *many lands* can be correct when it means separate territories.

Other changes:
- *knee*, sense 3 (*sit on someone's knee*) had the signpost *lap*, which suggested a different word. The signpost is now *sitting*, and the definition names the knee itself.
- *lamp*, sense 2 (*a heat lamp*, *a sun lamp*): it now says the lamp gives strong light for a special purpose, not "light or heat".
- *lane*: a road lane is for "one line of traffic", not only cars.
- *law*: the sense *the law* (*in trouble with the law*) was defined only as "the police". It now reads "the police, as the people who make sure that rules are obeyed".
- *language*, sense 5 (*sign language*, *body language*) was redefined as a system used to communicate without words.
- *leg*: the core idea, the one-sentence summary at the top of the entry, was rewritten. The phrase *on its last legs* now also covers a very tired person.
- *length*: *at length* now means "for a long time, or in a lot of detail", not both at once.

Rejected, with reasons:
- *see how the land lies* is kept as British and *go jump in a lake* as American. House rule: a region label means *mainly* used there.
- *shake a leg* keeps its old-fashioned label.
- The learner error *a skin jacket* stays. It is a common mistake from languages that use one word for skin and leather.

Two of the second reviewer's replies, on *law* and *leaf*, came back unreadable. Both were re-run once and came back complete, with no new issues.
