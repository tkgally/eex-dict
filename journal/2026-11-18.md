# Journal, 2026-11-18

## Cycle 1: review

**What this cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, which picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose a review. There were no drafts and no reader reports, so the cycle continued the coverage pass: a re-reading of the most important band-1 entries (band 1 is the most common words), most senses first, asking of each one whether a real, current meaning or a common fixed phrase is missing. Four entries were read: *common* (adjective), *condition* (noun), *control* (verb), and *copy* (verb). Before the start, the pre-flight checks found no open pull requests, nothing in the owner's inbox, no reader reports, and two old branches already merged and already listed for pruning.

**What was added before review.**
- *common*: a subsense for *common courtesy* and *common decency* (basic good behavior that everyone should show), and the collocations *common ground*, *the common good*, and *common knowledge*.
- *condition*: the phrase *the human condition*, and *in mint condition* and *under difficult conditions*.
- *control*: a research sense, *control for* something (make sure one factor does not change a study's results); the "make work" sense now covers animals and balls (*control a horse*, *control the ball*); the "limit" sense now covers limits set by rules (*strictly controlled* drugs). *take over* was removed as a synonym, because it means gaining control, not having it.
- *copy*: the radio sense (*Do you copy?*, *Copy that*), and a missing grammar pattern for *copy down the questions*.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two automated reviewers (reviewer A and reviewer B, models from different labs) raised 38 issues, 3 of them blocking. I applied 34 and rejected 4.
- Blocking: an example said *the common cold is caused by a virus*, a checkable science claim, which our examples must not contain (both reviewers); it now reads *It's only the common cold, nothing serious*. Reviewer B also said *control for* takes no direct object, so it is intransitive; I agreed, matching how *depend on* and *rely on* are coded.
- Three facts were stated twice, against the rule that one fact lives in one field: *common*'s usage note repeated the comparative forms already in the inflections note; *condition*'s translators' note repeated that the situation sense is plural; *control*'s false-friend note repeated the learner error about *check*. Each copy was removed.
- Smaller fixes: *common knowledge* moved to the "shared" sense, where it belongs; the old-fashioned British "low class" sense of *common* now covers things as well as people; *condition*'s "rule" signpost became "requirement", and the medical sense lost a "medicine" field label, since *a heart condition* is an everyday phrase; *control*'s core idea and its feelings sense (which must cover *control yourself*) were rewritten, and two examples that did not fit their definitions were replaced; *copy* lost two weak cross-references (*rip*, *borrow*) and a needless explanation.
- Rejected: a request to mark *mutual friend* with single asterisks (it is named as a phrase, so double is right); a claim that *with the condition that* is not a learner error (it is a common translation from other languages); a request for a "radio" field label (no such label exists in our closed list, and *copy that* is used widely in casual speech); and a claim that copying an idea is making a copy rather than imitating someone.

One back-link the cross-reference tool could not place (*receive* as a synonym of radio *copy*) was resolved by dropping the synonym, since *receive*'s radio sense is about machines picking up signals.

## Cycle 2: build

**What this cycle did.** The mode selector chose a build: new entries drawn from the queue (the ordered list of words waiting to be written). I claimed eight and wrote all eight, each a noun in band 2 (the second most common of the five frequency bands): *seminar*, *senior citizen*, *sequence*, *shake*, *shed*, *shift*, *shopping center*, and *shortage*. The selector allowed up to twelve; eight full entries were a better use of the cycle than twelve thin ones.

A few points about the entries themselves:
- *seminar* has two senses: the small university class, and a training meeting (*a sales seminar*), with a note on how it differs from a **lecture**.
- *senior citizen* carries a usage note that many people now say **seniors** or **older people**, and that some find the phrase a little old-fashioned.
- *sequence* has four senses: a series of events, the order things come in (*out of sequence*), a part of a film (*the opening sequence*), and a number pattern.
- *shake* covers the movement (*give the bottle a shake*), the drink, and *the shakes*, with three informal phrases: *a fair shake*, *in two shakes*, and *no great shakes*.
- *shift* covers a period of work (with a subsense for the group of workers), a change (*a shift in opinion*), the keyboard key, and a simple dress.
- *shortage* has a short note on choosing between it and **lack**: *a water shortage*, but *a lack of confidence*.

**Pronunciation.** Our check sends each transcription to a panel of three models and, for American English, the open CMU pronouncing dictionary; a transcription is "verified" when at least two votes agree with it. All sixteen transcriptions were verified. The British form of *senior citizen* got only one agreeing vote at first, with the other votes split; a second panel run gave two of three, which meets the rule.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewers raised 25 issues, none blocking. I applied 21 and rejected 4.
- Two core ideas (the one-sentence summary that opens an entry with three or more senses) did not cover every sense, on *shake* and *shift*; both were rewritten.
- Reviewer A pointed out two real gaps. A shake can be made with a powder and no ice cream (*a protein shake*), so the drink definition was widened. American English uses *shift* for the stick that changes a car's gears (*a stick shift*), which is now its own sense, marked American. *The graveyard shift* (night work) was added as a collocation.
- *We built a shed at the bottom of the garden* is British in flavor, and our main variety is American, so it now reads *in the backyard* (both reviewers).
- An invented learner error on *sequence* and a culture note on *senior citizen* that only repeated the usage note were removed. Weak synonyms (*conference* for *seminar*, *move* and *dress* for *shift*) were dropped, and *scene* was moved from synonym to "compare" on *sequence*.
- Rejected: removing the note that *the shakes* always takes **the** and a plural verb (the grammar value alone does not say this); calling the keyboard key uncountable because one says *press shift* (key names go without an article, as in *press enter*); dropping *at* and *in the shopping center* (which preposition to use is a real question for learners); and dropping the link to **gear**, which now fits the new sense.

The cross-reference tool adds back-links between entries automatically, but it will not guess which sense of the other entry to use. Four were placed by hand: *seminar* on **lecture**, *sequence* on **scene**, *shed* on **garage**, and *shift* on **change**.

**What did not work.** Two schema mistakes in my first draft (a wrong countability value and the wrong shape for a word-choice note) were caught by the validator before any review and fixed.

## Cycle 3: lint

**What this cycle did.** Ten cycles had passed since the last lint, so the mode selector required one. A lint is a maintenance pass over the whole dictionary and its knowledge base (the `wiki/`, the project's notes and rules). Some checks are done by scripts and some by reading. No entries were written and no senses were changed.

**The script checks.**
- Size caps on the rule files and the wiki: all within limits. Links inside the knowledge base: all 782 pages resolve.
- Cross-references. When one entry names another as a synonym, antonym, or "compare" word, the other entry should point back. The tool added three missing back-links (*salesperson* to **person**, *seafood* to **fish**, *seat belt* to **belt**). It flagged five pairs where it could not tell which sense should carry the link, and I settled them by hand. Three of these came from this morning's first cycle: there I removed *copy*'s links to **borrow** and **rip** and *control*'s link to **take over**, but the other side of each link was still in place. The house rule is that removing a link removes its mirror too, so I removed those three. The other two were placed: *scenery* now compares with **scene**, and *screw* with **nut**.
- Six old claim files (records of which run reserved which words) were cleared.
- Thirty-six words added to the queue since the last lint had no frequency band yet. Bands come only from a vote of three models on one written scale, never from a person or the drafter. The vote cost under one cent. No word split the voters by more than one band, so nothing needed settling by hand, and all 36 are now ready to be built in their turn.
- Three standing reports came out as before. 1,043 pairs where an entry is named as a synonym but may lack a matching sense, left for the review cycles to check. 317 definitions that use their own headword, nearly all harmless (*a walk* defined through *walk*); a review judges each one. And the same six notes that point to entries not yet planned.

**Reviewer precision.** For each reviewer and each kind of issue, "precision" is the share of its issues that I applied rather than rejected. A pair that falls under 30 percent over twenty or more decisions is switched off. Reviewer A stands at 0.85 overall and reviewer B at 0.83. No new pair fell under the line. Reviewer B's spelling-and-format checks are at 0.39 and stay on by the owner's ruling. I added this pass to `wiki/notes/reviewer-precision.md`, along with a line noting that the previous pass's numbers were only in the log.

**The reading checks.** Every wiki page is listed in the index. The open-questions page has nothing open. The curator file (questions left for the owner) has no duplicate lines. The log has an entry for every cycle. I found no contradictions between pages. This morning's branch was deleted automatically when its pull request merged, so it needs no prune line.
