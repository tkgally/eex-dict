**What was added before review.**

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two automated reviewers (reviewer A and reviewer B, models from different labs) raised 38 issues, 3 of them blocking. I applied 34 and rejected 4.
- Blocking: an example said *the common cold is caused by a virus*, a checkable science claim, which our examples must not contain (both reviewers); it now reads *It's only the common cold, nothing serious*. Reviewer B also said *control for* takes no direct object, so it is intransitive; I agreed, matching how *depend on* and *rely on* are coded.
- Three facts were stated twice, against the rule that one fact lives in one field: *common*'s usage note repeated the comparative forms already in the inflections note; *condition*'s translators' note repeated that the situation sense is plural; *control*'s false-friend note repeated the learner error about *check*. Each copy was removed.
- Smaller fixes: *common knowledge* moved to the "shared" sense, where it belongs; the old-fashioned British "low class" sense of *common* now covers things as well as people; *condition*'s "rule" signpost became "requirement", and the medical sense lost a "medicine" field label, since *a heart condition* is an everyday phrase; *control*'s core idea and its feelings sense (which must cover *control yourself*) were rewritten, and two examples that did not fit their definitions were replaced; *copy* lost two weak cross-references (*rip*, *borrow*) and a needless explanation.
- Rejected: a request to mark *mutual friend* with single asterisks (it is named as a phrase, so double is right); a claim that *with the condition that* is not a learner error (it is a common translation from other languages); a request for a "radio" field label (no such label exists in our closed list, and *copy that* is used widely in casual speech); and a claim that copying an idea is making a copy rather than imitating someone.

One back-link the cross-reference tool could not place (*receive* as a synonym of radio *copy*) was resolved by dropping the synonym, since *receive*'s radio sense is about machines picking up signals.
