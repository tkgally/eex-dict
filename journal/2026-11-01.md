# Run report, 2026-11-01

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The mode selector (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from what is most overdue) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue of words still to be added. It allowed up to twelve new entries and US$3 of paid model calls. Nothing was stranded from earlier runs: there were no open pull requests, the five leftover `claude/*` branches had nothing unmerged on them and were already on the owner's list of branches to delete, the inbox was empty, and there were no reader reports of problems with entries.

I took the next twelve band-2 verbs in queue order. Band 2 means the second most important of the dictionary's five importance levels. The twelve are *acquire*, *complain*, *enroll*, *forbid*, *launch*, *pose*, *resolve*, *respond*, *restore*, *restrict*, *resume*, and *reveal*. Each was written from scratch with every field filled in: signposted senses (the short labels that help a reader pick the right meaning), grammar patterns, two to four examples per sense, collocations (words that typically go together), synonyms and opposites, related words, and notes only where a learner would really go wrong. For example, *complain* and *respond* each have learner errors (*complain for the noise*, *respond my email*). *Resume* has a false-friend note: in some languages a word that looks like it means "summarize". *Resolve* and *acquire* have notes on sounds the spelling hides.

**Checks.** All 24 pronunciations, American and British, passed the pronunciation check (agreement between several models plus an open pronouncing dictionary, which is consulted at run time and never stored). All verb forms came from the rules, and *forbade*/*forbidden* came from the exceptions table.

**What the reviewers found and what I decided.** The two reviewing models raised 108 issues, 9 of them blocking (a blocking issue must be settled before an entry can be marked reviewed). I accepted and fixed 97, rejected 11, and sent none to the owner. The main changes:

- *restrict*: the second sense, "stop someone from doing what they want", really meant "limit" and overlapped the first. I merged the two and added the missing reflexive use, *restrict yourself to a few comments*.
- *restore*: the third sense had become a catch-all. I narrowed it to health or position and gave restoring files from a backup its own computing sense.
- *launch* got a sense for *launch yourself at someone*, and *pose* one for *posing to impress people*. Both had been missing.
- Four adaptation notes were removed. Adaptation notes are short notes for future translators. Two of these only listed the senses, and two repeated a learner-error note.

The rejections, each with a logged reason: a request to put *God forbid* under *forbid*. The house rule files an idiom under its first noun, so it belongs under *God*; *forbid* now points there and the phrase is queued for that entry. Requests to hand-mark *forbade* and *forbidden* in examples were also rejected, because the site bolds listed verb forms automatically. So was the claim that *by* + *-ing* (*responded by lowering its prices*) is the wrong pattern label.

The vocabulary gate failed on three definitions that used words outside the defining vocabulary (*reputation*, *investigation*, *rocket*/*missile*/*spacecraft*). I rewrote them in plain words. Thirty-one cross-references needed a matching link back from the other entry, and the tool could not tell which sense should carry it, so I placed them by hand in 29 existing entries (for example, *repair* sense 1 now points back to *restore*).

**Spend.** US$0.40 of the US$3 cycle ceiling and the US$15 daily cap. Reviewer A runs on the owner's subscription at no charge.

**What did not work.** Nothing broke. One small thing: my draft for *acquire* first wrote the American pronunciation without the syllable break that house rule 2 asks for in words like *fire*. A reviewer caught it and the recheck passed.

**What is next.** More band-2 verbs in queue order, then the review backlog. The next lint pass (the regular mechanical and judgment check over everything) is due in nine runs.

**What needs the owner.** Nothing new. The open items in `NEXT.md` under "For the owner" are unchanged.
