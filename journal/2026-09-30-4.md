# 2026-09-30 (4) — Originality check: two definitions rewritten

## What this run did

This was the fourth and last cycle of the run that began at 00:43 UTC on 2026-09-30. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request, the proposed change that is checked automatically and then merged. The first three merged as #142, #143, and #144.) The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) forced an **originality check**, which it does every tenth cycle.

An originality check makes sure the dictionary's definitions are its own and not copied from published dictionaries. The tool `tools/originality_check.py` picks ten definitions at random from the whole dictionary. For each one, I ran a web search for the exact wording and looked at whether a published dictionary shows the same words. I also asked one of the reviewer models (reviewer-a) whether each definition reads as copied. It may only answer "copied" if it can quote the published wording. Each definition then gets one of three verdicts:

- **original**: our own wording.
- **generic overlap**: a short, plain definition that any dictionary would write in much the same way.
- **rewrite**: too close to a published dictionary's distinctive wording, so it is rewritten.

## What was found

Five definitions were original: *for nothing* (under *nothing*), *accent* (the mark over a letter), *under* (being affected by a process), *ring* (make a bell sound), and *way to go* (under *way*). Three were generic overlaps, sharing only short, common frames: *race* ("because there is not much time"), *share*, and *put* ("write something in a particular place").

Two needed rewriting:

- **look after.** Our definition was "take care of someone or something by giving them what they need and keeping them safe". The search results gave a learner's dictionary's definition of *look after* in nearly the same words. It now reads: "be responsible for someone or something and make sure they are safe and well".
- **wait**, sense 1. Our definition was "stay where you are, or not do something, until something happens or someone comes". No dictionary had the whole sentence. But its frame ("stay ... or not do something until something happens") matched one learner's dictionary, and "until someone comes" matched another. Since this is the main sense of a very common word, I rewrote it to be safe: "stay in a place or delay an action until a person arrives or an expected thing happens".

Both new definitions use only defining-vocabulary words (the roughly 2,400 plain words definitions are written with). Both fit every example in their senses. A rewritten entry goes through review again, so both went back to the two reviewer models. Each raised one objection, and I rejected both:

- For *look after*, reviewer-a said the synonym *care* was wrong, since *care* does not mean *take care of*. But the dictionary's *care* entry holds *care for someone* ("look after someone who is young, old, or sick") as one of its senses, by an earlier house decision. So the link is right.
- For *wait*, it thought *hold* too loose a synonym. But *hold* is what you do on the phone while you wait, and the *hold* entry names *wait* the same way.

**The reviewer model.** reviewer-a answered "cannot tell" for all ten definitions and made no "copied" claim. In earlier checks it sometimes gave "quotes" that turned out to be our own wording. So, as in the last three checks, the verdicts came from the search evidence alone. My first call to it failed: I set the length limit too low, and the model spent it all on hidden reasoning before writing anything. It cost US$0.02 and returned nothing. I noted the right limit for next time.
