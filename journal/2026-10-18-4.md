# 2026-10-18, cycle 4 of the 00:43 UTC run: second review of *plate* to *practice*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle: a second reading of entries that had been through the two outside reviewers (the review panel, two language models from other companies) only once. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`) chose review because it was the most overdue kind of work, and the block is always the oldest such entries. This time that meant twenty nouns drafted on 2026-10-06, in alphabetical order: *plate, platform, player, pleasure, plug, pocket, point, poison, policy, politics, pool, port, position, possibility, post, pot, potato, powder, power, practice*. It follows straight on from cycle 1, which reviewed *peace* to *plastic*.

Each entry went to both reviewers again. I read every issue against the entry and either changed the entry (*applied*) or left it as it was (*rejected*, with a logged reason in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`). Nothing was escalated to the owner. This is the run's last cycle: a run has at most four.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

reviewer-a raised 34 issues; reviewer-b raised 3 and gave a verdict on every field. Six entries drew no issues (*pleasure, policy, port, possibility, post, potato*). In all I applied 25 and rejected 12.

Applied:

- ***poison***: both reviewers said the figurative use (*Jealousy is poison to a friendship*) is common enough to be its own sense; it had been tucked into a usage note. It is now sense 2, *something that harms or damages a relationship, a feeling, or a situation*, with three examples, and the usage note is gone. When two reviewers flag the same thing, that is a strong signal.
- ***pot***: the second sense was *a container for keeping something in, or for growing a plant in*, which made *pot* sound like a word for any container and listed *jar* as a synonym. It now covers only pots for plants, and the example of pens kept *in a pot on her desk*, which is not natural American English, is gone. The explanation on the drug sense only repeated a synonym, so it was removed.
- ***power***: the *physical force* sense used *This car has much more power*, which is really engine output, and the collocation *full power*, which is about machines; both were replaced (*She kicked the ball with all her power*). The mathematics sense no longer says the small raised number always shows repeated multiplication. *The powers that be* is now labeled humorous rather than informal.
- ***pocket***: the phrase *out of pocket* carried a British label, but its explanation also gave an American meaning (*away and impossible to contact*). The label is gone and the explanation now says that the money meaning is the British one.
- ***platform***: the computing sense said *a large online service*, but its own example spoke of small businesses; *large* is gone.
- ***politics*** and ***plug***: wording that is mainly British (*our politics teacher*, *a bath plug*) was replaced with neutral or American wording (*she teaches politics at a university*, *a bathtub plug*).

Rejected, with the main reasons:

- ***pool***: reviewer-b said the entry lacks the sense *a group of people or things available to use* (*a car pool*, *a pool of workers*). That sense, and the game played with balls on a table, come from a different word than the water *pool*, so by the house rule on homographs they belong in a second entry, `pool-n-2`, not in this one. The queue tool cannot yet add a second homograph by itself (a known tooling gap), so I listed it in `NEXT.md` with the other homographs waiting for a hand claim.
- *Make a pot of coffee* is ordinary American English; the tea-or-coffee sense stands.
- *Electricity* stays a synonym of *power* in its energy sense (*The power went off*).
- The formal labels on *position* (a job; an opinion), the American label on *step up to the plate*, and *theory* as the opposite of *practice* stand.

One of my own rewrites used *spoils*, a word outside the defining vocabulary, and the vocabulary check caught it; it now says *damages*.
