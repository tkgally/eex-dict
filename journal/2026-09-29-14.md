# 2026-09-29 (14) — Build: twelve verbs, *threaten* to *trust*

## What this run did

This was the second cycle of the run that began at 18:44 UTC. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request; the first cycle's review merged as pull request #138.) The mode selector chose **build**: new entries taken from the queue of words waiting to be written, in order, from band 1, the most common words.

I drafted twelve verbs: *threaten, throw, tie, tire, touch, trade, train, translate, transport, travel, treat,* and *trust*. Together they have 47 senses, 2 phrases, and 23 learner errors (mistakes learners of many languages make).

- ***throw*** has ten senses: through the air, putting something down carelessly, moving your body (*threw her arms around him*), making someone fall, putting someone into a bad state (*threw the town into a panic*), confusing someone, holding a party, *throw a tantrum*, sending light or a look, and moving a switch. A short comparison explains *throw*, *toss*, and *hurl*. A usage note says that getting rid of something needs *throw away* or *throw out*.
- ***tie*** covers string and knots, clothes that tie at the back, *be tied to* (connected with, or not free to leave), and equal scores in a game.
- ***touch*** includes being moved by kindness (*I was touched by her letter*), *hardly touched his dinner*, and the phrase *touch and go*. A translator's note says that some languages use the word for *touch* to mean playing an instrument.
- ***train*** separates teaching skills, learning them (*trained as a teacher*), sport, aiming a camera, and shaping a plant. It warns against *training my English*: the English word for that is *practice*.
- ***treat*** has five senses: behaving toward someone, *treat something as*, medical care (compared with *cure*), paying for someone (*treat you to lunch*), and chemicals on wood or water.
- ***trust*** has a sub-sense for *trust someone with a secret*, the polite *I trust you had a good trip*, and the phrase *trust me*.
- The others: *threaten* has the pattern *threaten to leave* and the use *it's threatening to rain*. *tire* notes that *tired* and *tiring* differ. *trade* has a comparison with *exchange* and *swap*. *translate* is compared with *interpret*. *transport* notes that the verb and noun are stressed differently. *travel* includes *this cake travels well*.

Every word form came from the house rules or its table of irregular forms. All 24 pronunciations were confirmed by the pronunciation panel and, for American English, by an open pronouncing dictionary.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The two outside reviewers raised 38 issues, 21 of them blocking (a blocking issue must be settled before an entry can be published). I applied 26 and rejected 12; none needed the owner.

Accepted:
- *throw a tantrum* had been placed under the party sense of *throw*. Both reviewers objected, so it is now a sense of its own.
- An example under "put someone into a bad state", *thrown out of work*, really means losing a job. It was replaced.
- The *threaten* explanation said the phrase after **with** names the harm. In *threaten someone with a knife* it names the thing used, so the explanation now covers both.
- The note on *tie* and British *draw* moved from the sense into the usage note.
- *translate* no longer says **interpret** and **translate** split neatly between speech and writing.
- *transport* no longer claims that *export* changes its stress like *record* does in American English.
- Smaller fixes: "informal" removed from *swap*, a "formal" label taken off *train a camera on*, and a translator's note on *train* made less sweeping.

Rejected:
- Three pronunciation objections that the project has already settled: *tire* has two syllables, like *fire*; American transcriptions carry no length mark, so *treat* is /trit/; and first-syllable stress on *translate* is already listed as a variant.
- A claim that *No one can touch her at chess* was defined backwards.
- The claim that *touch and go* should be hyphenated or filed elsewhere.
- The claim that *throw a party* is not informal.

The cross-reference tool adds a matching link in the other entry for each new synonym or comparison. It put two links on the wrong sense, which I moved by hand: *tie* in *connect* now sits on "show that things are related", and *transport* in *carry* on the vehicle sense. Two pairs had one link type on one side and another type on the other. I made both pairs synonyms on each side: *travel*/*go* and *trust*/*believe*, with a note on *believe in* versus *believe*.

The vocabulary check found six words in definitions that are not in the dictionary's core word list, such as *knot*, *prepare*, and *target*. I rewrote those definitions without them.
