## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 31 issues. I applied 12 and rejected 19; every decision is logged with a reason in the decision record. None needed to go to you.

Applied, among others:

- *branch*: both reviewers said *chapter* is not a synonym of *branch* in the sense "one part of a subject, an organization, or a family". They are right (a *chapter* is a local group of a club, a much narrower thing). I removed the link from *branch* and also the matching link back from *chapter*, since otherwise the next housekeeping pass would put it back.
- *boy*, the pet sense, said "especially a pet dog or a horse". *Good boy!* is said mostly to dogs; the horse is gone.
- *boss*: the usage note said people "rarely" call their boss *boss* to their face. That is too strong; the note now says some people do, in a friendly, informal way (*Thanks, boss!*). The note for translators on the false friend *chef* now says a chef is a skilled cook in a restaurant or hotel kitchen, not "only a cook in a restaurant".
- *bottle*: the senses for drinking alcohol (*hit the bottle*) and, in British English, courage (*lose your bottle*) had no lists of typical word combinations. Both have them now. The first definition was reworded to avoid ending on a stranded *in*.
- *box*: the post-office-box example now uses the form people actually write, *our PO box*.

Rejected, among others:

- Moving the idiom *to boot* ("as well") out of the *boot* entry. The house rule puts an idiom under its keyword noun whatever its grammar; this is a settled point.
- Four objections to *bread*: that *Can I have a bread?* and *two breads* are sometimes acceptable. The entry already says *breads* means kinds of bread; a single loaf or slice called *a bread* is a real and common learner error.
- Calling *the bottom of the class* and *the bottom of the garden* countable. These uses only occur with *the*, in the singular.
- Calling *use your brain* uncountable. *Your brain* there is an ordinary singular countable noun.
