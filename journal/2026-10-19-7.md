# 2026-10-19, run at 12:44 UTC, cycle 3: originality check

## What this run did

This was an **originality** cycle, the fortieth. Every tenth run the scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job) forces a check that our wording has not drifted toward a published dictionary's; this was run 470. The tool (`tools/originality_check.py`) picked ten fields at random from finished entries: six sense definitions, two phrase definitions, and two usage notes (a usage note is a short paragraph on a point of use).

The ten fields were:

- *tip* (noun), sense 1: "the pointed or thin end of something"
- *joy* (noun), sense 3: "success in getting what you want or need" (as in British *no joy*)
- *field* (noun), the usage note on *field* versus British *pitch*
- *shut* (verb), the usage note on *shut* versus *close*
- *survive* (verb), sense 4: "live longer than someone, especially a member of your family"
- *sentence* (verb), sense 1: "officially give someone a punishment in a court of law"
- *rise* (verb), sense 3: "appear in the sky"
- *happen* (verb), sense 2: "be done to someone or affect them, often in a bad way"
- *ever so*, a phrase in *ever* (adverb): "very"
- *put your feet up*, a phrase in *foot* (noun): "sit or lie down and rest"

For each, I searched the web for the exact wording and looked only at whether a published dictionary shows the same or nearly the same text. Nothing was copied from any source; only the verdicts are recorded, in `reviews/originality/2026-10-19.md`.

## What the searches found

No field matched a published dictionary. Two are **original** (no similar wording anywhere: the *joy* sense and the *field* note). Eight are **generic overlap**, meaning plain wording that any dictionary would write in much the same way. Examples: *ever so* is defined as "very" everywhere; *sentence* has to use the words *officially*, *court*, and *punishment*, but a learner's dictionary frames it differently ("say officially in court that somebody is to receive a particular punishment"); a learner's dictionary also notes that *shut* and *close* both go with doors, windows, and eyes, which is a plain fact about the words, said in a different frame. No field needs a rewrite.

## The reviewer question

The routine also asks one outside reviewer model whether each field reads as copied. I asked reviewer-a. Its first reply came back empty: the model spent its whole token allowance on hidden reasoning before writing anything. I sent the same question again with that hidden reasoning turned off, and it answered "cannot tell" for all ten, each with a one-line reason saying the wording is too plain to trace to one source. That agrees with the searches. In earlier checks this reviewer often answered "copied" without a real quote, so search results alone decided the verdicts; this time it made no such claims.
