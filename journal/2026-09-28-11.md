# 2026-09-28 (11) — Review: five conjunctions and six early verbs

## What this run did

This was the third cycle of the scheduled run that began at 12:44 UTC. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request, merged before the next begins.) The first two cycles, a review of the modal verbs (#118) and a lint pass (#119), had already merged.

The mode selector chose **review**: a second reading by the reviewer panel (two outside language models that check every field) of entries read only once. I took the eleven oldest: the conjunctions *when, where, whether, while,* and *yet*, and the verbs *appeal, appear, apply, argue, arrest,* and *arrive*, all written on 2026-09-22.

The verbs were correct but thin. None had a learner-error box or a usage note. Before sending anything to the panel, I added material by hand:

- **Verbs**:
  - The mistakes learners really make: *The idea appeals me*, *I applied a job*, *He applied for three universities* (should be *to*), *She appeared nervously*, *She argued her brother*, *arrested because of stealing*, *We arrived to the hotel*, *arrived to home*.
  - Short usage notes: *apply for* the thing you want, *apply to* the place you ask; *arrive in* a city, *arrive at* a building.
  - Missing senses: *appeal to* someone's feelings, *appear* for something beginning to exist (*Cracks began to appear*), *arrive* for deliveries, babies, and success (*she had finally arrived*).
  - One example named a real city, which house rules keep out of examples; it now says "the city".
- **Conjunctions**:
  - *when* for a sudden event (*I was just leaving when the phone rang*) and for short forms (*when necessary*, *when in doubt*), and the phrase *say when*.
  - *while* with an *-ing* form (*while driving*), and the very common error *During I was waiting*.
  - *yet* with the error *Although he was tired, yet he kept working*.
  - Some signposts were too long. A signpost is the short label that helps a reader pick a sense; the house limit is three words. *yet* has only one sense, so its signpost was cleared, as the style guide requires.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

The reviewers raised 43 issues. I applied 33 and rejected 10.

The main finding was a real mistake of mine. I had added a sense to *when* and *where* for reported questions (*I don't know where she lives*), with the word-order error *Do you know where is the station?* Both reviewers said independently that this use belongs to the question words *when* and *where*, which the dictionary treats as adverbs, and not to the conjunctions. The entries already pointed to those adverb entries for questions, so they contradicted themselves. I removed the senses and recorded the rule in `NEXT.md` so later runs follow it. *when* and *where* as adverbs are already in the queue, and the note tells whoever drafts them to include this use and its error. (*whether* is different: it has no adverb and stays a conjunction.)

Other changes I accepted:

- **Definitions**: *where*, *while*, and *yet* each had a contrast definition made of loose synonyms ("but, at the same time"). All three now use the house pattern for function words: "used to set one fact against another...".
- **appeal**: the most common senses (*be attractive*, *ask the public for help*) now come before the legal one.
- **argue**: I had claimed that *discuss* means talking calmly. It does not always, so that claim is gone.
- Smaller fixes: collocations for *arrest* (*arrest the decline*); the *arrive* sense for times and events now keeps its subject restriction in the explanation, as house rules require; a clearer example for *appear* on stage.

What I rejected:

- A claim that *appealed for witnesses to come forward* is wrong. It is a standard pattern.
- A claim that *doesn't apply in my case* is wrong. It is natural English.
- A claim that the sudden-event *when* is not a sense of its own. The clauses swap roles there, and many languages translate it differently.
- Straight apostrophes and sentence-style summaries, which are house style.
