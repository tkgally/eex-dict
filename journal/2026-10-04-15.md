## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, review every field (neither is from Anthropic). They raised 72 issues, 47 of them "blocking", which means they must be settled before an entry is published. I applied 47 and rejected 25, each with a logged reason.

The useful catches:

- The gymnastics sense of *horse* said people "jump over" a four-legged piece of equipment. It now covers both the vaulting horse and the pommel horse.
- *A hole in the wall* is a small, simple place that is easy to walk past. Being very good is not part of its meaning.
- *In the hole* means having lost money, not only owing it.
- Sense 3 of *hour* (*opening hours*) was marked plural only, but *an office hour* exists. It is now "usually plural".
- My usage note for *hobby* said watching television is never a hobby. That was too strong, so it was softened.
- *A host family* belongs to the sense about guests, not the sense about events. I moved it.
- A note in *honey* used the word *vowel*. Pronunciation notes avoid technical words, so I rewrote it.

The main rejections:

- Both reviewers wanted *hope against hope* moved to the verb. In that phrase the second *hope* is a noun, and the house rule files an idiom under its first noun, so it stays.
- Reviewer-a said *play at home* in sport is an adverb use. There, *at home* is a preposition with the noun, so the sense stays.
- *Hole in one* without hyphens is a standard spelling.

After the panel, the cross-reference tool reported two link-type mismatches. *Home* called *house* a synonym while *house* called *home* a "compare", and *host* and *guest* had the same problem. I made both pairs "compare" links with short notes.
