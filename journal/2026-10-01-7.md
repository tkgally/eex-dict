This was the third cycle of the run that began at 06:44 UTC on 2026-10-01. (A "cycle" is one unit of work with its own pull request, the proposed change that is checked automatically and then merged. The first two cycles merged as #162, a review of the verbs *drop* to *entertain*, and #163, twelve new nouns from *brain* to *building*.)

The mode selector (the script that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose **review**: a second reading of entries that have been through only one round of outside review. I took the next twelve by creation date, verbs written on 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25: *examine, excite, exercise, exist, expand, expect, express, fall, farm, fear, feed,* and *feel*.

Before sending them to the reviewers I read each one myself. They were already full: *fall* has nine senses and seven linked phrasal verbs, *feel* has eight senses. I added:

- A ninth sense to *feel*: *feel for someone*, "understand how unhappy someone is and feel sorry for them" (*I really feel for you*). It is common, and the entry did not have it.
- A learner error on *feel*: *I feel boring at home* for *I feel bored*. (A learner error is a mistake learners of many languages make, shown with a correction and a one-line reason.)
- A link from *feel* to the phrasal verb *feel up to* (*I don't feel up to going out*). It has no entry yet, so it was added to the queue.
- A learner error on *examine*: *I have to examine next week* for *I have to take an exam*. The teacher examines; the student takes the exam.

I also fixed one thing no reviewer raised. *exist*'s note for translators showed the wrong form *Exist many ways* in italics. A settled house rule (a "fence" in `NEXT.md`) says wrong forms appear only in the learner-error box, where this one already is. The note now gives only the correct sentence.

## What the reviewers found and what I decided

Two outside language models, reviewer-a and reviewer-b, read every field of every entry. They raised 29 issues, 16 of them marked blocking. I read each one against the entry and decided it myself: 12 applied, 17 rejected, none sent on to the owner.

Applied:

- ***excite***: the note for translators said English *excite* is "safe in ordinary conversation" because, unlike similar words in some languages, it is not sexual. That went too far, because *excite* can have the sexual meaning in English too. The note now says so, and that the everyday use is for happy, eager feelings.
- ***exercise***: the learner-error note said "*exercise* is for the body", which contradicted the entry's own sense of using a right or a power (*exercise your right to vote*). It now says "in this sense". A clumsy phrase in the note comparing *exercise*, *practice*, and *train* was fixed.
- ***fall***, sense 3 (prices falling): *decrease* was listed both as a synonym and as a word to compare. I removed the "compare" link here and its mirror on *decrease*. If only one side were removed, the next lint pass (the regular tidying run) would put it back. On sense 1, *drop* is still both a synonym and a word to compare, because both are true. Notes now explain the difference: *drop* can also take an object (*I dropped the cup*), and *fall* cannot. Sense 9 (hair falling over the shoulders) got its missing collocations.
- ***feel like*** ("want") lost its informal label. *I don't feel like cooking* is ordinary everyday English. Its grammar is now marked intransitive, the way this dictionary marks *fear for*.
- ***expect***: *You can't expect much from a cheap hotel* was moved out of the sense about duty, where it did not fit, and replaced with *The company expects a lot from its workers*.

Rejected, with reasons logged:

- Five objections that a translator's note opens with the headword in quotation marks, and two that the American transcriptions of *feed* lack a length mark. Both points are settled house practice.
- That *I expect you're hungry* (meaning "I suppose") is not mainly British. It is mainly British, and the region label in this dictionary means "mainly used there".
- That *fall asleep* and *fall silent* are not linking uses. They are.
- That *fear* sense 2 needs a formal label. Only the sentence-opening *I fear...* is formal, and the explanation already says so.
- That *feed* (to eat) should not be "used mostly of animals and babies". "Mostly" is already a hedge.

**Cross-references.** No back-links were added; one "compare" pair was removed from both sides.
