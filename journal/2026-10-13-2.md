# 2026-10-13, run at 00:44 UTC, cycle 2: review of *issue* to *kitchen*

## What this cycle did

This was a **review** cycle. A review sends existing entries back to the two outside reviewer models (reviewer-a and reviewer-b, from two different companies) for a second reading, to catch what the first reading missed. The scheduler (`tools/next_mode.py`, the script that picks each cycle's job from how long each kind of work has waited) chose it after this run's build cycle. No drafts were waiting, so I took the oldest entries that had been through the panel only once.

The scheduler allowed up to twenty entries. I took seventeen: the nouns written on 4 October at 21:05 and on 5 October at 00:48. They were *issue*, *item*, *jacket*, *jail*, *job*, *joke*, *journey*, *joy*, *judge*, *judgment*, *juice*, *justice*, *key*, *keyboard*, *kind*, *king*, and *kitchen*.

## What the reviewers found, and what I decided

The reviewers raised 47 issues, 39 of them "blocking" (an entry cannot stay marked as reviewed until each one has a decision). I applied 24 and rejected 23. None needed to go to the owner. Three entries came back with no issues: *judgment*, *keyboard*, and *kitchen*. Every decision is logged, with a short reason, in `reviews/decisions.jsonl`.

What I changed:

- ***judge*.** Sense 3 (*a good judge of character*) said "someone who has the knowledge or skill to give an opinion". A good judge is someone who decides well, not just someone who can give an opinion, so it now says "someone who can decide well how good, true, or valuable something is". The core idea (the one-sentence summary at the top) was changed to match. The link to *justice* now has a short note saying that a justice is a judge in some high courts, because *justice* also means fairness.
- ***juice*.** The informal sense said "electricity, or fuel for a car". It now says "electric power, especially in a battery, or fuel", which fits the example *My phone is running out of juice*.
- ***justice*.** The legal-system sense said only that people are "judged and punished". It now says the system "decides legal cases and punishes crime", which also covers cases that are not about crime.
- ***king*.** The sense in *the king of rock music* was too broad. It now says "the person or thing thought to be the best or most powerful of a particular type or in a particular place".
- ***jacket*.** The potato sense (*jacket potato*) is mainly British, so it now has a British label. *Life jacket* was removed from the coat sense's word combinations, because a life jacket is not a short coat.
- ***kind*.** The phrase *respond in kind* was defined with the word *kind* itself, which the house style does not allow. It now says "answer or treat someone in the same way that they treated you".
- ***joke*.** The note on the learner error *He said a funny joke* is softer: *say a joke* "sounds unnatural to most speakers" instead of being simply wrong. A comparison link between *joke* and *crack* was removed. I removed it from both entries, because a link removed from only one side comes back the next time the cross-reference tool runs.
- Smaller fixes: *item*, *jail*, *journey*, *joy*, and *key* each had one note, example, or list of word combinations corrected.

What I rejected, and why:

- Both reviewers wanted *kind of* (*I'm kind of tired*) moved out of the noun *kind* into its own adverb entry. The project's settled rule is that an idiom stays under its keyword, whatever its part of speech, so it stays.
- reviewer-a said *What kind of a car* is standard, not just informal. The entry already says it is common in speech and avoided in careful writing, which is accurate.
- reviewer-a called British *jail* an old-fashioned spelling of *gaol*. *Jail* is the ordinary current spelling in Britain.
- Three label complaints (*good job* as American, *justice* meaning a judge) went against the rule that a region label means *mainly used there*.
- reviewer-b raised two "blocking" issues on *job* while saying in the same breath that the text was correct.
