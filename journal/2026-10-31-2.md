# Run report, 2026-10-31 (second run)

## Cycle 1: build

**What the cycle did.** The selector (the tool that picks each cycle's kind of work) chose *build*: writing new entries from the queue, the ordered list of words still to write. Its reason was that build had the largest "debt" of the eligible kinds of work; the day's budget had US$14.04 left and no entries were waiting in draft. It claimed twelve band-2 verbs (band 2 is the second most important frequency group) in queue order: *obtain*, *occupy*, *overcome*, *paste*, *pause*, *permit*, *persuade*, *phone*, *picture*, *plug*, *pop*, and *post*. All twelve were drafted from scratch, checked, reviewed by both reviewers, corrected, and marked `reviewed`, which means they will appear on the public site.

Pre-flight found no open pull requests, no entry reports from the public, and nothing in the owner's inbox. Five old branches with no unmerged work remain; each already has a line in the curator file asking for it to be deleted, since this tool set cannot delete branches.

**Checks before review.** The inflection tool generated every verb form from its spelling rules and the exceptions table (*overcame*, *overcome*; doubled consonants in *permitted*, *plugged*, *popped*). The pronunciation check, a panel of three models plus the open CMU pronouncing dictionary consulted at run time, agreed with all 24 transcriptions (American and British for each verb).

**What the reviewers found.** The two reviewers (reviewer A, a Claude model; reviewer B, a Google model) raised 99 issues, 6 of them blocking (an issue that must be settled before an entry can be marked reviewed). Every one was decided by hand: 92 applied, 7 rejected, none escalated to you. The main changes:

- *occupy*: an example, *keep the kids occupied*, really showed the adjective *occupied*, not the verb; it was removed with its collocation. An example giving a fact about sleep (a third of our lives) broke the rule against checkable facts and was replaced.
- *overcome*: the translator's note claimed feelings take *with* and smoke takes *by*; in fact *overcome by fear* is normal too. The note now says so, and the feeling-or-smoke sense moved ahead of the *defeat* sense because learners meet it more often.
- Coverage: *pause* gained a sense for stopping a project, payments, or a membership for a while; *pop* gained *the cork popped* and *pop the trunk*; *post* gained *post bail* (labeled American and law) and sports scores; *phone* gained the informal phrase *phone it in* (do a job with little effort).
- Most minor issues were about formatting rules the reviewers are given: words named inside a translator's note must be marked in bold and illustrations in italics, and participle phrases such as *easily obtained* belong in the "phrase" collocation group. These were all applied; next time they will be drafted that way from the start.

**What was rejected, and why.** *Keep someone posted* was blocking for reviewer B, who wanted it under *post*; the house keyword rule puts an idiom with no noun under its first verb, *keep*, so it was rejected here and noted for *keep* when that entry is next reviewed. *Pop the question* goes under *question* by the same rule. Two requests to move *usually passive* out of the verb patterns were rejected because it is in the closed list of patterns. *Post something off* was kept: it is ordinary British usage.

**Cross-references.** The cross-reference tool adds a back-link in the other entry when it can tell which sense to attach it to. In 23 cases it could not. Ten were placed by hand where the link genuinely helps (for example, *glue* and *stick* now list *paste*; *let* lists *permit*; *defeat* and *beat* list *overcome*). The other thirteen were very general words (*put*, *send*, *pay*, *stop*, *exist*) that would mislead as synonyms of the new verbs in both directions, so they were removed from the new entries instead. Two phrasal verbs were added to the queue: *phone in* and *plug away*.

**Spend.** US$0.40 of the day's US$15 budget (reviewer B and the pronunciation panel; reviewer A runs at no charge). The day's total is US$1.36.

**What did not work.** Nothing failed. One definition used *social media*, which the vocabulary check read as the word *medium*, outside the defining vocabulary; it was reworded to *a website or an app*.

**What is next.** More band-2 verbs in queue order, or whatever the selector picks; the next whole-dictionary lint pass is due in seven runs.

**What needs you.** Nothing new from this cycle. The standing items are in `NEXT.md` under "For the owner".

## Cycle 2: site

**What the cycle did.** The selector chose *site*: rebuilding the public website from the entries and checking it in a real browser. Its reason was the same scheduling rule as before, that site work had built up the most "debt" (time since it was last done, weighted by how often it should be done) among the kinds of work allowed; the last site check was on 2026-10-29. This kind of work makes no paid calls, so its budget was zero.

**The build.** The site builder (`tools/build_site.py`) turns every reviewed entry into web pages in the `docs/` folder, which is never committed to the repository; GitHub builds the published site separately from the same entries. It wrote 3,423 entries onto 2,885 headword pages (words such as *post*, which have both a noun and a verb entry, share a page). No draft entries were held back, because there are none, and there are no renamed entries needing redirect pages.

**The check.** The browser check (`tools/site_check.py`) opens the built site in a headless browser, that is, a real browser running without a screen, at phone width (360 pixels) and at desktop width, and runs 79 tests: that pages load without script errors, that the search finds phrases and suggests corrections for misspellings, that the dark color scheme works, that the sense menu on long entries jumps to the right sense, that a cross-reference to an entry not yet written is shown in grey rather than as a broken link, and that no page on a phone needs sideways scrolling. All 79 passed, the sideways-scroll test across 2,891 pages. I also looked at the pages for the twelve verbs built in the first cycle: their bold and italic marks render as marks rather than as stray asterisks, and the new phrase *phone it in* appears on the *phone* page.

**What did not work.** Nothing; no tool or template needed a change.

**Spend.** US$0. The day's total stays at US$1.36 of US$15.

**What is next.** Whatever the selector picks; the next whole-dictionary lint pass is due in six runs.

**What needs you.** Nothing new from this cycle.
